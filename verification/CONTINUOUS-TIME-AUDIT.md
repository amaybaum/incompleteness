# The continuous-time audit (post-Level III, dynamics thread)

Level III completed the algebra, the state space and one **discrete** dynamics: the reversible
finite-range substratum update acts on the quasilocal algebra as an isometric `*`-automorphism.
A continuous-time Hamiltonian law was recorded there as additional structure, on the strength of a
countermodel: two Hermitian generators whose flows agree at every integer time and differ at
`t = 1/2` (`flow_genZero`, `flow_genTwoPi`, `flows_agree_integer`, `flows_differ_half` in
`OIBridge/RegionLimit.lean`).

That countermodel proves **non-uniqueness**, and only that. It does not show that no acceptable
interpolation exists. The question this thread asks is therefore the existence question, stated
algebraically so that no representation is assumed:

> Does the OI-induced discrete quasilocal automorphism admit a local or quasilocal Hamiltonian
> embedding?

Four questions are kept apart and not conflated:

1. **Existence.** Does a continuous flow reaching the one-step map exist at all?
2. **Locality.** Can its generator be local or quasilocal, rather than a wildly nonlocal logarithm?
3. **Time dependence.** Can it be generated by one time-independent `H`, or only by `H(t)`?
4. **Uniqueness.** Is the generator selected? Already answered: **no**, by the countermodel above.

Existence alone is cheap at any finite stage — a finite-dimensional unitary always has a Hermitian
logarithm — but that logarithm can be nonlocal, and the choices need not be compatible as the
region grows. A finite-stage `log U` theorem would therefore close nothing. There is also a
positive reason not to assume the infinite-lattice answer is automatic: locality-preserving
discrete automorphisms can sit in different topological components, the shift being the standard
example, so being continuously connected to the identity in the appropriate local class is a real
condition rather than a formality.

## First entry: the update is a depth-two local circuit, and each layer is driven locally

### What the update is

The substratum dynamics is second order in time. SM's general form is

> `φ(n, t+1) = C φ(n,t) + Σ_j M^(j) [φ(n+ê_j, t) + φ(n−ê_j, t)] + D φ(n, t−1)`,

and **reversibility forces `D = −I_K`**. So the phase-space map on the per-site pair
`(prev, cur)` — which is SM's own variable, `F : (x(t−1), x(t)) → (x(t), x(t+1))` — is

> `leap : (p, c) ↦ (c, F(c) − p)`

for a neighbourhood function `F`. The `− p` is not a feature of a particular rule; it is what
reversibility leaves.

### The factorization

`OIBridge/SecondOrderCircuit.lean` proves, for an **arbitrary** site type and an **arbitrary** `F`:

| Fact | Kernel |
|---|---|
| `leap = swapLayer ∘ shear`, with `shear (p,c) = (F c − p, c)` and `swapLayer (p,c) = (c,p)` | `leap_eq_swap_shear` |
| Each layer is an involution | `shear_shear`, `swapLayer_swapLayer` |
| Reversibility as a consequence, not an assumption | `leapEquiv`, `leap_leap_symm`, `leap_symm_leap` |
| Each layer is a product of single-site gates, themselves involutions | `gate_gate`, `swapGate_swapGate` |
| **The gates commute** | `gate_comm`, `swapGate_comm` |
| A layer restricted to a finite region is the honest finite composite of its gates | `gateList_eq_shearOn`, `gateList_eq_shear_of_mem`, `gateList_eq_self_of_notMem` |

The commutation has a one-line reason, recorded as `curOf_gate`: a shear gate **writes** only its
own site's previous component and **reads** only current components, which no gate writes. That is
also why the factorization survives everything the rule might do — it does not use linearity, the
alphabet, the dimension, the neighbourhood structure, or state-independence of the coupling. SM
states separately that bijectivity of the phase-space map holds "regardless of whether the coupling
is state-dependent", and the same generality applies here.

The swap layer is **on-site**: it exchanges two internal registers at one site. It is not a lattice
translation, so no shift arises, and the depth-two circuit is in the trivial component of the
locality-preserving class rather than a nontrivial one.

### The drive

An involution `g` lifted to the algebra is a self-adjoint unitary, and `p = (1 − g)/2` is a
projection. The exponential of `t π p` is therefore a **polynomial** — `p * p = p` collapses the
series — so the one-parameter unitary is exact and needs no functional calculus:

> `unit g t = 1 + (exp(π i t) − 1) • p`,  `unit g 0 = 1`,  `unit g 1 = g`,
> `unit g t * unit g s = unit g (t+s)`.

Kernel: `proj_mul_proj`, `unit_zero`, `unit_one`, `unit_mul_unit`, `star_unit`,
`star_unit_mul_unit`. Conjugation by it, `flow`, is a `*`-automorphism at every time
(`flow_mul`, `flow_add_map`, `flow_star`, `flow_one_eq_one`), and `gate_drive` collects the
single-gate statement. For a commuting family the composite is again a one-parameter group and
reaches conjugation by the product at time one: `unit_comm`, `flow_flow_comm`, `flowList_add`,
`flowList_one`, `layer_drive`.

### Why this is an infinite-volume statement

The gates are supplied as elements of the **quasilocal algebra of the infinite lattice**, not of a
finite lattice's matrix algebra. An involutive permutation of a finite region's configurations has
a permutation matrix that is a self-adjoint unitary (`permMat_involutive`), and `stage` carries it
into the completion: `localGate Λ σ = stage Λ (permMat σ)` satisfies `g * g = 1` and `star g = g`
in `Quasilocal` (`localGate_isGate`). The generator and the unitary lie in the image of the **same
finite region's stage** — `proj_localGate_mem_stage`, `unit_localGate_mem_stage` — so each is a
bounded, strictly local element of the infinite algebra rather than a formal infinite sum. Only
finitely many gates of a layer move a given local observable, which is the content of the
`gateList` lemmas above.

`quasilocal_drive` assembles it: on the quasilocal algebra, two layers of commuting local gate
involutions, driven over `[0,1]` and `[1,2]`, give a drive that starts at the identity, agrees with
itself at the junction, and at time two is conjugation by `W · S` — the one-step map — with each
layer flow a one-parameter group and `W · S` a self-adjoint unitary.

### The answers, and the one that is not given

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| 1. Existence | **yes**, and not by the cheap finite-stage logarithm |
| 2. Locality | **yes**: each generator is bounded and supported in one finite region |
| 3. Time dependence | **piecewise constant across the two layers**. A single time-independent generator is **not** obtained |
| 4. Uniqueness | **no**, by the frozen `RegionLimit` countermodel |
| A shift-type obstruction | **none arises**: depth two is the trivial component, and the swap is on-site |

### What is not claimed

That a single **time-independent** local Hamiltonian reaches the one-step map — the drive here is
piecewise constant in time, and nothing in the module bears on whether the two pieces can be
merged. That the drive is unique, in any sense; the frozen countermodel says it is not. That
continuous time is thereby part of the OI core: it remains additional structure, and this entry
shows only that the structure is available, locally and exactly, rather than that OI supplies it.
That anything here transports to a continuum limit — the lattice spacing stays fundamental. That
the flow has been connected to a physical time parameter.
