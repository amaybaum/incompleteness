The failed steps

The old anticommutator identity does not force each arbitrary commuting
operator coefficient to vanish: m=I and p=-epsilon give m*epsilon+p=0
with both coefficients nonzero. Having zero onsite diagonal also does not
exclude same-parity offsite hopping. Finally, squaring a chiral first-order
operator can produce a nonzero diagonal: D=[[0,1],[-1,0]] is chiral for
epsilon=diag(1,-1), while D^2=-I. Thus the diagonal of the original
second-order wave operator cannot by itself be identified with a
first-order mass term. These are substantive missing identifications,
not missing simp calls.

Recovered statements and hypotheses

1. In an associative unital ring with no additive 2-torsion, fix an
   involution epsilon, an epsilon-odd D, and any epsilon-even perturbation
   C. Then {D+C,epsilon}=0 iff C=0. C may include same-parity offsite
   terms; this is stronger than an onsite-only statement.

2. For any parity-graded site set and an arbitrary ring-valued kernel A,
   entrywise epsilon-chirality holds iff every equal-parity entry of A
   vanishes. No finiteness is needed, because multiplication by the
   diagonal grading is defined entrywise without an infinite sum.

3. Fix any opposite-parity hopping kernel D. Adding arbitrary onsite
   coefficients c_i preserves exact epsilon-chirality iff all c_i vanish.
   This needs no uniformity, no nonempty-site assumption, and no presence
   of both parity sectors.

4. For c_i=m*epsilon_i+p with uniform ring coefficients, the further
   conclusion m=p=0 follows when BOTH parity sectors occur. Coefficient
   independence is proved from these sectors, not assumed. The converse
   is also proved, and separately named directional witnesses are
   supplied for the manuscript biconditionals.

The arithmetic hypothesis is absence of additive 2-torsion, not merely
characteristic different from 2. Z/6 is a counterexample to the weaker
description; odd composite Z/9 is allowed. The finite ring Z/qZ satisfies
the stated hypothesis exactly when q is odd. No field, integral-domain,
nearest-neighbor, translational-invariance, or finite-site hypothesis is
needed for the recovered entry/onsite theorem.

Why this is not tautological encoding

OddSupported is the independent geometric condition that equal-parity
kernel entries vanish. ChiralEntries is the algebraic anticommutator
equation. Their equivalence is derived by parity case analysis and
doubling cancellation. The onsite coefficients are arbitrary input data;
their vanishing is derived from diagonal chiral equations. The uniform
coefficients are identified using two distinct parity sectors. Neither
chirality nor coefficient uniqueness is included among the conclusions'
premises. The result remains relative to a fixed hopping family; selecting
that family from the physical substratum is a separate problem.
