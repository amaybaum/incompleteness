## 1. Restricted chirality

I independently derived the operator identity. In a unital associative ring, if `D ε = −ε D` and `C ε = ε C`, then `{D+C,ε}=2Cε`. If `ε²=1` and the additive group has no 2-torsion, vanishing of this anticommutator implies `Cε=0` and then `C=0`; the converse is immediate. The premises do not include coefficient independence or the desired zero-perturbation conclusion.

For a diagonal parity sign, the entrywise anticommutator is `(ε_i+ε_j) A_ij`. The two signs give a complete classification: chirality is equivalent to zero entries within each equal-parity sector. Consequently, adding arbitrary onsite coefficients `c(i)` to a fixed opposite-parity kernel preserves chirality exactly when every `c(i)` is zero. Neither both-sector occupancy nor uniformity is needed for this onsite result. The empty carrier causes no exception: both sides are vacuous.

For the more specific onsite form `m ε_i+p`, both parity sectors are needed to identify the two uniform coefficients separately. The two sector equations are `m+p=0` and `−m+p=0`; no 2-torsion gives `m=p=0`. These equations are derived in the Lean proof, rather than assumed under the name of linear independence.

The exact load-bearing ring condition is **no additive 2-torsion**. Merely saying characteristic is different from 2 is insufficient over arbitrary rings: in `Z/6`, the nonzero even perturbation `C=3I` anticommutes with `diag(1,−1)`, and nonzero uniform `m=p=3` cancel. Characteristic different from 2 does suffice over fields or integral domains. Odd composite coefficient rings such as `Z/9` can also satisfy the needed condition; primality is unnecessary.

I independently replayed the preferred standalone module and its axiom harness using the author's actual Lean 4.33.0 executable. Both commands exited 0. `even_perturbation_iff` is axiom-free. The entry, onsite, scalar and reported directional statements depend only on Lean's `propext`. There are no recovery `sorry` declarations or additional axioms. The executable needed a tiny independently rebuilt `readlink` adapter to resolve the executor's `/proc/<pid>/exe` mismatch; its inspected source rewrites that exact own-process path to `/proc/self/exe`. It does not modify proof terms or the kernel. The complete commands, exits and axiom output are in `sm3-v2/independent-lean-replay.json`. I also replayed the earlier imported v1 and integrated v1 successfully; the integrated change preserves prior declarations and changes one overbroad comment plus adds the recovery declarations.

This is a recovery of restricted operator/entry algebra. It is not an identification of physical weak chirality, a proof of emergent spin, or a general equivalence between zero diagonal and chirality for arbitrary hopping. Same-parity offsite hopping is a counterexample to the latter equivalence. A first-order chiral operator can have a nonzero diagonal after squaring, so the second-order companion rule is not automatically covered by the first-order statement.

## 2. Multitime realization and native quotient

The linear uniqueness proof is correct with the stated chronological convention `M_w=M_last … M_first`. Define `S(M_w a)=M'_w a'` and extend linearly. A vanishing linear relation on source reachable vectors has zero pairing against every target continuation by equality of all concatenated word statistics. Target observability makes the corresponding target vector zero, proving well-definedness. Reverse the models and use reachability to obtain inverse maps. Appending one letter gives intertwining, and the empty continuation gives the evaluation identity. Reachability also gives uniqueness. This is linear minimal-realization uniqueness, not a new physical gauge theorem.

The finite comparison bound `n+m−1` follows from the direct-sum discrepancy realization of dimension `n+m`. Its reachable filtration stabilizes by that length; annihilating all vectors in the stabilized span annihilates every later word. Minimality and positivity are not needed for this comparison bound. A known competing dimension bound is needed.

The Hankel reconstruction bound `2d−1` and its matrix orientation are correct. Reachability and observability each admit a basis indexed by words of length at most `d−1`. With `H=OR` and `H_c=OM_cR`, one obtains `H^{-1}H_c=R^{-1}M_cR`. The longest prefix-letter-suffix query has length `2d−1`. Short observed Hankel rank does not itself prove a global rank upper bound. The author's exact two-state probability mixtures agree through length 2 and differ at length 3, so reducing the general bound to `2d−2` is invalid even for nonnegative normalized models.

The native passive result is stronger than generic GL similarity within its narrower class. A finite permutation and deterministic readout have a future-trace quotient that is again a permutation: finite order makes future equivalence invariant also under the inverse. For an `N`-state future-observable quotient, the prefix partitions refine strictly until discrete, so `N−1` symbols suffice for `N≥2`; the singleton case needs one symbol to identify its label. Given known `B≥max(N,N')` with `B≥2`, `(B−1)`-block support identifies quotient states, `B`-block support gives the unique successor, and block probabilities give the pushed-forward prior. Full support is required only on the quotient. The initial prior need not be stationary. The resulting isomorphism is unique, readout-preserving, dynamics-intertwining and prior-transporting.

The separately frozen v2 §4.5 corollary also checks mathematically: sufficiently long passive words on that full-support future-observable quotient have singleton start-state indicator effects, and their unnormalized retained-word vectors are positive multiples of singleton end-state vectors. A common word length is essential: the same permutation power makes those end states cover the quotient. Thus the quotient is automatically linearly reachable and observable, with Hankel rank equal to its state count. Matching word probabilities then makes the unique similarity a permutation with unit, rather than arbitrary positive, coordinate scale factors. This algebraic fact does not assume hidden-state control or physically selectable microscopic preparations. The v2 addition explicitly retains that scope.

The simplex bridge is valid as an additional premise: an invertible real linear map taking the entire normalized simplex onto itself permutes its extreme points and is a permutation matrix. Equivalently, both map and inverse are positive and normalization is preserved. Linear span/minimality alone gives none of these cone conditions. My independently constructed strictly positive normalized minimal two-state models have identical word laws and a unique nonmonomial, nonunitary similarity whose inverse has negative entries. They are stochastic predictive models, not native permutation counterexamples.

The postselection discussion in v1 is appropriately conditional. A retained sufficiently long nondisturbing visible record can single out a quotient state under full support, but this requires record retention, conditioning and time access in a physical application. It never separates microscopic states with identical future traces. A visible-preserving global permutation may depend on the visible fibre; identifying it with a single hidden-factor gauge requires another interface constraint. These distinctions preserve Pair C, Proposition 24.2 and the still-open all-partitions question.

## 3. Native finite-abelian history recovery

For any finite abelian group `G` and map `F:G→G`, the companion update `Φ_F(x,y)=(F(x)−y,x)` has inverse `(u,v)→(v,F(v)−u)`. Uniform phase counting is invariant, and `(x,y)→(x,F(x)−y)` is a bijection, so the classical one-step current-field pair law is uniform and independent of `F`. This statement concerns a population transition kernel under the named ensemble, not a coherent quantum channel.

Full labelled triples obey `P(a,b,c)=|G|^{-2} 1[a+c=F(b)]` under uniform phase counting. Therefore their support determines `F` in the fixed group/addition/update/readout family. Full support alone suffices for the support conclusion; it does not give the uniform numerical probabilities or one-step blindness. The proof and author code keep these facts separate. It is not an inference of the physical group, lattice, readout or preferred prior from observations.

The proper coarse `F5²` example checks exactly. For `F_c(x1,x2)=(x2,c x1)`, `c=1,2`, and readout `x1`, the first four readouts map phase states bijectively to `F5^4`, so all words through length 4 agree under uniform counting. The fifth satisfies `Y4+(2−c)Y2+Y0=0`, yielding different length-5 laws. I independently enumerated the actual native dynamics and recovered this distinction, without importing the author's recurrence to generate data.

The F7 local-patch control against the pinned #984 coefficient rules is also correct. On the stated 7³ torus all twelve radius-1/radius-2 axial parents are distinct. A patch with one radius-2 parent set to 1 and the remaining relevant inputs zero gives next target 0 for the radius-1 rule and 6 for the radius-1+2 rule. With only the smaller parent patch retained, the latter rule's unobserved independent F7 tail makes its conditional next-target law uniform, while the former is deterministic. This is a native conditional postdiction and an access-sensitive discriminator. It supplies neither physical accessibility nor a calibrated empirical prediction selecting a substratum.

The separate structured-prior adjunct is useful and correct. For `Φ(x,y)=(x−y,x)` on F5, assigning weight 2 to the full orbit of `(1,0)` and weight 1 elsewhere gives a stationary, full-support, nonuniform distribution with nonflat current-field transition rows. Hence even stationarity plus full support cannot replace the explicit uniform counting hypothesis in the blindness statement. Triple-support recovery survives. A single orbit or restricted prior also cannot be assumed to cover every input required to recover `F` globally.
