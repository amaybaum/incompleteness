CONDITIONAL RECOVERY OF THE STANDARD-MODEL GLOBAL GAUGE FORM
Local research candidate v1; 2026-10-28. No manuscript adoption or physical
selector is claimed. Group proofs below are ordinary exact mathematics, not
Lean-kernel declarations. The companion executable is a finite control, not
a replacement for the general proofs.

1. Premises and the source problem

On the selected single-copy H-link/H-cust branch, the canonical SM Theorem 5
gives the exact condensate stabilizer

  G0 = U(3) x U(2) x U(1).

It does not identify which central phases are observationally redundant.
The book ch05 paragraphs beginning "Reduction to the Standard Model gauge
group" and "The reduction proceeds as follows", and canonical SM Theorem 14,
use U(n) = SU(n) x U(1). That is a local Lie-algebra splitting, not an
isomorphism of these compact groups for n>1. The book additionally removes
every block's phase while retaining the singlet phase. Those two operations
cannot both describe a quotient by all independent block centers.

The recovery here has two positive statements. First, a specifically
identified correlated central two-torus in G0 has quotient S(U(3)xU(2)),
while retaining a nontrivial singlet-axis circle and removing the common
overall phase. Second, the stated charged SM family has faithful image
(SU(3)xSU(2)xU(1))/Z6, which is exactly S(U(3)xU(2)). The first statement
requires a physical identification of that two-torus as redundant; the
second requires the selected charged matter action. Neither follows from
amplitude-scale invariance alone.

2. Exact U(n) statements

For n>=1, define p_n: SU(n)xU(1) -> U(n) by p_n(A,z)=z A. It is a
homomorphism. For any C in U(n), choose z with z^n=det C and set A=z^-1 C;
then det A=1, so p_n is surjective. Its kernel is

  {(z^-1 I_n,z): z^n=1} = Z_n.

Consequently U(n) is isomorphic to (SU(n)xU(1))/Z_n. For n>1 the product
formula without this kernel is false. Quotienting U(n) by its scalar
U(1) gives PU(n). The restricted map SU(n)->PU(n) is surjective by the
same root construction and has kernel {zI_n:z^n=1}; hence

  PU(n) is isomorphic to SU(n)/Z_n.

Both surjectivity and kernel have been proved explicitly; this is not a
claim that the fundamental n-dimensional representation descends through
PU(n). Its center acts nontrivially on that representation.

For G0, removing the two independent scalar circles of U(3) and U(2)
therefore gives PU(3)xPU(2)xU(1), not SU(3)xSU(2)xU(1). Removing all three
independent scalar circles gives PU(3)xPU(2), with no abelian Lie algebra.
Removing only the common diagonal scalar circle leaves two abelian Lie
algebra directions. Thus a common amplitude normalization supplies at most
one of the two central directions needed by a Lie algebra su(3)+su(2)+u(1).
If the principle concerns real positive scales or finite-alphabet units,
its extension to any continuous observer-level phase circle is itself a
premise, not a consequence of this group calculation.

3. A correlated quotient that recovers the desired global form

Let H = S(U(3)xU(2)) = {(C',D'):det C' det D'=1}. Define

 F: G0 -> H,
 F(C,D,t) = (t^-2 (det C)(det D)^-1 C,
             t^3 (det C)^-2(det D) D).

All prefactors are scalar characters, so F is a continuous homomorphism.
Writing a=det C and b=det D, the determinants of its two outputs are

  t^-6 a^4 b^-3,    t^6 a^-4 b^3.

Their product is one. To prove surjectivity without roots, take (E,J) in H
and a=det E. The preimage (C,D,t)=(E,a J,1) satisfies det C=det D=a, so
F(E,aJ,1)=(E,J). This is also a continuous homomorphic section H->G0.
An alternative right section is (E,J,det E): its two scalar prefactors
are both one because det J=(det E)^-1.

If F(C,D,t) is the identity, each of C,D is a scalar matrix because it
equals a scalar multiple of the identity. Put C=c I3 and D=d I2 and
h=t c^-2 d. The output scalars are h^-2 and h^3. The conditions h^2=1
and h^3=1 force h=1. Conversely h=1 makes both outputs the identity.
Therefore the entire kernel, including its noncentral exclusion, is

  K = {(c I3,d I2,c^2/d): c,d in U(1)} = U(1)^2.

It is closed, connected, and central. The first isomorphism theorem gives
G0/K isomorphic to H. Equivalently write d=c u:

  K = {(c I3,c u I2,c/u): c,u in U(1)}.

The c-circle is the common overall phase on the 3+2+1 carrier. The other
circle is (I3,u I2,u^-1). This explicitly identifies the extra operational
redundancy that would be needed beyond a common phase normalization.
No physical argument here establishes that this second circle is redundant.

The singlet axis is retained: F(I3,I2,t)=(t^-2 I3,t^3 I2), whose kernel
is trivial since t^2=t^3=1 implies t=1. Neither independent color nor weak
center is killed: F(omega I3,I2,1)=(omega I3,I2) and
F(I3,-I2,1)=(I3,-I2), where omega=exp(2 pi i/3).

At Lie-algebra level, set C=e^(i alpha)A, D=e^(i beta)B,
t=e^(i tau), with A in SU(3), B in SU(2). Then

  F(C,D,t)=(e^(-2i theta) A,e^(3i theta) B),
  theta=tau-2 alpha+beta.

Thus the retained phase is a relative combination, not the elimination of
all block phases. This formula is local in alpha,beta because choosing A,B
has finite root ambiguities; F itself is globally well-defined by determinants.
H-link/H-cust remain the premises supplying G0. The physical interpretation
of K and the global topology of the emergent bundle remain extra questions.

4. A chosen product group and its exact central extension

Let P=SU(3)xSU(2)xU(1). It is a compact product group, not the universal
cover (the latter has R in place of U(1)). Define

  psi: P -> H,       psi(A,B,z)=(z^-2 A,z^3 B).

The determinant product is z^-6 z^6=1. Given (E,J) in H, choose z with
z^6=(det E)^-1=det J; then A=z^2 E and B=z^-3 J are special unitary and
psi(A,B,z)=(E,J). The kernel conditions are A=z^2 I3, B=z^-3 I2 and
z^6=1. On those sixth roots z^-3=z^3. Therefore

  ker psi = <g>,  g=(omega I3,-I2,exp(i pi/3)),  |g|=6.

Consequently P/Z6 is isomorphic to H. The restriction of F to the subgroup
(A,B,z) of G0 is psi; hence the correlated torus quotient and the finite
central extension are compatible global constructions, not competing
assertions based only on dimension counting.

5. Charged family action: exact full kernel

Choose the primitive integer convention q=6Y and the following complex
representations of P. Every listed color 3 is fundamental; the uR,dR,eR
entries are right-handed particle representations, not left-handed charge
conjugates. Chirality does not alter this group-action calculation.

 field   SU(3)   SU(2)   q       action
 Q       3       2       1       z (A tensor B)
 uR      3       1       4       z^4 A
 dR      3       1      -2       z^-2 A
 L       1       2      -3       z^-3 B
 eR      1       1      -6       z^-6
 H       1       2       3       z^3 B

Let rho be their direct sum. Suppose rho(A,B,z) is the identity. Its dR
block gives A=z^2 I3. Its L block gives B=z^3 I2. Its eR block gives
z^6=1. This proves, without assuming centrality of an arbitrary kernel
element, that ker rho is contained in <g>. Conversely for z^6=1 and
A=z^2I3, B=z^3I2 the six action scalars are respectively
z^6, z^6, 1, 1, z^-6, z^6, all one. Thus

  ker rho = <g> = Z6,   rho(P) is isomorphic to P/Z6 = H.

The fermion multiplets alone already give this kernel. Additional copies
of a generation, neutral singlet neutrinos, and adjoint gauge bosons do
not change it. Additional charged matter may change it.

For completeness the descent is constructive, not merely an appeal to the
kernel theorem. On (E,J) in H, use

  dR: E;           uR: (det E)^-1 E;    Q: E tensor J;
  L: (det J)^-1 J; eR: det E;          H: J.

Pulling these back by psi gives precisely the actions in the table. In
particular det E=z^-6 and det J=z^6. These determinant characters/dual
choices are genuine global data; availability of a nonabelian tensor
factor such as (3,2) does not by itself construct its q=1 charged action.
The theorem consumes the selected charged SM family. It does not prove
that this family emerges from the raw V_K tensor V_K* carrier, nor close
H-spin', H-chi', light-spectrum selection, or H-Y-vertex.

6. A general exact descent criterion

For a P irreducible, let r3 in Z/3 be its SU(3) triality, r2 in Z/2 its
SU(2) center parity, and q its integer U(1) charge. The generator g acts
by exp(2 pi i (2 r3+3 r2+q)/6). Therefore it descends to P/Z6 if and
only if

  2 r3+3 r2+q = 0 (mod 6).

Forward witness: a representation of the quotient is trivial on g, so
its scalar above is one. Backward witness: the congruence makes g and
all its powers trivial, so the representation factors through the quotient.
The six rows give residues 6,6,0,0,-6,6. The criterion also distinguishes
the other possible quotients: descent to the order-d subgroup quotient
(d=1,2,3,6) requires 2 r3+3 r2+q = 0 (mod d), since that subgroup is
generated by g^(6/d).

7. What is and is not physically selected

Every subgroup Gamma of ker rho acts trivially on the stated local fields.
Within quotients of this fixed P, the four choices Gamma=1,Z2,Z3,Z6 all
admit those fields. Their faithful image on precisely this field module
is the same H. Requiring a faithful action on exactly this module selects
the maximal Z6 quotient as a mathematical image. Local matter data do not
establish that the full physical gauge group must be this image: allowed
Wilson/'t Hooft lines, principal bundles, extra heavy matter, and other
global data can distinguish group presentations with the same local
charged fields and Lie algebra. A hypothetical additional vectorlike
SU(3)xSU(2) singlet pair of q=+1,-1 makes the common kernel trivial and
forbids quotienting by any nontrivial subgroup of this Z6 without adding
new chiral anomalies. Thus the global choice is not fixed
merely by the observed list or by amplitude normalization.

The standard global-form ambiguity is discussed in the primary paper
David Tong, Line Operators in the Standard Model, JHEP 07 (2017) 104,
arXiv:1705.01853v2, https://arxiv.org/html/1705.01853v2, Introduction and
section 3. The proofs in sections 2-6 above are self-contained; that paper
is context for the physical distinctions, not a substitute for them.

8. Status and next physical obligation

Recovered: the exact stabilizer survives unchanged at its stated
H-link/H-cust premises; an explicit central two-torus quotient of that
stabilizer gives H while retaining an abelian factor and the nonabelian
centers; the selected charged SM family gives the exact Z6 kernel and
faithful image H; the global quotient/descent conditions are explicit.

entire redundant central subgroup is K (or another demonstrably equivalent
choice), and derive or justify the selected charged matter action. Common
amplitude-scale invariance alone supplies neither that two-torus nor a
determinant-one lift. The physical gauge bundle/line spectrum must also
be specified before asserting a unique full global gauge group.
