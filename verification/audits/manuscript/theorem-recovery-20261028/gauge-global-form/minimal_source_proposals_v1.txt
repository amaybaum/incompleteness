D. Optional positive central-quotient lemma after canonical Theorem 5

This is useful if the manuscript wants a precise theorem for the proposed
amplitude-scale route, rather than leaving every quotient unspecified:

"Lemma (correlated central quotient). Put G0=U(3)xU(2)xU(1) and
K={(cI3,dI2,c^2/d):c,d in U(1)}. Then G0/K is isomorphic to
S(U(3)xU(2)). A surjective homomorphism with kernel K is

 F(C,D,t)=(t^-2(det C)(det D)^-1 C,
            t^3(det C)^-2(det D)D).

The determinant product of the outputs is one. Given (E,J) in the target,
delta=det E gives the right section (E,J,delta). In the kernel C=cI3,
D=dI2, and the output scalars (t c^-2 d)^-2,(t c^-2 d)^3 are both one
exactly when t=c^2/d. Thus K is the full kernel. It contains the common
phase (cI3,cI2,c), and its quotient retains the singlet-axis circle
t->(t^-2I3,t^3I2). Interpreting all of K as observationally redundant is an
additional physical premise; common amplitude normalization establishes
at most its diagonal subcircle."

The lemma is an existence and characterization theorem for one exact
quotient. No physical uniqueness of this choice among possible central
quotients is implied.
