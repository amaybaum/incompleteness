2. Fixed-family premises and anomaly conventions

Fix exactly one chiral fermion family, or several identical copies with
family-universal charges, in the nonabelian representations

  Q_L=(3,2), u_R=(3,1), d_R=(3,1), L_L=(1,2), e_R=(1,1).

Write their real U(1) charges as (a,u,d,l,e). These are right-handed
particle charges for u_R,d_R,e_R; converting them to left-handed
conjugates reverses their U(1) charge and conjugates color. No additional
charged chiral fermions, in particular no charged right-handed neutrino,
are allowed to cancel the following anomaly coefficients. Neutral
spectators and vectorlike pairs do not change these coefficients.

Up to common nonzero normalization factors, the relevant coefficients are

  C = 2a-u-d                  [SU(3)]^2 U(1),
  W = 3a+l                    [SU(2)]^2 U(1),
  G = 6a-3u-3d+2l-e           gravitational^2 U(1),
  A = 6a^3-3u^3-3d^3+2l^3-e^3   [U(1)]^3.

The chosen nonabelian family cancels the pure SU(3)^3 anomaly (two
fundamentals minus the two right-handed conjugate fundamentals), and
there are four SU(2) doublets counting color, an even number. SU(2) has
no perturbative cubic anomaly in its doublet. These facts concern the
fixed family, not a selection of it from all available modes.

3. Full anomaly classification, including the omitted branch

Assume C=W=G=A=0. The three linear equations give

  d=2a-u,  l=-3a,  e=-6a.

Directional linear witnesses are explicit: d-2a+u=-C, l+3a=W,
and e+6a=3C+2W-G. Thus these three relations are forced. Conversely
substituting them makes C,W,G vanish.

On this linear solution plane, direct expansion gives

  A = 18a(8a^2+u d)
    = -18a(u-4a)(u+2a),     d=2a-u.

This is an exact polynomial identity. Over the reals, a product is zero
only when one factor is zero. Hence the complete solutions are:

  (i) a!=0: (a,u,d,l,e)=(a,4a,-2a,-3a,-6a),
             or          (a,-2a,4a,-3a,-6a).

  (ii) a=0: (a,u,d,l,e)=(0,b,-b,0,0), for arbitrary real b.

Conversely every point in either branch gives all four coefficients zero,
as direct substitution shows. The two standard rays differ by the
u_R/d_R naming interchange; their distinction can be fixed by the
Higgs/Yukawa/electric-charge identification. Branch (ii) is a genuine
anomaly-free counterbranch, not an overall rescaling of a nonzero point
in branch (i). The all-zero assignment is included at b=0.

Therefore the strongest straightforward repair of Theorem 15 is:

  Given exactly the stated chiral family, no additional charged chiral
  anomaly-canceling fermions, cancellation of C,W,G,A, and a nonzero
  charge a of the quark doublet, the charges are proportional to the SM
  assignment, uniquely up to overall normalization and u_R/d_R interchange.

The nonzero-a premise is explicit and necessary. It is often already
implicit when a normalization sets a=1/6, but cannot be inferred merely
from the words "fundamental or singlet representations".

4. Complementary Yukawa-compatible recovery

Introduce one Higgs doublet of charge h and assume invariance of the
three interactions

  bar Q_L tilde H u_R,   bar Q_L H d_R,   bar L_L H e_R,

where tilde H is the SU(2)-conjugate Higgs and has charge -h. Invariance
gives u=a+h, d=a-h, e=l-h. These are allowed Yukawa interactions of the
selected chiral family; their physical realization is a premise here.
The weak anomaly W=0 gives l=-3a. Substitution then gives

  C=0,  G=h-3a,  A=(h-3a)^3.

Thus either G=0 or A=0 forces h=3a; after that, every anomaly coefficient
vanishes and the charges are (a,4a,-2a,-3a,-6a;3a). This is a second,
explicit proof route. It does not need a gravitational-anomaly assumption
if cubic gauge anomaly cancellation is used instead. If h!=0, then
a!=0 and the exceptional branch is excluded. "h!=0" means nonzero Higgs
U(1) charge, not merely a nonzero Higgs field or vacuum expectation.
If h=0, all charges can be zero; a nontrivial normalization is then absent.

This route supplies a precise conditional charge solution when the
selected Higgs/Yukawa sector is already assumed. It does not derive that
sector or the charged observer action from the finite substratum.

5. Normalization and the recovered global-form theorem

Fixing h=1/2 in the convention Q_em=T3+Y makes a=1/6 and yields the
usual numerical Y values. This is a hypercharge/electric-charge convention
and identification. The simultaneous rescaling Y->sY, g_Y->g_Y/s leaves
the U(1) covariant derivative unchanged; amplitude-scale invariance alone
does not select the numerical charge normalization.

For a compact product presentation P=SU(3)xSU(2)xU(1), charges are integer
weights in a chosen circle coordinate. If the selected family and Higgs
generate the primitive charge lattice, the nondegenerate solution can be
written (q_Q,q_u,q_d,q_L,q_e;q_H)=(1,4,-2,-3,-6;3), up to sign/orientation
and the stated u/d identification. It then has exactly the common Z6
kernel proved in global_form_proof_v1.txt. This connects the recovered
charge theorem to that faithful-image theorem without assuming every
numerical hypercharge as a separate input.

It still does not identify the second redundant circle in G0, or the
entire physical gauge bundle/line spectrum. The charged family/light
spectrum and its chiral observer realization retain the OI matter,
H-spin', H-chi', and selection premises. A full raw End(V) conjugation
carrier has the different kernel established in native_carrier_barrier_v1.txt.

6. Essential negative controls

Anomaly cancellation alone has branch (ii): (0,1,-1,0,0) cancels every
listed anomaly but has neutral Q,L,e. It cannot admit all three Yukawas
with a common nonzero h, because u=a+h forces h=1 while e=l-h would be -1.

If a charged nu_R with its Dirac Yukawa is admitted, put
n=l+h=h-3a. Its additional contributions are -n to G and -n^3 to A.
Then G=A=0 for every (a,h), so there is a two-parameter family. For
example (a,u,d,l,e,n;h)=(1,2,0,-3,-4,-2;1) cancels these anomalies and
admits all four Yukawas, but is not the SM ray. Thus "no charged nu_R or
other additional charged chiral cancelers" is consequential.

With several families and only TOTAL anomaly cancellation, charges need
not be family universal. For example take three quark sectors with
(a,u,d)=(1,4,-2), h=3, and lepton pairs
(l,e)=(-2,-5),(-4,-7),(-3,-6). Their total anomalies vanish and each
diagonal charged-lepton Yukawa is allowed. The first two families shift
by opposite family-lepton numbers. The one-family theorem therefore
extends immediately to identical copies, not to arbitrary family charges.

These examples are reproduced exactly in the companion control. The
polynomial coefficients and finite controls are exact arithmetic; the
written factor/zero-product argument supplies the all-real classification.
