# Introduction r32: prospective paper-only reader questions

Read only the pinned paper, with no repository, web, prior answers, evaluations, source map or intended answers. Answer once in your own words; do not claim a check you did not perform.

## Instructions for the reader

Read the paper once, at your own pace. Then put it away and answer each question in your own words, in a few sentences.
There are no right phrasings, and you do not need to use the paper's terms. Explain what you understood as you would to
a friend. If a question asks about something you did not follow, say so; that is useful too.

## Questions

1. **The basic idea.** What does the paper mean by "observational incompleteness"? Describe a situation in which
   someone is in this position.

2. **Randomness from hidden information.** How can a world that follows fixed, deterministic rules look random to
   someone inside it? Use an example of your own, or the paper's, and explain why the randomness appears.

3. **The main mathematical result.** In your own words, what does the paper show about the relationship between
   - random-looking (stochastic) observations,
   - deterministic systems with hidden parts, and
   - the records of quantum measurements?

   What does "on a finite time horizon" add?

4. **What the result does not show.** Why does this result not, by itself, show that quantum mechanics comes from
   hidden deterministic information? What would still need to be shown?

5. **Memory.** What does the paper say about memory? When must the hidden part of the world carry information about the
   past, and what can an observer learn about that hidden memory from what they can see?

6. **The two applications.** Why might this way of thinking matter for
   - the cosmological-constant problem (the mismatch between the predicted and observed energy of empty space), and
   - the black-hole information problem?

   For each, what does the paper establish, and what does it leave open?

7. **Anything unclear.** Which part of the paper did you find hardest to follow, or least convincing? Where did you
   have to guess what was meant?


**5a.** The paper's recurrence result compares forecasts made from different possible starting states. Why does it
require the hidden part to be set up in the same way for every starting state? What would go wrong if the set-up could
depend on the starting state? Answer in your own words, without quoting the paper's definitions.

**5b.** The same result also includes forecasts from starting states that never actually occur in the history being
observed. Why are those starting states kept in the comparison? Answer in your own words, without quoting the paper's
definitions.

**5c.** The paper says that, in some of the worlds it describes, a dependence of later observations on the past is
established by the mathematics and yet need not appear in any record an observer can obtain. How can both be true? What
does the mathematical result say about the world's whole cycle of evolution, and what can an observer actually see?
How does such a dependence differ from one that shows up at a step the observer does watch? Answer in your own words,
without quoting the paper's definitions.

**8. Hidden states and records.** In the known swap game, can two retained readings identify both cards even though the observed process has memory? What determines whether a hidden quantity can be inferred in the general known-model case? Does being embedded alone settle that question?

**9. Finite entropy example.** What is stipulated in the finite coherent entropy construction and its clocked release toy? What has been proved there, and which parts of a physical black-hole Page curve remain open?

**10. Capacity.** The paper states a checked capacity bound using a supremum over finite future blocks. Does that certify the same statement for a literal infinite future? What does the bound imply about hidden memory, and does it imply permanent uncertainty about the hidden state?
