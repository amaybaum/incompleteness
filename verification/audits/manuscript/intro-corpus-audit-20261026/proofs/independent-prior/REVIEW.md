## 2. The precise statement as proved

Fix a finite `C_V` (`n ≥ 2`) and a horizon `K ≥ 1`. Let `P` be a law on `C_V^{K+1}`. At every context `x_{≤k}` (`0 ≤ k < K`) fix a kernel `kern_{x_{≤k}}` that equals `P(·∣x_{≤k})` where that history has positive probability, and is arbitrary elsewhere.

**(T) Uniform tape.** Suppose every `kern` is rational, and let `D` be a common denominator. Set `C_H = (C_V∪{□})^K × Z_D^K × Z_{K+1}` and let `μ_H` be uniform on `{□^K} × Z_D^K × {0}`. There is a bijection `φ` of `C_V × C_H` such that, for **every** `x_0 ∈ C_V` and every `k ≤ K`, the law of `(π_V φ^1, …, π_V φ^k)` under `δ_{x_0} ⊗ μ_H` is `∏_j kern_{x_{≤j}}(x_{j+1})`. Consequently, under `P_0 ⊗ μ_H` the visible trajectory has law `P` restricted to `(x_0,…,x_k)`, for every `k ≤ K`. Here `μ_H` is one fixed probability law shared by all roots, and `φ` is one fixed map (time enters only through the hidden clock). `φ` depends on `K` and on `P`.

**(E) Response table.** The same holds for arbitrary real kernels, with `C_H = C_V^{N} × (C_V∪{□})^K × Z_{K+1}` and `μ_H(u, □^K, 0) = ∏_c kern_c(u_c)`. This includes irrational entries and laws with zero-probability histories.

**Corollaries proved alongside.** (C1): if `P` is non-Markov, some realized transition depends on the hidden state. (C3): `log₂|C_H| ≥ I*`, by data processing, valid for any realization. (C4): inherited, because the realization reproduces `P`'s differing past-conditionals. (C2): the static-bath form holds by construction.

**Not proved and not claimed.** Any property of the realized process after time `K` (the completion is arbitrary there). Consistency between the realizations for horizons `K` and `K+1` (no gluing; acknowledged at L618). A bound on `t_R`. Any formal kernel proof of the `x_0`-independence. The last is correctly disclosed at L500: `S_imp_D` uses a correlated initial law.

## 3. Check-by-check findings

1. **Construction.** The partial step, restricted to well-formed states at clock `c < K`, is injective. The image has clock `c+1`. The ledger's new last entry is the pre-image's `x`, the ledger prefix and the tape are unchanged, and the clock decrements. The image consists of well-formed states with clock in `1..K`. The completion exists by complement counting. On prior-supported orbits, every step up to time `K` lies in the domain of the formula: at clock `K−1` the ledger has `K−1` entries, and the step writes the `K`-th. Wrap-around is never used before time `K`. Each tape symbol `s_{c+1}` is read exactly once, at the step leaving clock `c`, which is the fact the marginalization needs. My reimplementation from the text alone verified all of this, with the full bijection checked by explicit enumeration on 60 laws (6 hand laws, 54 random), state spaces up to 373,248.
2. **Normalization and independence.** In (T), `μ_H` is uniform on `D^K` states: a probability law with no `x_0` argument. In (E), the product prior sums to 1 by `Fintype.prod_sum`. This is kernel-checked as `responseWeight_sum` and `responseWeight_marginal` in `RootedClassification.lean`, which are exactly the normalization and one-context-marginal lemmas needed. The prior has no `x_0` argument. Both prior laws are shared by every root, including roots of probability 0 (my checks run every `x_0`).
3. **Consistency across time.** Under `δ_{x_0} ⊗ μ_H`, the history `x_{≤c}` is a function of `(x_0, s_1, …, s_c)`, and `s_{c+1}` is independent of it and uniform. So `P(x_{c+1}=y ∣ x_{≤c}) = |Q^{-1}(y)|/D = kern(y)`, and the chain rule gives every joint point, not only marginals, for every `k ≤ K`. For (E), see G5. Verified exactly at every joint point, for every root and every `k ≤ K`. `φ` is one fixed permutation. “Time-invariant” is formally true, but only because the clock is hidden state (§6).
4. **Rationality and the Extension.** The Extension does remove the rationality restriction for every law. With every kernel entry treated as an independent indeterminate, the realized joint equals the kernel chain as a **polynomial identity** (n=2, K∈{1,2}; n=3, K=1; `response_generic.py`), so it holds for all real kernels at once. Concrete irrational kernels (`√2/2`, `π/4`) with zero-probability contexts are exact under sympy (`response_check.py`). Larger rational non-uniform cases: n=2, K=3 (domain 229,376) and n=3, K=2 (domain 6,377,292) are injective with the full joint exact. The rationality restriction *is* necessary for the uniform-tape form. Under any prior with rational masses, every conditional given `x_0` is rational, so an irrational kernel cannot be realized with a uniform tape for any `D`. The ledger/clock completion is fully specified up to the arbitrary completion, which is bijective. Size bounds: see G6.
5. **Counterexamples attempted (all failed to refute).** Non-Markov laws (hand-built, XOR, delayed revival, random). Laws with zeros, including `P(x_0)=(1,0)` and forced-zero kernel entries. `K=1`. `|C_V|=2` and `3`. A deterministic many-to-one visible step. Irrational entries (Extension). Generic symbolic kernels. Countercontrols, which must fail, all fired: (i) tape reuse (every step reads `s_1`) breaks the joint on the fair coin and the non-Markov law at `K=3`; (ii) a non-common-denominator `D` is rejected; (iii) a non-product prior with the correct one-context marginals, correlating `u_{(0)}` and `u_{(0,0)}`, breaks the joint at `(x_0=0, k=2)`. Disclosure: I initially predicted that tape reuse would also break XOR and the delayed revival. Both have a single non-deterministic step, so reuse is harmless there. I corrected those two expectations after the first run. The control is live on the laws with two or more random steps.
6. **The only false sentence found** is G3(c), which concerns the “equivalence” wording, not the construction.

## 5. Assumptions

Finite `C_V`, finite `K`. In (T), rational kernels. A target law given as a joint, with arbitrary kernels at zero-probability contexts (G1). Initial law `P_0 ⊗ μ_H`. Discrete time with one application of `φ` per visible step. No assumption of time-homogeneity, Markovianity, P-indivisibility, ETH or mixing. The characterization theorem's time-homogeneity hypothesis (L516) is not needed here.

## 6. Proof correctness versus physical interpretation

**Mathematically proved.** Every finite-horizon law (rational kernels for (T); any real kernels for (E)) is the visible marginal, through time `K`, of one finite bijection with a hidden prior independent of `x_0` and shared by every root.

**Physical readings the corpus attaches, and whether the proof supports them.**
- *Time-invariant substratum.* Supported only formally. All time dependence sits in a hidden clock, and `φ` restricted to the clock-`c` slice is an arbitrary rule `Q_{c+1}`. Any time-inhomogeneous law is realized, so “one fixed bijection” carries no physical constraint here. The map also depends on `K`, and is arbitrary after `K`.
- *Hidden memory / (C4) as readback.* The ledger is a perfect write-then-read record of the visible past. The randomness is pre-sampled (tape or response table) and is revealed by the history. The corpus says this explicitly at L80. The construction therefore shows that (C4) can be met without any physical storage/erosion dynamics. It supports “readback exists in some realization”, not “a physical hidden sector stores and reads records”.
- *(C2) static bath, τ_B = ∞.* This is definitional in a discrete-time model with no inter-step hidden dynamics. The clock does tick autonomously. It is not evidence about physical bath timescales. The ledger's “C2 conditional” wording (G7) mixes this with the separate physical-memory theorem.
- *The canonical measure.* The prior is concentrated on the sector `{ledger blank, clock 0}`. It is not φ-invariant and is not Lemma 3's counting measure. Under the counting measure on the full `C_V × C_H` the marginal would not be the target. The corpus treats the prior as a realization datum (L60, L502), which is consistent. But the physical reading of “nature = finite reversible substratum with its selected invariant measure” is not what this proof realizes.
- *Hidden size.* `(n+1)^K D^K (K+1)` and `n^{Σn^k}(n+1)^K(K+1)` are existence sizes, not physical estimates. The proof says nothing about the horizon's capacity beyond the universal `2^{I*}` floor.

## 7. Bearing on the broader framework

- Because every law is realizable, the existence of a realization has no discriminating content. The corpus states this correctly (L99, L534, L596–598). The `x_0`-independent prior removes one objection to `S_imp_D`'s trajectory carrier, in which the hidden state “knows” the trajectory correlated with `x_0`. It does not add empirical content.
- Kernel coverage is disclosed accurately at L500 and Intro L56/L208 (“proved on paper”). `RootedClassification.lean`'s `responseRealization`/`responsePrior` is **not** a formalization of this theorem. Its table is indexed by (root, phase), not by history, and it reproduces only the one-time rooted maps `Γ_t` (`rootedMap_responseRealization_of_pos_lt`). Its multi-time law makes the visible values at different times conditionally independent given the root, so it reproduces no non-trivial multi-time joint. What carries over is the product-prior normalization and marginal lemmas. Nobody should cite it for the multi-time `x_0`-independent claim. A Lean formalization of (E) would be short: the product-prior lemmas exist, `exists_perm_extending` exists, and one needs only the context-independence step (G5) and the chain rule.
- Intro L56 states only the rational form (“For a law with rational probabilities …”). That is accurate for the cited Theorem but understates the Extension, which covers every law. This is an understatement, not an error. If the Introduction wants the strongest supported claim, it could cite the Extension.
- The comb theorem (L460–462) reuses the response-table construction. Its correctness rests on the same G5 step. I did not check its size figure (`|S| = 124,416`).
