## The question

The exact characterization is `OIPlusPos`: implementation locality, elementary transition richness,
embedded observation. Elementary transition richness (`ElementaryTransitionRichness`) asks, at
every level `n` on the carrier `A × Fin n` of size `D`, for three things: every pair of distinct
states continuously driven (`flow (transition a b) t` available for every real `t`), every
exchange available (`permMatrix (Equiv.swap a b)`), and a quarter phase on every state
(`phaseGate a`). It is the one conjunct carrying the nonclassical selection, and it has not been
shown minimal.

> How much of the repertoire is needed? Is one driven transition enough? Is the quarter phase
> derivable? Is the full set of exchanges needed?

## The finding that precedes the kernel

The Lie algebra generated by the drives `−i(E_ab + E_ba)` on the edges of a graph on `D` states
lies inside a conjugate of `so(D)` whenever the graph is bipartite: a diagonal phase
`Λ = diag(i on one colour, 1 on the other)` carries every drive on a bichromatic edge to a real
antisymmetric matrix, and the real antisymmetric matrices are a Lie algebra with no diagonal
element. That is a theorem of the kernel for every colouring (`controlLie_le_colourAlg`). When the
graph is **not bipartite** the kernel proves generation of `su(D)` for one graph, the complete
graph (`hControl_perm`, `D ≥ 3`); for the other graphs below the generation is the exact
computation and nothing more. `repertoire_lie_probe.py` computes the generated algebra exactly
over `ℚ`:

| edge set | `D` | generated dimension | `su(D)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `D`-cycle | 3, 5, 7 | 8, 24, 48 | 8, 24, 48 |
| `D`-cycle | 4, 6, 8 | 6, 15, 28 | 15, 35, 63 |
| path | 3, 4, 5, 6 | 3, 6, 10, 15 | 8, 15, 24, 35 |
| complete graph | 3, 4, 5 | 8, 15, 24 | 8, 15, 24 |
| cycle plus one chord | 4, 6 | 15, 35 | 15, 35 |

The tested odd cycles `D = 3, 5, 7` generate `su(D)`; every even cycle fails, by the bipartite
obstruction, which the kernel proves for every `m` (`not_hControl_evenCycle` on `2m + 2` states).
So "one driven transition plus one cyclic permutation" is not a uniform finite-carrier repertoire:
every doubled level and every level over an even carrier is even. Whether every odd cycle, or every
connected non-bipartite drive graph, generates `su(D)` is computed at these cases only and is not
proved. The orbit of one driven pair under the exchanges is the complete graph, whose generation
is the theorem, so the primitive attacked here is:

> **Phase-free richness.** At every level with two or more states, some pair is continuously
> driven; at every level, every exchange is available. No quarter phase.

The exchanges themselves are generated by one full cycle and one adjacent exchange
(`Equiv.Perm.closure_cycle_adjacent_swap`), so the discrete part of the primitive is two
elements. The single-cycle candidate has an even-carrier countercontrol
(`not_hControl_evenCycle`); adding one adjacent exchange gives every permutation
(`perm_avail_of_cycle_swap`), and with it the complete orbit graph.

## The fork

**Outcome A — phase-free richness suffices.** Implementation locality, phase-free richness and
embedded observation are equivalent to exact finite endomorphic operational QM on every nonempty
finite carrier. The route has three parts, each stated in advance:

1. **Local theorem, `D ≥ 3`.** One driven transition and the permutations generate `su(D)`:
   the drives on all pairs are conjugates of the one drive; the bracket of two drives sharing a
   state is the imaginary transition on the third pair, which the quarter phase used to supply;
   the bracket of the real and imaginary transitions on a pair is the population difference; and
   the round-59 decomposition of a traceless skew-Hermitian matrix finishes. No phase.
2. **Local sharpness, `D = 2`.** The drive on the one pair and the swap commute; their Lie algebra
   is one-dimensional and contains no population-difference direction. This is the `D = 2`
   instance of the bipartite obstruction, stated for any bichromatic drive and any set of
   colour-compatible permutations, and it also covers the even cycle. It is an algebraic
   countercontrol, not a theory-level independence theorem.
3. **Descent.** At the levels with `D ≤ 2` — `(|A|, n) ∈ {(1,1), (1,2), (2,1)}` — control is
   inherited from a level with `D ≥ 3`: the unitary `U ⊗ 1` on `(A × Fin n) × Fin 3`, transported
   to level `3n`, is available by the local theorem there; iterated ancilla closure, which
   embedded observation derives, returns its uniform-attach-then-discard to level `n`; and the
   discard of a conjugation by `U ⊗ 1` is the conjugation by `U`. Classical coarse-graining
   (`availExt_coarse`) plays no part: it changes outcome labels at a fixed level and does not move
   operations between carrier dimensions.

**Outcome B — the qubit phase is genuine.** The local theorem holds, descent fails, and a
`FiniteOperationalTheory` carries implementation locality, phase-free richness and embedded
observation while missing a qubit `Z`-rotation. The package then keeps a `D = 2`-only phase
clause.

**Excluded before the kernel.** The cyclic variant, one drive and one cycle with nothing else:
the even-carrier countercontrol above. The round proves the obstruction as a theorem rather than
leaving it as a computation; the positive side at odd `D` stays a computation at `3, 5, 7`.

`discard_tensorOf_one` is an identity of linear maps, and `descend` consumes the closure rule at
exactly the level `n · (m + 1)` at which `control_at_level` applies.

## What the package says

`OIPlusMin := ImplementationLocality ∧ PhaseFreeRichness ∧ EmbeddedObservation`. Phase-free
richness is: at every level with two or more states, some pair is continuously driven and every
exchange of two distinct states is available. Through `phaseFree_of_cyclic` the exchange clause may
be read as one full cycle and one adjacent exchange. Everything else in the earlier packages —
validity, spectator independence, the seam, closure, observer recursion, Lie-rank richness, full
control, inverse accessibility, the quarter phase, the drives on all other pairs — is derived. The
one continuously driven transition is the sole nonclassical resource the characterization consumes.

## What this note does not claim

That one driven transition is minimal in any stronger sense — whether the *driven* pair can be replaced
by a discrete resource is exactly the physical question of the substratum audit, and this round
does not touch it; `OIPlusMin` is the most compressed package currently formalized, not a
minimality theorem. That every odd cycle, or every connected non-bipartite drive graph, generates
`su(D)`: the computation covers the cycles at `3, 5, 7` and the chorded cycles at `4, 6`, and the
kernel proves generation for the complete graph only. That the empirical addition of the mixing resource is supplied by the OI
substratum. That anything here bears on the OI-N freeze, the concrete-cut freeze, or CT3.
