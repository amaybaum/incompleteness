## 1. Formal setting and what finiteness does

Fix a finite visible alphabet $V$ with $|V|\ge2$. Let $H$ be an arbitrary (possibly infinite) measurable hidden space, $\rho$ a **normalized probability** on $H$ shared by all visible roots, and $F:V\times H\to V\times H$ a measurable update (bijective where the question concerns a reversible carrier). For $t\in\mathbb N$ define the rooted row-stochastic matrix
$$
\Gamma_t(a,b)=\rho\{h:\pi_V(F^t(a,h))=b\},\qquad \Gamma_0=I.
$$
The initial visible root is chosen separately and independently of $\rho$. This **root-shared product convention** is Main's convention, not a generic correlated or cell-conditioned initial state. For a family $\Gamma$, P-divisibility on $\{0,\dots,n\}$ means that for every $0\le s<t\le n$ there is a row-stochastic $\Lambda_{s,t}$ with $\Gamma_t=\Gamma_s\Lambda_{s,t}$. A single pair $s<t$ without such a divisor proves P-indivisibility within $n$.

There are **five different return predicates**:

1. **Global microscopic common period:** $F^N=\mathrm{id}$ on all of $V\times H$, at one common positive $N$. Finite carrier plus bijectivity supplies it; no such consequence holds for a general infinite carrier.
2. **Root-support microscopic common return:** for one positive $N$, $F^N(a,h)=(a,h)$ for $\rho$-almost every $h$, *for each root* $a$. This is weaker than global period, but implies the next condition.
3. **Rooted visible identity return:** $\Gamma_N=I$, i.e. for one common $N$, $\pi_V F^N(a,h)=a$ $\rho$-a.s. for every root. No microscopic return or return of the hidden record is required.
4. **Pointwise/state-dependent return:** for almost every $z$ there exists a time $n(z)$ at which $F^{n(z)}z$ returns to $z$ or a prescribed neighbourhood/cell. Times need not agree, and topological near-return does not mean exact equality. This is what finite-measure Poincaré recurrence can support (in its specified measurable-set/topological form); it is **not** condition 3.
5. **Complete accessible-history return:** equality of specified multi-time history laws or actual record configurations at the same synchronized time. A return of the *rooted one-time matrix* does not recover the hidden record, the joint past/future process, or the accessible history. Conversely a finite-horizon memory/readback witness does not imply condition 3.

On a finite bijective $V\times H$, the common order $N=\operatorname{ord}(F)$ establishes 1, hence 2 and 3, as well as periodicity of all time-indexed laws under a fixed initial state distribution. It does **not** put $N$ in the accessible range. Main's original one-step theorem additionally uses a **non-permutation rooted map**, derived at the first step under its *uniform hidden prior*, and Main's more general overlap result permits any normalized shared prior (without full support). Neither C1 alone at unspecified time nor recurrence alone supplies an overlap at a specified time.

## 3. Proof details for the conditional replacements (paper only)

### 3.1 The carrier-independent stochastic inverse

For a finite square row-stochastic $A$, if $AB=I$ with a row-stochastic $B$, $A$ is a permutation matrix (Main §2.3 and EquivalenceChain). Consequently, given *any* row-stochastic rooted family and times $s<n$ with $\Gamma_n=I$ and $\Gamma_s$ not a permutation, a divisor $\Lambda_{s,n}$ would be a stochastic inverse of $\Gamma_s$. Impossible. Finite microscopic cardinality is nowhere used in this last inference. Finite *visible* cardinality is load-bearing for the square-matrix lemma. A non-permutation square row-stochastic matrix must have distinct rows overlapping at some positive coordinate: otherwise its $|V|$ nonempty disjoint row supports would partition $|V|$ columns into singletons and it would be a permutation.

**Earliest missing inference on an arbitrary infinite carrier:** neither $\Gamma_n=I$ nor a concrete common normalized root-shared prior is supplied by finite access. The formal kernel statement is conditional on a row-stochastic real matrix; a carrier and compatible physically selected readout remain external.

### 3.2 Support-bounded exact periods

Suppose $\rho$ is normalized and for each $a\in V$ and $\rho$-a.e. $h$ there exists $p(a,h)\in\{1,\ldots,B\}$ with $F^{p(a,h)}(a,h)=(a,h)$. Take $N=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,B)$. On that full-measure set $F^N(a,h)=(a,h)$, so $\Gamma_N(a,a)=1$ for every $a$. Thus $\Gamma_N=I$. A non-permutation $\Gamma_s$ at a **strictly earlier** $s<N$ then gives P-indivisibility. This needs no finite *total* $H$, no full support, and no microscopic period on zero-prior states. The exact periodicity assumption, bounded **uniformly over all root-support states**, is much stronger than Poincaré recurrence; OI has not sourced it for nature.

If only **one** microscopic orbit is finite, none of the other rooted rows is controlled. If every microscopic orbit is finite but the lengths are unbounded, one cannot choose the finite lcm. Control C−2 makes that failure explicit.

### 3.3 Measured visible return versus Main's rooted convention

Take a normalized probability $\mu$ on a measurable carrier $X$, a finite measurable observation $\pi:X\to V$ with every $C_a=\pi^{-1}(a)$ having positive mass, and a measurable update $F$. The cell-conditioned matrix
$$
\widetilde\Gamma_t(a,b)=\frac{\mu(C_a\cap F^{-t}C_b)}{\mu(C_a)}
$$
has nonnegative entries and row sums one, since $(C_b)_{b\in V}$ is a finite partition. For any fixed $N>0$, $\widetilde\Gamma_N=I$ exactly when the union of all off-diagonal events has $\mu$-mass zero, i.e. $\pi F^N=\pi$ $\mu$-a.e. No invariance is required for this equivalence, although invariance matters for a dynamical reference law. The existing Lean result constructs an ENNReal ratio and proves the identity/change equivalence; the real-valued normalization argument just given is **paper-level**, not a newly claimed Lean theorem.

This is a **different convention** unless the initial measure disintegrates as a full-support visible prior times a single hidden prior $\rho$ and the root identification is consistent. No transfer from arbitrary correlated cell-conditioned laws to Main's root-shared family is automatic.

### 3.4 Quantitative same-time near-return, without exact identity

For two different roots $a,b$ let $m=\min(\Gamma_s(a,x),\Gamma_s(b,x))>0$ be an earlier shared mass. The probability-row overlap inequality gives
$$
d_{\rm TV}(\Gamma_s(a),\Gamma_s(b))\le 1-m.
$$
Suppose at **one common later time** $n>s$ the two roots satisfy $\Gamma_n(a,a)\ge1-\varepsilon$ and $\Gamma_n(b,b)\ge1-\varepsilon$ (other roots are unconstrained). Then $\Gamma_n(a,a)\ge1-\varepsilon$, and $\Gamma_n(b,a)\le\varepsilon$ because $\Gamma_n(b,b)\ge1-\varepsilon$ and $a\ne b$. Testing the event $\{a\}$ shows
$$
d_{\rm TV}(\Gamma_n(a),\Gamma_n(b))\ge1-2\varepsilon.
$$
If $2\varepsilon<m$, this is strictly larger than the earlier distance. This strict increase is condition C4r, and P-divisibility then fails at $(s,n)$ by the kernel theorem `CausalReadback.c4r_implies_pIndivisible` (CausalReadback.lean:134): every stochastic divisor contracts total variation. That theorem's Section A uses no hidden carrier, prior or reversibility, and assumes only a finite visible alphabet; the new content here is only the elementary bound establishing C4r. The **proof is independent of cardinality of the hidden carrier** and does not assert an exact return or a periodic all-time law. It does not show that such a common time falls inside an accessible experimental window.

**Earliest unsupported inference:** existence and source of that same-time two-root near-return (and its quantitative margin). Poincaré recurrence a.e. gives no such matrix bound. The condition may be reported as a sufficient mathematical premise, never introduced silently as a physical premise.

## 4. Positive and negative controls (exact analytic constructions; no simulation)

**C+ — finite coin-and-die positive control.** Let $V=\{0,1\}$, $H=\{1,\dots,6\}$ with uniform shared prior, and $F(v,h)=(v\oplus{\bf1}_{h\le2},h)$. This is an involutive bijection. Then
$$
\Gamma_0=I,\quad
\Gamma_1=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&1/3\\1/3&2/3\end{pmatrix},\quad
\Gamma_2=I.
$$
The two rooted rows have total variation $1/3$ at $t=1$ and $1$ at $t=2$; P-indivisible on $\{0,1,2\}$. This verifies the return/overlap chain and matches the preserved C1/C4 audit control. By contrast, on the separate alphabet $V=\mathbb Z/3$ with $H=\mathbb Z/4$, the uncoupled finite product $F(v,h)=(v+1\bmod3,h+1\bmod4)$ has period 12 but every rooted map is a permutation, hence remains P-divisible: **recurrence alone is insufficient**.

**C−1 — infinite probability-preserving, mixing, recurrent a.e. but P-divisible.** This is a simpler variant, without C4 memory, of two results already on `main`: the infinite-XOR counterexample (`verification/programmes/substratum/continuous-substates/infinite-xor-counterexample.md`), which has the same rooted family $\Gamma_t=J_2$ for $t\ge1$ together with positive C4 memory, and the kernel's `CausalReadback.pdFamily`, the same matrix family with P-divisibility kernel-checked (`pdFamily_pDivisible`). Let $X=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}$, let $F$ be the invertible two-sided shift, and let $\mu$ be fair product Bernoulli probability (invariant). Observe the coordinate at $0$; all other coordinates form $H$ with the common fair product prior. Conditioned on either root bit $a$, the bit at coordinate $t$ is an independent fair bit for each $t\ge1$. Therefore
$$
\Gamma_0=I,\qquad
\Gamma_t=J/2:=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{for all }t\ge1.
$$
Choose $\Lambda_{0,t}=J/2$ for $t>0$, and $\Lambda_{s,t}=I$ for $0<s<t$: every divisibility identity holds. $\Gamma_1$ is non-permutation, **yet the whole rooted family is P-divisible**. The shift is mixing for the product measure (independence of sufficiently separated cylinder events, then approximation) and Poincaré recurrent $\mu$-almost everywhere in the product topology. Almost-sure returns are at state-dependent times; none gives the matrix identity at a common $t>0$. The all-time family is not periodic because its identity root-time is never repeated. This refutes simultaneously:
- non-permutation plus a normalized invariant probability or a.e. Poincaré recurrence $\Rightarrow$ P-indivisibility;
- finite visible alphabet or finite measurement window $\Rightarrow$ global recurrence;
- any infinite-carrier extension of the **necessity** that all rooted realizable families be periodic.

It does **not** refute Main's finite-carrier theorem, and it has no C4 readback gap (the visible process is independent after conditioning on its finite initial root).

**C−2 — infinite invariant probability, *every microscopic point periodic*, no common rooted identity time.** Let
$$
H=\bigsqcup_{m\ge1}\{m\}\times(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z),\quad
\rho(m,k)=2^{-m}/m,\quad V=\{0,1\}.
$$
Define
$$
F(v,m,k)=\bigl(v\oplus{\bf1}_{k=m-1},\ m,\ k+1\bmod m\bigr).
$$
This is a bijection. Every microscopic state has period $2m$; with uniform visible prior, $\mu=\mathrm{Uniform}(V)\otimes\rho$ is an invariant probability because each $m$-component is traversed bijectively and has uniform mass. Fix any positive $n$, choose $m>n$ and seed $k=m-1$. Exactly one flip occurs during the first $n$ steps, so $\Gamma_n(a,1-a)\ge 2^{-m}/m>0$ for either root $a$. Hence $\Gamma_n\ne I$ for **every** positive $n$, although **every** microscopic state is periodic. This refutes extraction of a common visible return from pointwise finite orbits with no uniform period. It is a control **only against the return inference**; its P-divisibility is not claimed here.

**Class-specific consistency check (not a new test).** RO-3's accepted-scope OBL-RO3-2 paper argument concerns the prime-alphabet linear update on an infinite lattice under uniform Haar measure. It finds mixing and consequently no positive-time common visible identity return for nontrivial readouts in its cell-conditioned convention, while allowing a.e. Poincaré recurrence. It **does not decide P-indivisibility itself** for that update; C−1 is a separate analytic counterexample to an *unrestricted* generalization, not a surrogate physical OI realization.

## 5. Readback/C4, return scales and downstream dependencies

**C4/accessibly non-Markovian law:** A readback gap between two positive-probability histories ending at the same present state forces a positive conditional past–future information quantity (Main §2.3/§3.4). This does not require recurrence or a common global period. The implication is a statement about the **multi-time conditional law**, whereas P-divisibility here concerns **rooted one-time matrices**. The XOR control in Main shows conditional memory may coexist with P-divisibility on a short window. An overlap together with an independently supplied later exact or quantitative near-return *can* produce P-indivisibility on the larger window; one cannot replace that later event with “C4 holds”.

**Visible versus microscopic versus history:** $\Gamma_N=I$ concerns only each root's visible marginal at time $N$; it neither says $F^N=\mathrm{id}$ nor that all prior accessible records or joint histories return. On a finite carrier $F^N=\mathrm{id}$ does force the complete microscopic trajectory and fixed-prior multi-time statistics to repeat under shifts of $N$; on an infinite carrier neither a.e. recurrence nor visible return gives that strong history periodicity. No all-time theorem is inferred from the finite-horizon representation theorem.

**Stochastic ⇒ CP:** If a separately defined family of CPTP maps preserves computational diagonal states and restricts to $\Gamma_t$, P-indivisibility contradicts CP-divisibility for that family: any CPTP intermediate map would induce a stochastic intermediate map on the diagonal. This is the *conditional implication* of Main §3.2. An infinite hidden carrier has not been shown to supply that specific channel family or its diagonal-preservation assumptions. Outside the diagonal-preserving class, Main's Hadamard example defeats the unrestricted inference.

**Finite rooted all-time classification and cycle averages:** The Lean classification of finite-hidden rooted realizability by positive periodicity remains exactly valid in its finite-carrier scope. C−1 gives an infinite-carrier rooted family that is not periodic. GR's finite-cycle averages and A.9 constructions remain finite-cycle claims; almost-sure measure recurrence does not automatically supply a fixed period, a time-average equality, or an ensemble-selected physical Page curve. No replacement is proposed here. The continuous-time Hamiltonian discussion in Main is explicitly conditional on *common* near-total visible return, and notes that Poincaré recurrence does not deliver it for general mixing flows.
