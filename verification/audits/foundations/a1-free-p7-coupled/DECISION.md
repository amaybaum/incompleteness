## Smallest decisive future test

1. **Fix one coupled cut and one independently specified state source.** Declare the dynamics and its reversal, conserved energies including the interaction ledger, and the weight rule before looking at the calibration. Use two resolved shell energies or energy windows from the same source to make a relative-weight check possible. At one operating shell, name one accessible excitation and its reverse. If testing the reservoir-count explanation, that pair must resolve two reservoir energies. Keep the energy scale, weights and probe specification independent of the target $1/4$ and the desired detector slope.
2. **Check the source before a slope.** At that cut, test the stated shell invariances and normalized law, and compare the source's relative shell weights and projected occupancies against its preparation or state-selection evidence. Where the retained shell is not closed, the source must supply those quantities from the full shell under the coupled dynamics. A missing physical source returns **SOURCE GAP**. A demonstrated violation of the specified source conditions rejects that candidate at this check.
3. **Reserve the rate comparison for a sourced candidate.** At the one nonzero gap, check the observable pairing, positive rates and weighted flux against the declared error budget. Compare the reservoir and rate-ratio predictions at that gap only where their finite-gap error is specified. A discrepancy outside the budget falsifies the corresponding claim for the candidate. A pass permits the further shrinking-gap and same-transition checks in rows 4 and 5.

The already accepted conditional implication is $\beta_E E'(0^+)=\tau_K$ from the two controlled slopes on the same positive-rate transitions. With the independently supplied first-law and horizon slopes, it gives the stated $\hbar=c^3\epsilon^2/(4G\sigma)$ and area coefficient. [P7 conditional calibration][p7-scalar]
