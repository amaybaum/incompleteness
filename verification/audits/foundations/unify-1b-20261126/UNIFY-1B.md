**Outcome.** The reference branch supplies a dependency graph and an update-time ordering. Its weak-field comparison gives a prescribed metric component a clock-rate interpretation. Even when they do, the same binned records can conceal different proper-time bins for a held-out interval. The probe charge remains undefined on this interface.

## 2. One finite protocol and class of common metrics

Use a static exterior with test clocks at areal radii $r_A=R$ and $r_B=2R$ on the same radial line, for a fixed physical length $R>0$. Take the exterior Newtonian potential $\Phi(r)=-GM/r$ as a supplied input. Use the flat spatial part and the following lapse to define the class of marked metrics on $r\ge R$.

$$
g_\lambda=-c^2 N_\lambda(r)^2\,dt^2+dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2,\qquad
N_\lambda(r)^2=1-\frac{2\lambda}{r},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{GM}{c^2}\in J=[0,R/100].
$$

The time coordinate has the asymptotic lapse normalization. The center and marked timelike trajectories are fixed in the comparison, along with the radial signal path. The family is a precise surrogate for the $g_{00}$-only weak-field step, with a chosen completion of its spatial part. Equations below are exact for this declared family. Applying a threshold to physical signals would require bounds on omitted weak-field terms, clock imperfections, changes in the propagation path and backreaction. The source branch gives no such bound for this apparatus. [Weak-field comparison limits](https://github.com/amaybaum/incompleteness/blob/942bd5ea1fd09f4223012b0755bce2d6da07c44a/papers/GR.md#L693-L701)

The two clocks have a supplied identical, fixed proper-time period $\theta$. In a calibration block the emitter sends two matched pulses separated by $n=200$ periods of its own clock. The receiver gates an ideal elapsed-time counter at the first reception and retains the number of whole periods completed by the second reception. A reciprocal block exchanges roles with the same stationary source and marks. The records contain the direction, pulse labels without timestamps, and integer counts $(m_{AB},m_{BA})$. A third, held-out pair is emitted by $A$ with a declared separation of 300 periods. For the recoverability test retain its reception event identities and the two calibration counts. The held-out receiving count is withheld. All blocks have a finite common external window because the lapse is bounded away from zero on the domain, and the stationary flight distance and emitted periods are bounded. This defines an ideal finite interface conditional on local tick calibration, retained pulse identity and a fixed propagation path. Optical-clock comparisons are an external example of comparing physical clock rates at different gravitational potentials. [Chou et al.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1192720)

At fixed $r$, proper time satisfies $d\tau=N_\lambda(r)\,dt$. Stationarity makes the coordinate-time flight delay the same for the two pulses in each block. Thus the separation in coordinate time carries unchanged from the emission events to their matching reception events. The two one-way ratios of receiver to emitter proper duration are

$$
q(\lambda)=\frac{N_\lambda(r_B)}{N_\lambda(r_A)}
=\sqrt{\frac{1-2\lambda/r_B}{1-2\lambda/r_A}},\qquad
q_{BA}(\lambda)=q(\lambda)^{-1}.
$$

In particular, the upward signal's received period is greater for positive $\lambda$. The exact rate ratio is strictly increasing on $J$ since $r_A<r_B$ and the derivative of its square has the sign of $1/r_A-1/r_B$. The ratios are unchanged by a common constant rescaling of the stationary time coordinate. They compare durations on marked worldlines, whose identities and matching pulse events must agree on the overlap. The flat $\lambda=0$ case is the algebraic equal-rate control.

Under the specified ideal counter rule $m_{AB}=\lfloor 200q\rfloor$ and $m_{BA}=\lfloor 200/q\rfloor$. For $ij\in\{AB,BA\}$ put $I_{ij}=[m_{ij}/200,(m_{ij}+1)/200)\cap\mathbb R_{>0}$. With the marks and matching of events fixed, a common marked metric in this family exists for the two records exactly when

$$
Q_{\mathrm{joint}}=q(J)\cap I_{AB}\cap\{u^{-1}:u\in I_{BA}\}\ne\varnothing.
$$

Necessity follows by restricting one metric to the two pulse exchanges. For sufficiency take any $q$ in the intersection, use its inverse image $\lambda$ in the predeclared family, and use the same marked exterior with stationary propagation in both directions. The ideal clocks then give both bin labels. With independently bounded calibration or propagation uncertainties, a physical test would require an allowed nuisance-parameter domain shared between the two records. Two independent error bars suffice for the same set calculation only when their simultaneous feasibility has been justified.

**Compatibility control and a failing overlap.** The ideal pair $(m_{AB},m_{BA})=(200,199)$ admits the two positive parameters exhibited below. The pair $(200,198)$ fails the overlap even though each count is individually feasible in this family. The $AB$ bin requires $q<201/200$, and the $BA$ bin requires $q>200/199>201/200$. At $\lambda=R/100$, $q^2=99/98$ is strictly between $(200/199)^2$ and $(200/198)^2$, which admits the count 198 in the reciprocal direction by itself. These are symbolic controls on the exact diagnostic, with no observed clock data asserted.

## 3. Recoverability on the same records

For the held-out receiving events, the target is the proper duration on the marked $B$ clock trajectory binned in units of $\theta$. For every compatible $\lambda$ the proper duration is $300\theta\,q(\lambda)$. The bin factors through the two calibration records on their fiber exactly when $\{\lfloor 300q\rfloor:q\in Q_{\mathrm{joint}}\}$ has one element. The same check could be made on every fiber to test global factorization for this protocol.

It fails on the common compatible record $(200,199)$. Take $\lambda_1=R/250$ and $\lambda_2=R/125$. Then

$$
q(\lambda_1)^2=\frac{249}{248},\qquad
q(\lambda_2)^2=\frac{124}{123},\qquad
1<q(\lambda_1)<\frac{301}{300}<q(\lambda_2)<\frac{201}{200}<\frac{200}{199}.
$$

Both parameters give 200 in the $AB$ direction and 199 in the $BA$ direction. The held-out proper-time bins are 300 and 301, since $q(\lambda_2)<302/300$. The fixed emission/reception labels specify the event roles and are shared in both histories. No independent mass measurement is included in the retained record. Thus an observer can have jointly consistent geometric data without being able to recover a held-out geometric quantity at a finer effective precision. The example is a counterexample in the supplied metric-and-clock family. It leaves the existence of such a pair of native OI histories open.

The matter target is the gauge-invariant charge class of the designated receiving probe at the first held-out reception. The surrogate defines the geometry, event labels and counter behavior without assigning a material carrier or its charge. The reference OI branch also lacks a localized charged probe and a charge map on these same marked histories. A mass label or a lattice symbol gives no value for this target. Its factorization and any equal-record witness therefore remain undefined here. The relevant next source input is a physical probe and charge assignment under the existing matter and record obligations. ## 4. Disposition for #1602

The native comparison stops at the source-conditioned lapse or equivalent clock-rate map and the identification of a material clock and its records on the same configurations. Conditional overlap and proper-time nonrecoverability can coexist. Agreement supplies compatibility with one declared static class. Empty overlap, given the apparatus assumptions, excludes that class for the data. Primitiveness or emergence of the metric, or its dependence on the observer beyond differences in accessible records, requires further evidence. This diagnostic leaves the ontological question underdetermined. Extending the comparison requires a physically sourced clock law and calibrated records along two marked trajectories in one fixed source family. G3 and the existing observer/record and matter obligations are the accountable homes for the needed bridges.
