# Consecutive responses at one site in the rounded cubic rule

**Paper result.** A fixed two-slice read of the binary rounded cubic
rule can recover its first requested response at the next tick. It
cannot use the retained symbol itself to change a newly updated
response at the same site on that tick when the other inputs are held
fixed. The result follows from the center-free stencil. Both first
responses occur at positive mass under the stipulated product law.
The guiding draft linked from [#1407][issue] records the [independent mathematical review](https://github.com/amaybaum/incompleteness/pull/1408#pullrequestreview-5974677165) of the immutable checkpoint `c7109d93099f8aa0c24f1d8885c0c10702bd1d87` and the [focused review](https://github.com/amaybaum/incompleteness/pull/1408#issuecomment-7020303464) of its refresh onto `main`.

**Goal link (§A.47).** Decide whether the rounded-cubic evolution
supplies the local consecutive-request, retained-write mechanism
needed to transfer the P8–P12(b) bridge to a fixed two-slice observer. [Accountable Issue #1407][issue] · [Owner decision][decision]

## Model and observer

Let \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^3\), \(o=0\), and \(N(i)=\{i\pm e_j:1\le j\le
3\}\), where the \(e_j\) are the coordinate unit vectors. Use the
published rounded recurrence on the representatives of
\(\mathcal A_q=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\):

$$
G_i(y)=\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{j\in N(i)}y_j+1}{3}\right\rfloor,\qquad
F(x,y)=(y,G(y)-x)\pmod q.
$$

The observer test uses \(q=2\). The six neighbors remain distinct
on \(\Lambda\). Use the product law \(\mu_q\) in which the two
slices are independent and each slice has independent uniform site
symbols. This law specifies the reference ensemble. [Rounded rule, SM §4.2][rule] · [Two-slice reference law][oacc]

At the transition from time \(t\) to \(t+1\), fix the response site
\(o\) and the request coordinate \(r=e_1\) in its neighbor stencil.
Interpret \(R_t=x_t(r)=1\) as an arriving request and \(R_t=0\) as
no request. In the two-slice state at time \(t+1\), the observer
reads from the fixed patch \(\{o,r\}\) using the coordinates
\(x_t(o),x_t(r),x_{t+1}(o)\). Its response label is

$$
L_{t+1}=
\begin{cases}
x_{t+1}(o) & R_t=1,\\
\bot & R_t=0.
\end{cases}
$$

The separate retention projection is \(x_t(o)\). Every tick follows
the same recurrence. The projection reports \(\bot\) on a tick
without a request, and the field still updates on that tick. The
request convention and read projections are stipulated. The request
bit is an actual input to the native stencil, with a field effect
tested below.

The inverse is \(F^{-1}(y,z)=(G(y)-z,y)\pmod q\). Conditional on a
full slice \(y\) under \(\mu_q\), the updated field \(G(y)-x\)
is product-uniform. For any finite set of sites, its values are
coordinatewise bijective transforms of independent uniform symbols
from \(x\). These cylinder probabilities establish independence of
the updated slice from \(y\) and invariance of \(\mu_q\). In
particular, the two request bits at consecutive ticks are independent
fair bits in the binary test. Two consecutive requests have
probability \(1/4\). The one-tick label probabilities are \(1/4\)
for each of 0 and 1 and \(1/2\) for \(\bot\), since \(x_t(r)\) and
\(x_{t+1}(o)\) are independent.

## Request effect and supported paired histories

Fix the old origin symbol \(x_{t-1}(o)=0\) and fix the other five
neighbors in \(x_t\) with exactly one of them equal to 1. As the
request bit changes from 0 to 1, the neighbor sum changes from
1 to 2. The rounded term \(G_o(x_t)\) changes from 0 to 1, and
the newly updated origin symbol changes with it. These conditions
have positive mass. This verifies a causal effect of the designated
arrival on the native field for that background.

For the consecutive-response test, write \(u=x_t\), \(v=x_{t+1}\)
and \(w=x_{t+2}\), and let \(s=e_2\). For each \(a\in\{0,1\}\)
impose the following finite cylinder in \((u,v)\).

| Site | \(u\) | \(v\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| \(o\) | 0 | \(a\) |
| \(r\) | 1 | 1 |
| \(s\) | \(a\) | 0 |
| Each of the other four sites of \(N(o)\) | 0 | 0 |

Each cylinder has probability \(2^{-14}\) under the invariant law.
Its inverse under \(F\) satisfies
\(x_{t-1}(o)=G_o(u)-v_o=0\), because
\(G_o(u)=\lfloor(2+a)/3\rfloor=a\). Both cases have the same
first request and old origin symbol. The first write is the new
origin value \(v_o=a\), generated by the first stencil with the
varying data input \(u_s\). The inverse supplies a native precursor
for every completion of the cylinder.

Both cases have the same second request \(v_r=1\) and the same
second inputs \(u_o=0\), \(v|_{N(o)}\). Thus \(G_o(v)=0\)
and \(w_o=0\). At the second read the prior origin slot \(v_o\)
still yields \(a\). The two-request histories \((0,0)\) and
\((1,0)\) both occur, with the first response locally recoverable
on the second tick.

## Fixed-site obstruction

In the second update the freshly written symbol at the origin is

$$
w_o=\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{j\in N(o)}v_j+1}{3}\right\rfloor-u_o
\pmod q .
$$

Consequently any two supported trajectories with equal \(u_o\) and
equal \(v|_{N(o)}\) have identical \(w_o\), for any \(q\ge2\).
Holding the second request and read location fixed then gives
identical second response labels for any fixed decoding of \(w_o\)
at that request. The retained \(v_o\) is free to vary under these
equalities, as the two binary cylinders show. This is a pointwise
obstruction and uses the reference law only to certify support of
the exhibited pairs. A joint display of retained and new symbols
can differ through the retained slot even when the new response
agrees.

The test stops at the required fixed-input effect of the first
write on the new second response. It yields no candidate for the
requested positive labelled operation and therefore does not reach
the P8 freshness and positivity tests. The conclusion applies to
two consecutive updates at the same site with the response decoded
from the newly updated symbol. A second response at a neighboring
site can include \(v_o\) in its stencil. After another tick \(v_o\)
can enter the subtraction of the past at the origin. Both routes
lie outside the scope of this obstruction.

## Bare-read controls

Under \(\mu_q\), any fixed current-slice read projection gives
independent adjacent labels, by independence of the two whole
slices. Such a projection therefore gives a single next-label law
conditional on any supported present label. The comparison bridge
gives different next-0 probabilities after 0 and 1, respectively
\(5/12\) and \(1/4\). Its \(1/15\) third-tick gap belongs to that
separate constructed observer. [Comparison bridge at its reviewed commit][bridge]

The adjacent-independence control allows history dependence at
lag two. To calculate it on the binary rule, consider the bare
origin reads \(u_o,v_o,w_o\) with no request projection. The
rounded term \(G_o(v)\bmod2\) is 1 exactly when the six independent
neighbor bits sum to 2, 3 or 4. Its probability is
\((\binom{6}{2}+\binom{6}{3}+\binom{6}{4})/2^6=25/32\).
Because this term is independent of \((u_o,v_o)\),

$$
\Pr(w_o=1\mid u_o=0,v_o=0)=25/32,\qquad
\Pr(w_o=1\mid u_o=1,v_o=0)=7/32.
$$

Both histories have probability \(1/4\). Their third-read
total-variation distance is \(9/16\). This control uses \(u_o\),
the subtraction input, to distinguish the third reads. The
paired-request test varies \(v_o\) with \(u_o\) fixed.

## Evidence scope

The mathematical conclusion is the fixed-site consecutive-tick
obstruction together with the exact binary support and bare-read
controls proved above. A physical request producer, preparation
and executing read coupling on the same fixed histories remain
open. The source obligation is tracked in #866. The comparison
bridge supplies a benchmark, and the cited Issue and owner
decision record the scope and authorization. All derivations
use the recurrence and assumptions stated here.
