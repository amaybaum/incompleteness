## What the finite-horizon result identifies

Main §3.4 equates three classes of visible path laws on a finite alphabet and horizon. One law can have a finite reversible completion and a fixed-basis projectively read unitary representation. Neither construction reconstructs a physical metric or compares horizons and observers. The intervention dilation takes its action-labelled laws as input. The quantum instrument envelope result likewise starts with an independently specified quantum experiment, including its allowed instruments. These statements support a comparison of descriptions at a fixed interface. An ontological distinction between the geometry and the matter behind that interface would require an additional argument.

## A test on a fixed history domain

Let $X$ be a declared set of admissible physical histories and let $R_{o,p}:X\to\mathcal R$ give the record available to observer $o$ for a specific permitted protocol $p$. A candidate physical quantity $F:X\to Y$ is exactly recoverable on that domain if $F=\widehat F\circ R_{o,p}$ for some $\widehat F$ on the realizable records. This holds precisely when $F(x)=F(x')$ for every pair with $R_{o,p}(x)=R_{o,p}(x')$. Factorization implies the equality. Conversely, define $\widehat F$ on a realizable record as the common $F$-value of its preimages.

Apply the criterion to a relational geometric quantity and a matter quantity at the same access and precision. For an observable with gauge freedom, physical values in $Y$ must denote gauge-invariant quantities or equivalence classes. A failure to factor concerns that observer, protocol, and history domain. The test can therefore probe finite access even when $F$ is an observer-independent physical quantity. Its input includes $F$ and $R_{o,p}$, whose physical construction remains an obligation.
