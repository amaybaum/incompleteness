## P6 · Reciprocity on a measurable carrier

**Exact A1 step and evidence.** In the proof at SM:643, $F_{ij}(h)=\pi_H(\Theta\varphi(i,h))$ is a bijection between the forward transition fibre and the reversed one. The proof next takes the **cardinalities** of those fibres and divides by $|\mathcal C_H|$ to get the probability for the common counting prior. Thus only the counted hidden type needs to be finite for this argument. `Reciprocity.key`, `count_reciprocity`, `hT_pow`, `theorem_19` and `detailed_balance` (Reciprocity.lean:67, :79, :107, :129, :142) match these steps. `hT_pow` omits the finite-type assumption. The count and normalization steps use `Fintype H` (:40, :52–53, :129–137). The probability reading requires nonempty $H$ so the normalization is a probability. `detailed_balance` itself states the count equality at $\theta=\mathrm{id}$, and SM:641 gives its normalized specialization. SM:647–649 shows why reciprocal probabilities do not imply a visible Hamiltonian rephasable to a real one.

**Weakest input and standing.** The fibre step needs preservation of the **mass** assigned to the compared transitions by reversal. For all $n$, test the prior plan's transparent sufficient contract: a measurable reversible product carrier with measurable inverse, discrete finite observed states, a measurable partition-preserving involution $\Theta=\theta\times\theta_H$ obeying $\Theta\varphi\Theta=\varphi^{-1}$, a common normalized $\mu_H$ in every visible root, and a joint reference probability $u_V\otimes\mu_H$ invariant under both $\varphi$ and $\Theta$ ($u_V$ uniform). Measurability is needed for the transition fibres. For a general invariant joint law, the natural target is a **weighted** pair flux. To divide it to an equality of conditional probabilities, the compared root masses have to be positive and equal, as they are in the product contract. Stationarity under the dynamics alone supplies neither reversal invariance nor equality of the root weights. The count version remains a finite-model result. SM:625–627 and the native second-order inverse provide model-level time reversal and reversibility with the indicated cut. `RegionTower.uniform_family_consistent` (RegionTower.lean:447) checks consistency of finite-region uniform matrices. It does not select a physical invariant common prior. Even a native measure proof with a specified product law would still leave the selection question for nature.

**Cheapest test, named.** Attempt the measurable fibre-mass comparison for one arbitrary $n$ with a fixed common prior, using the existing reversal map and the checked power identity. The proof should explicitly recover the finite counting case and handle a nonuniform visible reference only through weighted flux. Complete the unit when the probability theorem follows for every stated $n$ on that contract. Failure of equal paired masses with all hypotheses in force would refute the proposed version. A failure without reversal invariance or with a different prior across roots would locate a missing input to the broader version, rather than refute the conditional theorem. Park a native or physical application if it lacks a selected invariant law or reversible cut. The gauge trace §4.4 preferred the narrower torus-to-infinite comparison. Its proposed scope needs suitable time reversal and matching patch marginals. Test the general measure contract first because it targets the stated measurable-carrier theorem directly and avoids the separate patch-matching and closure argument.

**Physical significance.** Under the named premises the result would constrain transition laws of the permitted class and supply the corresponding Stage 2(f) input. A product-reference result cannot justify P7's correlated shell state, and reciprocity does not choose a vacuum, a CKM phase, or a time arrow.
