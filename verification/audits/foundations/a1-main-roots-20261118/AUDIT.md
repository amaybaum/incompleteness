# Does Main require a finite physical universe?

**Disposition: INCOMPLETE for the full bounded target.** Main's exact finite-horizon law equivalence has no premise about the cardinality of nature. Its recurrence proof does use the finiteness of a *particular closed mathematical representative*. Identifying the physical system with that representative invokes the physical posit and needs independent evidence. Neither statement selects the hidden initial state, readout or control operations of an actual experiment. Further open edges are listed below, including exact storage of arbitrary initial states, finite-precision sampling, observer selection, and the physical transfer of the ledger. A1 remains **OPEN / UNADOPTED**. Native physical OI–QM/measurement sourcing remains **NOT PASSED**.

This paper-only source audit covers the nine questions frozen in [ROOTS.md](ROOTS.md). Its goal is to decide the dependence of the nominated Main conclusions on A1. It is the writer's assessment for independent non-author review, not that review. ## R1. The finite-horizon equivalence

Fix a finite visible alphabet V, a finite K, and a normalized nonnegative real law P on V raised to the K+1 trajectory slots. For **each** such law there is a finite hidden type and a permutation with a joint initial probability producing that law. Both hidden type and realization may depend on P and K. The quantum class permits a larger finite basis, a unitary, an initially basis-diagonal probability, projective measurement in that same basis at every step, and a fixed readout from basis labels to V. The observable is the complete trajectory law. These binders are explicit in M550–556 and `Equivalence` 141–217.

## R3. Four different recurrence and readback statements

1. **Finite closed model: FINITE-REPRESENTATIVE CONDITIONAL.** Fix nonempty finite V,H with at least two visible values, bijective φ on V × H, a hidden probability μ shared by *all* roots, and Γₜ(a,j) = μ{h : πV φᵗ(a,h)=j}. A visible prior, when used to sample histories, is independent of μ. The first-step counting formula specializes μ to uniform; M135–145 and the overlap theorem need no full support of μ. At the order L of the permutation, every microstate returns: ΓL = I and Γₜ₊L = Γₜ. A non-permutation stochastic Γₛ, s<L, cannot have a stochastic right inverse to I. Thus there is no divisor at (s,L). Trace: M111–137 → `RootedRecurrenceBridge` 22–73,78–108 → `CausalReadback.rootedMap_isRowStochastic` 190–211, finite permutation order and `EquivalenceChain.isPermMatrix_of_stochastic_inverse` 75–152. The uniform first-step non-permutation makes two rows overlap, yielding the TV proof at M119–127. C1 at an unspecified later step is not that premise. Registry 1652–1658 identifies the iterate-return bridge as a research candidate; Ledger 454–502 records the paper/overlap split.
2. **Histories and full support.** Two positive histories that meet at k force at least one non-permutation Γₛ for 1 ≤ s ≤ min(k,L−1), since permutations at all earlier rooted times would determine the root from the endpoint and the whole prefix from the root. That reasoning and the reduction from a non-permutation to overlapping rows in a finite square stochastic matrix are on paper (M135–139), distinct from the kernel overlap implication. With **full-support visible prior** the absence of all gaps below L gives chronological Markov kernels for *every* root; ΓL = I then makes every intermediate Γₛ a permutation by stochastic-inverse rigidity. If all are permutations, their inverses provide every divisor. Conversely any nondeterministic visible step of positive probability forces a non-permutation somewhere even without full visible support; an unseen root can prevent reversing the conclusion about memory. **FINITE-REPRESENTATIVE CONDITIONAL** for this cycle equivalence. The order need not be accessible. M184–198 gives the literal last-possible-order example L=2, k=1. An arbitrarily late *first positive conditional-memory order* at L−1 is a stronger reading: the delay in `verification/programmes/oi-qm/track-i/recurrence-scaling/result.md` 10–28 is a delay of rooted **indivisibility**, and REC 454–488 controls return and divisibility times. Neither is a proof of that stronger claim. Its first missing edge in this trace is a family controlling all earlier multi-time history gaps.
3. **Provided visible witness: A1-FREE.** P-divisibility through K means that for every s<t≤K a row-stochastic Λ exists with Γₜ = ΓₛΛ. C4r supplies two distinct roots whose row TV strictly increases at *one* such pair. Stochastic postprocessing contracts TV, so that pair forbids a divisor. C4e supplies an earlier row equality and a later inequality, hence C4r. Neither proof takes H, reversibility or recurrence: `CausalReadback` 43–68,76–96,96–115,126–148. With a supplied identity at n and earlier positive overlap, `RecurrenceHorizon` 43–87,104–124 constructs the revival first, then invokes that implication; 134–151 is the finite-realization specialization. With an earlier non-permutation use `ObservableFactorRecurrence.pIndivisible_of_visible_identity_return_nonperm` 68–84 instead. A sufficient **near** return is shared mass m>0 at s, followed at one n>s by both corresponding diagonal probabilities ≥1−ε with 2ε<m (Ladder 93–105,162). These are existing sufficient weaker proofs for the matrix conclusion. None supplies its own Γ, times, overlap or physical preparation. **C4 history readback alone is insufficient:** the fair-root finite XOR law at M630 has one bit of conditional memory and an identity intermediate divisor within its window. The formal matrix control `CausalReadback.control_separation` 216–229,254–299 proves that the I,J,J,… family is P-divisible with neither C4e nor C4r, alongside a different family with C4e and indivisibility at horizon 3. Its statement does not encode the XOR history-level bit; that belongs to the supplied multi-time law.
4. **Routed readback and all-time classification.** Both routed predicates at M145 assume a write, two positive overlapping rows at storage, distinct hidden conditionals, a later read and row reappearance. The storage-time posterior uses Hₛ; the seed version uses H₀. Their return-horizon proofs use only the positive overlap, then identity return; their existential return uses finite periodicity (`PhysicalC4Discharge` 109–135, `PhysicalC4StorageReadback` 244–275 → `RecurrenceHorizon` 140–151 → `CausalReadback` 134; `RootedClassification` 123–139). The predicates fail to imply each other at K=2 (`PhysicalC4StorageReadback` 488–501,689–702). These finite realizations are **FINITE-REPRESENTATIVE CONDITIONAL**. At the class level, Γ₀ = I, row stochasticity for all t and a common positive period characterize the *existence* of one finite root-shared realization for all t: necessity `RootedClassification` 32–36,116–139,339–345, sufficiency by response-table cycles `RootedClassificationT2` 21–81, `RootedClassificationAllTime` 136–167,192–198. This existence classification is **A1-FREE** about nature. M642 explicitly leaves compatible all-time gluing of complete multi-time laws and a physical one-model approximation open.

**Negative controls on physical recurrence.** A normalized invariant law gives almost-everywhere return to a set at point-dependent times under the REC contract, with domination needed for the rooted preparation; it neither gives ΓN = I at one time nor bounds an accessible common time. Both P-divisible and P-indivisible examples satisfy the contract (REC 583–607,620–625). A common global period is guaranteed by the finite-permutation specialization; particular infinite models can have periods too (Ladder 33–41,49–54,107–142). **A1-DEPENDENT** if finite order of *the actual universe* is asserted solely by this finite-cardinality argument. Actual recurrence, return-time accessibility and revival remain **OPEN**. M155's continuous-time statement assumes a common near-return in the required margin; pointwise Poincaré recurrence does not discharge it.

Non-nilpotent α excludes the near-identity route there. These are the existing analytical model-class controls, not new computations or an impossibility theorem for all observers (NR 69–121,166–205,206–292,293–311,335–391,771–795,817–826).
