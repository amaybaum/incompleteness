# Hydrodynamics downstream note — hidden-sector transfer as a possible singularity mechanism

This is a **strategic research note**, not a theorem, preregistration, execution result, manuscript edit, or claim that Operational Incompleteness (OI) resolves Navier–Stokes singularities.

It records one downstream mechanism to test inside the continuum-breakdown branch of `PROGRAMME.md` once the hydrodynamic bridge is strong enough to make the question mathematical rather than interpretive.

## Scope and prerequisite

The question opens **only after** the programme has an explicit microscopic-to-continuum map and convergence notion of the kind required by H4–H7. Before that point, the phrases *visible sector*, *hidden sector*, *transfer*, *loss of closure*, and *singularity of the projection* do not name sufficiently fixed mathematical objects for a theorem.

Nothing in this note changes the status of H1–H7, and nothing here is evidence that the present OI substratum has a Navier–Stokes limit.

## S2a — hidden-sector transfer test

The hypothesis to test is:

> a putative singularity of the emergent Navier–Stokes variables may occur while the full finite reversible OI evolution remains regular because information, correlations, conserved structure, or other dynamically relevant degrees of freedom leave the resolved hydrodynamic sector and become carried by degrees of freedom hidden from the chosen continuum projection.

Schematically,

`regular full OI evolution -> resolved hydrodynamic projection -> loss of regularity or closure`,

with the missing resolved information accounted for by a complementary hidden sector rather than by a singular microscopic update.

This is a **candidate mechanism**, not an interpretation to impose on a singularity after the fact.

## What must be defined before the test can run

A serious version of the test must specify, at minimum:

1. the full finite OI state space and globally defined reversible update;
2. the hydrodynamic coarse-graining/projection supplied by the H4–H7 bridge;
3. the resolved variables whose continuum limit is claimed to satisfy Euler or Navier–Stokes;
4. the complementary degrees of freedom called *hidden* relative to that projection;
5. a quantitative measure of transfer between the resolved and hidden sectors;
6. the regularity or closure property whose failure defines the continuum event being tested.

The hidden sector is therefore **relative to the proved observation/coarse-graining map**. It is not an extra ontology introduced solely to explain a blow-up.

## Distinct mechanisms that must not be conflated

The programme should distinguish at least four logically different outcomes:

- **Microscopic failure:** the underlying OI evolution itself ceases to be defined or regular. This is not a hidden-sector explanation.
- **Projection singularity:** the full evolution stays regular while a resolved continuum observable becomes unbounded.
- **Closure failure:** the resolved observables remain finite but cease to obey a closed hydrodynamic equation because unresolved variables become dynamically necessary.
- **Scale-transfer failure:** the continuum field loses regularity because structure is transferred into degrees of freedom or scales discarded by the chosen projection, while the full finite state remains regular.

The last three are possible OI-style mechanisms. None is established by microscopic reversibility alone.

## Falsifiable target

The useful theorem shape is not "OI explains singularities". It is closer to:

> for a stated OI substratum, a stated continuum map, and a stated regularity class, determine whether finite-time loss of regularity or hydrodynamic closure of the resolved variables can coexist with globally regular microscopic evolution; if it can, determine whether a quantitatively identified transfer into the hidden sector is necessary, sufficient, both, or neither.

A positive result would have to exhibit the transfer or prove the relevant balance law. A negative result would be equally informative: it could show that the chosen hidden sector cannot absorb the missing information/structure, or that visible blow-up forces failure of the microscopic assumptions instead.

## Relation to S1 and S2

This note refines, rather than replaces, the existing continuum-breakdown programme:

- **S1** asks whether regular finite approximants can converge to a singular continuum field.
- **S2** asks what property of the emergent map fails at the singular limit.
- **S2a** asks whether one such failure can be characterized specifically as transfer from the resolved hydrodynamic sector into degrees of freedom hidden by that map.

S2a is therefore downstream of the same H4–H7 prerequisite as S1 and S2.

## Controls

1. **No continuum map, no hidden-sector singularity claim.** The visible/hidden split must come from the actual hydrodynamic bridge.
2. **No analogy as evidence.** Existing hidden-sector or mixing structures elsewhere in the corpus do not transfer into this question without a theorem identifying the same object.
3. **No singularity-by-definition.** A hidden sector may not be defined retrospectively as "whatever is missing". Its variables and projection are fixed before testing the singular behavior.
4. **No conservation hand-wave.** If energy, momentum, information, entropy, correlation, or another quantity is said to move into the hidden sector, the corresponding balance or transfer statement must be explicit.
5. **No conflation of blow-up and closure failure.** Unbounded resolved fields and failure of a reduced equation to remain closed are distinct outcomes.
6. **No manuscript strengthening.** Until a theorem or classified endpoint exists, this remains a roadmap hypothesis only.

## Programme status

The hypothesis is **documented but not activated**. The current hydrodynamics programme has not yet supplied the H4–H7 continuum bridge required to state S2a at theorem strength. When that bridge exists, S2a can be turned into its own frozen research round with the visible/hidden decomposition, transfer observable, regularity criterion, and falsifiers preregistered before execution.
