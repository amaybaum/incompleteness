## 0. Two realization layers and three operational levels

**Realization layers.** Every statement below is assigned to exactly one of these.

- **Born layer (KIN).** States are density matrices on configuration Hilbert spaces. A "site" is a
  tensor factor. Effects and instruments are matrices and CP maps. Coherence (MIO and DIO) and OPRES's
  pair live here.
- **Ontic layer.** States are probability measures on configurations of the substratum. Dynamics are
  finite-range bijections. An outcome is a function of configurations, so the response is a
  configuration function. U and Proposition V live here, and the nonnegative-rank obstruction applies.

A Born-layer construction says nothing about the ontic layer unless an ontic realization with law
equality is given. #1504 Node 2(ii) records that none is given here.

**Operational levels** (the owner's provisional readout constraint, #1169; READOUT-ALG #1513 at
`0ba83cff`, provisional and treated here as a question). Born-layer statements are kept at the
following three levels, and each is said to be at one of them:

- (E) observable effects;
- (In) complete instruments, with their state updates;
- (Acc) operationally accessible observable algebras.

A mathematically available matrix effect or CP instrument is not thereby a physically implementable
measurement. Nothing below relies on the abstract full matrix algebra (Str:742/788, Main:722, GR:366)
to supply operations.

## 2. Node 1: compatibility of the coherence pair (Lemma P; Born layer)

**Lemma P ((N) as algebra; (I) on KIN, which gives "coherence" its meaning; X; level In).**

*Setting.*
- Φ is a CP map on the stage `M_{C_Λ}`.
- `Λ ⊆ Λ'` is a region, with `ext Φ := Φ ⊗ id_{Λ'∖Λ}` the inert-spectator extension.
- `M` is a monomial unitary on `C_Λ`, for instance the permutation unitary of a substratum bijection.

*Statement.*
- **(i)** Φ is MIO-type ⇔ `ext Φ` is MIO-type. Φ is DIO-type ⇔ `ext Φ` is DIO-type.
- **(ii)** Φ is MIO-type (DIO-type) ⇔ `Ad_M ∘ Φ ∘ Ad_M⁻¹` is MIO-type (DIO-type).
- **(iii)** The dual of the substratum automorphism `heisQ` maps the configuration algebra onto itself.
  This is step 1 of OPRES Proposition D, for the Koopman unitary of a bijection. So transport along the
  dynamics preserves DIO-type, and reflects it, in Heisenberg form.

*Proof.*
- **(⇒).** OPRES Lemma C gives closure under `⊗ id` and under composition. `Ad_M` and `Ad_M⁻¹` are
  monomial.
- **(⇐) for (i).** `Φ = Tr_{Λ'∖Λ} ∘ ext Φ ∘ (· ⊗ |x₀⟩⟨x₀|)`. The configuration append and the partial
  trace are both MIO and DIO (Lemma C).
- **(⇐) for (ii).** Conjugate back. ∎

**Corollary P (the two directions are stated separately, per §A.34).** Let 𝓘 be an interface closed
under inert-spectator extension (condition (iii), `InertSpectatorCompositionality`) and under transport
by the dynamics.
- **(→)** Suppose a stage `Λ₀` carries a creator and a detector. Then so does every stage that
  contains `Λ₀` or a transport of `Λ₀`, so the pair is present at every size `≥ |Λ₀|` at those stages.
  This follows from Lemma P.
- **(←)** If those stages carry a pair, then `Λ₀` does. This direction is trivial.

**Classification.** ELABORATING; a corollary of OPRES Lemma C. Gem under PLAN (c), as a reduction.

## 3. Node 2: Theorem W, restricted to the layer it lives on

**W(i) (ontic layer; (I) on KIN for the Born reading; X; ELABORATING, a corollary of U and Lemma C).**
- Suppose every element of a protocol class is DIO-type. Examples are OPRES's 𝒩⁺, and readouts whose
  effects lie in the configuration algebra.
- Then every outcome probability is `tr(ρ Φ*(E))`, with `Φ*(E)` in the configuration algebra.
- So it depends on ρ only through its diagonal, which is a probability measure on configurations.
- The class is therefore a classical ontic model in U's sense, and U and V apply under their premises.
  This adds nothing to U beyond Lemma C.

**W(ii) (Born layer only; (I); X; level In).**
- *Inputs.* `|Q| = 2`, together with:
  - the creator `|+⟩` (OPRES (n2⁺));
  - the detector `⟨±|` (CC-1);
  - the stated class's diagonal phases;
  - configuration readout;
  - Pauli feedforward.
- *Construction.* CC-1 gives `H` exactly on both branches. The ZXZ Euler form
  `R_z H R_z H R_z = R_z R_x R_z` covers U(2).
- *Conclusion.* A qubit's preparations and sharp effects are therefore available as instruments on
  two Born-layer tensor factors, at bounded depth.
- *Scope.* This is a statement about Born-layer support only. It is **not** a countermodel to U.
  - U concerns ontic realizations, and the Born layer leaves U's class.
  - PSD factorizations are not subject to the nonnegative-rank bound.
  - A detector realized on the substratum by dynamics with configuration records is again a classical
    ontic model. Under (xiv), U then still forces its ontic readout cones to grow.

**Consequence.** Whether a detector is present at the Born layer does not decide ontic readout growth.
What decides it is whether the readout's response is a configuration function on the finite-range
substratum, the ontic form of R-READ:
- if it is, U and V apply;
- if it is not, the realization is outside U's class, and what it is (for instance, a native non-classical
  ontic state space) is not identified here.

READOUT-ALG (#1513) addresses the effect level (E) of this question and is under review. Whether every
native instrument (In) is DIO, and what algebra (Acc) is operationally accessible, are both open.

## 4. Node 3: construction C-T (Born layer; typing layer only)

**Construction C-T ((I); X/P; level In).**

*Data.* The region tower 3B of #1504, over a countably infinite site set with `|Q| = 2`. Let 𝓘 be
generated by:
- 𝒩⁺(|+⟩): permutation unitaries of finite-support substratum bijections, configuration appends,
  configuration readout, feedforward, coarse-graining, discard, and appending `|+⟩`;
- the stated class's diagonal phases;
- the detector `⟨±|` on one site.

Close 𝓘 under composition, inert-spectator extension and transport. On each stage Λ, the available
families are the trace-preserving (normalized) families supported on Λ, ancilla regions included.

*Claim.* On every stage, these available families are exactly the normalized finite Kraus instruments.

*Proof.*
- **Completeness.**
  - W(ii) gives every single-qubit unitary.
  - Every multi-qubit unitary follows from single-qubit unitaries and CNOT, a permutation (Barenco et
    al. 1995).
  - Every instrument follows from Stinespring: append a configuration ancilla, apply a unitary, read the
    configuration of the ancilla, coarse-grain, and discard.
- **Soundness.**
  - Every generator is CP and trace-nonincreasing.
  - Composition, `⊗ id`, partial trace, feedforward and coarse-graining all preserve CP.
  - A normalized family is a family of CP maps whose sum is trace-preserving. In finite dimension each CP
    map has a finite Kraus form (Choi), so the family is a finite Kraus instrument. ∎

## 5. Node 4: the operational scope (ontic layer)

**5.1 Proposition V (per-setting unbounded readout; P; (I) on (xiv)).**

*Setting.*
- U's ontic setting, with a countable site set `ι`.
- Kernel finite range (`FiniteRange`, QuasilocalAlgebra.lean:919): each site has a finite, possibly
  non-uniform, neighbourhood. So every protocol of bounded duration has a finite readout cone `R`.
- The setting `n` is written into the configuration independently of the preparation. This is (xiv).

*Claim.* Exactly reproducing a qubit's sharp effects over its continuum of settings requires that, for
all but countably many settings, the readout cone is not contained in any finite region fixed
independently of the configuration.

*Proof.*
- The response of a bounded protocol is a function on `Q^R` for a finite `R ⊂ ι`, with finitely many
  outcomes.
- On a countable `ι` there are countably many pairs of a finite `R` and a response table, **whatever
  the rule catalogue**.
- Under (xiv), an effect is determined by its response.
- So the settings served by bounded-cone protocols carry countably many distinct effects.
- Distinct qubit settings are operationally distinct. ∎

*Premises, each shown load-bearing.*
- **(xiv).**
  - *Countermodel.* Let the preparation law depend on `n`: a Bernoulli law with bias `(1 + n·r_ψ)/2` on
    one site, read at depth 0. It is exact with a cone of one site.
- **Countability of `ι`.**
  - *Countermodel.* Take uncountable `ι`, with an injection `n ↦ i_n` of settings into sites and a
    product prior over sites. The prior has bias `(1 + n·r_ψ)/2` at `i_n` (it exists by Kolmogorov
    extension for arbitrary index sets) and is independent of `n`, so (xiv) holds.
  - The protocol for `n` reads site `i_n` at depth 0. It is exact, with a one-site cone for every
    setting.
  - The countable rule-catalogue clause (A4) of r1 is **not** load-bearing and is withdrawn.

*Relation to U.* U needs unbounded cones over the set of settings, and uses [55]. V needs them at almost
every setting, by cardinality alone. **Classification.** ELABORATING; gem under PLAN (b), for the two
countermodels.

**5.2 Bearing on D-W.** D-W is detector-free, so it lies in W(i)'s class. Its seed law is non-atomic.
On a finite-alphabet, site-factorized, countable lattice, Proposition V forces a lattice form of D-W to
have configuration-dependent readout cones.

**5.3 Lattice D-W reach (sketch; INCONCLUSIVE).**
- *The scheme.*
  - Store each seed as a digit stream.
  - Compare it, digit by digit, against a threshold computed in exact signed-digit arithmetic
    (Weihrauch 2000).
  - Stop at the first digit that decides.
  - The number of digits read satisfies P(> m) = 2^{−m}.
- *Missing.*
  - A finite-range reversible universal rule that carries out the scheme (Toffoli and Margolus 1990;
    Morita 2017).
  - Such a rule is nonlinear, which conflicts with A5 (ledger (vii)). Whether a linear rule admits exact
    configuration-dependent readouts for a continuum of settings is open (§7, PR-U3).

## 7. Preregistrations (not run; computation is not authorized)

**PR-U2: exact check of W(ii)'s generator identities (Born layer).**
- *Question.* Over `ℚ(i)`, with symbolic `cos` and `sin` parameters, does one-bit teleportation with
  Pauli feedforward give `H` on every branch? Does the ZXZ Euler form equal a generic `U(2)` element?
- *Decision.* Exact symbolic matrix equality. The outcome is one of three:
  - equal;
  - unequal, with a witness entry;
  - undecided, recorded as its own outcome.
- *Controls.*
  - *Positive.* `H² = I` must come out equal.
  - *Negative, failing for a known reason.* Replace `⟨±|` with the configuration readout `⟨0/1|`. The
    protocol is then DIO-type, and Theorem C(b) forbids `H`. The check must return unequal, with a
    witness. Any other return voids the run.

**PR-U3: the linear-rule question at finite resolution (ontic layer).**
- *Question.* Over GF(2), the readouts are Boolean combinations of affine conditions on the preparation
  digits and setting digits, with stopping depth at most `D`. Is there a family of preparation measures
  **independent of the setting** (that is, satisfying (xiv)) that reproduces `(1 + n·r)/2` exactly on
  every setting of an `m`-digit signed-digit grid?
- *Decision.* An exact rational LP feasibility problem for each `(m, D)`. The outcome is one of three:
  - accept, with a verified feasible point;
  - reject, with a verified Farkas certificate;
  - undecided, recorded as its own outcome.
- *Controls.*
  - *Positive.* This runs in the **relaxed LP**, where the preparation variables are indexed by
    `(ψ, n)`, so that the preparation may depend on the setting. The dropped-(xiv) countermodel of §5.1
    must be accepted there.
  - *Negative, failing for a known reason.* Run the main LP with readouts restricted so that they read
    no setting digit, at `m ≥ 1`. Two grid settings with different Born values for some ψ then cannot be
    told apart, so the check must reject with a certificate. Accept or undecided voids the run.
- *Scope.* Finite resolution only.
