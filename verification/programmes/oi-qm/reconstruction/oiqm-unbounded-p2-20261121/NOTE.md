## 0. Outcome, per scope (PLAN §5)

**Representational scope** (Born form on configurations, under KIN):

- **CONSTRUCTION, (I).** Exact finite operational QM holds on every region stage of the binary lattice,
  compatible across sizes, generated by the OPRES pair (§3) plus continuous diagonal phases.
- **OBSTRUCTION** to the coherence pair, for the detector-free class (§2). It is sharp: the countermodel
  of §2.2 drops the one premise it needs.

**Operational scope** (record laws):

- **OBSTRUCTION, (I) on (xiv).** Proposition V (§4) strengthens U per setting. Its premise, (xiv), is
  shown unavoidable by a countermodel.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** on the lattice form of D-W (§4.3). Its readout bound is a sketch.

**Main finding (gem (d), an exposed assumption in the target).** The target couples (a), a coherence
pair at every size, with (b), unbounded readout reach "forced by U". The two have disjoint scopes:

- U forces growth **exactly on the detector-free protocol class** (Theorem W(i)).
- (a) requires a detector at every size.
- Once a detector is present, bounded reach suffices (Theorem W(ii)).

So no tower can have both (a) and a reach that U forces. The coherence pair is what releases the
growth requirement. Reach growth is forced only where (a) fails.

## 1. Node 1: compatibility of the coherence pair (Lemma P)

**Lemma P ((N) as algebra; (I) on KIN, which gives "coherence" its meaning; X).** Take the following:

- a CP map Φ on the stage `M_{C_Λ}`;
- a region `Λ ⊆ Λ'`, with `ext Φ := Φ ⊗ id_{Λ'∖Λ}`, the inert-spectator extension;
- a monomial unitary `M` on `C_Λ`, for instance the permutation unitary of a substratum bijection.

Then:

- **(i)** Φ is MIO-type ⇔ `ext Φ` is MIO-type, and Φ is DIO-type ⇔ `ext Φ` is DIO-type.
- **(ii)** Φ is MIO-type (DIO-type) ⇔ `Ad_M ∘ Φ ∘ Ad_M⁻¹` is MIO-type (DIO-type).
- **(iii)** On the quasilocal algebra, the dual of the substratum automorphism `heisQ` maps the
  configuration algebra onto itself. This is OPRES Proposition D, step 1, for the Koopman unitary of a
  bijection. So transport along the dynamics preserves and reflects DIO-type in Heisenberg form.

*Proof.*

- **(⇒)** OPRES Lemma C: MIO-type and DIO-type maps are closed under `⊗ id` and under composition.
  `Ad_M` and `Ad_M⁻¹` are monomial, hence MIO and DIO.
- **(⇐) for (i).** `Φ = Tr_{Λ'∖Λ} ∘ ext Φ ∘ (· ⊗ |x₀⟩⟨x₀|)` for any configuration `x₀`. Appending a
  configuration state and taking a partial trace are both MIO and DIO (Lemma C), and the classes are
  closed under composition.
- **(⇐) for (ii).** Conjugate back by `Ad_M⁻¹`. ∎

**Corollary P (two directions, stated separately per §A.34).** Let 𝓘 be an interface on the region
tower that is closed under inert-spectator extension (condition (iii), `InertSpectatorCompositionality`)
and under transport by the dynamics.

- **(→)** If some stage `Λ₀` carries a creator and a detector, then every stage `Λ' ⊇ Λ₀`, and every
  transport of one, carries them. This follows from Lemma P(i)–(iii).
- **(←)** If every stage carries a pair, then in particular `Λ₀` does. This direction is trivial.

**Classification.** ELABORATING, as a corollary of OPRES Lemma C. It is a gem under PLAN (c): the
compatible pair "at every size" reduces to a pair at one size together with inert spectators. For the
pair, compatibility costs nothing beyond condition (iii).

## 2. Node 2: coupling through detection (Theorem W)

R-READ is not assumed in either direction. What matters is whether the protocol class contains a
non-DIO element. R-READ, together with OPRES §5's native class, is one way such an element could be
absent.

**Theorem W ((I) on KIN; X for (i), X/P for (ii)).**

- **(i) Detector-free ⇒ U applies.** Suppose every element of a protocol class is DIO-type: for
  instance, everything in OPRES's 𝒩⁺, together with readouts whose effects lie in the configuration
  algebra. Then:
  - every outcome probability is `tr(ρ Φ*(E))`, with `Φ*(E)` in the configuration algebra (Lemma C;
    Proposition D in Heisenberg form);
  - so it depends on ρ only through its diagonal, which is a probability measure on configurations;
  - the class is therefore a classical ontic model in U's sense, and U's factorization applies
    verbatim, as does Proposition V (§4).

  Exact qubit behaviour then forces readout cones that lie in no finite region. This holds under
  (xiv), as U requires.
- **(ii) A detector releases the growth (countermodel).** Let `|Q| = 2`. Admit the following:
  - one creator, appending `|+⟩` (OPRES (n2⁺));
  - one detector, the destructive `⟨±|` readout of OPRES CC-1;
  - continuous diagonal phases `diag(1, e^{iα})`;
  - configuration readout, and Pauli feedforward. Pauli X is a permutation and Z is a phase.

  Then:
  - one-bit teleportation (CC-1) gives H exactly, with two sites and constant depth;
  - the Euler form `U = e^{iδ} R_z(α) H R_z(β) H R_z(γ)` gives every single-qubit unitary exactly;
  - so every pure-state preparation (`U|0⟩`) and every sharp effect (`U†` followed by configuration
    readout) of a qubit is realized exactly, within two sites and a depth bounded independently of the
    setting.

  The qubit's prepare-and-measure behaviour is thus reproduced exactly with **bounded** reach. U's
  conclusion fails once the detector is admitted, so the detector-free premise of (i) is the
  unavoidable one.

**Consequence.** Requirement (a) demands a detector at every size. U demands growth only where no
detector acts. So "a native interface whose reach grows without bound, as U forces" and "a coherence
pair at every size" cannot both hold with the growth forced.

- On the detector-free class, (a) fails. Under R-READ and OPRES §5's reading of the native class, this
  is OPRES's native status, (N) as algebra, (I) on KIN.
- With a detector, growth is optional.
- What the owner's target can still mean: either (b) is a *permission* (reach may grow) and not a
  forced consequence, or (b) applies only to the detector-free sub-interface. §7 records this as a
  question for the owner.

**Classification.** NEW (candidate), relative to the corpus. It is a gem under PLAN (b) and (d):

- (b): the premise is shown unavoidable by the countermodel (ii);
- (d): it exposes that the target's (a) and its "U forces (b)" are coupled in opposite directions.

*Skepticism (this branch is favourable to a construction).* (ii) imports three things: the creator,
the detector and continuous phases. None is native. A continuous phase family is posited: ones-fixing
phases have a kernel no-go, `PhaseSource.lean:55`, and native phase sourcing is not checked here. So (ii)
shows only that U's premise is unavoidable. It shows no native bounded-reach interface.

## 3. Node 3: the construction, representational scope (C-T)

**Construction C-T ((I); X/P).** Take the region tower 3B of #1504 over a countably infinite site set
with `|Q| = 2`. Let 𝓘 be generated by:

- 𝒩⁺(|+⟩): permutation unitaries of finite-support substratum bijections, configuration appends,
  configuration readout, feedforward, discard and postselection, plus appending `|+⟩`;
- continuous diagonal phases;
- the detector `⟨±|` on one site.

𝓘 is closed under composition, inert-spectator extension and transport by the dynamics.

*Claim.* For every finite region Λ, the operations of 𝓘 supported on Λ, using ancilla regions, include
every normalized finite Kraus instrument on `M_{C_Λ}` exactly. The derivation:

- Single-qubit unitaries come from W(ii).
- Exact multi-qubit unitaries come from single-qubit unitaries and CNOT (Barenco et al., PRA 52, 3457
  (1995)). CNOT is a permutation.
- Instruments come from Stinespring: append a configuration ancilla, apply an exact unitary, read the
  ancilla in the configuration basis (OPRES (n3)), and discard.

Soundness is not claimed. 𝓘 may contain more than Kraus instruments only through postselection, and the
normalized sub-interface is what is meant.

## 4. Node 4: the operational scope

**4.1 Proposition V (per-setting stopping-time readouts; P; (I) on (xiv); native premises (viii) and A4
with a countable rule catalogue).**

*Setting.* This is U's setting: the detector-free class, by Theorem W(i). In addition:

- interventions come from a countable catalogue of translation-covariant finite-radius rules (A4);
- the measurement setting `n` is written into the configuration by the setting mechanism, independently
  of the preparation. This is (xiv).

*Claim.* Exact reproduction of a qubit's sharp effects over its continuum of settings requires the
following. For all but countably many settings, the readout's duration is not bounded independently of
the configuration. The outcome must be registered at a configuration-dependent time (a stopping time),
and that time is unbounded over the configurations.

*Proof.*

- Fix a protocol and a deterministic depth `K`.
- The outcome is a function of `(x_prep|_R, s(n)|_R)` for the finite cone `R`. As `n` varies, at most
  `|Q|^{|R|}` distinct response functions arise.
- There are countably many pairs of protocol and `K`, so the settings served by some bounded-depth
  protocol have countably many distinct effects.
- Distinct qubit settings are operationally distinct: some ψ separates them. So these settings form a
  countable set. ∎

*Stronger than U.* U needs unbounded reach over the set of settings, and uses [55]. V needs it at almost
every single setting, by cardinality alone.

*Unavoidability of (xiv) (countermodel).* Drop (xiv), and let the preparation law depend on `n`. Take
`μ_{ψ,n}` as a Bernoulli law with bias `(1 + n·r_ψ)/2` on one visible site, read at depth 0. This
reproduces every qubit statistic exactly with reach one. So (xiv) is load-bearing.

*Unavoidability of A4's countable catalogue.* The kernel's `ReversibleDynamics` admits site-dependent
rules, of which there are uncountably many. With such rules the protocol could carry `n`. The bound then
fails, and that protocol is not native under A4.

**Classification.** ELABORATING: a strengthening of U at the same premises plus A4. It is a gem under
PLAN (b) for the two countermodels.

**4.2 Bearing on D-W.** D-W (GATE-A r5 at `fd3cb064`, §2.2) is a classical stepwise simulator. It is
detector-free, so it lies in W(i)'s class. Its seed law is non-atomic. On a finite-alphabet,
site-factorized lattice under A4, Proposition V therefore forces a lattice form of D-W to use
stopping-time readouts.

**4.3 Lattice D-W reach (sketch; INCONCLUSIVE).** Each seed entry is a uniform real stored as a digit
stream. Compare it, digit by digit, against a threshold computed from the stored state in exact
signed-digit arithmetic (computable in the Type-2 sense: Weihrauch, *Computable Analysis*, 2000).
Stop at the first digit that decides.

- The number of digits read is geometrically distributed: P(> m) = 2^{−m}.
- If the threshold's cost is polynomial in the digits, the expected reach is finite and the supremum is
  infinite.

Two steps are missing:

- **mathematics:** a finite-range reversible universal rule that carries out this computation (for
  example a Margolus-type rule; Toffoli and Margolus 1990; Morita 2017);
- **conflict:** such a rule is nonlinear. The selected substratum is linear, (A5, ledger (vii)).
  Whether a linear rule admits exact stopping-time readouts for a continuum of settings is open. The
  first step is in §6.

## 6. Preregistrations (not run; computation is not authorized)

**PR-U2: exact check of W(ii)'s generator identities.**

- *Question.* Over `ℚ(i)` with symbolic `cos` and `sin` parameters, check two identities:
  - one-bit teleportation with Pauli feedforward equals `H` on a generic input, for every outcome
    branch;
  - the Euler form equals a generic `U(2)` element, with its parameters solved exactly.
- *Decision method.* Exact symbolic equality of matrices. Three outcomes are recorded: equal, unequal
  (with a witness entry), or undecided (the simplifier returns neither). Undecided is never folded into
  pass or fail.
- *Controls.*
  - Positive: an identity known to hold, `H² = I`, must return equal.
  - Negative, failing for a known reason: replace the `⟨±|` detector by a configuration readout
    `⟨0/1|`. Teleportation must then fail to give `H`, because the protocol is then DIO-type and
    Theorem C(b) forbids it. The check must return unequal, with a witness. Any other return voids the
    run.

**PR-U3: the linear-rule question of §4.3, at finite resolution.**

- *Question.* Over GF(2), take readouts that are Boolean combinations of affine conditions on the
  preparation and setting digits, with stopping depth at most `D`. Is there a preparation measure family
  reproducing `(1 + n·r)/2` exactly, on every setting of an `m`-digit signed-digit grid?
- *Decision method.*
  - For each `(m, D)`, write an exact rational LP feasibility problem over the response class.
  - accept: an exact rational feasible point, verified.
  - reject: an exact Farkas certificate, verified.
  - undecided: neither is obtained. It is recorded as an outcome in its own right.
- *Controls.*
  - Positive: the dropped-(xiv) countermodel of §4.1 must be accepted.
  - Negative, failing for a known reason: restrict the response class to readouts that do not read any
    setting digit, at `m ≥ 1`. Every setting then receives the same statistic for each preparation.
    Two grid settings `n ≠ n'` have Born values that differ for some ψ, so the check must reject, with a
    certificate. An accept or an undecided result voids the run.
- *Scope.* Finite resolution only. It does not decide the continuum question.
