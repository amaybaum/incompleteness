### Node 2 — Duality dictionary (P-here, standard)

Ω is compact abelian with discrete dual R². By Pontryagin duality, closed subgroups L ⊂ Ω and subgroups M ⊂ R² correspond bijectively through annihilators, with L = M^⊥ and M = L^⊥. Since q is prime, subgroups are F_q-subspaces.
- **(L1) ⇔ M is an R-submodule.** Translating a character is multiplication by a monomial.
- **(L2) ⇔ F̂M = M.**
  - (⇒) If F(L) ⊆ L and χ ∈ M, then χ∘F vanishes on L, so F̂χ ∈ M. The same argument with F^{−1} gives F̂^{−1}M ⊆ M.
  - (⇐) If F̂M ⊆ M, then for ξ ∈ L and χ ∈ M, χ(Fξ) = (F̂χ)(ξ) = 0, so Fξ ∈ M^⊥ = L. The same argument with F̂^{−1} gives F^{−1}L ⊆ L.
- **(L3) is the Lagrangian condition on L.**
  - Put J(a_1, a_2) = (−a_2, a_1). A character a, evaluated on ξ, equals ω(−Ja, ξ), so L = M^⊥ = (JM)^{ω⊥}.
  - J preserves λ: λ(Ja, Jb) = a_1b̄_2 − a_2b̄_1.
  - Hence M = M^{⊥λ} iff the module JM of finitely supported generators is maximal isotropic, with L its ω-orthogonal.

The rank of M is r = dim_K(KM) ∈ {0, 1, 2}.

### Node 3 — Rank and entropy endpoints (P-here)

**Lemma 3.1 ((L3) ⇒ r = 1).**
1. λ extends to a nondegenerate sesquilinear form on K², with Gram matrix [[0, 1], [−1, 0]].
2. M^{⊥λ} = R² ∩ (KM)^⊥, and K(R² ∩ V) = V for every K-subspace V (clear denominators). So rank M^{⊥λ} = dim (KM)^⊥ = 2 − r.
3. M = M^{⊥λ} then forces r = 2 − r, so r = 1. ∎

**Lemma 3.2 (r = 0).** A torsion-free module of rank 0 is zero. So M = 0, L = Ω, and the Haar measure of L is the product Haar law, with entropy density 2 log q. ∎

**Lemma 3.3 (r = 2 ⇒ entropy density 0).**
1. Take R-independent m_1, m_2 ∈ M, and set B = [m_1 m_2] and Δ = det B ≠ 0. From B·adj(B) = ΔI, Δe_i = adj(B)_{1i} m_1 + adj(B)_{2i} m_2 ∈ M.
2. So the characters (Δ, 0) and (0, Δ) annihilate L. Every (u, v) ∈ L has u, v ∈ X_Δ, where X_Δ = {η ∈ F_q^{ℤ^d} : Σ_w Δ(w) η(w + z) = 0 for all z}.
3. X_Δ has topological entropy density 0:
   - Choose ℓ ∈ ℤ^d with ℓ·w maximized at a single w* ∈ supp Δ. A lexicographic weight ℓ = (1, N, …, N^{d−1}) with N larger than the span of supp Δ does this.
   - Let ρ = max_{w ∈ supp Δ} |w − w*|_∞, and B_n = {0, …, n−1}^d.
   - Take s ∈ B_n with s − w* + supp Δ ⊆ B_n. The relation at z = s − w* gives η(s) as a combination of values at sites of B_n with smaller ℓ-weight.
   - By induction on ℓ-weight, η|_{B_n} is determined by η on the at most 2dρn^{d−1} sites of B_n not of that kind.
   - Hence |π_{B_n} X_Δ| ≤ q^{2dρn^{d−1}}, and the entropy density, the limit of n^{−d} log |π_{B_n} X_Δ|, is 0.
4. The Haar measure of L is supported on X_Δ × X_Δ, so its entropy density is 0. ∎

**Corollary 3.4.** If (L1) holds and the Haar entropy density of L is exactly log q (reading (L3′)), then r = 1. This holds because r = 0 gives 2 log q and r = 2 gives 0. ∎

So both readings of "Lagrangian" force a rank-one annihilator. Any further reading that implies either one is covered.

### Node 4 — The eigenline obstruction (P-here)

**Proposition 4.1.** Let q be prime and α ∈ F_q^×. An F̂-invariant submodule M ⊂ R² of rank 1 exists only if d = 1 and α² = 1.

*Proof.*
1. KM is an F̂-invariant K-line, so it is spanned by an eigenvector. Its eigenvalue λ₀ ∈ K is a root of the characteristic polynomial t² − Xt + 1 of [[X, 1], [−1, 0]].
2. R is a localization of a polynomial ring, hence a UFD and integrally closed. A root in K of a monic polynomial over R therefore lies in R.
3. From λ₀(X − λ₀) = 1, λ₀ is a unit of R. The units of R are cx^k with c ∈ F_q^× and k ∈ ℤ^d.
4. Then X = λ₀ + λ₀^{−1} = cx^k + c^{−1}x^{−k} has at most two monomials.
5. X has exactly 2d monomials, α x_j^{±1}, each with coefficient α ≠ 0. So 2d ≤ 2, which gives d = 1, {k, −k} = {1, −1} and c = c^{−1} = α. ∎

**Proposition 4.2 (d = 1, α² = 1: the invariant lines are not isotropic).**
1. The eigenvalues are λ₀ = αx^{±1}, with eigenvectors m = (−λ₀, 1). Check: XΦ + Ψ = λ₀Φ and −Φ = λ₀Ψ.
2. λ(m, m) = −λ₀ + λ̄₀ = α(x^{∓1} − x^{±1}) ≠ 0.
3. For r, s ∈ K^×, λ(rm, sm) = rs̄ λ(m, m) ≠ 0. So no nonzero submodule of Km is isotropic, and (L3) fails. ∎

**Theorem 1 (P-here).** Let q be prime and α ∈ F_q^×.

- (c) For d ≥ 2, every closed subgroup with (L1) and (L2) is either Ω or has Haar entropy density 0.

At the registered tuple (2, 1, 3), (a), (b) and (c) all apply.

*Proof.*

- (a): Lemma 3.1 gives r = 1, and Propositions 4.1–4.2 exclude r = 1 with isotropy.

*Remark (coset laws).* The reduction from a coset to its subgroup is needed only for a law assumed invariant under J1 rather than given by an L with (L1) and (L2). If the Haar law of ξ₀ + L is invariant, so is its support ξ₀ + L, and L = (ξ₀ + L) − (ξ₀ + L) is invariant because F and the translations are additive.

**Corollary 1.2 (periodic backgrounds).** For d ≥ 2, Theorem 1(a), (b) and (c) hold with F replaced by F^k for any k ≥ 1.
1. F̂^k has characteristic polynomial t² − D_k(X)t + 1, where D_k(λ + λ^{−1}) = λ^k + λ^{−k}.
2. D_k(y) = y^k plus lower powers of y of the same parity. The monomial x_j^{±k} occurs in X^k with coefficient α^k ≠ 0, through the single walk. It does not occur in X^{k−2i} for i ≥ 1, whose monomials have ℓ¹-length at most k − 2.
3. So D_k(X) has at least 2d ≥ 4 monomials, and the argument of Proposition 4.1 excludes an F̂^k-invariant rank-one M.
4. Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 3.4 then give (a) and (b). Lemmas 3.2–3.3 give (c). ∎
