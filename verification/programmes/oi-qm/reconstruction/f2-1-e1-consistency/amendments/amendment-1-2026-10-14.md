# F2-1 amendment 1 (2026-10-14): phase conventions, an exact density route, separated failure modes

Append-only amendment to `../RULES.md`, which is unchanged. Committed before the measurements it
affects are taken under it. Any measurement already taken under the unamended rules is kept as
prior evidence and is rerun under this amendment; only the rerun counts.

## A1. Phase and logarithm conventions

R2 fixes each Weil image only up to a global phase, and a principal logarithm or square root is
not invariant under that phase: when a global phase moves an eigenphase across the branch cut, the
half-step operation changes by more than a global phase.

- **Canonical convention.** Each whole-step operation is the image under the canonical Weil
  representation of Sp(2N, 𝔽_q) for odd prime q (a genuine homomorphism, by an explicit formula,
  not a projective choice). The principal branch of the logarithm and of the square root takes
  eigenphases in (−π, π].
- **Convention scan.** C3(a) and C3(c) are also evaluated for every global phase of the
  whole-step operation, equivalently for each of the finitely many positions of the branch cut
  between its distinct eigenphases, and for C3(c) for every square root of the whole-step
  operation modulo a global phase (one sign choice per eigenspace). Each is reported per
  convention, with the summary `ALL`, `SOME` (listing which) or `NONE`.

## A2. The density inference made exact

C3(c) is replaced by:

- **(c1) Clifford generation, exact.** The integer-time repertoire — the leap images for every
  symmetric coupling and the position translations, with at N = 2 the cross-site couplings —
  generates the full Clifford group modulo phases. This is checked exactly: the symplectic parts
  generate Sp(2N, 𝔽_q), and with the translations every Weyl operator, so that the group generated
  modulo phases has order q^{2N} · |Sp(2N, 𝔽_q)|.
- **(c2) A non-Clifford gate, exact.** A named repertoire element (for instance the leap at
  t = 1/2 under a named convention) does not normalize the Weyl group: U X U† or U Z U† is not
  proportional to any Weyl operator, decided exactly.
- **(c3) Density, by literature.** (c1) and (c2) give density in SU(q^N) by Campbell, Anwar and
  Browne (Phys. Rev. X 2, 041021 (2012)), Appendix D: for odd prime dimension, the Clifford group
  together with any non-Clifford gate is dense. The statement of that appendix used, and its
  hypotheses checked against (c1) and (c2), are recorded. Evidence type [X] for (c1) and (c2), [L]
  for (c3).
- **Supporting only.** A numerical infinite-order test is supporting evidence [N] and decides
  nothing. Infinite order is a property of the generated group: the half-period gate itself may have
  finite order while the group it generates with the Clifford images is infinite.

## A3. Separated failure modes

- Every negative verdict names the repertoire that failed. If the half-period gate fails (c2), the
  token is `E1-HALF-PERIOD-CLIFFORD` under the named convention, not a verdict on control. The round
  then reports, as separate cells, the durations t = 1/3 and t = 1/4 under the same conventions, and
  the all-duration repertoire of C3(a).
- **Global phase is not required.** C3(a)'s criterion is that the generated algebra contains
  su(q^N): its dimension modulo the identity is q^{2N} − 1. Control of the global phase is not
  part of the criterion. `E1-FULL-ALGEBRA` iff that holds.
- The outcome class `E1-CONSISTENT-NOT-CONTROLLING` is replaced by the tokens of the repertoires
  tested, each with its result.

## A4. Implementation checks

- **C1 coverage.** For each tested (q, N) and each symmetric coupling M, the words are every word
  of length at most 6 over the alphabet {the leap L_M} ∪ {the position translations τ_1, …, τ_N},
  enumerated exhaustively. The output prints the alphabet, the number of words of each length and
  the total. Passing establishes consistency over exactly those protocols. Words that mix couplings
  are not part of C1. The symplectic meter used in the two-time law is named in the output.
- **K6 dropped-generator mutation, named.** The mutation removes every position translation and
  every source term, leaving the leaps alone. C3(b) shows that repertoire commutes with the parity,
  so the generated algebra must be strictly smaller than su(q^N); the mutation is detected iff the
  dimension printed by C3(a)'s computation on the mutated repertoire is below q^{2N} − 1. Removing a
  single generator that may be redundant is not used as a mutation.
