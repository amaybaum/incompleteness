# Known-history tags and the post-Y reset contract

Research diagnostic. A fixed comparison decoder can accommodate the direct-Y
candidate's known-history tag, but this does not satisfy or change the strict
history-independent raw-carrier requirement. The useful additional result is
that removing this encoding obstruction would still leave a concrete
post-read reset obligation: unchanged post-Y samples leave the positive decoder
cone after X, R or Z terminal reads.

The construction below is an abstract positive control on supplied calibrated
laws. It does not splice the standalone direct-reset gadget into q8, prepare
new native resources, enlarge observer access, or adopt a tagged-interface
extension. It changes neither the exact-QM target nor an A2 verdict.

## The fixed map: known history is a coordinate

Write j=(X,Y,R,Z) for the twelve-state calibrated carrier. For a bit g define
one injective raw embedding

    E_g(j) = ((X,Y,1 XOR g XOR XY XOR Y),
              (Y,Y,1 XOR g),
              (R,Y,1 XOR g XOR RY XOR Y),
              (0,0,Z)).

The direct-reset candidate's entire output is exactly E_g(j) with g=xy,
where x was public earlier and y is its new Y outcome. This follows by
substituting X=y XOR u and R=y XOR v into its raw mixer formulas. A fixed
extraction reads j from the first fields of the first three cells and the
last field of the fourth. It reads g as one XOR the middle cell's last field.
It requires no choice of decoder after seeing x or y.

Let h be an explicitly retained classical record, containing x,y and all other
allowed public data. On measures over (h,E_g(j)), define one linear map

    D_bar(mu) = sum_h |h><h| tensor D_h(mu_h),
    D_h = D_0 after the same fixed raw-coordinate extraction,
    D_0(nu) = [mass(nu) I + E_nu((-1)^X) sigma_X
                          + E_nu((-1)^Y) sigma_Y
                          + E_nu((-1)^Z) sigma_Z] / 2.

Here h=(x,y,other records) contains an immutable original (x,y), and later
records are appended rather than rewriting those values. Each D_h uses the
same extraction specified above; the block notation does not introduce a
history-selected extraction rule. Here nu is the extracted j measure. The
direct sum is a classical
history register tensored with the quantum carrier. This is one globally
specified map; conditioning on a classical history selects a block of that
map, rather than choosing a new map. It can also discard the history blocks
by summation when only the carrier is compared.

Positivity holds on the cone generated by the eight supplied preparation
ensembles, embedded at each allowed history and tag. It is not asserted on
arbitrary ontic point masses. For example the direct post-Y distribution
has Bloch vector (0,(-1)^y,0) for either previous x. Different x values at y=1
still produce disjoint raw supports, TV=1. Their classical records already
differ, so a classical-record/quantum-carrier comparison need not identify
the complete recorded states. Only their conditional quantum carrier is the
same. Omitting x from an interface in which its tag is exposed would remove
this justification; the raw tag would then reveal an omitted distinction.

Consequently a known-history tag is not an algebraic obstruction to a fixed
global comparison. It remains an obstruction under the stricter common raw
encoding acceptance rule. Adopting the larger encoding class would be a
separate interface decision, and neither that decision nor native physical
closure follows here. A mathematical history register is not a newly built
native memory/readback mechanism.

## Minimal full-law obligation at the next reset

For each allowed preceding public history h0, including its earlier X outcome
x, and every admissible physical boundary law representing rho, a candidate
Y stage must supply a complete kernel. In the tagged example the success
part would obey

    Pr(y,m,G=1,raw=E_(xy)(j) | h0,rho)
      = Tr(P_Y,y rho) kappa(m | h0,y) (5/16) mu_Y,y(j).

m includes every additional exposed port field, prefix, clock/stopping record,
prior-record continuation and herald metadata. Kappa must be independent of
rho and the outgoing sample j. The appropriate condition is branchwise for
the actual admissible boundary, not just after pooling hidden histories.
The failure part must retain its complete declared records with weight 11/16
and a terminal no-carrier symbol; no post-failure qubit state is supplied.
The independent seed law must hold at use conditional on relevant input and
history, or the actual joint seed/input measure must be retained explicitly
in that kernel. Any different proposed herald probability changes this formula
and must be stated and justified; the constants here are the inherited gadget's.

For fixed y the successful decoded six-state law is

    j=(y XOR a,y,y XOR b,z),
    Pr((a,b)=00,10,11 | success)=5/10,3/10,2/10,
    z independent fair.

The three planes times two z values are the six supported carrier states;
this is not a claim that a six-cell native geometry has been constructed.
The whole raw cells at a subsequent terminal X or R read have the form

    (r,y,1 XOR xy XOR (r XOR y)y),

and hence are determined by the already-recorded (x,y) and designated outcome
r. The Y cell is determined by (x,y); the Z cell is (0,0,r). Those fields add
no extra information on this particular post-Y family. This does not prove
that arbitrary later dynamics is insensitive to the tag or residual memory.

To invoke #912's composition theorem, the actual output two-slice phase must
belong to the next boundary cone, and each complete-record branch must
intertwine under the fixed map. For a tagged implementation, future kernels
must respect that comparison on the full cone: equal decoded conditional
states must yield the same decoded branch law for every allowed continuation.
A transition table may use the public history as a classical control, but it
cannot expose a new residual distinction absent from the reference or silently
replace the dirty physical neighborhood by a ready socket. Equality of
terminal read probabilities is insufficient.

## Fresh-reset positive control and unchanged-sample failures

The exact positive control first reads the selected whole terminal port from
the post-Y ensemble, records it, and then independently samples the calibrated
ensemble mu_A,r for the selected axis A and outcome r. It retains h and encodes
the fresh sample by E_g with g equal to the original x*y. After an X or R
reset the fresh sample's Y coordinate may differ from the original public y;
g remains unchanged. The displayed E_g definition is therefore an explicit
general code extension for this law-level positive control. It is not the
output of the standalone direct-Y source at a later tick. This is a fully specified
abstract transition kernel, not an available native reset. It preserves the
whole earlier record jointly with the fresh output. Its fixed decoder gives
precisely the rank-one projective measurement/reset branch on the post-Y state.
All original G=0 branches remain terminal failures with no offered carrier.
No extra success-conditioned run is silently discarded.

In contrast, exposing a terminal port and offering the unchanged sampled
carrier gives the following determinants after conditioning. Determinants
refer to the normalized 2-by-2 operator produced by the fixed Bloch decoder.

| Read | Condition | Decoder determinant |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Y | repeated Y outcome | 0 |
| X | either X outcome | -1/4 |
| Z | either Z outcome | -1/4 |
| R | R=y, probability 4/5 | -1/64 |
| R | R differs from y, probability 1/5 | -1/4 |

For R=y, the conditional X mean is (-1)^y/4 while Y remains (-1)^y; for
R different from y, X=-(-1)^y and Y=(-1)^y. In both cases the Bloch norm
exceeds one. In the X and Z cases a second orthogonal component is fixed
while the original unit Y component persists. These calculations show that
metadata redundancy and a fixed tagged decoder do not by themselves make
unchanged samples reusable quantum outputs. A new physical reset/back-action,
a different justified comparison, or a stricter continuation contract is
still needed. The source certifies terminal reads only; no existing source
claim is contradicted by this proposed-extension failure.

## Validation and relevance

`PLAN.md` fixes the decision rule before computation. `checks.py` uses rational
arithmetic and explicit exceptions. It checks 256 seed assignments over all
four (x,y) histories, the full tagged success law and terminal failure mass,
eight positive preparation generators, and 28 full-record fresh-reset
branches. It enumerates all corresponding unchanged-carrier posteriors.

Controls detect the 5:2:3 calibration mutant by full-law and terminal-R TV
1/10 even though its terminal-X marginal is correct, preserve the strict raw
history TV=1 obstruction, and detect exposure of the tag when prior x is
omitted. Reusing the prior X-reset u makes X_new=x and fails the fresh post-Y
law. Normal and optimized runs reproduce `result.json` exactly. No floating
arithmetic or new Lean theorem is used.

This is an ELABORATING result with a concrete new constraint: the terminal-R
branches also fail the unchanged-carrier positive-cone requirement, including
the nonzero-probability branch with determinant -1/64. The globally fixed
history decoder itself is an application of the existing composition theorem,
not a new theorem of native realization. The actionable distinction is between
an informationally redundant tag, a physically compatible boundary, and the
post-read reset that restores a valid state; the last two still need native
construction.
