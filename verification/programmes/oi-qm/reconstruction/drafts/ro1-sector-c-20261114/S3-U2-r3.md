## 4. The quantum and classical reference programs

### 4.1 Proposed lemma L-1: Bell violation with initial setting factors requires setting–setting selection

**Status.** L-1 is a proposed lemma: a new hand derivation, [A-inf], author-side, and not verified.
- PLAN:871 stops a U2 row when "a verdict would need a new derivation". So L-1 is **routed** as a
  proposed lemma to #914 O-INS ("Composites and Bell structure") and to #1060 (R9).
- Every verdict of this note that rests on L-1 is conditional on its independent review.
- It is stated here because U1's AW-3 point (U1:738–739, :896) is an AC-1 question that U2's report
  must address. Its content matches Main:404–412, where the framework takes ontic parameter
  dependence as its Bell option. That match is context, not a reason to treat L-1 as established.

**Hypotheses, all explicit** (R3):
- (h1) the dynamics is deterministic;
- (h2) the settings `x, y` are binary factors of the initial configuration, unchanged up to the use,
  or copied there injectively;
- (h3) every other factor is collected as `λ`, and the law of `λ` is independent of `(x, y)` (S5,
  posit (xiv), Main:724);
- (h4) each wing's outcome is the content of a binary output factor, `A` or `B`, readable at `t_c`.

**Statement.** Under (h1)–(h4), suppose neither outcome is operationally I-sel with:
- the remote setting as selector;
- the local setting as carrier;
- `λ` fixed;
- `Y` the output factor (U1:423–427).

Then `|CHSH| ≤ 2`.

**Proof.** For each positive-mass `λ`, the partition of `y`'s two values induced by `y ↦ b(x, y, λ)` is
either trivial or discrete, and it is the same for `x = 0` and `x = 1`.
- For a binary output this gives `b(1, ·, λ) = b(0, ·, λ) ⊕ c(λ)`. So `b = b(0, y, λ) ⊕ x·c(λ)`.
- Likewise, `a = a(x, 0, λ) ⊕ y·k(λ)`.
- Then `a⊕b = α(x, λ) ⊕ β(y, λ)`, with `α = a(x, 0, λ) ⊕ x·c(λ)` and `β = b(0, y, λ) ⊕ y·k(λ)`.
- CHSH reads only `a⊕b`. So it equals the CHSH value of the local response functions `α, β` under a
  `λ`-law independent of the settings.
- That value is at most 2, by the pointwise bound over the sixteen deterministic response tables
  (Main:389 [M]). ∎

**Consequence, conditional on L-1's review.** Under (h1)–(h4), every program with `|CHSH| > 2`
contains setting–setting I-sel. That includes any realization of QR-1's `2√2` that meets the
hypotheses. Setting–setting I-sel is the class `T` belongs to (U1:726–729).

**Cases L-1 does not cover.**
- *Settings written from a seed.* If a setting is written at a later tick from a seed factor `u`, the
  I-sel test's selector and carrier are the seed factors (U1:414–419). Two steps of the proof then
  fail:
  - (a) if the seed is not binary, the "trivial or discrete" step fails;
  - (b) the seeds belong to `λ`, so `λ` is not independent of `(x, y)`, and (h3) fails as stated.

  A seed-level argument would need an S5-type named condition on the seeds. It is not given here.
- *Without S5.* The measurement-dependent route allows CHSH up to 4 (Main:416–423 [M]).
- *Flagged realizations.* Here an outcome is a non-injective function of a larger readable region,
  such as a thresholded real field on R-lin. The selection can then occur at record level while every
  configuration-level partition stays fixed. U1 records that case as I-off with the flag
  (U1:441–445).
- *Extension operations.* L-1 does not reach operations typed into `φ` or `Γ`. Those typed into `Obs`
  are at stop S7(a).

**Standing.** L-1 is the standard fact that remote-setting dependence which only relabels outcomes can
be absorbed into local responses, restated in U1's operational classes. If its review accepts it, it
decides U1's open AW-3 point for (h1)–(h4). It decides none of the cases above.
