## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Closure of `Γ_exe` relative to a surcharge

[P] Under U1, `Γ_exe(M)` is the set of programs of `M` whose outcome is *available* (U1:193–195), on
one `M` and one AD-1 instance (G6). K-acc with `Γ := Γ_exe(M)` (SPEC:242) then makes "`Γ` is closed
under the contract of §5" (SPEC:293–310) a property to be checked, not an axiom.

[P] **Composition surcharge.** For a closure rule S-A*k* and component programs `g₁, …, g_n` in
`Γ_exe(M)`, the surcharge `Δ_k(g₁, …, g_n)` is the set of inputs the composite needs that no
component needs: interface fit (REPORT:29–31), timing, retention to later use ticks and to `t_c`,
renewal of consumed resources, setting writes between stages, and channels (C-COMP, U1:650–674).

[P] **Relative closure.** `Γ_exe(M)` is *closed under S-A*k* relative to `Δ_k`*: a composite of
available programs is available exactly when every input of `Δ_k` is available. This holds by U1's
definitions [A-inf]: C-COMP requires the composite to be one program meeting C-PREP to C-REN, so its
outcome is the weakest of its components' outcomes and of `Δ_k`'s inputs (U1:676–677). The
substantive closure statement is therefore "`Δ_k` is available whenever the components are", and
PLAN's "This holds only where each composite is itself executable" (PLAN:396–398) is that statement.
A closure claim is reported as the list of `Δ_k`'s inputs with their outcomes (§3).

### 2.2 Reuse and the source update

[P] **Reuse (S-A4).** A program uses a region `X` at use boundaries `u₁ < u₂` when the law it
requires of `X` at `u₂` is the law `φ` delivers from the first use, with no renewal of `X` in
between. A reuse continuation that `φ` executes within the budget is in `Γ_exe` whenever its inputs
meet U1, and it is not removed by declaration (K-1; U1:663–664).

[P] **Source of a use.** For a use with settings `(x, y)` and outputs `(a, b)`, the *source* `λ` is
the collection of every factor the use reads other than the setting factors, the output targets and
the policy seeds (the factor sets of AD-1(a)).

[P] **Unchanged-source reuse.** The second use reads the same source `λ` that the first use read,
and applies the same response functions. Each output is written into a fresh blank (fresh-output
form) or by XOR into the register of the first use (same-register form; SEL-EXCL-1 ll. 169–179).
SEL-EXCL-1's `T` fixes `r` (l. 36), so its reuse is unchanged-source reuse. So is TSI's (TSI:202:
"There is no reset of `R, C` …").

[P] **Source update.** A use *updates its source* when the content the next use reads differs, as a
function of the first use's settings or outputs, from the content the first use read. A source update
is a `φ`-effected change of hidden content. By C-RET's parked-record clause (U1:570–574) and
C2-structural (Main:74), its persistence to the next use is a hidden-sector retention.

### 2.3 Renewal: two kinds, and freshness relative to `Γ_exe`

[P] U1's two resource kinds are kept (U1:603–612): **R-blank**, known or ready cells; **R-fresh**,
cells meeting REPORT's condition at the use boundary, "`P(U | H=h, X_relevant=x)=ν` on every
reachable positive-probability pair", with `X_relevant` including "all non-seed variables used by the
stage" (REPORT:59–63) [M-r].

[P] **Supplied, `μ`-booked and native renewal.** A bank outside `μ` is apparatus-assumed. A bank in
`μ` is `μ`-assumed, so programs using it are *available relative to `μ`* (U1:193–195). *Native
renewal* is renewal at U1's *derived* level: delivered by `φ` from the derived `μ` with no `Res` entry
beyond what `μ` and `φ` give (U1:196–197).

[P] **Freshness relative to `Γ_exe`.** REPORT's condition is stated for one stage of one program, and
it holds for a seed bank "only when earlier dynamics and access have not used it or conditioned on
it" (REPORT:65–67). Under access closure (#1169 c.6876591512, answer 4; K-1), every executable
composite whose earlier steps read or condition on a cell `U_j` before its scheduled use is a member
of `Γ_exe`. A conclusion drawn for the whole model from freshness, such as complete local-history
no-signalling of a fresh-cell implementation, therefore needs one of:
- `U_j` meets REPORT's condition at use `j` in **every** program of `Γ_exe(M)` that contains use `j`; or
- the complete local histories of the programs where it does not are shown no-signalling by a
  separate argument.

Freshness is then a property of the pair (bank, `Γ_exe(M)`), not of the bank alone. SEL-EXCL-1's
fresh-cell lemma holds within its declared grammar, in which "no admitted operation reads or couples
to them earlier" (l. 101); whether `Γ_exe` of a given tuple contains such an operation is the K-1
question U2 routed as a construction (U2:487–488).

[P] **Unread is not unreadable.** A region serves as a freshness reserve, or as the sink for a record
displaced by a reset (Lemma R, PREREG:177–182), only if no program of `Γ_exe` reads it in the
relevant window. That is a property of `φ` and AD-1, not of a program's declaration. #913's gate
"Observer-access closure" states the same rule: "do not infer privacy from an unread field".

### 2.4 Held channels (S-A6)

[P] A **held channel** from observer `V_A` to `V_B` is a region `Ch`, disjoint from both, such that,
on the program's fixed schedule (AD-1(e)), `φ` carries a record held in `V_A` at tick `t` through `Ch`
to a record held in `V_B` at tick `t′ > t`. The delivery is injective on the declared alphabet, with
no private decoder (R3), and the delivered record is retained to `t_c` (C-RET). Alphabet, budget and
schedule are counted (U1:666–667; SPEC:306–308).

[P] **Operational counting of side channels.** SPEC says "Arrival times, flags and side channels count
toward the budget" (SPEC:307–308). Two readings exist. *Ontic:* every `φ`-path from `V_A`'s content to
`V_B`'s records counts. *Operational:* a side channel is any dependence of the receiver's complete
local history, in the program with the counted message removed, on the sender's inputs or policy.
U3 uses the operational reading [P]. Ground: SEL-DISC-1 states that IC₂ "is an additional restriction
on the resulting operational theory", while "Its microscopic `xy` coupling is already global"
(l. 97); and complete local histories are the domain of comparison (l. 38). Under the ontic reading,
the PR gate's own coupling would exhaust any budget, and the test would compare nothing.
Consequences [A-inf on OFF]:
- single-use `T` has no operational side channel (SEL-EXCL-1 §2);
- unchanged-source reuse of `T` is one (SEL-EXCL-1 §7: `b₂ = x₂`, or `b₁ ⊕ b₂′ = x₂`);
- local quantum instruments without messages have none (SEL-DISC-1 l. 149 [OFF]).

The same reading applies to PR programs and to the quantum references (twin programs, U1 §5.1(ii)).

### 2.5 Granularity

[P] One tick is one application of the model's `φ` (U1:159). Following AW-U2-1 (U2:400–408):
- **GR-1.** A schedule verdict is a verdict on the `φ` it is stated for. In a designed tuple the
  designer fixes how much one tick does; on a native rule the rule fixes it. No transfer either way
  (K-5).
- **GR-2.** Absorbing a multi-stage circuit into one `φ` changes three things together:
  - the budget, through `ord(φ)` (AD-1(f));
  - what reuse means: the next tick re-applies the absorbed circuit to every absorbed cell;
  - the channel structure: no record is held between stages (U2 §2.6).

  Consequence [A-inf on OFF]: in M-PR(m), reading (g), `T` fixes every `r_i` (SEL-EXCL-1 l. 36). If
  `ord(φ_m) ≥ 3`, so that the budget can reach 2, the second tick is an unchanged-source
  continuation of all `m` cells inside the tuple, and K-1 keeps it in `Γ_exe`. So a granularity that
  makes the fresh-cell schedule native also makes RR-1's question native at the next tick, unless
  the budget excludes it. `ord(φ_m)` depends on the gate order of the circuit, which SEL-EXCL-1 does
  not fix (l. 87, l. 99). Deciding it is a construction (§10.6).

### 2.6 Comparison-tick holding

[P] As in U1 (U1:161–167, :668–672): every record that enters a complete local history used in a
no-signalling comparison, or a joint law, is held at the program's comparison tick `t_c`. A record
first held earlier enters only if retained to `t_c` (C-RET) or carried there through a held channel
(§2.4). A comparison of records each held only at its own tick is the joint law of R-seq, a
representation (K-4).

**RQ-SEQ (S-A1, sequence).**

**RQ-ADP (S-A2, adaptivity), two parts, as in U1 (U1:658–661) and AW-7 (U1:899).**
- Adaptive setting values: a record-conditioned value write with a blank target, classified by U1's
  partition test (U1:351–389). Not excluded on R-lin for affine writes (D5 [M-r]).
- Adaptive operations: record-selected operational I-sel; not available on R-lin (D5 [M-r]). So
  `Γ_exe` of an R-lin model is not closed under S-A2's adaptive operations, at D5's scope.
- In both, the record is held at the tick of use.

**RQ-PAR (S-A3, parallel composition).**
- `Δ₃`: disjoint regions of AD-1(a) over the one `μ` (K-src). No surcharge beyond disjointness is
  required by U1.
- What `Δ₃` does not supply: `Θ` (b) maps the common source to "one initial state, independent of the
  settings" (SPEC:373–374). That independence is S5 (ledger Q8), exposed, not assumed.

**RQ-REU (S-A4, reuse).**
- `Δ₄`: a `φ`-effected change of the setting between uses (C-SET). Written from a private seed as
  `z ↦ z ⊕ U`, it is a single-factor value write, I-off (U2:418–420). Also: a budget of at least 2
  below `ord(φ)` (AD-1(f)); for the fresh-output form, a second blank and the first record retained
  to `t_c`; for the same-register form, neither (U2:423–435).
- First missing step: the `φ`-effected setting change (U2:766).
- Under L-2 (§5), whenever `Δ₄` is available for a Bell-violating unchanged source, complete
  local-history no-signalling under reuse fails. That turns RQ-REU into RQ-UPD.

**RQ-UPD (reuse with complete local-history no-signalling; conditional).** This is a requirement on
any model in which reuse of a Bell-violating source is in `Γ_exe` and its complete local histories
are no-signalling. It has two parts.
- *RQ-UPD-1, conditional on L-2:* the use updates its source (§2.2). Unchanged-source reuse is
  excluded by L-2's conclusion for PR-type and quantum-calibrated realizations alike (§5).
- *RQ-UPD-2, for realizations of the quantum two-use law* [A-inf from TSI (9), [NP]]. For local
  projective Lüders instruments on `|Φ⁺⟩`, the second outcome equals the first when the setting
  repeats and is fair and independent of it when the setting changes, the two axes being orthogonal
  (TSI:228–239). In a deterministic realization this needs:
  - (i) the first outcome written into what the next use reads (repeatability; a source update that
    is a record);
  - (ii) at a changed setting, an output that depends on content the first use did not read, since
    it must be independent of the held `a₁`: a fresh resource, booked in `μ` or renewed.

  Fresh replacement of the whole source fails (i) (TSI:277–282). Unchanged-source reuse fails
  no-signalling (TSI (10); L-2). So recording into the source and renewal are both required by the
  quantum reference's reuse law.
- Whether a PR-type use can meet RQ-UPD-1 and RQ-UPD-2's analogue with its single-use table kept is
  not decided. Deciding it is a construction (§10.6).

**RQ-REN-B (S-A5, blanks).**
- Readiness is relocated, never created, by a bijection (Lemma R, invariance; PREREG:177–187 [NP]).
- Under μ-G no region is ready at any tick (D2, OA1:145–155 [M-r]). Native blanks come only by a
  held herald, at exactly the D3 cost `q^{−M}` for `M` `q`-ary cells (OA1:156–160 [M-r]; OA1:328), with
  the herald retained to `t_c` (C-RET). In M-PR5/μ-G that retention fails by `T`'s algebra (U2 §2.1).
- With a `μ`-booked supply (MP-0a, OA1:359–361 [M-r]), blanks are available relative to `μ`, and the
  total is the booked readiness.
- A reset moves the record's distinguishing content into a sink region (PREREG:181–182). The sink
  must be unreadable relative to `Γ_exe` (§2.3), and its capacity is booked.

**RQ-REN-F (S-A5, fresh cells).**
- REPORT's condition at every use boundary, with S5 named where settings enter `X_relevant`
  (U1:614–616), and freshness relative to `Γ_exe` (§2.3).
- Under μ-G [A-inf on M-r]: hidden content is uniform and independent of every function of the
  present visible configuration at every tick (D2). So freshness relative to *present-held* records
  is native under μ-G. Freshness relative to non-held relevant variables is not decided.
- Under μ-G, at D4's scope (a visible configuration that determines its predecessor), "complete
  in-`V` records and hidden influence on `V` exclude each other" (OA1:162–175 [M-r]). A program whose
  complete record specification needs such full in-`V` histories cannot then use a native fresh
  hidden cell to influence its records. That bears equally on PR programs and on the quantum
  references' programs. Partial records are D8, unreviewed (`oa-1-20261112/STATUS.md`:65).
- Per-use addressing: under one fixed `φ`, use `i` must act on cell `i`. That needs transport (cell
  `i` reaches the use site at tick `i`) or a clock- or record-selected operation (operational I-sel,
  CP-7's class). The second is not available on R-lin (D5 [M-r]). No held clock exists on K1
  (MR★:638) [NP]. Neither realization is on record, and exhibiting one is a construction (U2 §2.4).
- Total: a closed finite world bounds output entropy, `H(A^m) ≤ H(W₀) ≤ log₂|S|` (SEL-DISC-1 l. 79
  [OFF]), for PR, classical and quantum-realizing records alike (l. 53).

## 4. H-RR in its three cases

### 4.1 RR-1: same-seed reuse with retained records

**Definition** [P]. The program uses one source twice (§2.2) and makes a `φ`-effected setting change
between the uses (RQ-REU). It holds each wing's complete local history at `t_c` (RQ-TC).

**Requirements.** `Δ₄` of RQ-REU. For the same-register form: the blank `B₀`, the setting change, and
a budget of at least 2. For the fresh-output form: a second blank and `b₁` retained to `t_c`.

**What the record and L-2 show.**
- PR implementation [OFF]: same-seed reuse signals (SEL-EXCL-1 §7; U2 AW-U2-3). In M-PR5 the program
  is not available, because `T²=id` gives a budget of 1 (U2 F-3).
- Quantum-calibrated candidate [NP]: TSI's candidate matches all four first-read `2√2` correlations
  (TSI (1), (2)) and signals on unchanged-source reuse (TSI (10)–(11)).
- L-2 (§5; proposed) gives the general reason. Under unchanged-source reuse, complete local-history
  no-signalling forces Bell-local responses. So every deterministic realization with `|CHSH| > 2`
  that reuses an unchanged source signals, conditional on L-2's hypotheses. That includes
  realizations whose single-use law is the `2√2` table.

**Bearing on H-RR.**
- RR-1's exposure is not specific to PR. Conditional on L-2, it applies to every Bell-violating
  unchanged-source realization. Its content is the requirement RQ-UPD-1 that a use update its source.
- The quantum reference itself escapes through its state update: "Requiring an unchanged hidden seed
  after quantum measurement is **not** a quantum-reference assumption" (SEL-DISC-1 l. 50 [OFF];
  TSI:323–325 [NP]).
- So RR-1 supplies no discrimination between PR and quantum statistics by itself. It neither supports
  H-RR nor refutes it, since H-RR concerns recording, retention and renewal jointly. It does refute,
  conditionally on L-2, a narrower reading: that retained records with reuse alone discriminate. PLAN
  Point 3 already sets that reading aside (PLAN:561).
- *Status: undetermined.* Narrowed by L-2, conditionally: RR-1's bearing reduces to RQ-UPD.

## 5. Proposed lemma L-2: unchanged-source reuse and Bell locality

**Status.** A proposed lemma: a new hand derivation, [A-inf], author-side, not verified. It is routed
for review (§10.5). No verdict of this note rests on it unconditionally. Its two instances on record
are SEL-EXCL-1 §7 [OFF] and TSI §4 [NP]. It is probably an elementary known fact; the literature was
not searched.

**Hypotheses.**
- (k1) *Deterministic responses.* At each use the outputs are `a(x, y, λ)` and `b(x, y, λ)`, with `λ`
  the source of §2.2.
- (k2) *Unchanged source.* The first use leaves `λ` unchanged, and the second use applies the same
  response functions. Outputs go into fresh blanks, or by XOR into the first use's registers.
- (k3) *Two fixed policies per wing are executable* within `Γ_exe`: for each setting value `s`, the
  two-use policies `(s, s)` and `(s, s′)` with `s′ ≠ s`. The change is `φ`-effected (RQ-REU).
- (k4) *S5 at policy level.* The law of `λ` does not depend on the pair of policies.
- (k5) *Complete local histories held.* In the fresh-output form, both outputs of each wing are held
  at `t_c` (RQ-TC). In the same-register form, the register is held at `t_c`.

**Statement.** Suppose that, for each fixed own policy, each wing's complete two-use local history
has a law independent of the remote wing's policy. Then `a(x, 0, λ) = a(x, 1, λ)` and
`b(0, y, λ) = b(1, y, λ)` for almost every `λ`, for all `x`, `y`. For binary outcomes, the single-use
CHSH value is then at most 2.

**Proof.**
- Fix Alice's policy `(x, x)`.
- Under Bob's policy `(0, 0)`:
  - fresh-output form: Alice's history is `(a(x,0,λ), a(x,0,λ))`, whose two entries agree with
    probability 1;
  - same-register form: her register is `a(x,0,λ) ⊕ a(x,0,λ) = 0` with probability 1.
- Under Bob's policy `(0, 1)`, her history is `(a(x,0,λ), a(x,1,λ))`, or the register
  `a(x,0,λ) ⊕ a(x,1,λ)`.
- No-signalling makes the two laws equal. So `a(x,0,λ) = a(x,1,λ)` almost surely, for each `x`.
- Exchanging the wings gives `b(0,y,λ) = b(1,y,λ)` almost surely.
- So `a = α(x, λ)` and `b = β(y, λ)` almost surely. With (k4), the single-use table is a mixture of
  local deterministic responses under a setting-independent weight, and `|CHSH| ≤ 2` by the pointwise
  bound over the sixteen deterministic response tables (Main:389 [M]). ∎

**Checks against the instances** [A-inf on OFF and NP].
- `T`: `a = r` does not depend on `y`, so Alice sees no signal. `b = r ⊕ xy` depends on `x`. Under
  Bob's `(1, 1)` and Alice's `(0, 0)` against `(0, 1)`, Bob's fresh-output pair is `(r, r)` against
  `(r, r ⊕ 1)`. This matches SEL-EXCL-1 l. 179 up to the choice of `y₁`.
- TSI: `a = R` and `b = R ⊕ xy ⊕ C` with `(R, C)` unchanged (TSI (3), (8)). Bob's pair satisfies
  `b₁ ⊕ b₂ = z` (TSI (10)), as L-2 requires.

**What L-2 does not use, and does not cover.**
- It does not use U1's operational classes. It is stated on record functions, so flagged
  realizations (U1:441–445), which escape L-1, are covered whenever (k1)–(k5) hold.
- It needs no retention in the same-register form, and no adaptivity.
- It does not cover:
  - a use that updates its source, (k2) failing. The quantum reference is of this kind (TSI (9)).
  - fresh noise cells consumed per use. That is renewal, so (k2) fails.
  - models in which (k3) is not executable, such as M-PR5, where the budget is 1.
  - realizations without (k4): the measurement-dependent route (Main:416–423).
  - extension operations typed into `Obs`: stop S7(a) (U1 §6.1).

**Relation to L-1.** L-1 (U2:492–550; proposed): under (h1)–(h4), a Bell violation requires
setting–setting I-sel at a single use. L-2: under (k1)–(k5), a Bell violation, an unchanged source
and executable reuse together imply complete local-history signalling. They are independent. L-2
needs no binary setting factors of the initial configuration. It needs S5 at policy level, (k4),
where L-1 needs it for the settings, (h3).

## 6. A capacity bound on closure at the comparison tick

[A-inf] **Statement.**
- AD-1(a) declares "A finite family of disjoint proper subsystems `V_k ⊊ S`, each with finitely many
  distinguishable states" (U1:227).
- On one model with one AD-1 instance (G6), the records held at any one tick therefore take at most
  `N_held = ∏_k |C_{V_k}|` values.
- A program whose complete record must distinguish more than `N_held` values cannot hold it at `t_c`.
  Its joint law would be R-seq's, a representation (K-4).
- Its outcome is *not available*, at the scope of that model. The disproved input is a held region of
  sufficient capacity at `t_c`. The ground is the declared observer family, whose finiteness is the
  Definition's premise (Main:42).

**Consequences.**
- `Γ_exe(M)` contains complete-record programs only up to a bounded depth and alphabet. So it is not
  closed under unbounded iteration of S-A1, S-A4 or S-A5, for PR programs and quantum references
  alike. Carrying records through held channels (S-A6) moves them within the same family and does not
  raise the bound.
- For `m` uses with binary settings and outcomes, each wing's complete record needs at least `2^{2m}`
  values held at `t_c`. Heralds, ticks and flags add to this.
- SPEC's coverage target is one model `M*` covering every finite quantum experiment "in the agreed
  scope", which "includes arbitrarily large finite carriers" (SPEC:397–400). Under AD-1(a) read as a
  fixed finite family per model, complete records of unbounded size cannot be held at one tick of
  one model. That coverage then fails on every model, by capacity and target-neutrally. It is not a
  restriction chosen against PR.
- Performed experiments are finitely many, with finite records. The agreement half is not affected by
  the bound for a model whose family is large enough. Whether a given model's family is large enough
  is not checked.

**Two readings, not chosen here.** AD-1 is frozen (U1:222).
- (a) The family is finite and fixed per model. The bound holds.
- (b) The family a *program* uses is finite, while the model may carry unboundedly many observers.
  K-obs quantifies over each observer (SPEC:240), and K-dyn allows an infinite `S` (SPEC:239). The
  bound then lifts. Reading (b) would need a new U1 revision with a stated rule for which observers
  a program uses.

This is owner question 1 (§10.7).

**Pressure test.** The bound uses only G4's joint-hold clause, AD-1(a) and the completeness of
records (G5, K-rec). Relaxing joint holding to per-tick holding would admit R-seq, which U1 excludes
(U1:183, review B2). The bound does not depend on C-RET against R2 (§7).
