## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Closure of `Γ_exe` relative to a surcharge

[P] Under U1, `Γ_exe(M)` is the set of programs of `M` whose outcome is *available* (U1:193–195), on
one `M` and one AD-1 instance (G6). K-acc with `Γ := Γ_exe(M)` (SPEC:242) then makes "`Γ` is closed
under the contract of §5" (SPEC:293–310) a property to be checked, not an axiom.

[P] **Composition surcharge.** For a closure rule S-A*k* and component programs `g₁, …, g_n` in
`Γ_exe(M)`, the surcharge `Δ_k(g₁, …, g_n)` is the set of inputs of the composite whose verdict is
not inherited from a component. It has two parts:
- inputs no component needs: interface fit (REPORT:29–31), timing, retention to later use ticks and
  to the composite's `t_c`, renewal of consumed resources, setting writes between stages, and
  channels (C-COMP, U1:650–674);
- component inputs re-certified in the composite context, where a component's verdict depends on
  what precedes it: for example the freshness of a stage-2 cell relative to stage 1 (§2.3), or a
  blank that stage 1 may have consumed.

[P] **Relative closure.** `Γ_exe(M)` is *closed under S-A*k* relative to `Δ_k`*: a composite of
available programs is available exactly when every input of `Δ_k` is available. Both directions hold
by U1's definitions [A-inf]. C-COMP requires the composite to be one program meeting C-PREP to C-REN,
with its outcome the weakest of its inputs' outcomes (U1:650–651, :676–677). Every input of the
composite is either inherited, with its component's verdict, or in `Δ_k`. So when the components
are available, the composite is available if and only if every input of `Δ_k` is. The substantive
closure statement is therefore "`Δ_k` is available whenever the components are", and PLAN's "This
holds only where each composite is itself executable" (PLAN:396–398) is that statement. A closure
claim is reported as the list of `Δ_k`'s inputs with their outcomes (§3).

### 2.2 Reuse and the source update

[P] **Reuse (S-A4).** A program uses a region `X` at use boundaries `u₁ < u₂` when the law it
requires of `X` at `u₂` is the law `φ` delivers from the first use, with no renewal of `X` in
between. A reuse continuation that `φ` executes within the budget is in `Γ_exe` whenever its inputs
meet U1, and it is not removed by declaration (K-1; U1:663–664).

[P] **Source of a use.** For a use with settings `(x, y)` and outputs `(a, b)`, the *source* `λ` is
the collection of every factor the use reads other than the setting factors, the output targets and
the policy seeds (the factor sets of AD-1(a)). For a deterministic use, write the first use's outputs
as `a₁ = A₁(x₁, y₁, λ)` and `b₁ = B₁(x₁, y₁, λ)`.

[P] **Unchanged-source reuse.** The second use reads the same source `λ` that the first use read,
and applies the same response functions. Each output is written into a fresh blank (fresh-output
form) or by XOR into the register of the first use (same-register form; SEL-EXCL-1 ll. 169–179).
SEL-EXCL-1's `T` fixes `r` (l. 36), so its reuse is unchanged-source reuse. So is TSI's (TSI:202:
"There is no reset of `R, C` …").

[P] **Second-use content.** Write the second use's outputs as `a₂ = A₂(x₂, y₂, κ)` and
`b₂ = B₂(x₂, y₂, κ)`, where `κ` is the content the second use reads other than its setting factors,
and `A₂, B₂` are the response functions it applies. A tick-indexed response is the case where
`A₂ ≠ A₁` or `B₂ ≠ B₁`. Three cases are exhaustive:
- **(U) unchanged source:** `κ = λ` and `(A₂, B₂) = (A₁, B₁)`, as defined above.
- **(S) source update:** `κ` depends, at positive mass, on the first use's settings or outputs.
  A use *updates its source* in this case. A source update is a `φ`-effected change of the content
  read. Where that content is hidden, its persistence to the next use is a hidden-sector retention
  of the form C2-structural states (Main:74).
- **(N) setting-independent renewal:** the content read or the response applied differs from the
  first use's, but `κ` does not depend on the first use's settings or outputs. Examples: the second
  use reads a different preloaded cell (RR-2's fresh cells, §4.2); `λ` is changed by a
  setting-independent bijection; fresh noise is consumed; the response is tick-indexed.

Formally, `κ = G(λ, ν, x₁, y₁, a₁, b₁)` with `ν` any further content whose joint law with `λ` does not
depend on the policies. (U) and (N) are the cases where `G` does not depend on its last four
arguments almost surely, (U) being `κ = λ` with unchanged responses; (S) is every other case. The fresh-cell lemma (SEL-EXCL-1 §3; l. 181) shows that (N) can give
complete local-history no-signalling for PR.

### 2.3 Renewal: two kinds, and freshness relative to `Γ_exe`

[P] U1's two resource kinds are kept (U1:603–612): **R-blank**, known or ready cells; **R-fresh**,
cells meeting REPORT's condition at the use boundary, "`P(U | H=h, X_relevant=x)=ν` on every
reachable positive-probability pair", with `X_relevant` including "all non-seed variables used by the
stage" (REPORT:59–63) [M-r].

[P] **Supplied, `μ`-booked and native renewal.** A bank outside `μ` is apparatus-assumed. A bank in
`μ` is `μ`-assumed, so programs using it are *available relative to `μ`* (U1:193–195). *Native
renewal* is renewal at U1's *derived* level: delivered by `φ` from the derived `μ` with no `Res` entry
beyond what `μ` and `φ` give (U1:196–197).

[P] **Freshness relative to `Γ_exe`.** REPORT's condition is stated for one stage of one program, and
it holds for a seed bank "only when earlier dynamics and access have not used it or conditioned on
it" (REPORT:65–67). Under access closure (#1169 c.6876591512, answer 4; K-1), every executable
composite whose earlier steps read or condition on a cell `U_j` before its scheduled use is a member
of `Γ_exe`. A conclusion drawn for the whole model from freshness, such as complete local-history
no-signalling of a fresh-cell implementation, therefore needs one of:
- `U_j` meets REPORT's condition at use `j` in **every** program of `Γ_exe(M)` that contains use `j`; or
- the complete local histories of the programs where it does not are shown no-signalling by a
  separate argument.

Freshness is then a property of the pair (bank, `Γ_exe(M)`), not of the bank alone. SEL-EXCL-1's
fresh-cell lemma holds within its declared grammar, in which "no admitted operation reads or couples
to them earlier" (l. 101); whether `Γ_exe` of a given tuple contains such an operation is the K-1
question U2 routed as a construction (U2:487–488).

[P] **Unread is not unreadable.** A region serves as a freshness reserve, or as the sink for a record
displaced by a reset (Lemma R, PREREG:177–182), only if no program of `Γ_exe` reads it in the
relevant window. That is a property of `φ` and AD-1, not of a program's declaration. #913's gate
"Observer-access closure" states the same rule: "do not infer privacy from an unread field".

### 2.4 Held channels (S-A6)

[P] A **held channel** from observer `V_A` to `V_B` is a region `Ch`, disjoint from both, such that,
on the program's fixed schedule (AD-1(e)), `φ` carries a record held in `V_A` at tick `t` through `Ch`
to a record held in `V_B` at tick `t′ > t`. The delivery is injective on the declared alphabet, with
no private decoder (R3), and the delivered record is retained to `t_c` (C-RET). Alphabet, budget and
schedule are counted (U1:666–667; SPEC:306–308).

[P] **Operational counting of side channels.** SPEC says "Arrival times, flags and side channels count
toward the budget" (SPEC:307–308). Two readings exist. *Ontic:* every `φ`-path from `V_A`'s content to
`V_B`'s records counts. *Operational:* a side channel is any dependence of the receiver's complete
local history, in the program with the counted message removed, on the sender's inputs or policy.
U3 uses the operational reading [P]. Ground: SEL-DISC-1 states that IC₂ "is an additional restriction
on the resulting operational theory", while "Its microscopic `xy` coupling is already global"
(l. 97); and complete local histories are the domain of comparison (l. 38). Under the ontic reading,
the PR gate's own coupling would exhaust any budget, and the test would compare nothing. The ontic
reading would do the same for every deterministic realization of QR-1 with S5 at policy level: by
the elementary step of §4.2, such a realization has a positive-mass set on which one wing's
response depends on the remote setting, which is a `φ`-path from the sender's setting to the
receiver's record [A-inf]. So the choice between the readings is not made against PR.
Consequences [A-inf on OFF]:
- single-use `T` has no operational side channel (SEL-EXCL-1 §2);
- unchanged-source reuse of `T` is one (SEL-EXCL-1 §7: `b₂ = x₂`, or `b₁ ⊕ b₂′ = x₂`);
- local quantum instruments without messages have none (SEL-DISC-1 l. 149 [OFF]).

The same reading applies to PR programs and to the quantum references (twin programs, U1 §5.1(ii)).

### 2.5 Granularity

[P] One tick is one application of the model's `φ` (U1:159). Following AW-U2-1 (U2:400–408):
- **GR-1.** A schedule verdict is a verdict on the `φ` it is stated for. In a designed tuple the
  designer fixes how much one tick does; on a native rule the rule fixes it. No transfer either way
  (K-5).
- **GR-2.** Absorbing a multi-stage circuit into one `φ` changes three things together:
  - the budget, through `ord(φ)` (AD-1(f));
  - what reuse means: the next tick re-applies the absorbed circuit to every absorbed cell;
  - the channel structure: no record is held between stages (U2 §2.6).

  Consequence [A-inf on OFF]: in M-PR(m), reading (g), `T` fixes every `r_i` (SEL-EXCL-1 l. 36). If
  `ord(φ_m) ≥ 3`, so that the budget can reach 2, the second tick is an unchanged-source
  continuation of all `m` cells inside the tuple, and K-1 keeps it in `Γ_exe`. The re-application
  alone reuses each cell at its first setting, policy `(s, s)`. RR-1 also needs policy `(s, s′)`,
  hence a `φ`-effected setting change between the ticks (RQ-REU), which the absorbed circuit's
  re-application does not supply. So a granularity that makes the fresh-cell schedule native also
  makes RR-1's question native at the next tick where `φ_m`, or a program on the same tuple, also
  effects such a setting change and the budget does not exclude it. `ord(φ_m)` depends on the gate
  order of the circuit, which SEL-EXCL-1 does not fix (l. 87, l. 99). Deciding it is a construction
  (§10.6).

### 2.6 Comparison-tick holding

[P] As in U1 (U1:161–167, :668–672): every record that enters a complete local history used in a
no-signalling comparison, or a joint law, is held at the program's comparison tick `t_c`. A record
first held earlier enters only if retained to `t_c` (C-RET) or carried there through a held channel
(§2.4). A comparison of records each held only at its own tick is the joint law of R-seq, a
representation (K-4).

**RQ-ADP (S-A2, adaptivity), two parts, as in U1 (U1:658–661) and AW-7 (U1:899).**

**RQ-PAR (S-A3, parallel composition).**
- `Δ₃`: disjoint regions of AD-1(a) over the one `μ` (K-src). No surcharge beyond disjointness is
  required by U1.

  settings" (SPEC:373–374). That independence is S5 (ledger Q8), exposed, not assumed.

**RQ-REU (S-A4, reuse).**
- `Δ₄`: a `φ`-effected change of the setting between uses (C-SET). Written from a private seed as
  `z ↦ z ⊕ U`, it is a single-factor value write, I-off (U2:418–420). Also: a budget of at least 2
  below `ord(φ)` (AD-1(f)); for the fresh-output form, a second blank and the first record retained
  to `t_c`; for the same-register form, neither (U2:423–435).
- First missing step: the `φ`-effected setting change (U2:766).
- Conditional on L-2 (§5.1): whenever `Δ₄` is available for a Bell-violating unchanged source,
  complete local-history no-signalling under reuse fails. Reuse with no-signalling then needs (k2) to
  fail, by a source update (S) or a setting-independent renewal (N) (§2.2). That turns RQ-REU into
  RQ-UPD-1.

**RQ-UPD (reuse of a Bell-violating source; two parts with different grounds).**
- *RQ-UPD-1, conditional on L-2 and on complete local-history no-signalling under reuse.* A model in
  which reuse of a Bell-violating source is in `Γ_exe` with no-signalling complete local histories
  makes each such reuse a case (S) or (N) of §2.2, not (U). Unchanged-source reuse is excluded by
  L-2's conclusion for PR-type and quantum-calibrated realizations alike (§5.1). (N) can meet it for
  PR (SEL-EXCL-1 §3, l. 181).
- *RQ-UPD-2, for deterministic realizations of the quantum two-use law; conditional on L-3, not on
  L-2 and not on no-signalling.* For local projective Lüders instruments on `|Φ⁺⟩` with the CHSH
  settings, the second outcome equals the first when the setting repeats, and is fair and independent
  of the first outcomes when the setting changes, the two axes on each wing being Bloch-orthogonal
  (TSI (9), TSI:228–239 [NP]; re-checked by hand as the Lüders update [A-inf]). A program that `Θ`
  (S8) identifies with that two-use experiment needs, with S5 at policy level (k4′ of §5.2):
  - (i) *repeatability:* at `x₂ = x₁` the content Alice's second output reads determines `a₁`
    (likewise `b₁` at `y₂ = y₁`). On each wing whose first-use response depends on the remote
    setting at positive mass, that wing's second-use content must depend on the first use's outputs
    or on the remote first setting: a source update (S). Unchanged-source reuse (U) and
    setting-independent renewal (N) are both excluded for that wing, and so is dependence on the
    wing's own first setting alone (L-3, §5.2). Since the first-use table violates CHSH, at least
    one wing is of this kind (§4.2's elementary step). A wing with a Bell-local response needs no update: with `λ = (λ₀, λ₁)`, two
    independent fair bits, and `a = λ_x`, unchanged reuse meets that wing's part of (9).
  - (ii) *fresh outcome at a changed setting:* at `x₂ ≠ x₁`, `a₂` is fair and independent of
    `(a₁, b₁)`, so each wing's second-use content must carry information that the first use's records
    `(x₁, y₁, a₁, b₁)` do not determine. It may be preloaded in `λ` (booked in `μ`) or renewed.

  Fresh replacement of the whole source by an independent one is case (N); it fails (i) on every
  wing, since `a₂` is then independent of `a₁` (TSI:277–282). So the quantum
  reference's reuse law needs, together, a source update on the remote-dependent wing and content
  that the first use's records do not determine (conditional on L-3).
- Whether a PR-type use can meet RQ-UPD-1 and RQ-UPD-2's analogue with its single-use table kept is
  not decided. Deciding it is a construction (§10.6).

**RQ-REN-B (S-A5, blanks).**

- Under μ-G no region is ready at any tick (D2, OA1:145–155 [M-r]). Native blanks come only by a

- With a `μ`-booked supply (MP-0a, OA1:359–361 [M-r]), blanks are available relative to `μ`, and the
  total is the booked readiness.
- A reset moves the record's distinguishing content into a sink region (PREREG:181–182). The sink
  must be unreadable relative to `Γ_exe` (§2.3), and its capacity is booked.

**RQ-REN-F (S-A5, fresh cells).**

- Under μ-G, at D4's scope (a visible configuration that determines its predecessor), "complete

## 4. H-RR in its three cases

PLAN's H-RR: "Record creation, retention and renewal, taken *together* in one fixed model, might
impose a restriction on executable non-quantum correlations that none of them imposes alone"
(PLAN:348–349). It is assessed here as a hypothesis (#1169 c.6890079967, safeguards). PLAN's
support, refutation and undetermined conditions (PLAN:359–384) are applied unchanged. Under the
amended target, "Excluding PR is no longer a goal" (c.6891338304), so a restriction H-RR describes
would be reported for its bearing on the C/Q relation and on agreement with performed experiments.
It is not an aim.

### 4.1 RR-1: same-seed reuse with retained records

**Definition** [P]. The program uses one source twice (§2.2) and makes a `φ`-effected setting change
between the uses (RQ-REU). It holds each wing's complete local history at `t_c` (RQ-TC).

**Requirements.** `Δ₄` of RQ-REU. For the same-register form: the blank `B₀`, the setting change, and
a budget of at least 2. For the fresh-output form: a second blank and `b₁` retained to `t_c`.

**What the record and L-2 show.**
- PR implementation [OFF]: same-seed reuse signals (SEL-EXCL-1 §7; U2 AW-U2-3). In M-PR5 the program
  is not available, because `T²=id` gives a budget of 1 (U2 F-3).
- Quantum-calibrated candidate [NP]: TSI's candidate matches all four first-read `2√2` correlations
  (TSI (1), (2)) and signals on unchanged-source reuse (TSI (10)–(11)).
- L-2 (§5.1; proposed) gives the general reason. Under unchanged-source reuse, complete
  local-history no-signalling forces Bell-local responses. So every deterministic realization with
  `|CHSH| > 2` that reuses an unchanged source signals, conditional on L-2 and its hypotheses. That
  includes realizations whose single-use law is the `2√2` table.
- L-3 (§5.2; proposed) adds, independently of L-2: such a realization also fails the quantum
  two-use law's repeatability on the remote-dependent wing (conditional on L-3).

**Bearing on H-RR.**
- RR-1's exposure is not specific to PR. Conditional on L-2, it applies to every Bell-violating
  unchanged-source realization. Its content is the requirement RQ-UPD-1: reuse with no-signalling
  needs (k2) to fail, by a source update (S) or a setting-independent renewal (N) (§2.2).
- The quantum reference itself escapes through its state update: "Requiring an unchanged hidden seed
  after quantum measurement is **not** a quantum-reference assumption" (SEL-DISC-1 l. 50 [OFF];
  TSI:323–325 [NP]). By L-3, its escape is of kind (S) on the remote-dependent wing (conditional on
  L-3). PR's escape on record, fresh cells, is of kind (N) (§4.2); it realizes repeated fresh runs,
  for which no two-use target law is set.
- So RR-1 supplies no discrimination between PR and quantum statistics by itself. It neither supports
  H-RR nor refutes it, since H-RR concerns recording, retention and renewal jointly. It does refute,
  conditionally on L-2, a narrower reading: that retained records with reuse alone discriminate. PLAN
  Point 3 already sets that reading aside (PLAN:561).
- *Status: undetermined.* Narrowed by L-2, conditionally: RR-1's bearing reduces to RQ-UPD-1.

### 4.2 RR-2: fresh-cell renewal, supplied

**Definition** [P]. One `μ`-booked bank of `m` fresh cells and their blanks, each cell used once on a
fixed schedule (SEL-EXCL-1 §3; U2 CP-3).

**Requirements.**
- RQ-REN-F, freshness relative to `Γ_exe` included (§2.3);
- RQ-REN-B for `2m` output blanks, copy blanks and setting targets;
- per-use addressing (RQ-REN-F);
- RQ-TC for `2m` held records per wing, within RQ-CAP.

Per-use counts are U2's (U2:804–809).

**What the record shows.**
- U2: available relative to `μ_m` in reading (g), and requires assumed apparatus at the schedule in
  reading (c) (U2:379–391) [A-inf on OFF].
- The fresh-cell lemma gives complete local-history no-signalling within the declared grammar
  (SEL-EXCL-1 ll. 93–95 [OFF]).
- *Probe composite, PR instance* [A-inf on OFF]. A composite in which a wing
  reads `r_j` before use `j` and then uses it at setting 1 lets that wing learn the remote setting
  from its own records: for `T`, `b_j = r_j ⊕ x_j y_j`.
- *Elementary step* [A-inf]. Let a use have deterministic responses `a(x, y, λ)`, `b(x, y, λ)` with
  binary settings and outcomes and a `λ`-law independent of the settings (S5). If `a(x, 0, λ) =
  a(x, 1, λ)` and `b(0, y, λ) = b(1, y, λ)` almost surely for all `x, y`, the table is a mixture of
  local deterministic responses and `|CHSH| ≤ 2` (Main:389 [M]). So a Bell violation gives a set of
  `λ` of positive mass on which `a(x, ·, λ)` depends on `y` or `b(·, y, λ)` depends on `x`. This is
  the last step of L-2's proof, read in reverse. It holds for any source law, atomic or not, and
  uses the unconditioned table, with no postselection; a herald that is a setting-independent
  function of `λ` restricts it to the success branch without changing the argument. It does not use
  L-1, which places the same dependence in U1's operational class (setting–setting I-sel) at finite or
  atomic source laws, conditional on L-1 (U2:528–530; PR #1294 review 5909913170).
- *Freshness relative to `Γ_exe`, compared across realizations* [A-inf]. By the elementary step,
  every deterministic realization of QR-1 with S5 has such a positive-mass set. On it, a wing whose
  response depends on the remote setting, Bob's if `b(·, y, λ)` depends on `x`, Alice's if
  `a(x, ·, λ)` depends on `y`, learns that setting from its own records when it reads the whole `λ`
  before the use. The probe is then on that wing. The probe vulnerability is therefore shared at the
  level of deterministic realizations. It is absent from the quantum reference law, which has no `λ`
  to read.
- *Fresh replacement against the quantum reuse law.* Supplying a fresh source for each use is "a
  different experiment from reusing the measured pair" (TSI:280–281 [NP]). It is case (N) of §2.2.
  RR-2 can therefore realize repeated fresh preparations, but not the quantum two-use law
  (RQ-UPD-2; conditional on L-3).

**Bearing on H-RR.**
- RR-2 defeats RR-1 for PR (PLAN:356).
- It supplies fresh-`λ` runs to PR programs and to QR-1 realizations alike (SEL-DISC-1 P5, l. 53).
- It is `μ`-assumed, so under SNR-1 nothing resting on it counts as a refutation (PLAN:380–382).
- *Status: undetermined.* No support, and no refutation that counts.

PLAN's conditions stand. U3 adds the following, each conditional as marked.
- **Where support could lie** [A-inf]. RR-1, conditional on L-2, and the probe composites of RR-2,

## 5. Two proposed lemmas on reuse

### 5.1 L-2: unchanged-source reuse and Bell locality

**Status.** A proposed lemma: a new hand derivation, [A-inf], author-side, not verified. It is routed
for review (§10.5). No verdict of this note rests on it unconditionally; each use is marked
"conditional on L-2". Its two instances on record are SEL-EXCL-1 §7 [OFF] and TSI §4 [NP]. It is
probably an elementary known fact; the literature was not searched. Its independent review found the
proof correct under its hypotheses, with (k3) in the product form stated here (REVIEW §1); that
review is not a verification of record.

**Hypotheses.**
- (k1) *Deterministic responses, binary.* At each use the outputs are `a(x, y, λ)` and `b(x, y, λ)`,
  with `λ` the source of §2.2, binary settings `x, y` and binary outputs.
- (k2) *Unchanged source.* Case (U) of §2.2: the first use leaves `λ` unchanged, and the second use
  applies the same response functions. Outputs go into fresh blanks, or by XOR into the first use's
  registers.
- (k3) *Product policy pairs jointly executable.* For each `x`, the pairs (Alice `(x, x)`, Bob
  `(0, 0)`) and (Alice `(x, x)`, Bob `(0, 1)`), and for each `y` the mirror pairs (Alice `(0, 0)` or
  `(0, 1)`, Bob `(y, y)`), are each executed by a program on one `M` and one AD-1 instance (G6). The
  proof does not use how the second setting reaches the use: a `φ`-effected write (C-SET, RQ-REU) or
  a preloaded per-use slot (TSI:57, :87–94) serves equally.
- (k4) *S5 at policy level.* The law of `λ` does not depend on the pair of policies.
- (k5) *Complete local histories held.* In the fresh-output form, both outputs of each wing are held
  at `t_c` (RQ-TC). In the same-register form, the register is held at `t_c`.

**Statement.** Suppose that, for each fixed own policy, each wing's complete two-use local history
has a law independent of the remote wing's policy. Then `a(x, 0, λ) = a(x, 1, λ)` and
`b(0, y, λ) = b(1, y, λ)` for almost every `λ`, for all `x`, `y`. The single-use CHSH value is then
at most 2. The argument is measure-theoretic and needs no atomic source law.

**Proof.**
- Fix Alice's policy `(x, x)`.
- Under Bob's policy `(0, 0)`:
  - fresh-output form: Alice's history is `(a(x,0,λ), a(x,0,λ))`, whose two entries agree with
    probability 1;
  - same-register form: her register is `a(x,0,λ) ⊕ a(x,0,λ) = 0` with probability 1.
- Under Bob's policy `(0, 1)`, her history is `(a(x,0,λ), a(x,1,λ))`, or the register
  `a(x,0,λ) ⊕ a(x,1,λ)`.
- No-signalling makes the two laws equal. So `a(x,0,λ) = a(x,1,λ)` almost surely, for each `x`.
- Exchanging the wings gives `b(0,y,λ) = b(1,y,λ)` almost surely.
- So `a = α(x, λ)` and `b = β(y, λ)` almost surely. With (k4), the single-use table is a mixture of
  local deterministic responses under a setting-independent weight, and `|CHSH| ≤ 2` by the pointwise
  bound over the sixteen deterministic response tables (Main:389 [M]). ∎

**Checks against the instances** [A-inf on OFF and NP].
- `T`: `a = r` does not depend on `y`, so Alice sees no signal. `b = r ⊕ xy` depends on `x`. Under
  Bob's `(1, 1)` and Alice's `(0, 0)` against `(0, 1)`, Bob's fresh-output pair is `(r, r)` against
  `(r, r ⊕ 1)`. This matches SEL-EXCL-1 l. 179 up to the choice of `y₁`.
- TSI: `a = R` and `b = R ⊕ xy ⊕ C` with `(R, C)` unchanged (TSI (3), (8)). L-2's own comparison,
  Bob's `(1, 1)` with Alice's `(0, 0)` against `(0, 1)`, gives Bob's pair `(R⊕C, R⊕C)` against
  `(R⊕C, R⊕C⊕1)`. TSI (10) is a different witness of the same signal: Bob's `(0, 1)`, with Alice
  varying `x₂`, gives `b₁ ⊕ b₂ = z`. TSI's settings sit in preloaded per-use slots, which (k3)
  admits.
- Heralded failure with a setting-independent herald (TSI `h < 1`) does not affect the argument
  [REVIEW §1].

**What L-2 does not use, and does not cover.**
- It does not use U1's operational classes. It is stated on record functions, so flagged
  realizations (U1:441–445), which escape L-1, are covered whenever (k1)–(k5) hold.
- It needs no retention in the same-register form, and no adaptivity.
- It does not cover (k2) failing:
  - a source update, case (S). The quantum reference is of this kind (TSI (9); L-3).
  - a setting-independent renewal, case (N): a different preloaded cell (RR-2's fresh cells), a
    setting-independent bijection of `λ`, fresh noise consumed per use, or a tick-indexed response.
    The fresh-cell lemma shows that (N) can be no-signalling (SEL-EXCL-1 §3).
- Nor does it cover:
  - models in which (k3) is not executable, such as M-PR5, where the budget is 1;
  - realizations without (k4): the measurement-dependent route (Main:416–423);
  - extension operations typed into `Obs`: stop S7(a) (U1 §6.1).

**Relation to L-1.** L-1 (U2:492–550; proposed): under (h1)–(h4), a Bell violation requires
setting–setting I-sel at a single use. L-2: under (k1)–(k5), a Bell violation, an unchanged source
and executable reuse together imply complete local-history signalling. They are independent. L-2
needs no binary setting factors of the initial configuration. It needs S5 at policy level, (k4),
where L-1 needs it for the settings, (h3). L-2 needs no atomic source law; L-1's literal scope does
(PR #1294 review 5909913170).

### 5.2 L-3: the quantum two-use law and the second-use content

**Origin.** REVIEW R2 states, as its own unverified inference, that the quantum two-use law (9)
excludes unchanged-source realizations of the `2√2` table directly, without L-2 or a no-signalling
premise. It is checked here by hand. **Verdict of the check: it holds**, at the scope stated below,
and it extends from case (U) to case (N). The inference is credited to the review.

**Status.** A proposed lemma: an author-side hand derivation, [A-inf], not verified. Routed for
review with L-2 (§10.5). Each use is marked "conditional on L-3". TSI states its conclusion for its
own candidate (TSI:239, :293 [NP]).

**The target law.** TSI (9) with the first-use table (1) (TSI:228–239 [NP]). Hand re-check [A-inf]:
after local rank-one projective measurements with outcomes `a₁, b₁` on `|Φ⁺⟩`, the state is the
product of the two outcome eigenstates. A second measurement on a wing along the same axis returns
the first outcome with probability 1; along the other axis, Bloch-orthogonal to the first (as the
CHSH settings are on each wing), it returns a fair outcome independent of `(a₁, b₁)`. The two wings
factor. This is (9).

**Hypotheses.**
- (k1) of §5.1, at both uses: the first use gives `a₁ = A₁(x₁, y₁, λ)`, `b₁ = B₁(x₁, y₁, λ)`; the
  second gives `a₂ = A₂(x₂, y₂, κ)`, `b₂ = B₂(x₂, y₂, κ)`, with `κ` as in §2.2. Write `κ_A` and
  `κ_B` for the parts of `κ` on which `A₂` and `B₂` depend: the second-use content of each wing.
- (k3′) For each `x`, the pairs (Alice `(x, x)`, Bob `(0, y₂)`) and (Alice `(x, x)`, Bob `(1, y₂)`)

  In the same-register form, `Θ` identifies the register with `a₁ ⊕ a₂`, and repeatability reads
  "the register is 0".
- No failure branch (`h = 1`), or a herald that is a function of the source with policy-independent
  law; the argument is then run on the success branch, with the full-attempt law unchanged.

**Statement.** Suppose that, for wing A, `κ_A` does not depend on `y₁`, `a₁` or `b₁`:
`κ_A = G(λ, ν, x₁)` for a fixed `G`. Then `A₁(x, 0, λ) = A₁(x, 1, λ)` almost surely, for each `x`.
Likewise for wing B with the roles exchanged. Consequently:
- (a) on every wing whose first-use response depends on the remote setting at positive mass, that
  wing's second-use content must depend on the first use's outputs or on the remote first setting,
  at positive mass. That is a source update, case (S) of §2.2. Cases (U) and (N), and dependence on
  the wing's own first setting alone, are excluded for that wing;
- (b) since (1) has `|CHSH| = 2√2 > 2`, at least one wing is of that kind (§4.2's elementary step);
- (c) at a changed setting, each wing's second-use content carries information not determined by
  `(x₁, y₁, a₁, b₁)`.

**Proof.**
- Fix `x` and `y₂`. Under the pair (Alice `(x, x)`, Bob `(y₁, y₂)`), (9) gives `a₂ = a₁` almost
  surely: `A₁(x, y₁, λ) = A₂(x, y₂, G(λ, ν, x))`.
- The right side does not depend on `y₁`. By (k4′) the law of `(λ, ν)` is the same under `y₁ = 0` and
  `y₁ = 1`. So `A₁(x, 0, λ) = A₁(x, 1, λ)` almost surely.
- In case (U), `G(λ, ν, x) = λ` and `A₂ = A₁`, and the single pair with Bob `(0, 1)` gives
  `A₁(x, 0, λ) = A₁(x, 1, λ)` directly. This is the reviewer's form.
- Exchanging the wings gives the statement for B. If neither `κ_A` nor `κ_B` depends on the first
  outputs or the remote first setting, both responses are local almost surely, and with (k4′)
  `|CHSH| ≤ 2` (Main:389 [M]). This contradicts (1), which proves (a) and (b).
- For (c): at `x₂ ≠ x₁`, (9) makes `a₂` fair given `(a₁, b₁)` and the settings. If `κ_A` were a
  function of `(x₁, y₁, a₁, b₁)` alone, `a₂` would be determined by them and the settings. ∎

## 6. Capacity at the comparison tick: a K-3 report on the frame

**Kind of result.** This section reports a consequence of the frame's own definitions under one
reading of AD-1(a). It is a K-3 report (PLAN:416: the criterion "must keep the coherent operations,
incompatible measurements and Bell experiments the target needs, or state that it does not"). It is
not a native fact about OI models, it disproves no input of any model, and it is not a *not
available* outcome counted under SNR-1.

**Grounds.**
- AD-1(a) declares "A finite family of disjoint proper subsystems `V_k ⊊ S`, each with finitely many
  distinguishable states" (U1:227).
- Its source, the Definition (Main:42), gives the finiteness of *each* observer: "a proper subsystem
  with finitely many distinguishable internal states". The finiteness of the *family* is AD-1(a)'s
  own wording, and its fixedness per model is reading (a) below.
- G4's joint-hold clause, "Records compared jointly are all held at the comparison tick", is U1's
  [P] choice (U1:183; U1 §5.2), grounded on the exclusion of R-seq.

**Statement** [A-inf], under reading (a).
- On one model with one AD-1 instance (G6), every record held at a tick is a function of the joint
  present configuration of the declared family (G4, AD-1(d)). So the records held at any one tick take
  at most `N_held = ∏_k |C_{V_k}|` values.
- A program whose complete-record law needs a support of more than `N_held` values cannot have its
  compared records jointly held at `t_c`. It has no execution in U1's sense (U1 §2.1) on that model:
  its joint law would be R-seq's, a representation (K-4).
- `Θ` (e) requires equality of complete-record laws, so the record map cannot be many-to-one, and the
  support size is what counts.

**Count.** Per wing, for `m` uses with binary settings and outcomes, the support of the wing's
complete-record law has at most `2^{2m}` values. It has `2^{2m}` for a randomized setting policy with
full-support settings and outcomes, and `2^m` for a fixed setting policy whose outcomes have full
support (for example alternating Bloch-orthogonal settings, where the Lüders update, iterated as in
(9), makes each outcome fair and independent of the earlier ones). Heralds, ticks and flags add to this.

**Consequences under reading (a).**
- `Γ_exe(M)` contains complete-record programs only up to support `N_held`. So it is not closed under
  unbounded iteration of S-A1, S-A4 or S-A5, for PR programs and the quantum references' programs
  alike. Held channels (S-A6) move records within the same family and do not raise the bound.
- **Two coverage targets, reported separately.**
  - *Answer 3's scope.* SPEC's coverage target is one model `M*` covering every experiment "in the
    agreed scope", which "includes arbitrarily large finite carriers" (answer 3, #1169 c.6876591512;
    SPEC:397–400). Experiments that read every part of a large carrier, and unboundedly long
    sequences, need complete records of unbounded support. Under reading (a) no model of the frame
    meets this target. U1 §5.3 reports G4's joint-hold clause as coverage-neutral; that holds per
    program, and at answer 3's scope it holds only up to `N_held`. The frame, under reading (a),
    therefore does not keep this scope. This is the K-3 report.
  - *The amended AC-1's coverage half,* "A restriction may not exclude what perceived quantum
    phenomena need" (c.6891338304). Each perceived phenomenon has finite records. Under reading (a)
    the bound prevents a model from covering a given finite set of them only if that model's
    family is too small for their records. Whether any model's family is large enough is not checked,
    and no model covers them on record (U2 §6.1).
- **Agreement half.** Performed experiments are finitely many, with finite records. As for the
  second coverage target, the bound matters only for a model whose family is too small; not checked.

**Readings of AD-1(a), not chosen here** (AD-1 is frozen, U1:222). They bear on ledger Q1, which
SPEC carries as a coverage dependency: "one observer interface, or a stated family of nested
partitions" (SPEC:404–405; it "stays with #914", SPEC:620–621).
- (a) The family is finite and fixed per model. The bound holds, with the consequences above.
- (b) The family a *program* uses is finite, while the model may carry unboundedly many observers.
  K-obs quantifies over each observer (SPEC:240), and K-dyn allows a non-finite `S` (SPEC:239). The
  bound then lifts. Reading (b) would need a U1 revision with a stated rule for which observers a
  program uses.
- (c) Ledger Q1's stated family of nested partitions: each level a finite family, the levels nested.
  The bound holds at each level and lifts across levels when the family of levels is unbounded. This
  would need a U1 revision stating the family and how a program's level is fixed.

This is owner question 1 (§10.7).

**K-1 and K-2.** The bound singles out no program class: it applies to PR programs and to the quantum
references' programs alike, and its ground is observer finiteness (J1: "a proper subsystem with
finite momentary capacity", SPEC:277; Main:42, :724 (ii)) together with G4. Nothing in it is chosen
for quantum outcomes. It is not a breach of K-1 or K-2.

**Pressure test.** The bound uses only G4's joint-hold clause, AD-1(a) under reading (a) and the
completeness of records (G5, K-rec). Relaxing joint holding to per-tick holding would admit R-seq,
which U1 excludes (U1:183, review B2). The bound does not depend on C-RET against R2 (§7).

**Class.** ELABORATING. The bound is the immediate consequence of J1 under G4, and a held-capacity
analogue of SEL-DISC-1's entropy bound (l. 79 [OFF]).
