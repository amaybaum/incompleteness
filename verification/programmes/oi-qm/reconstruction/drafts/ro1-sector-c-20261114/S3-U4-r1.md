## 6. The parked-record case (decision A), in the four-way separation

[P] **Definition used** (U1 C-RET, parked-record clause): a record held at `t₁` whose support passes
through the hidden sector `H` and returns to a region readable under AD-1 by `t₂` is *retained* when
its return is effected by `φ` within the budget. A return by recurrence is global and beyond the
budget, so it is not retention. Three return kinds are kept apart:
- *intact return:* readable at `t₂` without a private decoder;
- *scrambled return:* readable only with content still held in `H` (a key), so not retention under
  C-RET;
- *coherent uncomputation:* the record is undone, so that no region holds path-correlated content.

### 6.1 Class (i), native mechanism

- **PK-1. One-tick parking is native on K1 under A-cur, and only there** [A-inf, from the step and
  AD-1(b)]. `φ(u_prev, u) = (u, A u − u_prev)` moves each current value into the retarded slice, which
  A-cur hides (SM:252, Lemma 1c (iii)), and returns it additively in `u_{t+2} = A u_{t+1} − u_t`, mixed
  with neighbour contributions. Where `V` does not hold all those neighbours the return is scrambled
  in C-RET's sense. Under A-reg both slices are read and nothing is parked at site level. The
  owner's case is therefore present on K1 at the one-tick scale as content transport, conditional on
  the slice form. As a *record* (held, R1) it is undecided (C-4) and blocked at the ready region
  (C-3).
- **PK-2. On K1 the location of a record does not enter interference** [A-inf, from S-L1 and S-L5,
  named premise; global reading of (α)]. The carrier's transfer `Φ_{DΣ}` to the recombination region
  is fixed. Whether a record is held in `V`, parked in `H`, returned intact, returned scrambled or
  dispersed changes nothing that `D` receives from the carrier. Every change of visibility at `D` is
  an additive offset of carrier-independent content. **The parked-record case has no record-tied
  interference consequence on K1.**
- **PK-3. The native marker is itself a parked structure** [A-inf, §3.2]. Its path information is
  masked by uniform content and survives jointly with that content (one-time pad; OAC:132–134). A
  parked copy of the masking content that later returns would be the form a sourced retention of the
  masking content takes, and with it (β) would hold at the return tick. None is sourced, so D-1 stays
  "unsourced at (β)–(δ)" where masking is complete.
- **PK-4. Parking and S-L7's epistemic restriction exclude each other at the one-tick scale**
  [A-inf, from S-L7, MR★:603–618, record on `main`]. S-L7: a single-time current-slice access is an
  epistemic restriction (isotropic readouts); "An access that reads both slices, or retains the
  earlier of two consecutive reads, is not." Under A-cur, reading both slices is reading the parked
  value directly, and retaining the earlier read is recovering it. So on K1 an epistemic-restriction
  reading of the access (the property by which CC4-type models reproduce which-way and eraser
  phenomenology, AW-MR5) holds exactly while the one-tick parked value is **not** recovered. A
  parked value that becomes accessible again removes that restriction.
- **PK-5. Renewal parks records** [A-inf, from Lemma R, PREREG:177–182, named premise]. "No bijection
  `g` of `S` satisfies `g(r, o) = g(r', o) = (r_0, o)`"; "the distinguishing information moves into
  `E` or `L`". Resetting a marker to ready therefore parks its old record outside the marker. If that
  record returns within the budget into a region a later use reads or conditions on, the later use's
  resource is not fresh (`composition-access-20261024/REPORT.md`:57–69, [M-r]). This links recording
  and renewal (H-RR), for PR and quantum references alike.
- **PK-6. At the fixed-basis layer, parking and return is Main's hidden-memory readback** [M]. "The
  hidden sector acts as a memory register … carries the record 'I was at `x = 0`.' Step 2 reads this
  record … a revival of distinguishability" (Main:200); C2-structural (Main:74); the revival
  condition (Main:157); the delayed-revival family (Main:439). These are realization-level results
  about visible laws (the transition-statistics layer). Per-law realizations are representable, not
  available (K-4). Whether the K1 step executes such a readback of a which-path record under AD-1 is

### 6.2 Class (ii), conditional quantum interpretation

Imports, named: P-super (superposed carrier), unitary marker and environment dynamics, the Born
dictionary (P-Born) for visibilities. With `|Ψ⟩ = (|L⟩|E_L⟩ + e^{iθ}|R⟩|E_R⟩)/√2`, where `|E_·⟩` is the
state of the whole complement, hidden part included, the unconditional visibility is
`V = |⟨E_L|E_R⟩|` (PREREG §3.5 benchmark, extended from the marker to the whole complement).
- **QM-1. Parking and intact return leave `V` unchanged.** A unitary transfer of the record within the
  complement preserves `⟨E_L|E_R⟩`. While parked and after an intact return, `V` stays low. On return,
  a complementary readout of the record gives conditional fringes whose full masses sum to the
  unsorted pattern (T4, PREREG:490–491); a path readout gives none.
- **QM-2. Scrambled return leaves `V` unchanged.** The information remains in the complement.
  Conditional fringes need a readout that includes the scrambling key.
- **QM-3. Coherent uncomputation restores `V` and removes the record.** When the complement's
  conditional states re-overlap (`|⟨E_L|E_R⟩| → 1`), by autonomous evolution (revival) or applied
  reversal, unconditional fringes return. At that tick the record exists nowhere.
- **QM-4. Return of a record and revival of coherence exclude each other.** With `D` the best path
  discrimination over the whole complement, `D² + V² ≤ 1` (Englert 1996, as cited at PREREG:232–233).
  An intact return keeps `D` and `V` where they were; a revival raises `V` only by lowering `D`.
- **QM-5. GR:350 states the same reading** [M]: "environmental decoherence, in which which-path
  records pass to unobserved degrees of freedom such as emitted radiation while the total evolution
  remains unitary". Visibility depends on the existence of distinguishable path-correlated content
  in the complement, not on whether an observer holds it.

### 6.3 Distinguishing the parked-record return from quantum revivals and environmental memory

**What tells a record's return from a coherence revival, within QM.** [A-inf] Both are information
returning from an environment, in the sense of trace-distance backflow (Main:153 cites the
literature [4, 5]). They differ in **which pair of preparations** regains distinguishability:
- a record's return increases the distinguishability of the path preparations `|L⟩`, `|R⟩` as seen by
  the carrier together with the accessible region (path basis);
- a revival increases the distinguishability of the phase preparations `|±⟩` as seen at the
  recombination (phase basis), and lowers path distinguishability in the whole complement (QM-4).

The observable is the per-run joint record of `V` at recombination and of `D` retrieved from the
returned record, at the same tick and preparation. That separates the two cases inside QM; it is
defined, class (ii).

**What OI's native layer has of each** [A-inf]. OI's revival on record (PK-6; the book's revival
measure `𝒩`, ch18 §18.3, "the total-variation distance of their visible population laws, equivalently
the trace distance of the corresponding diagonal states") is a distinguishability of visible
population laws: the path-basis kind. It is the native counterpart of a record's return (R-c), not
of a coherence revival (R-b). A phase-basis revival needs a coherent preparation of `|±⟩`, which is
P-super or an operation not shown available (track (a); §4.3, P).

**Against OI as a whole: no distinguishing observable.** [A-inf] For OI's native mechanism, on K1
the record has no interference consequence (PK-2), so it predicts neither QM's record-tied visibility
loss nor any alternative to it; on σ12 and on the lift nothing is derived. The corpus's nearest
proposal-level prediction, the capacity-controlled revival `𝓕(r) = 2r − r²` (ch15, "a proposed model
prediction rather than a theorem-derived one"), concerns path-basis distinguishability of visible
laws and is not tied to which-path coherence; partial revival "is standard open-systems
phenomenology" (ch18:78). Finite-environment revivals and environmental memory effects are predicted
by QM in the same terms. **Per the owner's refinement, the parked-record case and route M are
reported as explanatory compatibility, not independent experimental support.**

**A candidate reading that would be distinguishable, and fails.** [A-inf] An *accessibility* reading,
in which unconditional visibility depends on whether `V` holds or can retrieve the record, would
predict fringes while the record is parked and their loss when it returns. Against QM-4 it would
show high `V` during parking together with high `D` at retrieval, which is stronger than quantum in
the QM-4 test. But this region is not unperformed: E-2's delayed-choice erasers park a record in an
unread degree of freedom and later retrieve it, and the unsorted pattern shows no fringes while it
is parked (E-1, E-2; to verify first-hand). So the accessibility reading disagrees with performed
experiments as attributed. It is not a discriminating prediction of OI: no OI source derives it, and
on K1 PK-2 excludes it. It is recorded here as a reading that a candidate C must not imply; it is not
a clause added to C (§0).
