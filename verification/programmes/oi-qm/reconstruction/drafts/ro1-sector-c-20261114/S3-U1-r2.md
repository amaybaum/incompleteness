## 2. The common frame

### 2.1 Model, programs, executions

[P] **Model.** `M = (S, φ, μ, Obs, Γ, R, Res)` (SPEC:110–120). U1 defines the set `Γ_exe(M)` and
leaves S7's second part open: under the *derived* reading `Γ := Γ_exe(M)`; under the *component*
reading `Γ` is a component and K-acc requires `Γ = Γ_exe(M)` (SPEC:242). The criterion is the same
under both readings (§6.2).

[P] **Bare model.** A model with no extension E, with `Obs` read-only (AD-1(c)), and with no stated
intervention principle: no phase operator (P-phase, Main:80, :588) and no state-mixing resource
(P-mix, Main:588). Its operations are those `φ` generates. DEPS's Interface B, `permTheory` on the
configuration space, is the configuration-level theory of a bare model; Interface A,
`substratumClass`, includes the phase operators by a stated intervention principle and so is **not**
bare (DEPS:178, :275–283).

[P] **Program.** A program `g` is a finite declaration, made before the run and identical for every
setting value: its regions (observers `V_k` of AD-1, carriers, setting regions `Z`, ledgers `L`,
resource regions); a budget `T`; the use boundaries, each with the law `g` requires of named regions
at a named tick; its read schedule (which `V_k` at which ticks); its **comparison tick** `t_c`, the
one tick at which its complete record is taken; and its complete record specification. The only
dynamical step is `φ`.

[P] **Execution.** `g` is executed in `M` when, from `μ` (or from `μ` conditioned on an executed
herald, with the complementary mass retained), `φ^t` delivers at every use boundary the law `g`
requires, and:
- every record that a later step of `g` uses is held (G4) at the tick of that step;
- **every record that enters `g`'s complete record, or any joint comparison, is held at `t_c`.** A
  record first held at an earlier tick enters only if it is retained to `t_c` (C-RET) or carried to
  a region held at `t_c` (S-A6). Otherwise the joint law it would enter is a representation (K-4).

This applies to outcome records, setting records (K-rec, SPEC:243), herald and failure marks, and
to every history a later classification uses, for example SEL-EXCL-1's complete local histories
`H_A^(i)` (SEL-EXCL-1 at `0ecfddeb`, l. 91).

### 2.3 Levels, source verdicts and outcomes

[P] **Levels** (MR-1, PREREG:36–43; PLAN:169–179):
- **Representable.** A mathematical representation exists. This includes inverse images,
  conditioning on an unrecorded event, per-law realizations (Main:482, :556, :582), and R-seq.
- **Available** (= *executable through `Obs`*). G1–G6 hold, and every input's verdict is *derived*
  or *assumed as a datum of `μ` or an entry of `Res`*. Where any input is an assumed `μ`-datum, the
  program is reported as **available relative to the declared `μ`**, with the datum listed.
- **Derived.** Available, with `μ` the measure Lemma 3 selects (Main:54) or a law PC-1's fork rule
  fixes within the OI premises (PREREG:137–140), and no `Res` entry beyond what `μ` and `φ` give.

[P] **Three kinds of input**, which the plan's "assumed apparatus capability" (PLAN:277–279) splits:
- *sourced*: verdict *derived*;
- *`μ`-assumed*: a datum of the fixed `μ`, booked in `Res`. It is not a producer (#866 rule above),
  so its verdict is *assumed*;
- *apparatus-assumed*: supplied outside `(φ, μ, AD-1)`. It fails G1.

[P] **Four outcomes for a program, or for an action within it.** They replace r1's single "not
available" (review R1):

| Outcome | When | Counts as a negative under SNR-1? |
|---|---|---|
| **Available** (derived, or relative to `μ`) | every input derived or `μ`-assumed | — |
| **Unsourced** | some input has no producer identified and none is disproved | No |
| **Requires assumed apparatus** | some input is apparatus-assumed | No |
| **Not available** | some input is disproved, at the disproof's scope and status | Yes, at that scope; if the disproof is [NP], conditionally on that premise (PLAN:791–794) |

[A-inf] Allowing `μ`-assumed inputs at the available level keeps the criterion from removing a
program by a choice of `μ` alone (K-1); listing them keeps them apart from sourced inputs (K-2,
§A.29). The four outcomes are what SNR-1 needs: PLAN:784–797 itself separates *unsourced or assumed*
from *disproved*.

### 4.1 C-PREP: preparation

- the law of `X` at `t₀`, under `μ` or under `μ` conditioned on an executed herald `E`, is `ν`;
- the herald, if any, is a held record (G4) produced under AD-1, and `P(E)` and the rejected mass

- where later steps depend on which preparation was made, a held record certifies it at the tick

### 4.2 C-SET: setting choice

takes at least two values of positive mass, with:
- **a named source:** `Z`'s content is either a datum of `μ`, with verdict *derived* under the
  derived `μ` and *assumed* under a declared datum, or is written by `φ` from the observer's own held
  records (OA1:111–113: "A setting is a configuration of designated cells. It is either an initial
  condition or written by `φ` from the observer's own records");
- **a held record of its value**, held at every tick a later step uses it and at `t_c` (K-rec,
  SPEC:243; §2.1);
- **its joint law with every response-relevant variable stated** as a property of `μ` and `φ`,

  criterion. Any use that needs it, such as `Θ` (f) (SPEC:380–382) or a Bell ceiling (Main:405–410),
  carries S5 (ledger Q8) as a named condition;

Whether the value selects an operation is C-INT's question, not C-SET's.

analyst's choice of which region or tick to report (AD-1(e)).

### 4.3 C-INT: intervention

**(i) Criterion.** [P] Three classes, all reported. They generalize S-C1(a) (MR★:498–503: the
carrier's contribution is "the same homomorphism for every value of every other region. A setting
enters only as an additive offset") to rules that are not additive. Following S-C1(a)'s quantifier,
the *carrier* in what follows is **any region other than the selecting setting**, other setting
regions included. r1 restricted carriers to non-setting regions; that restriction had no source and
is dropped (review B1).
- **I-sel, setting-selected operation.** There are setting values `z ≠ z′` of positive mass, a region
  `Y` readable under AD-1 and a tick `t` such that, with every region other than the carrier and the
  selecting setting fixed at a value of positive mass, the partition of the carrier's content induced
  by `s_carrier ↦ pr_Y φ^t(·)` differs between `z` and `z′`.
- **Q1 test.** I-sel is **withheld** when, under AD-1 and the same preparation, a common refinement of
  the partitions for `z` and `z′` is available as a record held at the tick of use. This applies the
  owner's working definition, as PLAN:283–285 and :870 require: "A common refinement that is
  genuinely available through the declared native access interface, under the same preparation,
  should withhold the setting-controlled-interaction label. Do not award the label merely with a
  qualification flag. This is a working definition, not a proof that such a refinement physically
  exists" (#1007 c.6719788955, quoted in #866 c.6719811801). It agrees with S-C1(b) (MR★:504–507) and
  OA-1 D7 (OA1:204–205): reads that are coarse-grainings of one joint record do not select. [A-inf]
  When the carrier is a setting region whose value C-SET requires to be held, its held record is
  such a refinement, so a setting acting on another setting never earns I-sel under Q1.
- **I-off, setting-dependent output.** Setting dependence that leaves every such partition unchanged:
  an additive offset on K1, a translation under any affine rule (OA-1 D5). Recorded as output
  dependence, not as I-sel (PLAN:291).
- **Flag on I-off.** [A-inf] Averaged over the law of the other regions, an offset that is not
  additive in the setting and another region (for example `z·n`) can switch which carrier property is
  legible in `Y`, while every fixed-rest partition stays the same. Such cases are recorded as I-off
  with the flag. Under Q1 the flag awards no I-sel ("Do not award the label merely with a
  qualification flag"). On an additive rule the offset is additive in all non-carrier regions jointly,
  so the flag cannot arise there.

An intervention is available when `φ` effects it (G1) and its setting meets C-SET. For a bare model,
an operator declared outside `φ` is apparatus-assumed. For an operation an extension adds, the
verdict follows the typing (§6.1): typed into `φ`, these clauses apply unchanged; typed into `Γ`, it
requires assumed apparatus; typed into `Obs`, **[STOP S7(a)]**.

### 4.5 C-RET: retention

**(i) Criterion.** [P] A record held at `t₁` is retained to `t₂` when, under every continuation of
the program that does not act on its support, it is recoverable at `t₂` from a configuration readable
under AD-1, without a private decoder, within the budget (AD-1(f)). `t₂` is a tick at which a later
step uses the record, or `t_c`. Global information preservation by bijectivity is retention only when
the information is in a readable region at `t₂`.

**Divergence from MR-1's R2, stated.** R2 requires recoverability "at every later accessible time up
to `T`" (PREREG:163–164). C-RET is a readback form: it requires recoverability at the ticks where the
record is used. It is grounded in C2-structural, "a hidden record of visible history, once written,
persists to the readback that uses it" (Main:74; PLAN:293). Consequences:
- every record meeting R2 meets C-RET, so MR-1's positive results stated with R2 carry over;
- a negative stated with R2 does not carry over without a check, since a record can fail R2 and meet
  C-RET. In particular S-C4a's "no confined pointer" (MR★:564–571) concerns confinement at every
  slice and is not by itself a negative for C-RET;
- C-RET is U1's [P] choice (§5.2). The owner may prefer R2; the criterion would then be strengthened
  at this clause only.

**The parked-record case.** Owner decision A assigns to U4, as a named hypothesis, a record retained
in the hidden sector that later becomes accessible again (#1169 c.6890079967). C-RET defines what
would count: a record whose support passes through `H` between `t₁` and `t₂` is retained when its
return to a readable region is effected by `φ` within the budget. A return by recurrence (S-L6) is
global and lies beyond the budget, so it is not retention. The clause assumes no mechanism.

**(ii) Levels.** Derived: retained by `φ` from the derived `μ`. Available relative to `μ`: with a
`μ`-assumed register, booked. Representable: an analyst's history projection.

## 5. Countercontrols on the criterion as a whole

### 5.1 K-1: PR does not vanish by declaration

[P] Four checks.
- **(i) No record-law clause.** G1–G7 and C-PREP to C-COMP read the program's regions, inputs,
  producers, schedule and records, and never its record law. So no clause can exclude a program for
  its correlations.
- **(ii) Twin programs.** Two programs with the same regions, inputs, schedule and record
  specification, differing only in one step's map, get the same verdict on every input. Their
  verdicts differ only on whether that step is effected by `φ` (G1) and on its C-INT class.
- **(iii) Negatives only by disproof.** A program, or an action within it, counts as a negative only
  with the outcome *not available*, which needs an input disproved at its scope and status. *Unsourced*
  and *requires assumed apparatus* are reported and do not count (§2.3; SNR-1, PLAN:784–797).
- **(iv) The PR gate on record under C-INT.** [A-inf, by hand from
  `T(x,y,r,A,B) = (x,y,r, A⊕r, B⊕r⊕(x∧y))`, SEL-EXCL-1 at `0ecfddeb`, l. 33; off `main`]
  - *Algebra.* For fixed `(x, y)`, `T` restricted to `(r, A, B)` is the setting-free linear map
    `(r, A, B) ↦ (r, A⊕r, B⊕r)` followed by the translation `(0, 0, x∧y)`. The only setting
    dependence is the offset `x∧y`, a product of the two wings' settings.
  - *Non-setting carriers.* With carrier `r`, `A` or `B`, every partition induced by any output
    register is the same for all `(x, y)`. This is I-off **with the flag**: averaged over `y`, `r` is
    legible in `B` for `x = 0` (`B = B₀⊕r`) and masked for `x = 1` (`B = B₀⊕r⊕y`).
  - *A setting as carrier (review B1).* With `x` selecting and `y` as carrier, `Y = B`: the map
    `y ↦ B₀⊕r⊕(x∧y)` is constant for `x = 0` and injective for `x = 1`. The partitions differ, so
    before the Q1 test this is I-sel: `x` selects whether `y` reaches `B`.
  - *Q1 test.* The program requires Bob to hold `y` (SEL-EXCL-1 l. 44, "Bob chooses/records y"; C-SET).
    His held `y` is a common refinement of both partitions of `y`, available under AD-1 and the same

    refinement for the flag case, recovering `r` from `(B, x, y)`, exists only when Alice's held `x`

    carrier, with Q1), `T` carries no I-sel: its setting dependence is the cross-wing product offset

    not reach this implementation of PR.

    | r1: carriers restricted to non-setting regions (unsourced) | I-off with the flag | No |

    | Any non-selecting region as carrier, with Q1 (frozen) | I-off; I-sel withheld by the held `y` | No |

  - *Scope.* The specific gate `T`, under these definitions. The frozen row's conclusion depends on the
