### 2.2 `Γ_exe(M-PR5)` in full

[A-inf] `T` fixes `x, y, r`, and `T² = id` on every configuration (SEL-EXCL-1 l. 36), so `T` has order 2.
AD-1(f) then bounds every program's budget by 1 (U1:232). So every program of M-PR5 reads at ticks 0
and 1 only, with `V_A = (x, r)` and `V_B = (y, r⊕(x∧y))` at `t₁` under `μ_PR`. Hence `Γ_exe(M-PR5)`
consists of CP-1 and its sub-reads: CP-2 at held `x = 0`, and Alice's record `a = r` of the used cell.
CP-3 with `m ≥ 2`, CP-4, CP-5, a probe of `r` by Bob that does not depend on `x`, CP-7 and CP-8 are
**not in `Γ_exe(M-PR5)`**. The reasons are native facts of the tuple: one cell, settings that `T`
leaves constant, budget at most 1, and no channel region. None of them is a declaration (K-1, PLAN:414).

These negatives concern one designed tuple. They are author-side, so under SNR-1 they count only at
that scope and only once verified (PLAN:784–797).

### 2.5 CP-4, same-seed reuse

- *M-PR5:* **not in `Γ_exe`** (§2.2).
- *The enlarged grammar of SEL-EXCL-1 §7:* "allow the two controllers to change their setting
  registers" (l. 169). That is an exogenous label applied between steps, which U1 rules out as a
  setting (U1:331). So the program as specified **requires assumed apparatus** at the setting change.
- *First missing step:* a `φ`-effected change of the setting registers between the two uses (C-SET).
  [A-inf] Written from a private seed as `z ↦ z⊕U`, the change is a single-factor value write, I-off
  (U1:365). Constructing a tuple whose `φ` contains `T` and such a writer is #1143's work.
- *What the same-register witness needs* [A-inf]. With `T` applied twice to the same registers,
  `y₁ = 0`, `y₂ = 1` and `B₀ = 0`, the second output is
  `B₂ = B₀ ⊕ (x₁∧y₁) ⊕ (x₂∧y₂) = x₂`. The cell `r` cancels, so the witness needs:
  - the setting change;
  - a budget of at least 2, below the enlarged `φ`'s order.

  It needs no retention of `b₁` and no clock. The fresh-output variant needs `b₁` retained to `t_c` and
  a blank second register reached by `T`, which is the same sequencing as CP-3.
- *M-PR(m), reading (g), budget at least 2:* applying `φ_m` again reuses every cell with unchanged
  seeds. Whether the resulting complete local histories signal is not decided. It is a calculation per
  policy family, routed to U3 and #1143.
- *R-lin:* the uses are **not available** (D5).

### 2.6 CP-5, the one-bit message (IC₂ protocol)

Steps, in order (SEL-DISC-1 ll. 85–105 [OFF]):

2. `x ← Z₀⊕Z₁` and `y ← K`;
3. one use of the box;
4. `M ← Z₀⊕a`;
5. `M` held in a one-bit channel region and carried to Bob (S-A6);
6. `G ← M⊕b`;
7. retention of the inputs and of `G` to `t_c`, and joint readout.

[A-inf] Every write in steps 2, 4 and 6 is affine over `𝔽₂`, so I-off (U1:364). The only I-sel step
is the box.

- *SEL-DISC-1's extension:* **requires assumed apparatus**. "The appended channel is a mathematical

- *Granularity does not absorb this program* [A-inf]. S-A6 requires a channel region that carries a held
  record, "with alphabet, budget and schedule counted" (U1:666–667). If the whole protocol were one
  step of `φ`, no message would be held in a channel region at any tick, and the one-bit budget could
  not be stated. So CP-5 needs a multi-tick realization with a fixed `φ`. None is on record (§8.3).
- *R-lin:* the scaffolding (steps 1–2, 4–7) is not excluded by D5. The channel is unsourced

- *QR-3:* the comparators D6 and D7 are target laws under `Θ`. They rest on "quantum comparator

## 4. The quantum and classical reference programs

### 4.1 Lemma L-1: Bell violation requires setting–setting selection

[A-inf, by hand.] **Statement.** Take a deterministic two-wing program with:
- binary settings `x, y`, each a factor of the initial configuration or written from such a factor;
- every other factor collected as `λ`, with the law of `λ` independent of `(x, y)` (S5, posit (xiv),
  Main:724);
- each wing's outcome the content of a binary output factor, `A` or `B`, readable at `t_c`.

If neither outcome is operationally I-sel with the remote setting as selector, the local setting as
carrier and `λ` fixed (U1:423–427, `Y` the output factor), then `|CHSH| ≤ 2`.

**Proof.** For each positive-mass `λ`, the partition of `y`'s two values induced by `y ↦ b(x, y, λ)` is
the same for `x = 0, 1`. For a binary output this means `b(1, ·, λ) = b(0, ·, λ) ⊕ c(λ)`, so
`b = b(0, y, λ) ⊕ x·c(λ)`. Likewise `a = a(x, 0, λ) ⊕ y·k(λ)`. Then `a⊕b = α(x, λ) ⊕ β(y, λ)`, with
`α = a(x, 0, λ) ⊕ x·c(λ)` and `β = b(0, y, λ) ⊕ y·k(λ)`. CHSH reads only `a⊕b`, so it equals the CHSH
value of the local response functions `α, β` under a `λ`-law independent of the settings. That value is
at most 2 by the pointwise bound over the sixteen deterministic response tables (Main:389 [M]). ∎

**Consequence.** Under S5, every deterministic program with outcome factors and `|CHSH| > 2`, QR-1's
`2√2` included, contains setting–setting I-sel. That is the class `T` belongs to (U1:726–729).

**Scope.** L-1 is conditional on S5. Without S5, the measurement-dependent route allows CHSH up to 4
(Main:416–423 [M]). It concerns outcomes that are contents of output factors. Where an outcome is a
non-injective function of a larger readable region, such as a thresholded real field on R-lin, the
selection can occur at record level while every configuration-level partition stays fixed. U1 records
that case as I-off with the flag (U1:441–445), and L-1 does not reach it.

**Standing.** L-1 is the standard fact that remote-setting dependence which only relabels outcomes can
be absorbed into local responses, restated in U1's operational classes. It decides U1's open AW-3
point (U1:738–739, :896) in the output-factor case. It does not decide the flagged case.
