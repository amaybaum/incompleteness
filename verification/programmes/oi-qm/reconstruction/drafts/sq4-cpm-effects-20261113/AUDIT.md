## 1. Exact proof interface

Take the fixed `(S, φ, V, H, μ, A)` and the operational prepare–measure fragment from OA2. Its admissible preparation laws `P` on the de-setting record `Z₀` have support-union `U`; put `W = span(P)`. Let `E_CPM` be the *actual available measurement effects under C-PM*, for all permitted measurement settings, with one fixed set of label cells excluded as measurement effects for the whole fragment: the union of the setting, coin, herald and other label cells of its admitted preparations, as OA2:451–454 treats the selector cells of both procedures.

For the particular ontic model used in E(b), `ι(p) = law_p(Z₀)`. Preparation noncontextuality of that model requires the following implication for **admissible preparations p,q**:

    for all e ∈ E_CPM, e(p) = e(q)  ==>  ι(p) = ι(q).

Call this **SEP(P,E_CPM)**. This is an operational separation requirement, **not** an assertion that every individual singleton indicator of `Z₀` must be a permitted effect. It is sufficient, for example, that an admitted set of effects be informationally complete on the differences of the reachable laws. An even stronger sufficient condition is an effect-evaluation map injective on `W`. Under convex mixture closure, a full-rank effect matrix on `U` suffices.

By contrast **(SP)** in OA2 E(b) says `W = ℝ^U`, a state-span condition. It does not assert that `E_CPM` separates these laws. For a mathematical control, let `U={0,1}`, `P={δ₀,δ₁}` (and mixtures), but give the operational fragment only the null and unit effects. Then `W=ℝ²` satisfies SP, while `δ₀` and `δ₁` are operationally indistinguishable and `ι(δ₀) ≠ ι(δ₁)`. Thus SP alone does **not** validate the preparation-NC step for this chosen `ι`. This abstract control is **not** claimed to satisfy OA2's native physical hypotheses, and the trivial operational fragment has a one-point classical model. It diagnoses a logical nonimplication, **not** a counterexample to the theorem's conclusion. It lies outside C-PM's effect class, which contains every post-processing of held non-label records (OA2:114); the instance inside that class is Finding CX-1 (§2.1).

The stronger hypothesis **(LF)** from P0 N5 ensures that the relevant measurement-stage records are jointly held in cells that do not hold preparation-stage labels. It supplies individual C-PM indicators and therefore SEP for the appropriate label-free ontic variable `Z_e`. LF is a **sufficient repair route**, not yet shown necessary for OA2 E(b) or OA3 N4.3. A different set of label-free effects could be separating without including joint singleton indicators.

## 2. OA-2: theorem E(b), proof steps

- **Step 1 / (H4):** One formally joint held history is assumed. This is a retention hypothesis; it does not itself decide which held cells are forbidden preparation labels under C-PM.
- **Steps 2–3:** The affine translation `Z = Z₀ + h(c)` and the formula `ι(s)=law(Z₀)` reproduce statistics for each *admitted* effect. The source argument remains usable as a conditional construction.
- **Step 4, preparation NC:** “because singleton indicators are available” (OA2:534) requires proof that these indicators are available **as measurement effects under C-PM**, rather than just as mathematical post-processings of a joint held history that includes excluded label cells. OA2:112–115 excludes preparation labels; (H4) at OA2:502 asserts joint holding by the held record, label cells included, and not by the label-free cells. Two steps are missing:
  - *Typing of `Z₀`.* If `Z₀` keeps label coordinates, the literal `ι` is preparation-contextual whenever two equivalent preparations differ in a label value, even for a classical fragment [W]: cells `x ∈ H` and `p₁, p₂, r ∈ V` over `F₂`, rule `(x, p₁, p₂, r) ↦ (x, p₁, p₂, r + x)`, preparations with `x = p₁ + p₂`; `(p₁, p₂) = (0,0)` and `(1,1)` agree on every label-free read and receive different `ι`. `Z₀` is therefore the label-free part of `Z − H_c c`.
  - *Availability of its indicators.* Under held-at-use herald typing (OA1:127, :131) or the single-tick effect class (OA2:107–108, record variant (ii) at OA2:805), the stated hypotheses, with (H4) read literally, do not supply the label-free joint indicators, and E(b)'s conclusion fails: Finding CX-1 (§2.1). Under ensemble-conditioned heralds (OA3:415–416) they are supplied. §2.2 states E(b) at the scope where step 4 holds.
- **Step 5, measurement NC:** Once the ontic law `ι` is fixed and effects on `U` are well defined, SP makes operationally equivalent effects equal on `U` (Proposition B(ii) ⇐). That step is distinct from and does **not** repair step 4. The state-span and effect-separation directions must not be confused.
- **Conclusions affected:** the claim of a simplex embedding of the *C-PM prepare–measure fragment* in E(b), the corresponding N2.4 “NO” verdict (OA2:550–560), Corollary E1's generalized form and its contrapositive (OA2:579–592) hold at the scope of §2.2, not under literal (H4) with held-at-use or single-tick effects. The affine translation statement E(a), the restricted result of step 5, and other independent classicality arguments are not challenged by this audit.

### 2.1 Finding CX-1: literal (H4) with retention held only in a label cell [W]

**Instance.** Cells over `F₂`: `x, w ∈ H`; `ℓ, r ∈ V`; `ℓ` is the preparation's setting cell. One rule `M(x, w, ℓ, r) = (x, x + r, ℓ, w + ℓ)`, additive with `k = 0`, bijective with inverse `x = x′, ℓ = ℓ′, r = w′ + x′, w = r′ + ℓ′`. Declared ready cells `w = r = 0` (MP-0a, OA2:388). Preparations `[a, b]`: `x = a`, `ℓ = b`, admitted as point laws; coin mixtures over `b` use `ℓ` as the coin (C-PM, OA2:112–113, as at OA2:449–454). Horizon `T = 2`.

**Trajectory.** `V(0) = (b, 0)`, `V(1) = (b, b)`, `V(2) = (b, a + b)`. `ℓ` is never written. With `u := r(1) = b` and `v := r(2) = a + b`, `(a, b) ↦ (u, v)` is a bijection of `F₂²`.

**Hypotheses of E(b), by their text.**
- (H1) (OA2:499): an additive bijection of a finite abelian group.
- H-AR: `V`'s cells read at full resolution. (H3): one layout, no supplied control. (xiv): no measurement settings, so it holds trivially, as at OA3:423.
- (H4) (OA2:502), literally: `V(2)` determines `r(1) = ℓ(2)` and `r(0) = 0`; `V(1)` determines `r(0)`. C-PM itself calls label cells held records (OA2:114, :451–452), so nothing in the text confines (H4) to the label-free record.
- (SP) (OA2:535–536): `Z = (V(0), V(1), V(2))` and the label-free record `(r(0), r(1), r(2)) = (0, b, a + b)` are both injective in `(a, b)`, so their four point laws span `ℝ⁴`.
- C-PM: `ℓ` is a label cell and is excluded from effects. Finiteness of `S` is the total-finiteness posit (ledger (ii), Main:724). No other premise enters.

**Effects.** Under single-tick or held-at-use typing an effect is a post-processing of the present `V` without `ℓ`, that is, of one `r(τ)`, `τ ≤ 2`. At `τ = 2` the value `b` of `r(1)` is held only in `ℓ`, and every function of `V(2)` without `ℓ` is a function of `a + b`; so "herald `r(1)`, then read `r(2)`" is not available. The available response functions span `{1, [u = 0], [v = 0]}`, of dimension 3 < 4, so Proposition B(i)'s hypothesis, `R` injective on `W` (OA2:244), fails.

**No preparation-noncontextual model.** `A := ½[0,0] + ½[0,1]` gives `(u, v) ∈ {(0,0), (1,1)}` and `B := ½[1,0] + ½[1,1]` gives `(u, v) ∈ {(0,1), (1,0)}`; both have uniform `u` and `v` and `r(0) = 0`, so `A ∼ B` under every available effect. `[u = 0]` and `[v = 0]` are sharp on the four point preparations, so their ontic supports `μ_{uv}` are pairwise disjoint; preparation noncontextuality with convex-linearity gives `½(μ₀₀ + μ₁₁) = ½(μ₀₁ + μ₁₀)`, with disjoint supports on the two sides. ∎ (OA-2 Claim 2's argument, OA2:443–458.) The fragment is a gbit and is not simplex-embeddable, so no other ontic model rescues E(b) in this reading.

**Consequences in this reading.** E(b) (OA2:528–544): every hypothesis holds and the conclusion fails; step 4 is false. E1's generalized form (OA2:579–586) fails, since with no settings the settings trivially translate the joint held record; the contrapositive and its affine sub-bullet (OA2:587–592) fail, and the open-exit list (OA2:600–604) omits this exit. Lemma 1 (OA2:231–234) fails here as well: with `π` = "herald `r(1) = 0`", `π(A) = [0,0]` and `π(B) = [1,0]` are separated by `r(2)`, while `A ∼ B`. The same holds with the preparation made of setting cells only (`x` replaced by a held label cell `p ∈ V`, rule `(p, w, ℓ, r) ↦ (p, p + r, ℓ, w + ℓ)`). The first instance is per horizon: at `T = 3`, `r(3) = a` separates `A` from `B`; at `T ≥ 4`, (H4) fails. In the settings-only variant `p` and `ℓ` stay held, so (H4) holds at every horizon, and only the gbit is per horizon.

**Readings in which CX-1 is classical.** Ensemble-conditioned heralds (OA3:415–416): `[r(1) = 0]·[r(2) = 0]` takes ½ on `A` and 0 on `B`, and the law of the label-free record is a noncontextual model. MP-0b retention of the non-label record (OA1:554–555; record variant (i), OA2:804): `(r(1), r(2))` is held outside `V` and is not a label cell, so it is jointly available. C-PM′ (OA2:119–120): `M` may not read `ℓ` into `r`. Role-typed labels: `ℓ` counts as prepared content once `M` couples it in. Which herald typing E3 uses is open (OA3:124–128).

**Bearing.** The gbit is imposed by the cell-typed label convention, as OA-3 N4.2's is (OA3:450). CX-1 bounds the scope of a mathematical statement under C-PM. It is not a nonclassicality witness, adds no premise, and bears on no physical observer, no native mechanism and not on R-lin with (xiii). Hidden assumption exposed: OA2:115 and :117–118 take excluding label cells to remove no read of the prepared configuration; under (H1) a fixed rule can route a label value into the prepared record. Routing is not needed for literal (H4) [W]: cells `c₀ ∈ V`, `c₁ ∈ H` and static label cells `s_x, s_y ∈ V`, rule `c₀′ = c₁, c₁′ = c₀, s′ = s` (an additive bijection), point preparations `[x, y]` with `s = (x, y)`, `c₀ = x`, `c₁ = y`. The rule never reads a label, so the cells stay labels under role typing as well; `s` determines every earlier read, so (H4) holds literally at every horizon; (SP), (xiv), (H3) and H-AR hold; the label-free reads are `c₀(τ)`, equal to `x` at even and `y` at odd `τ`, one at a time, and `A = ½[00] + ½[11]`, `B = ½[01] + ½[10]` give the same gbit at every horizon `T ≥ 1`. It is classical under ensemble heralds and under MP-0b retention, and fails (H4) under C-PM′. More generally, when label cells that `φ` leaves unchanged determine the initial configuration (point preparations selected by held settings, every other cell declared), literal (H4) holds at every horizon, since the initial configuration determines every later read. Only the witness is shown to yield a gbit. So role-typed labels do not restore literal (H4) in general, and (H4′) is the reading step 4 needs whenever selecting label cells stay held. This is an assumption-watch marker for every result stated under C-PM rather than C-PM′, including 𝔊_K Claim 2's selector-cell step (OA2:451–456).

### 2.2 E(b) and E1 at the corrected scope [W]

- **Label-free record.** `Z^{lf} = (Z_τ^{lf})_{τ ≤ T}`, the restriction of each held record to cells outside the fragment's fixed label-cell set (§1), so that `Z^{lf}`, the effect class and `ι` do not depend on the preparation; `Z₀ := Z^{lf} − H_c^{lf} c`, well defined by Theorem E(a) restricted to those cells.
- **(H4′).** Within the horizon, the C-PM-available part of the held record at the latest read determines every earlier available read. MP-0b retention of the non-label record realizes it; retention held only in label cells does not (CX-1).
- **(SP).** The reachable laws of `Z₀` span `ℝ^{U}`, `U` their support union. It follows from (SP) for the full record, since the pushforward along a coordinate projection is linear and surjective.
- **E(b), held-at-use or single-tick typing.** Assume (H1), H-AR, (H3), (H4′), (xiv), C-PM and (SP). By (H4′) every available effect, heralded composites included, is a post-processing of `Z^{lf}` at the latest read, and the singleton indicators of `Z^{lf}` are post-processings of held non-label records, hence available (OA2:114). So `ι(s)` = law of `Z₀` under `s` is preparation-noncontextual (step 4), step 5 is unchanged, and the prepare–measure fragment is simplex-embeddable.
- **E(b), ensemble-conditioned heralds.** Assume (H1), H-AR, (H3), (xiv), C-PM and (SP). Closure gives the composites `Π_τ [Z_τ^{lf} = z_τ]·(final read)`, the joint indicators of `Z^{lf}`, with (H4) redundant (OA-3 N4.3's route). The same conclusion follows.
- **E1.** The generalized form holds with (H4′) for (H4) and `Z₀` label-free. Its contrapositive reads: keep (H4′), (SP) and (xiv); then a G3 escape requires settings that do not merely translate the joint label-free record. Literal (H4) without (H4′), under held-at-use or single-tick typing, is an exit within (H1) ∧ (H3), convention-imposed under cell-typed C-PM and excluded under C-PM′.

## 3. OA-3: N4.3 and N4.4

OA3 N4.3 replaces (H4) with an effect class (V-ii-H), adding heralded composites `e ∘ π`. The proof says that singleton indicators of the entire formal multi-tick `Z` are available as “heralds at every tick followed by a final read” (OA3:468–469).

The **missing typed step to examine** is whether each such composite is an effect *of the prepared system* under C-PM, with all preparation-label reads removed, rather than an admitted **preparation herald** or an ensemble-conditioned statistic. OA3:410–416 itself separates held-at-use herald typing from ensemble conditioning. With held-at-use typing, OA3:474–475 says V-ii-H is retention-type, but does not on its own prove that all retained history values used for indicators are label-free C-PM effects.

- If a reviewer exhibits a fully specified C-PM measurement-side realization of the needed separating effects for **every admitted preparation**, with no label leakage, N4.3 step 4 is discharged on its stated scope.
- If not, N4.3's proof needs SEP (or a narrower physically justified access class) before its simplex conclusion can be invoked. The proof's step 5 still uses SP on the state side.
- N4.4's assertion that the V-ii-H effect cone is generated by joint indicators requires the same check of which joint indicators are **operationally permitted under C-PM**. “In the mathematical cone of all history functions” and “in the physically allowed effect cone” must not be identified without proof.
- **Scope of N4.3 and N4.4 (§2.2).** With `Z₀` label-free, N4.3 holds under ensemble-conditioned heralds, and under held-at-use heralds when its retention convention is (H4′), for example MP-0b retention of the non-label record; retention held only in label cells does not supply the composites (CX-1). Under ensemble typing the N4.4 cone is generated by the joint indicators of `Z^{lf}`, not by `[Z = z]` with label coordinates. Under held-at-use typing with literal (H4), CX-1's cone is generated by the `u` and `v` marginals alone, and the cones of OA3:477–484 differ.

This is a **dependency gap**, not a claim that all heralded composites are forbidden or that N4.3/N4.4 fail at the scope stated above. Some measurement-side heralds may remain available; the reviewer must classify them by exact preparation/measurement boundary and use time.

## 4. Controls and limits

1. **Do not infer theorem falsity from an unstated step alone.** A different simplex model may exist even when the particular `ι(s)=law(Z₀)` model is not preparation noncontextual. The two-state toy above illustrates this. CX-1 is a different case: it admits no preparation-noncontextual model at all, and it is confined to the readings named in §2.1.
2. **Do not import a counterexample.** P0 N8.2(b)'s additive ring-conveyor gbit violates the R-lin/(xiii) class, and raw (SP) fails. It cannot refute OA2 E(b) or prove that native R-lin admits a gbit. It illustrates why full history and label-free measurement access must be distinguished.
3. **Do not impose LF globally as an axiom.** P0 proves a conditional LF subclass result and returns O-U for the full class. The minimal theorem repair, if needed, may be SEP, a concrete construction, or a more precise reading of C-PM/V-ii-H.
4. **Keep independent results:** OA2 E(a) translation, state-span ⇒ measurement-NC conditional step, and the model's reproduction of allowed effect statistics remain on their prior scope. No statement about all OI observers follows from this audit.
5. **One-way logic only:** a sufficient separation condition yields NC for the candidate model, not its necessity in all simplex embeddings and not a native QM derivation.
