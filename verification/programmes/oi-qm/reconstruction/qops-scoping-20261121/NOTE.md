## 0. Summary

1. **What OI derives (§1).** OI derives a representation layer: every finite observable law has
   an exact fixed-basis unitary and Born representation, `S ⇔ D ⇔ Q_fb` [P, partly K]. For a
   bounded experiment, a reversible realization reproduces any finite quantum instrument family to
   any finite accuracy, given the quantum data as input [P]. The corpus derives no native
   coherent operation (`GR:269`); this is an absence of derivation, not an impossibility. In the
   kernel, the theory generated by the substratum class on `Fin 2` has no composite unitary
   control (`StructuralClosure.lean:330`). A theory generated by a monomial class executes no
   layer flow at time one half (`LiftAudit.lean:200`) [K]. The substratum class contains every
   `permMatrix σ * diagonal d`, and its theory has `PhasesAvailable` (`RouteB.lean:297`
   `substratumTheory_derivedOI`), so both no-gos hold with the phase intervention stated. Exact finite operational quantum
   mechanics is characterized relative to named conditions (five completion conditions; the
   layer-flow form), and none of those conditions is supplied by the stated substratum dynamics
   with observer access (`Main:564`, `GR:269`) [P, K].
2. **Gap list (§2).** Against the ingredients scalable quantum computation needs:
   - *Assumed:* qubit encoding, the clock, efficient measurement and the error model. These are
     the hypotheses of the conditional BQP model, `Comp:94`.
   - *Absent as native mechanisms:* coherent preparation, a coherent mixer, phases, a
     non-Clifford resource, selective classical control, and entropy removal for fault
     tolerance.
   - *Derived only at the representation level:* the Born form and passive record persistence.
     The persistence is not shown for localized, accessible, re-armable records (MQ, AW-MQ-2).
3. **Smallest candidate mechanisms (§3), all hypotheses.**
   - *Gates and interference (H-QOPS-G).* An executable continuous layer flow of one native
     involution, with no phase intervention, read for encoded universality only. The corpus
     needs the flow together with the stated phase intervention to obtain *exact* operational QM
     (`GR:265–267`). Scalable computation needs less: approximation, and an encoded subspace.
     Saturation of the flow algebra on orbit complements is generic (BQ). T1 therefore measures
     the orbit and commutant structure of the native gates, which saturation does not decide.
   - *Preparation and fault tolerance (H-QOPS-S).* A non-invariant entropy sink or supply. PR1
     shows that reversible native dynamics creates no preparation supply in law. Fault tolerance
     needs entropy removal at a positive rate [LIT-R]. Either fresh ancillas (ABIN 1996) or
     non-unital noise (Ben-Or, Gottesman and Hassidim 2013) can supply it.
   - *Measurement (H-QOPS-M).* A bounded breach acting as a latch, the AW-MQ-2 task. It shares
     its node, the bounded breach, with NU's decisive question for Q.
   - *Control (H-QOPS-C).* Native universality (NU) or a supplied program register.
4. **Dependencies (§4).** A positive NATIVE-UNIV answer for Q is necessary for native classical
   control, but it cannot by itself supply interference: a universal permutation automaton stays
   in a monomial class. NU answers Goal A positively at sketch level only, through Margolus's
   comparison rule. A universal rule in OI's update form does not establish universality of OI's
   native rule. The BQP lower bound (BQ item I2) acquires native support for a hypothesis
   only where an H-QOPS item is established. The upper bound I3 and the width ceiling I6 do not
   depend on QOPS.
5. **Assumption-watch markers (§5).**
   - AW-QOPS-1: exact unitary control and computational universality are different targets.
   - AW-QOPS-2: fault tolerance and the preparation regress share one obligation, a
     non-invariant entropy sink or supply.
   - AW-QOPS-3: the realization theorem is a classical simulation with supplied data.
   - AW-QOPS-4: native layer flows annihilate the indicator of every orbit of the native gates.

   Their classifications are proposals (§5).

## 3. Minimal candidate mechanisms and the cheapest decisive tests (plans only)

Each item is a **hypothesis**. It is stated with the results it must preserve, which become its
controls (§A.29), and with a test, a decision rule and controls fixed before any run. No test
here is authorized to run. Each would need its own owner authorization, its own preregistration
commit before its script, and an independent review.

### 3.1 H-QOPS-G: gates and interference from one executable layer flow, without a phase

**Hypothesis.** The physical dynamics makes the continuous flow `unit g t` (G2) executable for
the native shear and swap gates of a finite region, at intermediate `t`. It supplies no relative
phase.

**Why it is the smallest candidate [W].** G4 shows that the monomial class executes no layer flow
at `t = 1/2`. Any coherent mixer therefore needs at least a non-monomial operation. For a
permutation involution `g` that moves `a` to `b`, `unit g t` has entries
`(1 + e^{iπt})/2` at `(a, a)` and `(1 − e^{iπt})/2` at `(a, b)`. Both are nonzero for
`0 < t < 1`, so `unit g t` is non-monomial. G5 shows that the layer flow *together with* the stated
phase intervention gives exact QM. The phase intervention is a further assumption on the class
(`GR:267`). The computational question needs strictly less than G5's conclusion: approximate
gates on an encoded subspace. G6's `not_hControl_evenCycle` shows that a real antisymmetric
algebra fails *exact* control. [LIT2] Real-amplitude quantum computation is BQP-universal
(Bernstein–Vazirani 1997), and Toffoli together with a real basis-changing single-qubit gate is
computationally universal (Shi 2003; Aharonov 2003). So exact-control obstructions do not decide
the computational question (AW-QOPS-1).

**What it must preserve (controls).**
- G1 (the native step at `t = 1`).

### 3.2 H-QOPS-S: a non-invariant entropy sink or supply, for preparation and fault tolerance

**Hypothesis.** The computing register has access to entropy removal at a positive rate, in one of
two forms:
- (S1) a non-invariant low-entropy supply, such as a boundary or time-slice law with an incoming
  stream (PR1's NEQ-T or NEQ-O), delivering fresh ancillas;
- (S2) an effective non-unital channel on the visible register, arising from the trace-out, with a
  fixed point other than the maximally mixed state and a strength below a threshold.

The same supply would serve fiducial preparation.

**Literature [LIT-R].**
- *Aharonov, Ben-Or, Impagliazzo and Nisan, "Limitations of Noisy Reversible Computation"*
  (arXiv:quant-ph/9611028). Abstract read; the main text was not re-read.
  - In noisy reversible circuits, each wire is flipped with probability `p` at each time step,
    and all inputs are present at time 0. Any such circuit must have size exponential in its depth
    to compute a function with high probability.
  - For quantum circuits: any noisy circuit that is "not worthless", and for which all inputs are
    present at time 0, must have size exponential in its depth. The abstract adds: "fault tolerant
    QC must use a constant supply of inputs all the time".
- *Ben-Or, Gottesman and Hassidim, "Quantum Refrigerator"* (arXiv:1301.1995). Abstract, Theorem 1.1
  and Theorem 6.1 read.
  - Setting: no fresh ancillas during the computation; noise `C` on each qubit at each step.
  - Theorem 1.1 (Main Theorem), for a non-unitary channel `C` close to the identity, by the limit
    of repeated `C` up to unitary equivalence:
    - if the limit is the centre of the Bloch sphere (depolarizing), computation is possible for
      `Õ(log n)` steps, tight up to `log log n` factors;
    - if the limit is a diameter (dephasing), `O(n^a)` qubits can compute for `O(n^b)` steps when
      `a + b < 1`, and a single unknown qubit cannot be stored beyond `O(n³)` steps;
    - if the limit is a point other than the centre (amplitude damping), computation is possible
      for an exponential number of steps.
  - Theorem 6.1: for any single-qubit `P ≠ I/2` there is a threshold `p_T` such that, for any
    non-unital qubit channel `C` with `‖C − I‖_⋄ < p_T` and fixed point `P`, any computation of
    width `n` and depth `D` can be performed with `O(n polylog(nD))` physical qubits and depth
    `O(D polylog(nD))`. The paper notes that the theorem still holds with nearest-neighbour gates
    in two dimensions.
  - The authors characterize the classes by entropy. Depolarizing noise raises the entropy of
    every state, dephasing noise does not lower it, and amplitude-damping noise can lower it,
    "we can use the noise itself to cool the ancilla qubits".
- The independent review also cites Shtanko and Sharma (arXiv:2411.04819) as extending this to
  geometrically local circuits. That paper was not read for this note.

**Consequence [W, conditional on the literature above and on a noise model].** Scalable
fault-tolerant computation needs entropy removal from the register at a positive rate. Fresh
ancillas (ABIN) and non-unital noise (BGH) are two ways to supply it. Fresh ancillas are not
necessary.

**In OI [W].**
- P1 excludes a net preparation supply into a fixed block from native history under any invariant
  law.
- S1 is one of P1's listed escapes, a boundary law or stream.
- S2 is not a native supply either. It redistributes a low-entropy resource that the law, the
  boundary or the hidden sector must already hold. P1 classes that as redistribution of a supplied
  resource, which it permits.
- Whether OI's trace-out yields a non-unital effective channel on a register is open. `Comp:140`
  says the frozen-bath term of `𝒟` is set by the spread of the bath-conditioned Hamiltonians. If
  that term is a mixture of unitary evolutions, it is unital: a random-unitary channel fixes
  `I/2`. S2 would then need a different, dissipative part. This reading has not been checked
  against Main §3.2, and T3 step 3 checks it.

**Controls.** PR1's exclusion lemma and budget, which are to be preserved. AW-MQ-3: the background
is a choice within an open set, priced at 32 bits per site.
