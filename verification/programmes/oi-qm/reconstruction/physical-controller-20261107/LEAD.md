## 3. The concrete lead screened: state-dependent ring loading

### 3.1 Why this is a physical lead rather than a new gate

[SM §3.1 L104–118](https://github.com/amaybaum/incompleteness/blob/b1d782f954f02cec578a10e071b73e48f86eb3dd/papers/SM.md#L104) already proposes a graph responding
to the field state. Its explicit ring example chooses the right neighbor
`i+2` when `x_(i+1)=0`, otherwise `i+1`, with left neighbor `i-1`.
This source motivation predates the measurement target. The selected binary
specialization and two-tick component are in
[physical-interface §§2–5](https://github.com/amaybaum/incompleteness/blob/b1d782f954f02cec578a10e071b73e48f86eb3dd/verification/programmes/oi-qm/reconstruction/oi-qm-ext-1/physical-interface.md#L41),
blob `3229ef3cd234e613a4b0cb63bab548e0fec666a9`.
Only that explicit rule is used, not SM's wider graph-regularity, isotropy or
continuum assertions.

For `L>=9`, the existing component writes

    f_i(u) = u_(i-1) XOR (u_(i+1) OR u_(i+2)),
    F(u,v) = (f(u) XOR v,u).

There is one fixed F for both settings. The binary OR comes from the
already specified neighbor selection; it is not a new controlled opcode.
The rule has an explicit inverse. Its reversible state-dependent response is
a genuine positive local coupling, conditional on the stated source.
It is distinct from the fixed linear wave and from Native A's three-bit rule.
There is no common-model bridge identifying their controllers.

### 3.2 The exact setting-loading stop

[Preparation-loading §§3–4](https://github.com/amaybaum/incompleteness/blob/b1d782f954f02cec578a10e071b73e48f86eb3dd/verification/programmes/oi-qm/reconstruction/oi-qm-ext-1/preparation-loading.md#L108),
blob `1802ea3c335eb7457739fa5af13b3aefd0c603af`, gives the full conditional
loader. At the incoming cut, write `W_(-1)=(X,Y)`. Its visible request
root is

    X_0=X_1=Y_0=0, Y_1=c.

Its **supplied common hidden source eta**, shared by both requests, requires

    X_(-1)=X_2=X_3=0,
    Y_(-2)=X_(-3), Y_(-1)=X_(-2),
    Y_3=X_4 OR X_5,
    Y_4=X_5 OR X_6,
    Y_5=X_4 XOR (X_6 OR X_7),

with the remaining X/Y fields independent fair bits, including free
`Y_2`, and `Z=Y_2 XOR X_4`. These are the existing paper's hypotheses,
reproduced here to identify the missing input precisely, not a new
preparation proposal.

Given this root and eta, one ordinary forward F tick loads the requested
controller value and yields exactly the prior component's common carrier
preparation law. Changing only `Y_1` changes only the loaded program
coordinate at that cut, pointwise. This is a useful common-preparation
transport identity. The operation that creates/selects `Y_1`, with the
uncontrolled source held fixed, remains outside that identity.

The paper's canonical-root countercontrol gives

    loaded A_1 = c XOR (X_2 OR X_3).

Thus a visible request alone with unprepared uniform hidden fields does not
guarantee the requested load; its match probability is `1/4` under that
specific counting source. The eight hidden precursor constraints have
conditional mass `2^-8`; the full root-plus-resource event has mass
`2^-12`. These are conditional ensemble masses, not herald or factory
success probabilities. At a fixed time F preserves counting; the calculation
transports the readiness resource rather than manufacturing it. The count
does not cover adaptive stopping, an ordered reservoir, an enlarged
apparatus or another independently sourced initial law.

**Selection stop:** no new native device or independent physical source for
this complete ready/request precursor was identified in the inspected
sources. The first localized sub-obligation is a physical producer of the
quiet control neighbors `X_2=X_3=0` while `Y_1` remains selectable with
the same uncontrolled source. Those two zeros alone would still not supply
the other six constraints, request generator, records or a complete apparatus.
Taking the inverse precursor of a desired target would repeat the same
unsourced input.

### 3.3 Knowledge, timing, failures and outgoing state already visible

The existing paper declares complete two-slice receiver/controller access at
sites 0 and 1, not receiver-only access. The request is present at `t=-1`,
loaded at `t=0`; the interaction follows at ticks 1 and 2. At those later
ticks its controller is

    C_1=(Z,c), C_2=(c,Z).

The controller learns Z in either setting. The constant receiver history
for `c=1` does not hide Z from the whole declared observer. If an actual
preparer reads both `X_4` and `Y_2`, its retained records may expose Z
even earlier; the conditional source law does not require such a read, so
that exposure is a requirement to check, not an asserted unavoidable fact.

[Preparation-loading §§6–7](https://github.com/amaybaum/incompleteness/blob/b1d782f954f02cec578a10e071b73e48f86eb3dd/verification/programmes/oi-qm/reconstruction/oi-qm-ext-1/preparation-loading.md#L290)
retains the entire native outgoing state and correlations, and the complement
of the ready event. It supplies no accessible acceptance flag for that
event. Every field has an F successor; failure of the desired preparation
contract is not undefined dynamics. At `t=2`, `Z=1` violates the initial
controller previous-slot zero, while `c=1` violates the receiver
previous-slot zero. No automatic reset follows. A different compatible
next-stage contract could be useful, but still needs a construction.

These are conditional source-paper results, not a new timing/failure
derivation of a physically sourced controller. The factory's attempt count,
clock, failure/no-read law, physical logging, consumed resource and renewal
remain unsupplied.
