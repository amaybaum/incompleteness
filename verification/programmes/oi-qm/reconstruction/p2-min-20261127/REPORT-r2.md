### 3.1 Lemmas

**Lemma P (P-here, from cited results).**
1. **Settings.** Lemma A (stated on F_q^N; its transfer to ℤ^d is P1-11, P-here) has three
   hypotheses: one fixed affine bijection; the setting in controller coordinates at time 0; and the
   rest of the initial law independent of the setting (AP1 C1). Any setting-independent background
   law meets them. So the settings part of SET/M is BLOCKED for x5, x7 and x8, at any q.
   Applying Lemma A to x6 on the real lift is a further short transfer, which is not written; it is
   stated as such.
2. **NC at odd q, affine rule (x5, x7, x8).**
   - In Gross's frame R-W, every classical probability law has a nonnegative Wigner function.
   - Affine symplectic maps permute phase points.
   - Appending a cell with a nonnegative law and marginalizing keep the Wigner function
     nonnegative: R-W factors over cells (W3, W1).
   - So no Wigner-negative state arises.
   - Any unitary channel a protocol realizes keeps every nonnegative-Wigner input nonnegative, so it
     is Clifford (W6). The Clifford group is finite at fixed n, so there is no infinite-order encoded
     unitary.
   - This reaches every NC target as defined. It does not need Proposition C, so it also covers x7,
     whose non-algebraic laws may have R-W images outside Theorem W's scope.
   - The scope is windowed, as P1-12.
3. **Scope of Proposition AS.** Proposition AS concerns an ontic bijection's action on the **full**
   space of signed laws: that action is CP iff the bijection is affine symplectic. It is silent on
   actions restricted by ancillas. Item 2 is the argument the NC cells use.

**Lemma E (P, C-ENV D1 R3, with standard facts).** F and F⁻¹ are sliding block codes, so
h(F_*μ) = h(μ) for every translation-invariant μ, and weak limits do not lower h. A law of density
h₀ arises only from a law of the same density.

**Lemma U (uniform current slice; P-here).** Let q be prime and α ≠ 0. Let R be a time reversal of F
in one of these classes:
- R ∈ {F^k T, F^k(−T)}, with T(u, v) = (v, u) in (current, previous) coordinates, so that
  T F T = F⁻¹;
- or a composition of these with translations that keeps the fixed set translation-invariant.

Let μ be the Haar law of Fix(R). Then at every time the current slice's marginal on every finite
window W is uniform. A register value a added at t₀ to the current slice and read through the
current slice satisfies I(a; w_t) = 0 for all t.

*Proof.*
1. **The current slice is one Laurent polynomial applied to an iid field.**
   - For R = T: Fix(T) = {u = v}, so x(t₀) = x(t₀ − 1) = u with u iid uniform. The recursion gives
     x(t₀ + s) = Q_s(αS)u, with Q_0 = Q_{−1} = 1 and Q_{s+1}(y) = yQ_s(y) − Q_{s−1}(y). Q_s is monic
     of degree s, and the same holds for s < 0 by symmetry.
   - The leading monomial of Q_s(αS) is α^s x_1^s, which is nonzero. So the current slice is a
     nonzero Laurent polynomial f_s applied to an iid uniform field.
   - For −T and FT the argument is the same. For −T, x(t₀ − 1) = −u. For FT, the current slice
     carries a free iid component: u is free at q = 2, and u = (α/2)Sv at odd q.
2. **A nonzero Laurent polynomial f applied to an iid uniform field has uniform window marginals.**
   - Choose a weight ℓ that has a unique maximizer w* on supp f.
   - The map from the variables u_{s−w*}, for s ∈ W, to y|_W is triangular in ℓ-order, with
     diagonal f_{w*} ≠ 0. It is therefore surjective onto F_q^W.
3. **Masking.** The window content is a·g_t + h_t with h_t uniform on F_q^W and independent of a.
   So g_t ∈ H = F_q^W, and I = 0 by Lemma 8.1. ∎

### 3.4 x6: KVP

**Q0 (G): fails (P-here).**
- At finite t a stationary law arises only from itself, since F is a bijection.
- Limits. Per mode the second moments rotate by tθ(k). Their Cesàro limit is the phase average,
  which per mode is a J-invariant form λ(k)·g_J(k)⁻¹ with λ(k) = ½ tr(g_J(k) C₀(k)). This needs
  second moments bounded uniformly in t, which holds because E is conserved and coercive for
  abs(αμ) < 1/d.
- If a weak limit of F^t_*μ₀, or of its averages, exists with uniformly integrable second moments,
  its covariance is that phase average. KVP needs λ(k) constant in k.
- The corpus's candidate initial laws on the real lift give non-constant or zero λ:
  - white noise gives the thermal law E⁻¹, per the reviewer's computation (I + JJᵀ)/2;
  - the thermal law E⁻¹ is a fixed point;
  - the vacuum gives 0.
- An initial law with constant λ would be chosen by reference to g_J, which is the target.
- No convergence theorem for this discrete map is used. Dudnikova, Komech and Spohn (L) treat
  continuous-time harmonic crystals and are cited only as context, outside their scope.
- The r1 wording "conserves every quadratic density's mean" is corrected: only the means of
  conserved quadratic forms are conserved.

**Q0 (S): fails.** Maximal entropy at a fixed mean of a conserved quadratic Q gives covariance
∝ Q⁻¹. The conserved forms include E, g_J, SM:296's Q_B and P_C, and lane 2's forms ω_B. Choosing
g_J is the vacuum choice, which is target-motivated, and the scale c is free.
