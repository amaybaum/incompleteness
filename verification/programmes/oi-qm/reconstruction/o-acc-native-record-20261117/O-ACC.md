## 3. Analysis

### 3.1 Time indices and the selected channel

Set `X=x(t-1)`, `Y=x(t)`, `Z=x(t+1)`. The proposed earlier message is `M=m(Y)`.
At the later read, Y is the previous slice and J selects sites of Z. In particular, J is not
required to be a function of Z, which is the restriction that leaves this case outside P2-G's
current-slice readout argument. Conversely, this unit does not cover M that also depends on X,
selection after inspecting Z, or a history register containing information beyond Y.

The later state does contain Y as its first coordinate. That exact carry is an algebraic fact;
neither accessible Y nor an apparatus executing `g_s(Y)` follows from it. The theorem below
tests a supplied selector's information channels before granting any physical interpretation.

### 3.2 Conditional value law and both recovery directions

**Proposition 1 (finite case).** Under H1, H2-F and H3, for every fixed s,

\[
\Pr(M=m,J=j,U=u\mid s)=\Pr(M=m,J=j\mid s)q^{-k}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here `|s` denotes the separately defined test law for a fixed label, not conditioning on an
unspecified random apparatus setting. Thus U is independent of the pair `(M,J)` in that law.

**Proof.** For a fixed y, the map `x -> Ay-x` is a bijection of `Q^Lambda`, with inverse
`x=Ay-z`. Since X is uniform and independent of Y, Z conditional on `Y=y` is uniform on
`Q^Lambda`. In particular, all k coordinates at the distinct sites of `V(g_s(y))` have
the joint law `q^(-k)`. The tuple positions do not reveal a different alphabet or length for
different j. Summing this constant conditional probability over y with `m(y)=m` and
`g_s(y)=j` proves (1). The same argument shows the pair `(Y,Z)` has the independent uniform
law; it does not establish that nature selects this law. No property of A beyond its being
the fixed, defined map of H1 is needed for the subtraction argument. Q need not be a field.

Proposition 1 is the joint-law form of the independence of consecutive slices under the product
law (the A-prime observation recorded in the earlier O-ACC audit, section 3, for finite S).
What this unit adds is the address/value decomposition and Proposition 2 for previous-slice
selection, the infinite-cylinder form of section 3.3, and the false-positive control (3).

**Proposition 2 (address recovery).** Under the same hypotheses, exact recovery of M from
`(J,U)` is possible if and only if exact recovery from J alone is possible, for each fixed s.

**Forward direction.** Suppose `d_s(J,U)=M` almost surely. For any j with positive probability,
every u has conditional probability `q^(-k)>0`, for every m with
`Pr(M=m,J=j|s)>0`. If two different such messages existed at that j, the same `d_s(j,u)`
would have to equal both. Therefore there is a unique message at each supported j. Define
`h_s(j)` to be that message (arbitrarily elsewhere). Then `M=h_s(J)` almost surely.

**Reverse direction.** If `M=h_s(J)` almost surely, use `d_s(j,u)=h_s(j)`, ignoring u.
This proves exact recovery without adding a value channel.

More generally, for every supported j and every u,

\[
\Pr(M=m\mid J=j,U=u,s)=\Pr(M=m\mid J=j,s).
\tag{2}
\]

This follows by dividing (1) by `Pr(J=j|s)q^(-k)`. Thus observing U cannot improve any
message estimate beyond the information in J under this law; the result is stronger than
merely denying perfect recovery. If the readout gives only U, its message law is the prior
law for that setting. A nonconstant M cannot be recovered exactly from U alone. A constant M
is trivially decodable and is not a record of distinguishable alternatives.

Addresses include any visible site identity, tuple ordering, region shape or other label from
which j can be inferred. Representing a labelled field as an unlabelled vector on paper does
not remove that information physically. If an apparatus exposes additional labels, they must
be included in J and meet H3 before (1)-(2) can be used. This theorem does **not** prove that
the address cannot be a native record; it identifies the address as the entire candidate
record channel within this subclass and leaves its physical write, hold and read to H6.

### 3.3 Infinite carrier without A1

**Proposition 3 (finite-output extension).** On `Lambda=Z^d`, take H2-I in place of H2-F.
The unchanged local neighbour sum in H1 defines `(AY)_i` at every site. For each fixed j,y,
the event `U=u` requires

\[
X_{V(j)}=(Ay)_{V(j)}-u.
\]

Its X-probability is `q^(-k)` by the product law and distinctness of the sites. Integrating
over the Y-event `{m(Y)=m, g_s(Y)=j}` gives exactly (1). This integration uses only a finite
cylinder of X for each j, not the probability of an individual infinite configuration.
The recovery proofs and (2) consequently apply unchanged. No finite total S, global counting
law, finite recurrence period or finite-shell surrogate is used in this extension. H2-I is
still a supplied measure, and the functions in H3 are still supplied reads, not derived
finite observers. A1 is therefore unnecessary for this conditional constraint; its physical
inputs remain unresolved. No assertion about infinite-lattice ergodicity is needed.

### 3.4 Two policies and the false-positive test

For a manual algebraic countercontrol, use the same rule at `q=2`, take a site i and two
distinct read sites `a_0,a_1`, and set `M=Y_i`. Let the two test policies be
`J_s=M xor s` for `s=0,1`, with `V(j)=(a_j)`. These are specified functions, not two native
selectable operations. Both finite and infinite product versions give

\[
\Pr(J=j,U=u\mid M=m,s)=
\begin{cases}
1/2,&j=m\mathbin{\oplus}s,\\
0,&j\ne m\mathbin{\oplus}s.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]

An analyst who can read the address recovers `M=J xor s` perfectly; an analyst given only U
sees a fair bit for either message. There has been no constructed native write, address
register, moving observer or public read. Equation (3) is an explicit way an already supplied
message can appear to have been measured simply by choosing and reporting a site. It is
**CONVENTION** as an asserted measurement procedure, with native implementation **UNSOURCED**.
It is not a no-go for a future physical apparatus whose dynamics actually writes such an address.

For a genuine random setting S, the same proof works conditional on S only under H5 (or another
proved condition giving the same conditional law of X). Independence of S from `(X,Y)` is
sufficient. Correlation between S and M must be retained: if `S=M`, the setting already discloses
the message even with constant J. That disclosure is not evidence of measurement or retention.
If S carries information about X, H5 can fail and none of the conditional-independence conclusions
is licensed merely by conditioning on a displayed setting. No law for physical S is supplied here.
