# C-SPH-1: Poisson reference construction and fixed-pool coupling bound

> **DRAFT — NOT FROZEN.** Owner direction (2026-10-22):
> - **Target:** bounded approximate equivalence with fixed-size pools.
> - **Mathematical reference:** the Poisson model.
>
> Poisson statistics are not derived from OI. They enter as a reference construction [CAL]. Exact reconstruction (A1)
> is a separate target. Component checks are in `reference_checks.py`; the witness is in `herald_checks.py`.

## Setting

**Stations.** The stations and the readout are those of COLLECTOR.md §1, with m = 8 stations S_w. There are two pool
variants:
- **(P_μ) Poisson reference.** Each station's particle count is Poisson(μ). Counts are independent across
  stations.
- **(B_N) Fixed pools.** Each station holds exactly N particles.

In both variants, the particles' states are independent given the counts:
- depth z ~ U[0, L);
- transverse position uniform on the torus;
- direction uniform on S² [ADD];
- common speed v.

**Aperture and horizon.**
- **Aperture.** A crossing passes the aperture with probability η, independently of (crossing time, direction),
  because transverse positions are uniform.
- **Horizon.** T ≤ L/v.
- **Crossing probability.** q = η·vT/(4L) is one particle's probability of an aperture crossing within the horizon,
  and Λ = μq (or Nq).

**Admissible protocols Π_T.** Protocols are sequential, with at most K calls. At each step, given the visible
record so far and independent protocol randomness, the protocol does one of two things:
- **Call a station** at a time t no earlier than the last record, with a window (t, t + τ] ending by T. The herald
  is the first aperture arrival strictly after t. A repeat call therefore cannot select the same crossing twice.
- **Read the current carrier along a menu axis a.** This gives s = sign(a·λ) at the fixed delay Δ, and the carrier
  is absorbed. The instrument's update is the routed call to S_{sa}, whose address is determined by the visible
  (s, a).

**Preparations.** A preparation procedure selects its station internally, by a coin, and calls it. It does so on a
schedule (t, τ) that is a fixed function of the protocol step, so the schedule does not depend on the coin.

**Visible record.** The observer sees exactly these fields:
- **Preparation calls:** (t, τ), the success bit and the exact crossing time. The station address and the coin are
  not included.
- **Routed calls:** (t, τ), the success bit and the exact crossing time. The address sa is a function of visible
  fields.
- **Probe calls:** calls the protocol makes to a station of its own choice, at (w, t, τ). They show the success bit
  and the exact crossing time.
- **Reads:** (a, s).

**What is not visible.**
- **Hidden state:** absorber states, preparation addresses and coins.
- **Readout:** ideal, i.e. the point-aperture limit with continuous directions.
- **Status of the restriction.** Hiding the preparation address is an access assumption. The Poisson theorem does not
  supply it: if the address were visible, Z-mix and X-mix would have disjoint records, at TV distance 1.

## Reference theorem (P_μ)

**Lemma 1 (marking).** In each station, aperture crossings during [0, T] form a Poisson process on [0, T] × S². Its
intensity is (Λ/T) dt ⊗ μ_w(dλ).
- **Proof.** The particles form a Poisson process. Mapping each particle to its crossing and restricting to aperture
  crossings within [0, T] gives a Poisson process whose intensity is μη·(vc/L)·1[t < L/(vc)] dt dΩ/4π. For
  t ≤ T ≤ L/v, the indicator equals 1 for every c > 0. The intensity is then (μηv/4L) dt · (c₊/π) dΩ.
- **Check.** V1 checks it symbolically.

**Lemma 2 (independence).** The m station processes are independent. This needs independent counts and independent
particle states. Poisson marginal counts alone do not suffice, and conditioning on a fixed total count reintroduces
correlations.

**Lemma 3 (record as a function).** Under any π ∈ Π_T, the visible record is a measurable function of three things:
- the station processes on [0, T];
- the protocol randomness;
- the preparation coin.

**Why it holds.**
- The herald of a call (w, t, τ) is the first point of Φ_w in (t, t + τ], strictly after t.
- Points arriving while the shutter is closed are absorbed.
- Each particle crosses at most once.
- Directions enter the record only through outcomes.
- Hidden fields (addresses of preparation calls, coins) are dropped, which is a further measurable map.

**Lemma 4 (strong Markov).** Every decision time is a stopping time of the filtration generated by the processes up
to time t, the protocol randomness and the coin. At such a time, the joint marked Poisson process restricted to the
future is again Poisson with the same intensity, and independent of the past. This is the standard independent-
increment property of a Poisson random measure with intensity dt ⊗ ν.

**Theorem R.** Assume (P_μ), ideal readout, the routing as specified, and the visible record and access
restrictions above. Take any protocol π ∈ Π_T, write r = Λ/T, and let H be the visible history before a call
(w, t, τ) that π selects. Then:
- **Failure.** P(F | H, w, t, τ) = e^(−rτ).
- **Success.** P(U ∈ du, λ ∈ dλ, success | H, w, t, τ) = r·e^(−ru)·1[0 < u < τ] du μ_w(dλ). Here U is the waiting
  time after t, and λ is the new carrier's direction.

**What the history can and cannot affect.**
- It enters only through the selected call parameters (w, t, τ).
- Waiting-time statistics are the same for every station, whether or not it has fired before.
- The direction law depends on w.
- A read of a carrier from S_w gives s with probability (1 + s·a·w)/2.

**Corollary.** Consider a preparation procedure that uses station w with probability p_w on a coin-independent
schedule. Its visible-record law depends on the procedure only through Σ p_w·w, because the only factor that depends
on w is affine in w. Procedures representing the same quantum state are therefore exactly indistinguishable by every
π ∈ Π_T, failures and exact timestamps included.

**Proof.** Combine Lemmas 1–4 and induct over the record's steps.

**Scope.**
- The theorem concerns the reference model, not OI.
- It is conditional on isotropy, independent pools and the declared access restrictions.
- Finite-aperture readout and direction discretization need their own bounds.

## Coupling bound (B_N)

**Theorem C.**
- **Per-particle coupling.** In (B_N), each particle contributes a point with probability q. Given that it does, the
  point's law is Unif[0, T] ⊗ μ_w, which is the conditional law of a Poisson(q·ν) point.
- **Per-particle distance.** TV(Bernoulli(q), Poisson(q)) = q(1 − e^(−q)), which is at most q² (V2).
- **Summing.** Summing over particles and stations, the arrival records satisfy
  TV((B_N) arrival records, (P_N) arrival records) ≤ mNq² = mΛ²/N.
- **Data processing.** Every admissible transcript is the same function (Lemma 3) of these records, together with
  identical independent randomness. So the transcript law is within mΛ²/N of the reference law.
- **Two equivalent preparations.** For two procedures representing the same state,
  d_TV(P_N⁽¹⁾, P_N⁽²⁾) ≤ min(1, 2mΛ²/N), which is min(1, 16Λ²/N) for m = 8.
- **Coverage.** The bound covers every admissible bounded adaptive protocol within the horizon, failures and exact
  timestamps included.

**Checks.**
- V3 checks TV(Binomial(N, q), Poisson(Nq)) ≤ Nq² with interval arithmetic on 42 grid points. This is the
  per-station distance of the full arrival records, since the conditional point laws coincide.
- V4 checks that protocol W's exact fixed-pool difference stays within min(1, 16Λ²/N) in 24 cases. The largest
  ratio is 0.20, so the bound is conservative.

## First numerical target (owner proposal; arithmetic certified in `freeze_checks.py`)

**Proposed values.**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Horizon | T = L/v |
| Calls | at most K = 8, one per fixed slot |
| Readout delay | Δ = T/100, with maximum flight time below Δ |
| Call window | τ = 23T/200, so that K(τ + Δ) = T exactly |
| Reference failure per attempted call | e^(−rτ) = 0.05 |
| Expected aperture crossings per station | Λ = (200/23) ln 20 ≈ 26.04985 |
| Fixed pool | N = 1,100,000 per station. The minimum for the pool term to stay below 0.01 is 1,085,752; the earlier 920,000 figure left no latency allowance |
| Aperture fraction | η = 4Λ/N ≈ 9.4727·10⁻⁵ |
| Pairwise transcript-error target | 0.03: 0.01 each to pools, aperture and directions |

**Accounting, all in the pairwise transcript TV metric.**
- **Pools.** The term is 16Λ²/N ≈ 0.0098705.
- **Aperture.**
  - The discrepancy is at most δ_ap = 4ρ/(d − ρ) per read. Over at most R = 8 reads it contributes at most 2Rδ_ap.
  - So ρ/d ≤ 1/6401 meets 0.01. At that value δ_ap = 1/1600.
- **Directions.**
  - **For the checker's model.** The read law is analytic: (1 + s·a·w)/2, read off continuous directions. The
    direction term is then 0.
  - **For a finite carrier set.** The same accounting requires a per-read outcome error of at most 1/1600. The
    rational spheres measured so far do not reach it (fast-band errors between 0.002 and 0.008, and erratic in N).
  - **Owner decision.** Whether to fix a finite-carrier rule now, or to accept the checker on the analytic read
    law.

**Qualifications.**
- **The 5% failure rate is a Poisson-reference guarantee.** It is not a conditional guarantee after every fixed-pool
  history. The coupling controls unconditional transcript events, so failures stay in the acceptance metric, and the
  completion probability is reported separately.
- **Timestamps stay continuous in the checker's model.** Results come by analytic integration or certified bounds.
  A rounded timestamp law compared directly with a continuous one is at TV distance 1.
- **The existing depth-3 D6 tests** and their terminal-state checks are kept.
