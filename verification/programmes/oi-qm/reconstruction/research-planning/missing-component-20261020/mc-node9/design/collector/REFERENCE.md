## Setting

**Stations.** The stations and the readout are those of COLLECTOR.md §1, with m = 8 stations S_w. There are two pool
variants:
- **(P_μ) Poisson reference.** Each station's particle count is Poisson(μ). Counts are independent across
  stations.
- **(B_N) Fixed pools.** Each station holds exactly N particles.

In both variants, the particles' states are independent given the counts:
- depth z ~ U[0, L);
- transverse position uniform on the torus;
- direction uniform on S² [ADD];
- common speed v.

**Aperture and horizon.**
- **Aperture.** A crossing passes the aperture with probability η, independently of (crossing time, direction),
  because transverse positions are uniform.
- **Horizon.** T ≤ L/v.
- **Crossing probability.** q = η·vT/(4L) is one particle's probability of an aperture crossing within the horizon,
  and Λ = μq (or Nq).

**Admissible protocols Π_T.** Protocols are sequential. At each step, given the public record so far and independent
protocol randomness, the protocol does one of two things:
- **Call any station** at a time at or after the last record, with a window ending by T. This yields a success bit,
  and on success the exact crossing time and a new carrier.
- **Read the current carrier along a menu axis.** This yields s = sign(a·λ) at a fixed delay, and the carrier is
  absorbed. The instrument's update is the routed call to S_{sa}, which is one admissible choice.

**Preparations and record.**
- A preparation procedure picks its station by an unrecorded coin and calls it.
- The public record consists of every call, success bit, crossing time and outcome.
- Absorber states are not read.
- Readout is ideal: the point-aperture limit, with continuous directions.

## Reference theorem (P_μ)

**Lemma 1 (marking).** In each station, aperture crossings during [0, T] form a Poisson process on [0, T] × S². Its
intensity is (Λ/T) dt ⊗ μ_w(dλ).
- **Proof.** The particles form a Poisson process. Mapping each particle to its crossing and restricting to aperture
  crossings within [0, T] gives a Poisson process whose intensity is μη·(vc/L)·1[t < L/(vc)] dt dΩ/4π. For
  t ≤ T ≤ L/v, the indicator equals 1 for every c > 0. The intensity is then (μηv/4L) dt · (c₊/π) dΩ.
- **Check.** V1 checks it symbolically.

**Lemma 2 (independence).** The m station processes are independent. This needs independent counts and independent
particle states. Poisson marginal counts alone do not suffice, and conditioning on a fixed total count reintroduces
correlations.

**Lemma 3 (record as a function).** Under any π ∈ Π_T, the public record is a measurable function of three things:
- the station processes on [0, T];
- the protocol randomness;
- the preparation coin.

**Why it holds.**
- The herald of a call (w, t, τ) is the first point of Φ_w in [t, t + τ).
- Points arriving while the shutter is closed are absorbed.
- Each particle crosses at most once.
- Directions enter the record only through outcomes.

**Lemma 4 (strong Markov).** Every decision time is a stopping time of the filtration generated by the processes up
to time t, the protocol randomness and the coin. At such a time, the joint marked Poisson process restricted to the
future is again Poisson with the same intensity, and independent of the past. This is the standard independent-
increment property of a Poisson random measure with intensity dt ⊗ ν.

**Theorem R.** Under (P_μ), with ideal readout, the routing as specified and the declared access restrictions, take
any protocol π ∈ Π_T.
- **Calls.** Each call (w, t, τ) behaves the same whatever the past record:
  - it succeeds with probability 1 − e^(−Λτ/T);
  - on success, its crossing time is distributed as t + Exp(Λ/T), conditioned to fall in the window;
  - its carrier direction is distributed as μ_w.

  The call is independent of the whole past record. Its law is the same for every station, whether or not that
  station has fired before.
- **Readings.** A reading along a of a carrier from S_w gives s with probability (1 + s·a·w)/2.
- **Dependence on the preparation.** The record law depends on the preparation procedure only through its Bloch
  vector, because the only preparation-dependent factor is affine in w. Procedures representing the same quantum
  state are therefore exactly indistinguishable by every π ∈ Π_T, failures and exact timestamps included.
- **Proof.** Combine Lemmas 1–4 and induct over the record's steps.

**Scope.**
- The theorem concerns the reference model, not OI.
- It is conditional on isotropy, independent pools and the declared access restrictions.
- Finite-aperture readout and direction discretization need their own bounds.

## Coupling bound (B_N)

**Theorem C.**
- **Per-particle coupling.** In (B_N), each particle contributes a point with probability q. Given that it does, the
  point's law is Unif[0, T] ⊗ μ_w, which is the conditional law of a Poisson(q·ν) point.
- **Per-particle distance.** TV(Bernoulli(q), Poisson(q)) = q(1 − e^(−q)), which is at most q² (V2).
- **Summing.** Summing over particles and stations, the arrival records satisfy
  TV((B_N) arrival records, (P_N) arrival records) ≤ mNq² = mΛ²/N.
- **Data processing.** Every admissible transcript is the same function (Lemma 3) of these records, together with
  identical independent randomness. So the transcript law is within mΛ²/N of the reference law.
- **Two equivalent preparations.** For two procedures representing the same state,
  d_TV(P_N⁽¹⁾, P_N⁽²⁾) ≤ min(1, 2mΛ²/N), which is min(1, 16Λ²/N) for m = 8.
- **Coverage.** The bound covers every admissible bounded adaptive protocol within the horizon, failures and exact
  timestamps included.

**Checks.**
- V3 checks TV(Binomial(N, q), Poisson(Nq)) ≤ Nq² with interval arithmetic on 42 grid points. This is the
  per-station distance of the full arrival records, since the conditional point laws coincide.
- V4 checks that protocol W's exact fixed-pool difference stays within min(1, 16Λ²/N) in 24 cases. The largest
  ratio is 0.20, so the bound is conservative.

## Parameters to freeze before numerical acceptance tests (owner decision; values here are illustrative)

**Parameters.**
- **Horizon T.** At most L/v.
- **Windows.** Each window has length τ = T/K, which allows at most K sequential calls per run.
- **Resource budget.** At most K calls per run; protocols do not continue past T.
- **Success.**
  - Per-call success is 1 − e^(−Λ/K).
  - Failures stay in the primary record. Conditioning on rare successes can amplify the error.
- **Latency.** The fixed readout delay is Δ, and each call adds its waiting time.
- **Total error allowance.**
  - It is ε = 2mΛ²/N, plus the aperture term 4ρ/(d − ρ) for each readout, plus the direction-discretization
    term of the finite realization.
  - Each term is bounded separately.

**Example.** With K = 8 and per-call failure at most 0.05:
- Λ ≥ 8 ln 20 ≈ 24.0;
- the pool term ≤ 0.01 then needs N ≥ 16Λ²/0.01 ≈ 9.2·10⁵.

At per-call failure 10⁻³, Λ ≈ 55.3 and N ≈ 4.9·10⁶.
