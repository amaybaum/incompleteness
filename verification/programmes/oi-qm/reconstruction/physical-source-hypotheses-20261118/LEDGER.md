### R-U. What must a one-tick U1 reader retain?

**JUSTIFIED, conditional paper reduction.** Take the exact modular-linear step on the finite torus or on the infinite lattice, with local neighbor operator $A$, alphabet $Q=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$, $q\ge2$, and the explicitly supplied independent product-uniform slices $X,Y$ at the inspected tick. Write $Z=AY-X$. Fix the test setting $s$. Let the finite message $M=m(Y)$ and the finite address $J=g_s(Y)$ use only the previous slice at the later read. For each address choose an ordered tuple of the same finite number $k$ of distinct sites. Let $U$ be the bare tuple of values of $Z$ there; all accessible site labels, ordering information, flags and timings that convey the choice must be counted in the address or held fixed consistently with this contract. On that one-step law, if a decoder of $(J,U)$ recovers $M$ almost surely, a decoder of $J$ alone recovers $M$ almost surely. Conversely, in the very same setting and output specification, an address-only decoder gives a full-output decoder by ignoring $U$. This concerns exact recovery of the nominated message under the supplied prior.

For fixed $y,j$, the event for a specified value tuple $u$ constrains $X$ on the $k$ sites to one tuple, $(Ay)_{V(j)}-u$. Independence of $X$ and its product-uniform cylinder law assign that event probability $q^{-k}$. Integrating over the $Y$'s with $m(Y)=m$ and $g_s(Y)=j$ gives
$\Pr_s(M=m,J=j,U=u)=q^{-k}\Pr_s(M=m,J=j)$.
This uses a finite cylinder of $X$ even on the infinite lattice. Every supported message at a fixed address is compatible with every $u$. If two messages had positive probability at that address, a single decoder could not be almost surely correct for both. Hence each supported address has a unique decoded message. The antecedent permits arbitrary correlations of $M$ with $J$, including a constant address. The argument, rather than a constraint on the selector's definition, determines when it can recover the message.
