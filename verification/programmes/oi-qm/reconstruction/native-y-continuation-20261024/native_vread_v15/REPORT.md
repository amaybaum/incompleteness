## Key cells and the native permutation

A key of neighbor q9+e is a current-slice cell that influences q9+e at L+1
and influences neither q9 at L nor another neighbor at L+1. With the
parity of the second-order rule (a current cell influences (t,w) only if
dist<=t and t-dist is even), the keys are exactly the six axial cells
q9+(L+2)e. The written argument is in PLAN.md; K2 checks it by brute force
for n=2..7.

| Direction | Key cell | Classification | Nearest public receiver |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| +x | ((L+2),S,77) | pinned, ready ball of port 4 (radius S) | port 4 at L-476 |
| -x | (-(L+2),S,77) | free complement | port 3 at L+79 |
| +y | (0,S+(L+2),77) | free complement | port 5 at L+79 |
| -y | (0,S-(L+2),77)=(0,-79,77) | free complement | port 0 at 341 |
| +z | (0,S,77+(L+2)) | pinned: this is N | (operand direction) |
| -z | (0,S,77-(L+2)) | pinned, ready ball about (0,S,-68), radius R | port 5 at L-75 |

The -x key is the cell at which Native A places its fair source F9, chosen
there to make V uniform (native_x_v12 section 3). The +z key is N itself:
the own-N permutation already retained by native_route_v13 is this same
axial mechanism at q10.

For a free key and every fixed value of all other initial variables, the
key's own neighbor at L+1 is a permutation of the key triple by the unique
axial first arrival in arbitrary background (native_v11/REPORT.md,
"Native proof in arbitrary background"). p, q10 at L+1 and the other four
neighbors are at distance L+2 or L+3 from it and do not depend on it.
XOR translation and H are bijections, so V is a permutation of the key.
K3 checks every step of this on complete small native worlds.

Consequences. For each of the 48 accepted table rows of the v14 fewest-seed
law and each of the eight residual sums of the other neighbors, a uniform
key gives a uniform V (384 cases, computed with the actual p and q10 codes).
For every q10.A law the same holds, since the argument does not use the
law. Under a complement law in which the key is not uniform, for example
a fixed known complement, V is a deterministic function whose leakage is
not computed here; route_v13 does not specify that law, whereas Native A
fixes F9 as fair.

## What the read exposes, given the old record

Given p (public at L), Sigma -> V is a bijection with

    V.A = p.A XOR Sigma.B,  V.B = p.B XOR Sigma.D,
    V.D = p.D XOR Sigma.A XOR Sigma.B*Sigma.D,

checked on all 64 (p,Sigma) pairs. The read therefore exposes the whole
triple q10 XOR (other neighbors), never q10.A alone: whatever cleans the A
field must come from the other neighbors, and the same neighbors would
have to pad B and D. On the 48 accepted rows of the fewest-seed law,
q10.A XOR p.D = a XOR b, because p.D = r_old = s XOR a XOR b: inside V.D
the sign carried by q10.A cancels the sign of the old record. Under that
law q10 takes 4 distinct whole codes for each value of s across the 24
contexts, so a whole-triple exposure of q10 also exposes known-seed
information. These are diagnostics outside the decision rule.

## Where the stop lies

Delivery through V needs the free keys known. Pinning them is a readiness
extension of the q9 cone at L+2, a change to the initial preparation. A
blank radius-(L+2) current shell about q9 would also meet eight reserved
bank coordinates, four of them active at distance exactly L+2 (reproducing
native_x_v12): port 0 at deadline+2, reaching the -y and -z neighbors;
ports 2 and 3 at deadline+2, reaching +x,+y,-z and -x,+y,-z; port 5 at
deadline+2, reaching +y and -z. Those bank variables enter their own
ports' records at tick deadline+2, before L. The free -y key already
couples V to port 0's record. Any such extension therefore needs its own
joint public-record audit, besides the V kernel. Reading the key neighbors
at L+1 instead is an access change. This test adopts neither and stops.

## Scope

Established, for this one-tick stage, this source geometry and this
declared read: the read is keyed by free complement cells, so it delivers
no fixed readout of s for every complement value, and under a uniform key
it carries no information about q10.A. Not established or excluded:
privacy of V under a non-uniform complement law; multi-tick stages, other
reads or ports, other layouts, readiness extensions, previous-slice keys
(not classified); Native A's own geometry and pool; the acceptance
predicate, a carrier law or a reusable interface; any access rule or
preparation change; A1 or A2 reconstruction; OI generally. The
classification relies on the ready-ball, source and bank inventory that
native_route_v13's geometry check uses; a ready region missing from it
would change the classification of a key.
