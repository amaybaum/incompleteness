## Admission and stop rule

1. **Charge and field.** Look for a source-defined local charge density, its transported current or an equivalent charge account, and a local electric field on the same state history. Require a detector-relevant localization and calibration, together with a charge-to-field relation and post-discharge evolution that can be checked independently of the pulse record. A scalar symbol, a phase label, a proposed observer wave and a conditional gauge representation each need a physical map before they can supply these observables.
2. **Preparation.** If the first link is sourced, identify the biased electrodes, gas and working resources in the joint state. Trace the ready distribution, input coupling, and two selectable, recorded requests to an independently specified physical initial or boundary law or a forward source history. Account for the uncontrolled background across requests, the origin of the bias, and the phase and energy supply of the clock and record resources. A fit to successful detector histories cannot act as the producer.
3. **Two uses.** If the first two links are sourced, follow the same history through the first input, interaction and physical write, quench, recovery, and second opportunity. Identify the first record while the detector re-arms, its independent read and the complete residual for the second use. Keep independently tagged trial opportunities with no incident, unchanged or subthreshold responses, preparation failures, spurious events and read failures. An external list of past samples supplies no native memory resource.
4. **Stop** at the first unsupported charge, field or preparation mechanism and give the source needed to reopen that link. Later tests remain conditional on admission. Report separately the classical benchmark, the conditional mathematical description on \(F\), and independent physical sourcing.

## Detector and readout control

The external targets are Costrell's detector dead time, recovery toward full tube pulse amplitude, and the resolving interval of the tube plus readout. The LACCEI study concerns slower instrument indications and informs the readout control. These data motivate tests of a native map and serve as independent comparators. [Costrell (1949), §II and Figs. 11–12](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/42/jresv42n3p241_A1b.pdf) · [Vargas Segura and Jimenez Carballo (2026)](https://doi.org/10.18687/LACCEI2026.1.1.2386)

A later admitted test would hold the tube-side path fixed and compare the same detector-side waveforms with and without an artificial electronic veto applied to the records. Each possible second incident needs timing independent of a second recorded pulse and a calibrated input coupling. Normalize the second-response amplitudes and detection probabilities against matched fully recovered trials. Keep the input class and detector bias fixed in the delay comparison. An unchanged detector-side response with suppressed downstream reports measures the effect of the veto. Recovery of tube-side response with the gate bypassed is a separate observable. Preserve an independent first record through both conditions and include zero or unreadable responses rather than classifying only accepted pulses. The external instruments serve as comparators and introduce their own calibrated response and thresholds. [Detector and readout distinction](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/42/jresv42n3p241_A1b.pdf)

If admission succeeds, fix the observable map, eligible input, latency and timing calibration, short and recovered windows, and uncertainty bounds before inspecting a prediction test. A confirmed second opportunity with continued tube-side suppression beyond the candidate's stated recovered-window bound would falsify that candidate in the tested regime. A disappearing downstream response with unchanged detector-side data would diagnose the readout-only veto for that comparison. The present protocol specifies the decision rule and leaves numerical calibration to a source-backed case. It authorizes no apparatus operation, simulation, new microscopic rule, new physical premise, manuscript change or integration.
