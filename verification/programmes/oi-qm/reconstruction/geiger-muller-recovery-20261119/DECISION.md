## What the laboratory evidence establishes

Costrell distinguishes the interval with no tube response, the interval before full pulse amplitude can recur, and the resolving interval of the counter plus electronics. His explanation is that the first avalanche leaves positive ions near the anode, where their space charge reduces the field. As the ions move outward the field recovers, allowing smaller pulses before full recovery. Figures 11 and 12 compare dead time and time to **63 percent** of full pulse size against pressure or overvoltage, with a readout-resolution curve in Fig. 12. Those measurements supply pulse evidence. The ion-sheath explanation identifies the physical variables to seek. The pulse-triggered apparatus does not independently label each incident that failed to produce a second pulse. [Costrell (1949), §§I–II and Figs. 11–12](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/42/jresv42n3p241_A1b.pdf)

Vargas Segura and Jimenez Carballo report second-scale responses in analog and digital instrument outputs after changes in dose rate, using video sampled at \(1/30\) second. Their published abstract attributes these delays primarily to downstream signal processing. This is evidence about numerical, audible and visual indications. It supports using a readout control and supplies no direct tube-pulse timing or ion-motion data for this check. [LACCEI (2026), published abstract](https://proceedings.laccei.org/index.php/laccei/article/view/7854)

## Adverse classical control and falsifier

At a confirmed second opportunity with delay \(\Delta\), consider two classical descriptions. Fix a fully recovered positive pulse amplitude \(A\) and a threshold strictly between \(0\) and \(A\). In one description the tube produces zero amplitude for \(\Delta<\tau\) and amplitude \(A\) afterward, with the threshold always applied. In the other the tube always produces \(A\), with an electronic veto for \(\Delta<\tau\). Both give the same recorded second-count indicator \(1_{\Delta\ge\tau}\), even when the same first result and timing metadata are kept. The detector-side amplitudes differ inside the interval. This is an exact paper control for non-identification from a recorded hard threshold, independent of the candidate OI update. Actual tube recovery can also include partial pulses. [Protocol](PROTOCOL.md) · [Costrell on resolving time](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/42/jresv42n3p241_A1b.pdf)

A physical comparison would use the same independently tagged opportunities and calibrated tube-side response with the veto enabled or disabled downstream. The first record must stay readable in both cases. Fix the short and recovered windows, uncertainty, input coupling and baseline response before examining the candidate's prediction. A confirmed opportunity with continued tube-side suppression in the declared recovered window beyond the stated bound would falsify that claimed recovery in the tested conditions. If the veto changes accepted counts while the tube-side response agrees, the count change is explained by the readout control. The source stop precedes this comparison on \(F\).

**Evidence that would reopen the first link.** On the same fixed histories, provide a detector-region charge or current observable and a calibrated local electric-field observable with a justified correspondence to the physical source and geometry. A minimal independent discriminator is a post-discharge change of localized charge and near-anode field at matched bias, with a derived change of the calibrated field under \(F\) before using the output counts as an endpoint. The physical producer of the biased ready state and selectable request would then need its own source trace.
