## 1. The two sides

### 1.1 OI as a hypothesis set

- Definition (M:42): "An *observation* is a triple $(S, \varphi, V)$: a total system $S$, a dynamics $\varphi: S \to S$, and an observer $V \subsetneq S$ — a proper subsystem with finitely many distinguishable internal states, coupled to the complement $H = S \setminus V$ through $\varphi$." Axioms 1 and 2 (M:36); Lemmas 1–3 (M:46, :48, :54).
- M:60: "only finite visible resolution is a theorem"; the bijective substratum "is a representation choice"; total finiteness "is a premise of the results that use it".
- Posit ledger (M:724), fourteen items beyond Axiom 1: (i) recurrence; (ii) total finiteness; (iii) injectivity, part-empirical; (iv) maximal-entropy measure; (v) hidden-sector mixing; (vi) isotropy; (vii) linearity of the update; (viii) site factorization; (ix) bounded degree; (x) nearest-neighbour range; (xi) center independence; (xii) background independence; (xiii) second-order form; (xiv) measurement independence, which "enters only the Bell analysis of §3.3".
- Main gives no definition of "bare (finite) OI" as a hypothesis set. The corpus uses the phrase for two objects, OI-L ("Bare finite OI representability", M:360) and OI-C (M:356, whose witnesses realize "the same finite C1–C4 process"; GR:240), and distinguishes both from "the concrete OI substratum" (GR:260) and "the current OI substratum" (GR:284), here OI-I and OI-S.

- PG:525–545: endpoints F1, "`OI + explicitly justified physical conditions  <->  target QM`"; F2, a minimal extension theorem whose "active form proves sufficiency first"; F3, exact classification without uniqueness.
- #1169 [I]: one consistent model for the whole of `J_exact(A)`, both directions (EP:53–64). #1216 [I]: Level A, finite-horizon statistical approximation; Level B, uniform operational approximation.

Reading of these terms against the candidates of §2: A is T0, T1 and T1∞. F1 is two-directional, F2's active form is a (⇒) target with necessity assessed separately, and F3 is a classification, not an equivalence.
