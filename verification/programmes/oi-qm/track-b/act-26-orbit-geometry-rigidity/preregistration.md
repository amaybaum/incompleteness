## The extension route, recorded here as the freeze's reading and not as a finding

`A26-0` is stated for an arbitrary real inner-product space `E` that is finite-dimensional, a
subset `S ⊆ E` and a map `f : E → E` with `∀ x ∈ S, ∀ y ∈ S, dist (f x) (f y) = dist x y`, and
**nothing else is assumed of `f`**. The freeze reads the classical argument as follows.

1. **Polarization.** For `x, y, z ∈ S`, `⟪x − z, y − z⟫ = (‖x − z‖² + ‖y − z‖² − ‖x − y‖²) / 2`
   (`real_inner_eq_norm_add_mul_self_sub_norm_mul_self_sub_norm_mul_self_div_two`, or the
   equivalent form), so `f` preserves every inner product of differences of points of `S`.
2. **An affine basis from the subset.** `affineSpan ℝ S` is finite-dimensional; a finite affinely
   independent family `p₀, …, p_m` of points **of `S`** with the same affine span exists
   (Mathlib's `exists_affineIndependent` gives, for any set, an affinely independent subset with the
   same affine span; finiteness follows from the dimension). If `S` is empty the statement is
   vacuous and `g` is the identity.
3. **The linear isometry of the direction.** On `vectorSpan ℝ S = span ℝ {p_i − p₀}`, the vectors
   `p_i − p₀` are linearly independent, so a linear map `L₀` is defined by
   `L₀ (p_i − p₀) = f p_i − f p₀`; by step 1 it preserves the Gram matrix of the basis, hence every
   inner product, hence every norm: `L₀` is a linear isometry of the direction subspace into `E`.
4. **Extension to the whole space.** `LinearIsometry.extend` extends `L₀` to a linear isometry
   `L : E →ₗᵢ[ℝ] E`; in finite dimension an injective linear map is bijective, so `L` is a linear
   isometry equivalence.
5. **The affine assembly.** `g x := f p₀ + L (x − p₀)` is an affine isometry equivalence of `E`.
   For `x ∈ S`, write `x − p₀ = ∑ c_i (p_i − p₀)`; the vector
   `w := (f x − f p₀) − ∑ c_i (f p_i − f p₀)` has `‖w‖² = 0` by expanding with the inner products step
   1 preserves — every term of the expansion is an inner product of differences of points of `S`
   on the `f` side equal to the corresponding one on the `S` side, and the latter sum vanishes —
   so `f x = f p₀ + L (x − p₀) = g x`.
6. **Uniqueness on the affine hull.** Two affine maps agreeing on `S` agree on `affineSpan ℝ S`, by
   the span's induction principle.

**The instantiation** takes `E` the ambient space with its real structure, `S = normalizedSet Γ₀`,
and, for `f` with `IsSurjIsometryOn (normalizedSet Γ₀) f`, adds `g '' normalizedSet Γ₀ =
normalizedSet Γ₀` from the first two conjuncts of the predicate. **Nothing in the route reaches
outside the library's linear algebra and this round's definitions**, and no property of the
normalized space beyond its being a subset of `E` is used.

## The census route, recorded as the freeze's reading

For `(π, τ)` and unit `z`, by act 25's `mixedTriple_relabel2`,
`Ψ ((π, τ) F(z)) p = Ψ (F(z)) ((π, π, π) p.1, (τ, τ, τ) p.2)`, and by `fourier_coord_uniform` every
coordinate of `Ψ (F(z))` is `S_q * z ^ A_q * (star z) ^ B_q / 64` with `S_q ∈ {1, −1}` and natural
`A_q, B_q ≤ 3` fixed by the index `q` alone. So **a relabelled circle is the Fourier circle with its
coordinate data `(S, A, B)` permuted along `σ_{(π, τ)} := (π, π, π) × (τ, τ, τ)`**.

1. **Coincidence with `C₀`, decided coordinatewise.** By act 24's separation and gauge invariance,
   `[(π, τ) F(z)] = [F(z')]` iff `Ψ ((π, τ) F(z)) = Ψ (F(z'))` iff for every `p`,
   `S_{σp} z ^ A_{σp} (star z) ^ B_{σp} = S_p z' ^ A_p (star z') ^ B_p`. `C_{(π, τ)} = C₀` iff this
   system has a unit solution `z'` for every unit `z` and conversely. **The freeze's reading**: a
   coordinate `p` with `A_p = B_p` — constant on `C₀` — that `σ` sends to a coordinate with
   `A_{σp} ≠ B_{σp}`, or two coordinates with the same monomial on `C₀` sent to coordinates with
   different ones, forces non-coincidence at a suitable `z` for every `z'`; and when the permuted
   data equal the original data up to a common reparametrization `z' = ζ z` or `z' = ζ star z`
   with `ζ` a fourth root of unity, the circles coincide. Which of the `576` pairs falls on which
   side is **not computed here**.
2. **The branch theorem.** `SINGLE`: `∀ π τ, ∀ z, star z * z = 1 → ∃ z', star z' * z' = 1 ∧
   GramPhaseEquiv ((π, τ) F(z)) (F(z'))`. `SEVERAL`: `∃ π τ z, star z * z = 1 ∧ ∀ z', star z' *
   z' = 1 → ¬ GramPhaseEquiv ((π, τ) F(z)) (F(z'))`, the non-coincidence read through the named
   coordinate. Both are finite statements — `decide` over `Fin 4` and its permutations is permitted
   — and one of them is true.
3. **The count `A26-1-N`.** A set of representatives `R ⊆ Perm (Fin 4) × Perm (Fin 4)` with
   `C_{r} ≠ C_{r'}` for distinct `r, r' ∈ R` (exhibited non-coincidences) and, for every `(π, τ)`,
