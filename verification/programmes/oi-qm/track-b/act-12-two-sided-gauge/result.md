## `TG2` — the fibre-Gram orbit theorem, both directions

`FibreGram`, `GramPhaseEquiv`, `gramPhaseEquiv_of_twoSided`, `exists_unitary_of_gram_eq`,
`weak_diagonal_phase`, `twoSided_of_gramPhaseEquiv`, `twoSided_slice_iff`, `twoSidedRelated_iff`,
with `fibreGram_mul_weak_apply` and `weak_mul` as the right-action mechanism.

**Forward.** `fibreGram_left_mul` shows the left factor leaves every `G^{(i)}` invariant
(`(L_i X)ᴴ (L_i X) = Xᴴ X`), and `fibreGram_mul_weak_apply` shows the right weak factor conjugates
by the anchored phases, `G^{(i)} ↦ Dᴴ G^{(i)} D`, entrywise `conj(c_j) G^{(i)}_{jk} c_k`.

**Converse, through the Gram-isometry lemma.** `exists_unitary_of_gram_eq` is the round's
load-bearing technical step: two families of vectors in `ℂ^A` with the same Gram matrix,
`Xᴴ X = Yᴴ Y`, are related by a unitary, `M X = Y`. **It is stated for arbitrary families** —
hazard 10 — the anchored components in a fibre being linearly dependent whenever some `Γ_{ij} = 0`:
the map `X c ↦ Y c` is well defined on `range X` and isometric there because
`‖X c‖² = cᴴ Xᴴ X c = cᴴ Yᴴ Y c = ‖Y c‖²`, and Mathlib's `LinearIsometry.extend` extends it to all of
`ℂ^A`. The converse then absorbs the phase `D` into a weak element (`weak_diagonal_phase`), applies
the lemma fibre by fibre, assembles the blocks into `L ∈ 𝒢_L`, and — since `L·U·D` now agrees with
`U'` on the anchored columns — lets act 11's `gaugeRelated_strong_iff_agree_on_anchor` absorb the
off-anchor freedom into a strong, hence weak, element. **The converse rides on act 11's orbit
theorem and one lemma**, exactly as frozen.

**The lifted form** `twoSidedRelated_iff` is the slice-wise corollary: two `ℕ`-indexed lifts are
two-sided related iff their fibre-Gram data are `∼_D`-equivalent at every time. **Per slice only.**

## `SH1` — the shape theorem, with `SH1-C1` and `SH1-C2`

`RealizableGram`, `sh1_necessity`, `posSemidef_factor_of_rank_le`, `sh1_sufficiency`, `sh1_shape`,
`sh1_c1`, `sh1_c1_fibreGram`, `posSemidef_apply_eq_zero_of_diag_eq_zero`,
`fibreGram_of_deterministic`, `sh1_c2_at_most_one`, `sh1_c2_unique_orbit`.

**Necessity, at level 2.** Fibre-Gram matrices are `Xᴴ X`, hence PSD (`fibreGram_posSemidef`); a
fibre has dimension `|A|`, so `rank ≤ |A|` (`fibreGram_rank_le`); the anchored columns of a unitary
are orthonormal, so the fibre-Grams sum to `I` (`sum_fibreGram`); the diagonal is the readback
(`fibreGram_diag_of_admissible`).

**Sufficiency, at level 2 — the frozen fallback is NOT used.** `posSemidef_factor_of_rank_le`
factors each PSD `G^{(i)}` of rank at most `|A|` as `W_iᴴ W_i` with `W_i` of `|A|` rows, through the
spectral decomposition `G = E Λ Eᴴ` with `Λ ≥ 0` and — by the rank hypothesis — at most `|A|`
nonzero eigenvalues placed at an injective choice of slots. **This is where the rank constraint is
consumed** (hazard 9): dropping `rank G^{(i)} ≤ |A|` leaves no room for the nonzero eigenvalues,
and sufficiency is false without it. The `W_i` stack into `|V|` columns indexed by `V × A`,
orthonormal because `∑_i G^{(i)} = I`, and Mathlib's
`Orthonormal.exists_orthonormalBasis_extension_of_card_eq` extends them to an orthonormal basis
whose matrix is the unitary, with the stacked columns in the anchored slots. `sh1_shape` states the
iff.

**Together, the sentence the round is for, asserted at full strength:**

> **The visible law is exactly the diagonal of the fibre-Gram data. The residual lift freedom,
> after the two-sided uniform gauge `𝒢_L × 𝒢ʷ_{a₀}`, is exactly the off-diagonal fibre-Gram data
> modulo the anchored phases — per time slice.**

**`SH1-C1` — zero-diagonal support.** `sh1_c1`: for every realizable tuple,
`Γ_{ij} = 0 ⇒ G^{(i)}_{jk} = G^{(i)}_{kj} = 0`, proved from positivity alone
(`posSemidef_apply_eq_zero_of_diag_eq_zero`: along `e_k − t·conj(G_{kj})·e_j` the form is
`G_{kk} − 2t|G_{kj}|²`, which cannot stay nonnegative for large `t` unless `G_{kj} = 0`);
`sh1_c1_fibreGram` is the same statement read directly off a dilation. **Bounded reading**:
off-diagonal Gram freedom can occur *only* between visible inputs that both have positive support
into the same output fibre. **No converse is claimed** — rank and `∑_i G^{(i)} = I` can force an
overlap entry to zero — and **no memory claim is made**: a memoryless Markov slice can have overlap.

**`SH1-C2` — deterministic slices have at most one Gram tuple.** `fibreGram_of_deterministic` gives
the closed form `G^{(i)}_{jk} = δ_{jk} Γ_{ij}` for every admissible dilation of a deterministic slice:
two inputs landing in different fibres have a vanishing cross-term by `SH1-C1`, and two landing in
the same fibre have a cross-term equal to the full column inner product `⟨U e_j, U e_k⟩ = 0`, by
unitarity. `sh1_c2_at_most_one` is the consequence. **Stated as "at most one", never as
existence** (hazard 13): several inputs may collapse deterministically onto one visible output, and
the rank bound can then obstruct realization at small `|A|`. **The orbit consequence is reported
together with the `TG2` converse, which landed**: `sh1_c2_unique_orbit` — a realizable
deterministic slice has a unique two-sided orbit.

**Post-round interpretation, one sentence, as frozen:** rule-1 freedom is supported only on
stochastic overlap — deterministic slices have no Gram-orbit selection freedom, while stochastic
overlap is necessary but not sufficient for such freedom.
