## 1. Blocking

### BG-1. The rule cannot return (A); F1 and F2 assume the regularity view; F4 is missing

**Disposition: accepted.**

- **Item 1, the asymmetry.** I checked it before writing r2 (N2:44–48).
  - The premise base contains N1–N3-G and N5-G, and Lemma U holds at every OI world. r1's clause (A),
    which barred non-realizability, could therefore not be met by any per-world formalization, while
    (b1) carried no bar.
  - The rule could return only (B) or (C), and it did so before any analysis.
  - r2 states this as a defect of r1's rule: R-1 at r2:215–226.
  - **The repair.** Formalizations are classified by content. The non-vacuity bar applies to both sides
    (r2:144–172). The outcome is decided by which kinds of formalization remain admissible (r2:159–167).
  - **(A) is reachable under the repaired rule.** It obtains if every CoC-content formalization is
    refuted and a grounding-content one survives (r2:224–226).
  - **Not tuned.** The repaired rule was applied and gave (C), not r1's (B). It was written after the
    review named (C) as the expected outcome, and the provenance note says so (r2:116–122).
- **Item 2, Lemma U restates the reductio.** r2:306–310 states Lemma U as "the inference N1–N3 + N5 ⊢ ¬N4
  that unit 2(a) states as Reading G … specialized to a law term". It shows that no world with a grounding
  term is an OI world, and it "does not by itself decide whether the witness's law requires such a
  term".
- **Item 3, F1 and F2 build in the regularity view.** r2 labels both as regularity-view, CoC-content
  formalizations (r2:175–179). Corollary E is read on that view (r2:317–318). Node C-1 records that CoC
  is admissible, not entailed (r2:493–494).
- **Item 4, F4.**
  - F4 is added as R-3 (r2:182–186, :229–231).
  - **Assessed.** r2 §3.4 (r2:333–364): F4 is admissible, since P-D, GR:342, SM:158 and GR:162 do not
    contradict it. Under F4 with Lemma U, the witness's law is realized at no OI world (r2:346–348).
  - **One refinement.** F4 keyed to "the right-hand side" classifies a presentation, since the witness's
    uniform term can be moved to the left as part of an effective Λ. r2 therefore states F4 on scalar
    energy content (r2:335–343, F4-1 at r2:453–454). This keeps the review's content.
  - **Three conditions** were found by testing F4 as the branch favourable to decoupling:
    - F4-3 (r2:458–466): with diachronic relata and a static-patch partition the chain is acyclic, so F4
      excludes the witness only with synchronic or whole-history relata, or with GR:40's event-horizon
      partition. This also corrects r1's A5, which placed the refutation of diachronic grounding in P-D;
      that holds only for F3 and for macro-to-micro grounding.
    - F4-5 (r2:471–481): the corpus calls partition-defined quantities "gravitating" or curvature-producing
      (GR:384, GR:482, GR:695). F4 survives only by classing them as a geometric identity or as
      thermodynamic intermediaries, which is SM:160's distinction between input (iii) and inputs
      (i)–(ii). This weakens the support the corpus's vocabulary gives F4.
    - F4-6 (r2:482–485): on the corpus's conditional Jacobson route the source is the null flux, and a
      uniform term has none (U1r5:603–605). F4 then does not reach the witness, whose dependence moves to
      the selection of the integration constant (U1r5:596–600).
  - These conditions enter the verdict (r2:539–547) and BG-F5 (r2:632). They narrow the review's statement
    "Under F4 … excluded" and do not reverse it.
- **Item 5, the uniformity claim depends on CoC.** Accepted. r2:308–310 reads: "On CoC the same step covers
  `G_μν[g]`. Unit 2(a)'s C2(b) admits the nonlinearity because it 'runs through … no emergent relatum'
