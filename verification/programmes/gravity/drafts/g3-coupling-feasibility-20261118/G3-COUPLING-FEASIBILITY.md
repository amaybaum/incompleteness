# G3 coupling feasibility

**Verdict: conditional continuum feasibility. The native OI source is open.** The difference of two complete, conserved stress tensors on the same test metric is insensitive to a common vacuum offset when the physical reference is fixed. In the test-source limit it retains the difference in total rest energy. Given continuum geometry and a physically normalized scalar energy, the geometry-first proposal still needs a local matter-to-boundary stress or flux map from one fixed substratum evolution. The cosmological background remains an input.

## Conditional construction on a common metric

Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and $c=1$ in the continuum equations. Let $X$ and $X_0$ be two preparations, including the matter that provides forces, boundaries and supports. Suppose one covariant matter theory supplies symmetric tensors $T_{\mu\nu}[g,X]$ and $T_{\mu\nu}[g,X_0]$ on the same test metric $g$. Both tensors use the same physical couplings and normalization. The interior binding stresses and interface forces belong to the complete tensors. At each candidate metric on which the field equation below is used, both preparations must solve their respective matter equations on that metric. Their responses can be evaluated on a common test metric even when the two preparations ultimately give different gravitational solutions.

For example, a diffeomorphism-invariant matter action supplies a metric response through
$T_{\mu\nu}=-(2/\sqrt{-g})\,\delta S_{\rm m}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, taking the metric variation with matter variables held fixed and then evaluating in the prepared configuration. Its diffeomorphism identity gives $\nabla_g^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=0$ on the complete matter equations. A direct conserved tensor or matched local flux law can furnish the same physical input. The construction only needs the conserved tensors. Compare [Hollands and Wald, stress conservation in perturbative curved-spacetime QFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0404074).

Define $\Delta T[g;X,X_0]=T[g,X]-T[g,X_0]$. It is covariant when $g$ and both preparations transform together. Since the same connection acts on both tensors, $\nabla_g^\mu\Delta T_{\mu\nu}=0$, including interface balance where the matter equations are distributional. A common addition $-\rho_0\int\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ to the matter action shifts each tensor by $-\rho_0 g_{\mu\nu}$ for any constant density $\rho_0$. The shifts cancel pointwise. Any identical metric-only response on that domain also cancels. Fixed-metric transition probabilities leave the two metric responses undetermined. A preparation-dependent term, such as one that changes with plate spacing, can produce different metric responses even if it acts as an additive energy shift within each fixed configuration.

A conditional field equation with an explicit reference is
$$
G_{\mu\nu}[g]+\Lambda_\ast g_{\mu\nu}
  =8\pi G\big(T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm ref}[g]+\Delta T_{\mu\nu}[g;X,X_0]\big).
$$
Here $T^{\rm ref}$ is a separately conserved, physically specified reference source, possibly zero for a chosen vacuum reference. Holding the selection of $X_0$, $T^{\rm ref}$ and the spacetime constant $\Lambda_\ast$ fixed under the common shift makes the full equation invariant. A native completion needs a physical selection rule for those data. Choosing a reference separately for each comparison still gives the conservation identity for any pair on the same metric, but can move the baseline used in the field equation. Each solved metric has its own connection. A difference formed across two solved metrics needs a separate conservation argument.

For example, with $T^{\rm ref}_{\mu\nu}=-\rho_{\rm ref}g_{\mu\nu}$, the effective cosmological constant is $\Lambda_{\rm eff}=\Lambda_\ast+8\pi G\rho_{\rm ref}$. The cancellation at fixed reference data leaves its value and its response to a physical vacuum change open. The null-flux route already reviewed in unit 1 leaves the constant sector open as well. [SM, conditional local-horizon inputs](https://github.com/amaybaum/incompleteness/blob/192bbf29402d23fd49acc1a6d1a65c8cd0ff41b7/papers/SM.md#L158-L162) · [Jacobson, eq. (6)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9504004).

## Controls on the difference

For static, localized differences, evaluate the sources on a common nearly flat background. Use distances short compared with the background curvature radius and the test-source limit, in which changes in the stresses induced by the metric perturbation, including any response of a material reference, enter only beyond the order kept. Assume negligible self-gravity of the preparations. The leading weak-field Einstein equation gives
$\nabla^2\Delta\Phi=4\pi G(\Delta T_{00}+\sum_i\Delta T_{ii})$ in units $c=1$.
With sufficient spatial decay, integrating $\partial_i(x^j\Delta T^{ik})=\Delta T^{jk}$ gives zero integrated spatial stress. Thus the far-field mass difference is $\Delta E/c^2$ with units restored, where $\Delta E$ is the change in the complete systems' rest energy. The two controls below keep the interaction contribution in the energy budget. A nonzero monopole difference requires a nonzero total energy difference after support and reservoir contributions, which can compensate the interaction energy. An isolated apparatus that rearranges itself while retaining all work and released energy can have $\Delta E=0$. [Giulini, Laue's theorem and its hypotheses](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09320).

| Control | What the source must retain |
| --- | --- |
| Binding energy | A stationary bound system and a stationary reference containing the same constituents can differ in total energy. Match or account for the forces that realize the configurations, and track emitted energy or work if relating the preparations by a physical process. The total energy budget includes the binding contribution with its sign. Nuclear mass-defect and photon measurements give an inertial mass–energy benchmark. The gravitational response above is the conditional weak-field prediction. [Rainville et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/4381096a) |
| Casimir energy | Compare two stationary plate separations with the same material theory. The configuration-dependent interaction energy survives subtraction of the common bulk offset. Match or account for the responses of the plates, supports and work reservoirs. Momentum balance at the plates requires the stresses carrying their reaction forces. Standard calculations for idealized plates retain the finite interaction term and absorb individual-plate self-energies into renormalized plate masses. These give theoretical gravitational benchmarks. [Fulling et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.76.025004) · [Milton et al., §§IV–V](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0784) |
| Common offset | Keep both preparations and the physical reference data fixed. A common constant density cancels at every point. A shift applied only to one preparation, or to the interior of the cavity, changes the source comparison. At a boundary of the shifted region the resulting pure-trace stress has a distributional divergence unless another stress carries the exchange. |

These are tests of differential sourcing in an externally justified continuum description. Passing the algebraic controls with supplied tensors does not identify those tensors with a source generated by the OI evolution.

## First missing physical input

After granting continuum geometry and physically normalized scalar energy, the first additional input for this comparison is a **native local stress or flux map for one complete pair of localized matter configurations**, from the same fixed $(S,\varphi)$. The map must give the responses on a common test metric, reproduce the pair's relative energy and account for momentum and energy transfer at interfaces. The distinction from scalar energy is already visible in flat space. Adding $(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu-\eta_{\mu\nu}\Box)f$ for a smooth, static, spatially compact function $f$ preserves conservation and leaves the integrated energy unchanged because the added energy density is $\nabla^2 f$. It can change the local tensor.

GR §6.2 names the total classical source and §8.3 asserts its correspondence to relative energy. The conditional horizon argument uses that source in its heat flux. These inputs leave the local stress or flux of the binding and plate configurations undetermined. The differential comparison supplies a check for the missing map. The cosmological-constant explanation remains conditional on a native coupling and physical selection of the reference and constant sector.
