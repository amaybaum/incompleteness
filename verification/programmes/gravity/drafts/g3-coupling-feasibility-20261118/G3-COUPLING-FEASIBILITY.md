# G3 coupling feasibility

**Verdict: conditional continuum feasibility. The native OI source is open.** Subtracting two complete, conserved stress tensors on the same test metric removes a common constant vacuum stress and preserves the difference between the two systems' weak-field mass sources. The geometry-first proposal has yet to supply the first input to this construction: a physically normalized, local matter-to-boundary stress or flux map from one fixed substratum evolution. Even granting the scalar shell energy leaves that input open. The comparison supplies a test for a coupling, with the cosmological background still an input.

## Conditional construction on a common metric

Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and $c=1$ in the continuum equations. Let $X$ and $X_0$ be two preparations, including the matter that provides forces, boundaries and supports. Suppose one covariant matter theory supplies symmetric tensors $T_{\mu\nu}[g,X]$ and $T_{\mu\nu}[g,X_0]$ on the same test metric $g$, with each preparation on its matter equations there. Both tensors use the same physical couplings and normalization. The interior binding stresses and interface forces belong to the complete tensors. The test metric is a common argument of two matter responses, even when the two preparations ultimately give different gravitational solutions.

For example, a diffeomorphism-invariant matter action supplies a metric response through
$T_{\mu\nu}=-(2/\sqrt{-g})\,\delta S_{\rm m}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, taking the metric variation with matter variables held fixed and then evaluating in the prepared configuration. Its diffeomorphism identity gives $\nabla_g^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=0$ on the complete matter equations. A direct conserved tensor or matched local flux law can furnish the same physical input. The construction only needs the conserved tensors. Compare [Hollands and Wald, stress conservation in perturbative curved-spacetime QFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0404074).

Define $\Delta T[g;X,X_0]=T[g,X]-T[g,X_0]$. It is covariant when $g$ and both preparations transform together. Since the same connection acts on both tensors, $\nabla_g^\mu\Delta T_{\mu\nu}=0$, including interface balance where the matter equations are distributional. A common addition $-\rho_0\int\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ to the matter action shifts each tensor by $-\rho_0 g_{\mu\nu}$ for any constant density $\rho_0$. The two shifts cancel in $\Delta T$. A fixed-metric Hamiltonian phase shift does not supply this common metric response. In particular, a term whose coefficient varies with the plate spacing or the metric can carry a relative response even if it is state independent within each fixed configuration.

A conditional field equation with an explicit reference is
$$
G_{\mu\nu}[g]+\Lambda_\ast g_{\mu\nu}
  =8\pi G\big(T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm ref}[g]+\Delta T_{\mu\nu}[g;X,X_0]\big).
$$
Here $T^{\rm ref}$ is a separately conserved, physically fixed reference source, possibly zero for a chosen vacuum reference, and $\Lambda_\ast$ is a spacetime constant. The Bianchi identity is consistent with this equation. Holding the physical choice of $X_0$, $T^{\rm ref}$ and $\Lambda_\ast$ fixed under the common offset makes the full source and the field equation invariant. A native completion must justify how those data are selected and how they respond to a shift. Choosing a new reference in each configuration or subtracting two tensors evaluated on different solved metrics supplies no such conservation argument. The differential comparison does not determine the reference source or the cosmological constant. A binding or Casimir measurement can calibrate the difference while leaving the absolute source undetermined. The null-flux route already reviewed in unit 1 has the same limitation on the constant sector. [SM, conditional local-horizon inputs](https://github.com/amaybaum/incompleteness/blob/192bbf29402d23fd49acc1a6d1a65c8cd0ff41b7/papers/SM.md#L158-L162) · [Jacobson, eq. (6)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9504004).

## Controls on the difference

For static, localized differences with negligible self-gravity, use a common nearly flat background and keep only the leading weak-field response over distances short compared with the background curvature radius. The linearized static Einstein equation gives
$\nabla^2\Delta\Phi=4\pi G(\Delta T_{00}+\sum_i\Delta T_{ii})$ in units $c=1$.
With sufficient spatial decay, integrating $\partial_i(x^j\Delta T^{ik})=\Delta T^{jk}$ gives zero integrated spatial stress. Thus the far-field mass difference is $\Delta E/c^2$ with units restored, where $\Delta E$ is the change in the complete systems' rest energy. This check concerns the mass monopole. Local stress and support loads still matter to the conservation equation. [Giulini, Laue's theorem and its hypotheses](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09320).

| Control | What the source must retain |
| --- | --- |
| Binding energy | A stationary bound system and a stationary reference with the same constituents can differ in total energy. Include the forces that realize each configuration and account for emitted energy or work if relating the preparations by a physical process. The mass difference includes the binding contribution with its sign. Nuclear mass-defect and photon measurements provide an inertial mass–energy benchmark. The gravitational response above is the conditional weak-field prediction. [Rainville et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/4381096a) |
| Casimir energy | Compare two stationary plate separations with the same material theory. The configuration-dependent interaction energy survives subtraction of the common bulk offset. Include the response of the plates, supports and work reservoirs in the total comparison. Prescribing plate positions as external forces and using field stress alone does not close the momentum balance at the plates. Standard calculations retain the finite interaction term and absorb individual-plate self-energies into renormalized plate masses. The cited calculations give theoretical gravitational benchmarks. [Fulling et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.76.025004) · [Milton et al., §§IV–V](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0784) |
| Common offset | Keep both preparations and the physical reference data fixed. A common constant density cancels at every point. A shift applied only to one preparation, or to the interior of the cavity, changes the source comparison. At a boundary of the shifted region the resulting pure-trace stress has a distributional divergence unless another stress carries the exchange. |

These are tests of differential sourcing in an externally justified continuum description. Passing the algebraic controls with supplied tensors does not identify those tensors with a source generated by the OI evolution.

## First missing physical input

The smallest missing input for this route is a **native local stress or flux map for one complete pair of localized matter configurations**, from the same fixed $(S,\varphi)$ and using the same test geometry for their continuum response. The map must give an independently normalized nonzero relative energy and account for the transfer of energy and momentum at interfaces, so a conserved total source can be checked before any vacuum subtraction. GR §6.2 names the total classical source and §8.3 asserts its correspondence to relative energy. The conditional horizon argument uses that source in its heat flux. None of those steps constructs the map for the binding or plate configurations in this assessment. The proposed comparison therefore sharpens the source obligation for G3. Selecting the cosmological reference and testing how it responds to a physical change of the convention-fixed ground state are further obligations. The cosmological-constant explanation remains conditional.
