# The phase-source propagation round — the manuscripts at the sourced boundary

Owner-called, publication-only, written from `main` at `05e290b`, the merge of the phase-source
audit. The kernel states the boundary: the sourced baseline is `SourcedOI`, the theory generated by
the stated substratum and observer access; `DerivedOI` is `SourcedOI` with one further conjunct,
`PhasesAvailable`, which the stated substratum `(S, φ)` and observer access do not derive
(`PHASE-SOURCE-AUDIT.md`, `onesFixing_not_phasesAvailable`, `permClass_onesFixing`,
`substratumClass_not_onesFixing`). This round makes the manuscripts state that boundary, and does
nothing else. Preregistered here and committed alone before any manuscript is touched.

## The three items, fixed in advance

The round is bounded to three items, the owner's list, together with the mirrors §A.25 requires.

**Item 1, the two normalization sentences.** Under the migrated implementation semantics of
`INSTRUMENT-MIGRATION-AUDIT.md`, an intervention is available when it is realized by one protocol
built from admissible operators, and probability normalization in aggregate is a theorem of that
realization (`instAvail_trace`), not a clause of the generation. Two sentences of `[GR §3.3]`
state the clause:

| site | current text |
|---|---|
| GR §3.3, *Primitive-source form*, principle 1 | "Operational interventions are generated by finite families of admissible implementation operators with aggregate probability normalization; admissibility is invariant under relabelling and under adjoining an uncoupled spectator." |
| GR §3.3, the paragraph after the boxed equivalence | "the normalization half of operational validity is explicit in implementation generation" |

The target reading: interventions are those realized by one protocol built from admissible
implementation operators, an admissible isometric step, a register readout with admissible
projectors, classical coarse-graining of the outcome, sequential composition with
outcome-dependent continuation, and the discard of a uniformly attached ancilla, the branches of
an intervention arising together as the outcomes of one protocol; aggregate normalization is a
consequence of that realization. No manuscript names the kernel predicate.

**Item 3, the substratum-source sentence.** `[GR §3.3]`, *Substratum-source form*, states: "The
concrete OI substratum — finite states, bijective microscopic dynamics (Axiom 2), and the phase
structure — supplies the structural part in full." The phase-source audit found the phrase "the
phase structure" underdetermined: it names no operation the observer performs, and every
operation the manuscripts state is ones-fixing and sources no quarter phase. The target reading:
the relative-phase access is stated explicitly as an assumption, an intervention principle on the
substratum class, and not as a consequence of `(S, φ)` with the stated observer access. The
wording distinction of the phase-source scope amendment survives intact, in both parts:

- *necessary*: an access that supplies the phases has an admissible operator, selectable at every
  level, that moves the all-ones vector off its ray, since no ones-fixing class supplies a quarter
  phase;
- *sufficient, as tested*: the quarter-phase intervention itself, a member of the substratum class
  as stated (`phase_monomial`); moving the all-ones ray is not shown sufficient, and the
  manuscript sentence does not say that any ray-moving operator supplies the phases.

The sentence that follows the boxed substratum endpoint, "The controllability resource is not
entailed by A1–A6; it is an empirical extension of the current substratum, entering as a
hypothesis on the extended architecture", is completed by the same assumption on the phase
intervention, so that the left side of the box, "current OI substratum", reads as the substratum
class with the phase intervention stated.
