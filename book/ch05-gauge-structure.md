## 5.2 The minimal substratum and the wave equation

The framework's content rests on a minimal mathematical structure: a set $S$ (finite under the open hypothesis A1, Chapter 2 §2.4), a deterministic bijection $\varphi: S \to S$, and a partition of $S$ into visible and hidden sectors. The lemmas of Chapter 1 extracted three structural features from this minimal object — visible finiteness, causal partition, determinism — and the conditions C1–C4 identified when an embedded observer can exist. This section develops two further structural features of the minimal object that the Standard Model derivation requires.

**The factorization problem.** Let $S$ be a finite set with $|S| = q^N$ for some $q \geq 2$ and integer $N$. There are many ways to write $S$ as a Cartesian product $S = S_1 \times S_2 \times \cdots \times S_N$ with $|S_k| = q$ for each $k$. Different factorizations correspond to different choices of which elementary degrees of freedom to identify as "local" — different bases in which to decompose the configuration space. The factorization is not given by the set $S$ itself; it must be determined by some additional structure.

Given a bijection $\varphi$ and a product factorization, define the *coupling graph* $G_\varphi$ on vertices $\{1, 2, \ldots, N\}$, with vertices $i$ and $j$ connected by an edge if the $i$-th component of $\varphi(s)$ depends on the $j$-th component of $s$ for at least one state $s \in S$. The coupling graph is not an additional structure imposed on the system; once the factorization is given, it is read off from the bijection. The factorization itself is an input — site factorization, in the posit ledger of [Main §4.5] — and the dynamics alone has not been shown to select it.

Minimizing the coupling degree — the number of edges per vertex — over all product decompositions does not select it. The wave equation in one dimension is
$$x_i(t+1) = x_{i-1}(t) + x_{i+1}(t) - x_i(t-1) \mod q,$$
On a ring of four sites over $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ the ring's adjacency matrix diagonalizes, and in its eigenbasis each normal mode evolves alone, $c_k(t+1) = \lambda_k c_k(t) - c_k(t-1)$: the coupling graph of the normal-mode factorization has maximum degree 1, against 3 for the spatial one. And composing a factorization with $\varphi$ gives one with the same coupling graph, which differs from it whenever some component of $\varphi$ depends on two components; a minimizer of that kind is therefore not unique. Whether the observable law, the observer's registers or the dynamics select the spatial factorization within a stated class is open.

The earlier extension from spectral dimension to a canonical $2d_s$ **physical internal carrier** is not established. Quasi-isometry and heat-kernel scaling constrain large-scale geometry but do not manufacture signed internal link channels. H-link and the exact $(3,2,1)$ carrier construction are simple-cubic-branch statements, computed in the spatial site factorization with the nearest-neighbour coupling graph.

**Space as coupling structure.** Every "spatial" property used in the derivation chain is a property of the coupling graph $G_\varphi$. The *graph boundary* $|\partial V|$ of a region $V \subset S$ — the quantity the area-law bound is stated in — is the number of edges crossing from $V$ to its complement. The *dimension* is extracted from the causal partial order via the Myrheim-Meyer estimator. The *area-law bound* — that the information shared across a region's boundary is at most proportional to the boundary rather than the volume — follows from the spatial Markov property on any graph with range-1 dynamics. The *dispersion relation* describing how perturbations propagate requires translation invariance of the underlying dynamics. The regular cubic lattice $\mathbb{Z}^3$ is one graph with all these properties; it is not the only one.

The framework's account of space dissolves the container problem that has been a recurring objection to discrete models of spacetime. The coupling graph is not embedded in a pre-existing space. There is no ambient manifold in which the graph "lives." The vertices are labels for degrees of freedom, the edges record which degrees of freedom are dynamically coupled. Any spatial embedding is a representation for human convenience, not a physical fact. The coupling graph is not made of material — the vertices are not atoms of space and the edges are not physical connections. A degree of freedom is a component of the state, an abstract mathematical entity rather than an object. Asking what a site is "made of" is a category error: it is a label for a variable, not a name for a thing.

The relationship between locality and the dynamics is the precise inverse of the conventional picture. In the conventional picture, space exists first as a manifold, and locality is the property that nearby points in space have causally connected dynamics. In the framework, the dynamics exists first as a bijection, and space emerges as the topology that locality induces on the coupling graph. Two sites are neighbors because the bijection couples them — because the state at one affects the future of the other. This is a dynamical fact, not a spatial relationship. The topology is defined by the interactions; the apparent spatial arrangement is the topology made visible.

**The alphabet size.** The alphabet size $q$ is the number of distinguishable states at each lattice site. Several results hold for every $q \geq 2$ and contain no $q$: the P-indivisibility theorem, center independence, the boundary scaling of the area law, and the dispersion relation of the observer-level operator. The gravitational sector depends on $q$ only through the entropy $\sigma$ assigned to one boundary cell, which cancels from the Bekenstein-Hawking formula; at $\sigma = 1$, one nat per boundary mode, neither the gap equation $\hbar = c^3 \epsilon^2 / (4G\sigma)$ derived in Chapter 7 nor the Bekenstein-Hawking formula contains $q$, while $\sigma = \ln q$ would place $q$ in the discreteness scale $\epsilon = 2\sqrt{\ln q}\, l_p$. Other results carry $q$: the period of the wave map on a ring of $L$ sites is $qL$ for prime $q$ coprime to $L$, the coefficient of the area law and the capacity count of the hidden sector contain $\log q$, and the substratum normalisation $\alpha = 1/d$ requires $3 \nmid q$.

That the emergent observables do not depend on $q$ at all — that two substrata $(S, \varphi)$ and $(S', \varphi')$ with the same coupling structure and different alphabet sizes produce the same emergent transition probabilities — is the *alphabet-size hypothesis*. It is not proved for every observable, and because the visible transition matrices of the two substrata act on configuration spaces of different sizes, stating it requires a correspondence between them that is not constructed. Under the hypothesis the mod-$q$ state space at each lattice site plays the role of the gauge potential $A_\mu$ and the coupling structure with its emergent consequences that of the field strength $F_{\mu\nu}$. Generator (ii) of the substratum gauge group $\mathcal{G}_{\text{sub}}$ developed in Chapter 2 §2.5 is stated under this hypothesis.

The proposed amplitude-scale gauge uses affine relabellings $\boldsymbol{\phi}\mapsto\lambda\boldsymbol{\phi}+\mathbf c$ of a fixed alphabet, with $\lambda$ a unit. The affine-rule criterion separately assumes first differences independent of the base point (SM §4.1). Affine relabelling covariance alone allows nonlinear rules. The alphabet-size hypothesis compares different $q$. On the gauge branch the amplitude-scale premise supplies at most the common phase of the proposed observer action (SM Proposition 14a).

## 5.3 Selection of $d = 3$

The framework's structural lemmas operate at any integer dimension $d\ge1$. The observer-level quadratic-isotropy argument and dimension-dependent lattice calculations can be formulated in other dimensions, but the six-link physical-carrier construction is not a dimension-general theorem: H-link is presently tied to the simple-cubic signed-link representation used at $d=3$. The question of why our universe has three spatial dimensions is therefore a question about which dimension produces a self-consistent observed physics. The framework's answer is that exactly three works.

Within A4's periodic-lattice class and the continuum conditions of SM §3.1–§3.2, propagating gravity and stable matter select $d=3$; boundary-entropy concordance is an additional consistency check. This is a conditional self-consistency selection using the stated physical inputs.

**Filter 1: Propagating gravity requires $d \geq 3$.** The gravitational sector developed in Chapter 7 requires propagating gravitational degrees of freedom — gravitational waves, the structural content of the LIGO observations beginning in 2015. The Weyl tensor, which captures the propagating part of the gravitational field, vanishes identically in $d \leq 2$: there is no gravitational radiation in two or fewer spatial dimensions. Jacobson's thermodynamic derivation of Einstein's equations from local causal horizons requires propagating gravity. The filter therefore excludes $d \leq 2$.

**Filter 2: Stable matter requires $d \leq 3$.** The Coulomb potential in $d$ spatial dimensions falls off as $1/r^{d-2}$ for $d \geq 3$ and gives a logarithmic potential for $d = 2$. In $d \geq 4$ the Coulomb potential gives unstable atoms — there are no normalizable bound states of the $d$-dimensional Coulomb Hamiltonian, by Ehrenfest's argument and its quantum-mechanical refinement. Gravitational orbits are similarly unstable for $d \geq 4$ by Tangherlini's analysis. Without stable matter, no embedded observers exist to define the framework's partition. The filter therefore excludes $d \geq 4$.

The intersection of Filter 1 and Filter 2 is already $d = 3$ uniquely. A third filter provides a consistency check.

**Filter 3: Boundary-entropy concordance gives $d = 3$ exactly.** The cosmological observation that the universe is spatially flat near the critical density — $\Omega_{\text{total}} \approx 1$, inferred under the standard cosmological model — is compared in the framework's gravitational sector developed in Chapter 7 with the thermal energy of the horizon's boundary modes. The ratio $\rho_s / \rho_{\text{crit}}$ between the boundary-derived entropy density and the critical density, computed in $d$ spatial dimensions, satisfies
$$\frac{\rho_s}{\rho_{\text{crit}}} = \frac{2}{d - 1}.$$
This ratio equals unity exactly at $d = 3$. At $d = 2$ the ratio is $2$ (overclosure); at $d = 4$ the ratio is $2/3$ (deficit, requiring an unsourced gravitational contribution). The ratio comes from the horizon first-law normalization, the area-law entropy and the Friedmann equation, with $\epsilon$ and $\sigma$ cancelling; at $d = 3$ the equality restates the flat Friedmann density, since $\rho_{\text{crit}}$ and the Hubble volume are both computed from $H$. The filter bears on the observed flatness only if the boundary energy is identified with the gravitating density, a source identification that Chapter 7 leaves open.

Within A4's assumed periodic-lattice class and the stated continuum conditions, the first two bounds select $d=3$ and boundary-entropy concordance supplies a further consistency check. The specified Yang–Mills coupling is dimensionless at $3+1$ spacetime dimensions; its renormalizability result is downstream of that selection. The six-link octahedral carrier and H-link used here are stated for the selected simple-cubic branch. A comparison with another dimension requires its own carrier and matter assumptions.

**Integer dimension.** The self-consistency argument assumes that the coupling graph has a well-defined integer dimension. A general bounded-degree graph need not have one: besides exponential growth ($|B(r)| \sim k^r$ for some $k > 1$), fractal growth ($|B(r)| \sim r^\alpha$ for some non-integer $\alpha$) and integer-polynomial growth ($|B(r)| \sim r^d$ for some integer $d$), there are graphs of intermediate growth (Grigorchuk) and graphs with no single growth exponent. Under A4 (Chapter 2) the integer is an input: the coupling graph is a finite periodic lattice, the quotient of a rank-$d$ lattice by a finite-index sublattice, so $|B(r)| \sim r^d$ below the system size and none of the other classes arises. Independently, self-consistency excludes the first two classes.

Exponential growth fails because the wave equation on exponentially-growing graphs does not produce Lorentz-invariant dispersion at low energies — the spectral gap does not close in the correct functional form — so Jacobson's derivation of Einstein's equations fails. Additionally, the effective dimension $d_{\text{eff}} \to \infty$ is incompatible with the stable-matter filter.

Fractal growth fails because Jacobson's argument requires the Raychaudhuri equation, which requires geodesic congruences on a manifold structure that fractal spaces do not support. The staggered fermion decomposition that produces the framework's three-generation structure requires a hypercubic Brillouin-zone structure $\eta \in \{0, 1\}^d$, which has no fractal-graph analog. The cubic-group decomposition of $2d$ link directions, which constrains the gauge structure, requires cubic lattice symmetry — absent in fractals.

The integer $d$ therefore enters through A4 as the rank of the translation lattice, not through Gromov's theorem, which concerns finitely generated groups and their Cayley graphs; statistical isotropy alone does not supply it. Applied to the continuum description, conditional on the continuum step of §3.1 of the SM paper, the three filters then select $d = 3$.

**Corollary.** *Any bijection whose coupling graph is a $d$-dimensional periodic lattice with $d \neq 3$ fails at least one of the filters E5–E7 in its emergent description. The coupling-graph dimension is not a free parameter or a property of a specific $\varphi$; it is the unique value compatible with the filters.*

The argument's structure is worth marking explicitly. The framework's definition of observation requires an embedded observer as a constitutive element — the partition $V \subset S$ is defined relative to an observer that the substratum's structure must support. The requirement that such an observer exist, combined with the boundary-entropy concordance calculation, conditional on its source identification, constrains $d$. This is not a standard anthropic argument selecting from a pre-existing multiverse of dimensions. The observer is a structural prerequisite for the framework's definition to have content, not a contingent feature of one universe among many.

Within the assumed A4 periodic-lattice class and the selected continuum description, the E5–E7 filters select $d = 3$. Integer rank is supplied by A4, while the value $3$ is selected using the physical filters. The continuum-curvature identification remains open, as stated in SM §3.1. This selection concerns that branch rather than every finite bijection or embedded-observer architecture.

## 5.5 The cubic decomposition and the Standard Model gauge group

The six signed simple-cubic links, defined in the spatial site factorization with the nearest-neighbour coupling graph ([Main §4.5], items (viii) and (x)), form an exact six-dimensional representation of the cubic group. This section first computes that representation unconditionally. Its use as the **physical** internal/gauge carrier then assumes H-link single-copy; the custodial condensate-stabilizer step additionally assumes H-cust.

**The cubic-group representation on the six links.** The six nearest-neighbor link directions $\{\pm\hat{e}_1, \pm\hat{e}_2, \pm\hat{e}_3\}$ of the cubic lattice, with one component per link direction. The cubic rotation group $O$ — the group of orientation-preserving symmetries of the cube, with twenty-four elements — acts on these six link vectors by permutation: rotations of the cube permute the six link directions among themselves, with rotations through a face center cycling four link directions and rotations through a vertex cycling three.

The action of $O$ on the six-dimensional space spanned by the link directions is a representation of $O$ with character $\chi(O) = 6, 0, 2, 0, 2$ at the five conjugacy classes of $O$ (identity, eight three-fold rotations, three two-fold rotations through face centers, six two-fold rotations through edge midpoints, six four-fold rotations through face centers). Computing the inner products of $\chi$ with the characters of the five irreducible representations of $O$ — labeled $A_1, A_2, E, T_1, T_2$ with dimensions $1, 1, 2, 3, 3$ — yields the decomposition
$$6 = T_1 \oplus E \oplus A_1$$
with multiplicities $(3, 2, 1)$. The two remaining irreducible representations $A_2$ and $T_2$ have multiplicity zero in the six-dimensional link representation.

The decomposition is forced by group-theoretic content rather than chosen. The six link directions form a specific representation of the cubic rotation group; the decomposition of this representation into irreducibles is computed mechanically from the character table. The framework's commitment to the cubic lattice as substratum, combined with the requirement of spatial isotropy, makes the $(3, 2, 1)$ multiplicity structure inevitable.

**Conditional condensate-stabilizer reading.** The link representation itself is $T_1\oplus E\oplus A_1$. Under **H-link single-copy + H-cust**, the observer-level physical carrier is this six-dimensional space and the pre-condensate kinetic structure is custodially scalar (at the symmetric point the kinetic clause follows from the signed-permutation symmetry of the components under H-blind, [SM §4.4]). An $O$-equivariant condensate then has block form
$$\Sigma=aP_{T_1}+bP_E+cP_{A_1},$$
and for generic distinct block values its unitary stabilizer is
$$\mathrm U(3)\times\mathrm U(2)\times\mathrm U(1).$$
This stabilizer theorem is exact at those stated premises. It does not prove H-link, select $m=1$, or by itself perform the later $\mathrm U\to\mathrm{SU}$ reduction.

**Reduction to the local Standard Model gauge algebra.** The pointwise $O$-commutant on the six-link space is $U(1)^3$. The condensate stabilizer instead has Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)^3$. The amplitude-scale premise accounts for at most the common phase of the assumed observer action. A specified charged matter action and anomaly cancellation on the chiral family select the hypercharge direction (§5.6 and SM §4.9).

The resulting local gauge algebra on the stated branch is
$$\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_Y.$$
The color and weak factors use the $T_1$ and $E$ blocks. For the specified charged family and Higgs, the local action has faithful image $(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1))/\mathbb Z_6$ (SM Proposition 15a). The choice of global bundles and line operators requires independent data.

**Nearest-neighbor Bravais comparison.** On the same generic condensate construction, the nearest-neighbor representation of body-centered cubic is $A_1\oplus A_2\oplus T_1\oplus T_2$, with stabilizer $U(3)^2\times U(1)^2$. Face-centered cubic gives $A_1\oplus E\oplus T_1\oplus 2T_2$ and generic stabilizer $U(3)^3\times U(2)\times U(1)$ before charged-action reduction. The other eleven Bravais lattices lack a three-dimensional point-group irrep. The simple-cubic lattice alone gives the required $(3,2,1)$ block pattern in this nearest-neighbor comparison (SM Theorem 7b).

This comparison selects simple cubic for the stated physical gauge branch. **Local gauge covariance from background independence.** The step from global to local is what background independence (A6) supplies: the local transformations act covariantly once the coupling data on the links are transformed with them.

Background independence, as the reconstruction's assumption A6 states it, is a covariance requirement: a site-dependent internal-index transformation is a symmetry of the dynamics provided the coupling data on the links are transformed with it. It is not the requirement that the coupling structure be held fixed and left invariant — for an invertible coupling that stronger fixed-background condition holds only for transformations that agree across every coupled pair of sites — and it is not the state-dependent coupling graph $G(x)$ on which the Bell branch of Chapter 1 rests, under which the graph itself evolves with the state; those are different principles that share a name. Under H-link and H-cust (§§5.4–5.5), the cubic decomposition of the link directions fixes the block multiplicities $(3, 2, 1)$ at each site; spatial isotropy fixes those multiplicities, not distinct block values. The coupling on each link, $M(\mathbf{n}, \hat{e}_j)$, is the data the site-dependent transformations act on; where the equivariant condensate takes distinct block values, its stabilizer $\mathrm{U}(3) \times \mathrm{U}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$ (SM Theorem 5) acts at each site, and the resulting gauge transformations are site-dependent.

Under a site-dependent transformation $G(\mathbf{n})$ of the form $\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{n}) \to G(\mathbf{n})\,\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{n})$ with $G(\mathbf{n}) \in \mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$ at each site, the matrix wave equation is invariant provided the coupling matrix transforms as $M(\mathbf{n}, \hat{e}_j) \to G(\mathbf{n})\,M(\mathbf{n}, \hat{e}_j)\,G(\mathbf{n} + \hat{e}_j)^{-1}$. The real part of the trace of the plaquette is gauge-invariant — recognizable as the Wilson plaquette action of lattice gauge theory.

## 5.6 Chirality, anomaly cancellation, and hypercharges

The local gauge structure in §5.5 uses a specified charged action on the condensate branch. To reproduce observation, the weak factor must act chirally, the color coupling must be vector-like and the family charges must satisfy the anomaly equations. The open physical chirality hypothesis is H-χ' (SM §4.8).

**Spin–taste grading and physical chirality.** Weak $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ couples only to left-handed fermions in the Standard Model. On the stated checkerboard branch the trace-out selects one spin–taste grade, containing both spin chiralities. A spin-chiral weak action on that grade requires H-χ'.

The starting point is the staggered Dirac structure of the wave equation, established in §5.4. The Susskind decomposition assigns one Dirac spinor component to each hypercube corner of the cubic lattice, with the parity of the corner determining the ε-grade — the product $\gamma_5 \otimes \xi_5$ of spin and taste chirality — of the assigned component. Sites with $|\eta|$ even span the $+1$ eigenspace of $\gamma_5 \otimes \xi_5$ and sites with $|\eta|$ odd the $-1$ eigenspace; each grade contains both spin chiralities in equal dimension (certified exactly by `chirality_grading_probes.py`). The sublattice parity $\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})=(-1)^{\sum x_\mu}$ realizes this product grading.

The staggered Dirac operator has a structural property that the framework's chirality derivation uses: it couples only across sublattices. The even-even and odd-odd blocks of $D_{\text{st}}$ vanish identically because the staggered Dirac operator anti-commutes with the sublattice parity, $\{D_{\text{st}}, \varepsilon\} = 0$, which is the lattice form of chiral symmetry established in §5.4. The full Dirac operator has the block form
$$D_{\text{st}} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & D_{eo} \\ D_{oe} & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
with off-diagonal blocks coupling the two sublattice grades and diagonal blocks vanishing.

The framework's checkerboard partition assigns even sites — the $+1$ grade of $\gamma_5 \otimes \xi_5$, carrying both spin chiralities — to the visible sector $V$, and odd sites — the $-1$ grade — to the hidden sector $H$. This is not an arbitrary choice; it is what the framework's structural partition produces for a cubic lattice with bipartite coupling. The visible sector that the embedded observer reads out is therefore one $\gamma_5 \otimes \xi_5$ grade — which carries BOTH spin chiralities, so calling it the "left-handed sublattice" would prejudge the taste-selectivity question; the complementary grade is part of the hidden sector that is traced over.

The trace-out structure determines the grading content of the emergent quantum field theory; physical chirality enters through the taste-selectivity hypothesis H-χ' (SM §4.8). Integrating out the hidden sector (the odd sublattice) via the Schur complement — a standard procedure in lattice quantum field theory — produces an effective action whose external legs lie in the visible half of the spin⊗taste space, where spin- and taste-chirality coincide:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} = \bar\psi_+\,(D_{eo}\,G_{oo}\,D_{oe})\,\psi_+ + \cdots,$$
where $G_{oo}$ is the hidden-sector propagator. The hidden-grade components appear only as internal lines; the observable external states carry both spin chiralities, graded so that spin chirality equals taste chirality.

The two gauge factors couple differently to this graded effective theory. The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ factor acts on internal $T_1$ components of the cubic decomposition — the three-dimensional spatial-vector representation. Both left-handed and right-handed Dirac components carry the same color charge, since the color index is internal to the $T_1$ block and independent of the sublattice parity. The trace-out preserves color coupling, and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ remains vector-like, coupling identically to left-handed and right-handed components. The $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ factor acts on internal $E$ components — the two-dimensional quadrupole representation. The internal $E$ index commutes with the grading, so the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ embedding as constructed is taste-blind; its observed spin-chirality therefore requires taste-chirality selectivity of the physical embedding — the named hypothesis H-χ' (SM §4.8), which the construction does not supply.

On the stated taste-blind color embedding, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is vector-like. The chirality of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ rests additionally on H-χ', which is open. The framework's account of parity violation is correspondingly conditional: the substratum supplies the ε-grading and the visible-sector identity (spin chirality $=$ taste chirality on the visible half), and the remaining step — H-χ' — is stated as an open hypothesis rather than derived from which sublattice the embedded observer occupies.
