## Overview

The book develops Observation Incompleteness (OI) from its foundational claim through physics and applications. The foundational result is an exact finite-horizon equivalence: finite stochastic laws, finite reversible deterministic realizations, and fixed-basis unitary/Born representations coincide. History readback then identifies the nontrivial OI sector by forcing hidden predictive memory. In the spatial theory, the coupling graph supplies exact causal cones for finite-range substrata. Bell then imposes a fork on the deterministic completion: retaining measurement independence means quantum Bell violation requires ontic parameter dependence. In the specific SM/GR branch the target realization is preparation-indexed adjacency in the existing state-dependent graph $G(x)$; H-Bell is the open requirement that those prepared graphs preserve operational no-signaling and the metric/Ollivier--Ricci continuum limit. The measurement-dependent alternative is recorded quantitatively but not adopted.

The manuscript therefore uses “equivalence of quantum mechanics and embedded observation” in a precise sense rather than as a slogan. The stochastic/quantum-representation equivalence is exact and bidirectional for finite observable laws. With supplied quantum probabilities, one finite response table covers a fixed initial state, finite instrument menu and finite horizon. A finite instrument net gives arbitrary prescribed accuracy for bounded instrument families at fixed initial state. The remaining qualification concerns full operational quantum mechanics: the entire coherent instrument/composite hierarchy must be represented by one compatible standard local quantum theory. That residual is kept explicit without weakening the equivalence already proved.

## Structure of the book

The book is organized into four parts following the framework's intellectual cascade.

**Part I — Foundations** (Chapters 1–4) presents the central theorem, the substratum-level construction, the hierarchical structural-realism interpretation, and the methodological posture.

**Part II — Physics** (Chapters 5–9) develops the Standard Model derivation, the gravitational sector, the JUNO neutrino retrodiction as a focused case study, and the framework's relationship to string theory and other universality classes.

**Part III — Emergence** (Chapters 10–12) traces the cascade from substratum to chemistry to biology to intelligence: the structural chain from $d = 3$ through orbital structure to organic chemistry, the conditions for heredity and evolution in a template replicator, and the existence question.

**Part IV — Applications** (Chapters 13–19) develops the framework's empirical reach into working scientific fields: quantum biology, the conditional BQP theorem for a uniform scalable model with efficient quantum controls, efficient simulation, operational closure and charged conditioning, engineered quantum systems, medicine and epigenetics, bioinformatics, the framework's forward predictions beyond QM and GR, and a systematic inventory against the major open problems of fundamental physics.

A back matter section provides the prediction-status table, mathematical derivations, common objections, glossary, and bibliography.
