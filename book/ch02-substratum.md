## 2.1 What this chapter constructs

Chapter 1 established the exact finite observable-law equivalence between stochastic processes, finite reversible deterministic realizations, and fixed-basis unitary/Born representations. It also showed that genuine history readback forces hidden predictive memory in every faithful completion. This chapter now asks the inverse structural question: what is the finite reversible object $(S,\varphi)$ whose observer-level projection has that description?

Three questions must be kept separate. First, what local lattice/gauge outputs follow **on the selected cubic branch**? Theorem 23 gives conditional answers under that branch's additional physical premises; it does not derive a unique microscopic realization from E1–E7 and A1–A6 alone. Second, which state changes preserve coherent channels, rooted populations, or observables across all partitions? Theorem 24 gives some exact invariances and a fixed-interface non-exhaustiveness result, not a full classification. Third, can a common Bell-compatible, gravity-compatible physical realization be constructed? H-Bell and the physical emission/readout map remain open.

The quantum input to this reconstruction should be read in the scoped sense established in Chapter 1. The visible law has an exact quantum representation, spatial OI provides graph causal cones, and finite quantum records have finite reversible realizations. The still-conditional operational-lifting step for arbitrary coherent instruments/composites is not silently consumed as though it had already been proved; where the reconstruction uses stronger standard-QM structure, that structure is an empirical input or a named modeling premise.

## 2.2 The substratum as a finite lossless memory

The framework's central object is a triple. $S$ is a finite set whose elements are the distinguishable total states. $\varphi: S \to S$ is a bijection — a deterministic, reversible transition rule that maps each state to its unique successor. $V \subsetneq S$ is the visible sector, the observer's window into $S$; the complement $H = S \setminus V$ is the hidden sector. These three together — $(S, \varphi, V)$ — fix everything: the dynamics, the observer's access, the marginalized description.

The most useful physical interpretation is in terms of memory. $S$ is the set of all distinguishable states the system can occupy — a finite storage capacity. $\varphi$ is a bijection, so information is never created or destroyed; the substratum is a *lossless* memory in the precise information-theoretic sense. The partition $V$ defines the observer's access to this memory, both read and write. The observer reads the visible sector by measuring $x$, and writes to the hidden sector — every visible-sector interaction imprints correlations onto $H$ through the coupling $H_{\text{int}}$.

The observer-level statistics of this read-write cycle have an exact finite quantum representation. The structural form of C2 is persistence: information written into the hidden sector must survive until the readback that uses it; a slow bath is one sufficient mechanism rather than the definition. C4 is what makes the stored information operationally matter. Chapter 1 proves that such readback forces hidden predictive memory in every faithful completion. Interference and entanglement are represented quantum-mechanically in the corresponding observer-level description; identifying every possible coherent intervention with the standard quantum instrument algebra is the narrower operational-lifting statement kept explicit there.

This memory interpretation makes several of the framework's structural quantities concrete. The $10^{122}$ vacuum-energy discrepancy of Chapter 1 is the compression ratio between total storage and readable storage — between the full configuration space $S$ and the visible sector $V$. The dark sector of cosmological observation is the gravitational effect of the unreadable storage in $H$. The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy is the storage capacity of the partition boundary, the number of independent modes crossing $\partial V$ times the entropy $\sigma$ of one mode. The action scale $\hbar$ is the conversion factor between storage geometry (boundary area) and read-write statistics (the unitary group's parameter). Chapter 7 develops each of these under the conditions it names; for the present chapter they motivate the interpretation.

The substratum dynamics is defined on the full configuration space, not on the visible sector alone. The wave equation that selects $\varphi$ among second-order reversible nearest-neighbor dynamics — developed in detail in Chapter 5 — operates on both $V$ and $H$. The partition imposes an observer-access structure on this common dynamics but does not change the dynamics itself. Both sectors are governed by the same underlying rule; the only difference is which states the observer can read.

## 2.3 What the substratum is and is not

The substrate objection asks what physical degrees of freedom realize the reversible state space $(S,\varphi)$. OI proposes a structural, rather than material-substance-first, ontology: the observer accesses only part of the microscopic evolution, and different representations can agree on its accessible data. That is an interpretive commitment, not a theorem showing the substratum is the unique complete account of reality. Which differences are *gauge* must be established relative to a specified observation interface (§2.5), and a deeper physical implementation of the model is not ruled out.

A closer analog than physical substance is the abstract structure of a computation. A Turing machine has three components: a tape (the storage medium), a head (the read-write access window), and a transition function (the update rule). The mapping to the substratum is direct: $S$ corresponds to the tape, $V$ to the head's access window, $\varphi$ to the transition function. But three differences are physically significant.

First, a Turing machine's tape is potentially infinite while $S$ is finite. Finiteness is essential for the Poincaré recurrence argument that drives the P-indivisibility theorem of Chapter 1, and for the holographic bound on storage capacity of any bounded region.

Second, a Turing machine is generally irreversible — it can erase, overwrite, and halt. The substratum bijection $\varphi$ is reversible by Lemma 3 of Chapter 1: nothing is erased, nothing is created, there is no halting state. Reversible Turing machines (Bennett, Fredkin–Toffoli) are a well-studied subclass; the substratum sits in this class.

Third, a Turing machine computes an *extrinsic* function — input from an external user, output to an external observer. The substratum computes no extrinsic function. It is a closed permutation that cycles through its states; no input, no output, no halting condition. The appearance of dynamics, probability, particles, and forces is entirely the observer's perspective — what the permutation looks like through the partial window $V$.

The third difference motivates a question about a component observing a larger reversible computation. Chapter 1 establishes the finite observable-law correspondence and its predictive-memory criterion. It does not establish permanent state opacity or a unique full physical quantum completion from subsystem status alone.

The computational analogy concerns access to a reversible state update. It does not identify finite hidden-state inference with Gödel incompleteness or Turing's halting problem. A proper instantaneous readout can omit coordinates that a retained sequence later identifies. The exact finite-law representation and predictive-memory results do not assume that every hidden distinction remains unknown.

For known dynamics, preparation support and retained records, the compatible record fibre determines what can be inferred [Substratum Proposition 24.5]. Under $(v,h)\mapsto(h,v)$ the two readings $(X_0,X_1)=(v_0,h_0)$ identify the initial state and the current hidden bit $H_1=X_0$. With a uniform initial law, $X_2=X_0$ still gives one bit of conditional readback memory. Thus C4 can hold while each full-history hidden posterior is a point mass. Persistent passive ambiguity requires non-singleton future-trace classes on the admissible support; new preparations or interventions can change those classes.

Two further kinds of hidden data must be distinguished by the actual observer interface. An independently prepared, exactly decoupled deep sector changes no coherent visible channel, even when its ancillary Hilbert space is infinite-dimensional with a normalized trace-class state and the partial trace is defined. The local alphabet size $q$ is not in general a gauge quantity: Chapter 5 proves specific results for every $q$, while equality of transition laws across distinct alphabets is an additional hypothesis. The hidden state itself is inaccessible only to the extent established by the observer's preparation and readout; the inaccessible degrees of freedom are not automatically one complete gauge orbit. Section 2.5 distinguishes these comparisons rather than asserting a single completed quotient.

The local reconstruction in the next section derives specified lattice/gauge **outputs on a selected cubic branch**, with the nearest-neighbor wave rule, H-link, the $m=1$ carrier and the relevant matter and horizon hypotheses named. The empirical inputs and A1–A6 alone have not been proved to select that branch uniquely modulo all observational equivalences. Bell-inclusive existence remains conditional on H-Bell and uniqueness of that completion is open.

## 2.4 The reconstruction theorem

Chapter 1 developed a universal representation of finite visible laws by reversible deterministic models. This **existence** direction does not invert to a unique microscopic dynamics. Section 2.4 therefore records the physical and structural consequences of a *selected* local cubic branch, distinguishing these conditional consequences from the still-open uniqueness of a substratum consistent with observations.

Theorem 23 uses empirical and structural inputs together with additional branch premises. E1–E7 describe idealized observed structures; A1–A6 restrict the candidate models. The nearest-neighbor cubic wave rule, H-link with $m=1$, the octahedral representation, amplitude-scale gauge and the relevant physical hypotheses specify the branch on which the local conclusions follow. These premises define a conditional calculation. They do not establish uniqueness within the broader A1–A6 class.

**Empirical inputs.** The reconstruction draws on seven structural inputs about observed physics, six established by experiment and one (E3) a theoretical bound:

*(E1) Unitary quantum mechanics.* The observed microscopic physics is described by states in a complex Hilbert space evolving unitarily, with observables as self-adjoint operators and measurement outcomes following the Born rule. This is Chapter 1's framework input.

*(E2) Bell violations.* Observed correlations between spatially separated measurements violate Bell-type inequalities, ruling out local hidden-variable theories satisfying factorizability. Confirmed experimentally since Aspect's 1982 measurements and refined in loophole-free tests since 2015.

*(E3) Finite boundary entropy.* The entropy of any bounded region of spacetime is finite and scales as the boundary area rather than the volume. This is the holographic bound — a theoretical bound rather than a direct observation — supported by black-hole thermodynamics and applied to the cosmological horizon.

*(E4) Spatial isotropy.* Observed physics is rotationally invariant; no spatial direction is preferred. Confirmed across observations from the CMB to laboratory tests of fundamental constants.

*(E5) Propagating gravity.* Gravitational degrees of freedom propagate as waves with the observed signature of two transverse polarization states, directly detected since LIGO 2015.

*(E6) Stable matter.* Atoms, planets, and bound states exist on observed timescales. This requires both gravitational and atomic-scale stability against the dynamics.

*(E7) Boundary-entropy concordance.* The cosmological parameter inferred from observation under the standard cosmological model is spatial flatness near the critical density: $\rho_s / \rho_{\text{crit}} \approx 1$. The framework's general-$d$ form of this ratio is $\rho_s / \rho_{\text{crit}} = 2/(d-1)$, which equals unity only at $d = 3$.

The inputs E5, E6, E7 each provide an independent dimensional constraint. Propagating gravity (E5) requires $d \geq 3$, since the Weyl tensor governing gravitational-wave propagation vanishes in $d \leq 2$. Stable matter (E6) requires $d \leq 3$, since the Coulomb potential gives unstable atoms in $d \geq 4$ (Ehrenfest's theorem) and gravitational orbits are unstable in $d \geq 4$ (Tangherlini's result). Boundary-entropy concordance (E7) gives $d = 3$ exactly. Without stable matter no embedded observers exist; without propagating gravity no observable GR exists; without concordance the spatial-flatness data is unexplained. The three filters converge on $d = 3$ by independent routes, making the spatial dimension a forced consequence rather than an input to the reconstruction.

The convergence has an external echo. A literature independent of this framework reaches the same selection from different starting points: Ehrenfest's stability analysis (atoms and planetary orbits are stable only for $d = 3$), the sharp-propagation property of the wave equation (Huygens' principle holds cleanly only in odd spatial dimensions, and distortion-free signal propagation singles out $d = 3$, as Hadamard's analysis of the wave equation shows), and Tegmark's predictability argument (field equations well-posed enough for observers to process information select $3+1$). These are convergent arguments rather than theorems, and they are not an independent axiom base in the sense of the reconstruction theorems of Chapter 1: like the framework's own three filters, they condition on the existence of stable structure and embedded observers. Their force is corroborative texture — several instruments, pointed from different premises within the same observer-admitting family, land on the same integer.

**Structural assumptions.** The reconstruction restricts attention to candidate substrates satisfying six properties:

*(A1) Finiteness.* The configuration space $S$ is finite. Lemma 1 of Chapter 1 delivers finiteness of the observer's distinguishable states; the extension to all of $S$ is a posit, read as the choice of the minimal finite representative and supported by E3 (the holographic bound read as a dimension cutoff).

*(A2) Determinism.* The dynamics $\varphi: S \to S$ is a bijection — deterministic and reversible. Its status is two-part (Chapter 1, Lemma 3): the bijective substratum is a representation choice — the minimal recurrent bijective representative of the hidden dynamics — and injectivity is anchored by a dilemma argument with one structural prong (finiteness and recurrence) and one empirical prong (the observed statistics).

*(A3) Bounded coupling degree.* Each site of the substratum is coupled to a bounded number of neighbors through $\varphi$. This is required for the emergence of a coupling graph with well-defined dimensional structure and underlies locality in the emergent description.

*(A4) Center independence.* The dynamics does not depend on a choice of preferred site or origin: $\varphi$ commutes with lattice translations up to gauge, and the update at a site contains no explicit copy of that site's present value. The second property is what the derivation of the wave equation in Stage 2 below uses.

*(A5) Linearity.* The wave equation governing $\varphi$ is linear. This is a sharpened stipulation: it is equivalent to amplitude-scale gauge — the unobservability of the absolute field scale — which is an adjoined operational input rather than a theorem; nonlinear alternatives are excluded exactly to the extent that input is assumed, and would otherwise require a separate derivation.

*(A6) Background independence.* The dynamics is covariant under spatially varying internal-index transformations: a site-dependent transformation of the internal index is a symmetry provided the coupling data carried on the links are transformed with it. The content of the assumption is this covariant interface — the coupling is link-valued data on which the transformations act — and once it is in place the local transformation law imposes no further condition on the rule. It is not the stronger fixed-background condition that the same transformations leave the dynamics invariant with the coupling held fixed, which for an invertible constant coupling forces the transformation to agree across every coupled pair of sites and fails at the symmetric point of the coupling matrix for every transformation that differs across a coupled pair; the global commutant symmetry — the transformations constant across the lattice that leave the coupling unchanged — is the specialization from which the local gauge reading of Chapter 5 proceeds. The state-dependent coupling graph, under which the graph itself evolves with the state, is a separate principle that shares the name.

Theorem 23 gives **conditional selected-branch results**, not a unique inverse reconstruction. Besides E1–E7 and A1–A6, its local gauge/matter conclusions depend on a nearest-neighbor second-order cubic rule, H-link at $m=1$, the octahedral representation, amplitude-scale gauge and the separate [SM] physical assumptions. The relation $\hbar=c^3\epsilon^2/(4G\sigma)$ further requires H-slope and the horizon/frame conditions of Chapter 7. Equality of visible channel families does not entail the hidden-unitary conjugation required for general semigroup-transfer uniqueness; the six-state Pair C counterexample establishes this limitation. A common Bell-violating completion is separately conditional on H-Bell, and its uniqueness is open.

**Stage 1: From observed quantum mechanics to a deterministic embedding.** The inputs are the quantum-mechanical content E1, the Bell-violation content E2, the boundary-entropy bound E3, the finiteness and determinism assumptions A1, A2, and the two-clause modeling premise M1 — M1-T puts the temporal data used in Stage 1 in the history-readback sector, while M1-B puts Bell-violating composites on the measurement-independent, ontically parameter-dependent branch. Chapter 1's characterization theorem (§1.8) supplies the construction: any accessible non-Markovian process arises from marginalization of a deterministic bijection satisfying C1, C3 and C4 for some partition — and every finite observable law, memory-free sectors included, admits some deterministic realization (C2 outside the equivalence, [Main §3.4]). M1-T's observed temporal memory structure certifies the C1/C3/C4 history-readback sector, while M1-B's Bell violation independently requires Bell-relevant cross-wing coupling/ontic parameter dependence; the observed memory-bearing phenomenology — not quantum representability, which is universal — grounds C2 and C3, with C2's slow-bath necessity conditional on the hidden-sector mixing hypothesis (ETH its physical motivation) and its persistence form holding without it. The output of Stage 1 is a triple $(S, \varphi, V)$ with $S$ finite, $\varphi$ a bijection, and $V \subset S$ the visible-sector partition, with the observed quantum dynamics reproduced as the marginalization of $\varphi$ over the hidden sector $H = S \setminus V$.

At Stage 1, the Stinespring theorem controls only the **finite channel/dilation datum used by the temporal reconstruction**: its familiar dilation freedom lies in hidden-sector/gauge directions. It does not make the full triple $(S,\varphi,V)$ unique across coherent composites, does not select a preparation-indexed Bell graph, and does not discharge the separate finite-predictive-dimension, local-tomography or operational-purification requirements of the full lift.

**Stage 2: Conditional cubic-lattice architecture.** Starting from an explicitly selected nearest-neighbor second-order cubic wave-rule branch, the remaining assumptions and empirical filters constrain dimension, internal-index representation, gauge/matter outputs and discrete symmetries. Bounded coupling (A3) alone does not select nearest-neighbor dynamics; H-link and $m=1$ are additional branch choices. The calculations below remain valid at their cited scope, rather than an exhaustive inversion of every compatible substratum.

The dimension argument follows the three-filter convergence described above. By Gromov's theorem combined with statistical isotropy (E4), the coupling graph is quasi-isometric to $\mathbb{Z}^d$ for some integer $d$. The three filters from E5, E6, E7 then force $d = 3$.

The wave equation follows from center independence (A4), isotropy (E4), and linearity (A5), together with the nearest-neighbor restriction (an assumption beyond A3's bounded coupling degree). The unique second-order reversible nearest-neighbor dynamics compatible with these constraints has the form $f = \alpha(x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_{2d}) \bmod q$, with coupling coefficient $\alpha$. The coefficient is not the propagation speed: causal support travels at most one lattice edge per update whatever its value, and on the observer-level normalized branch it equals $1/d$.

Under those hypotheses it yields the stated candidate sectors, representations and hypercharge relations, including $(Y_Q,Y_u,Y_d,Y_L,Y_e)=(1/6,2/3,-1/3,-1/2,-1)$; it is not an unconditional deduction of three physical generations and a Higgs doublet from incomplete observation alone.

T-invariance of the bijection $\varphi$ combined with detailed balance gives reciprocal visible transition counts and narrows the QCD angle to $\theta\in\{0,\pi\}$; it does not fix $\bar\theta$, which is open (SM §5). The QCD theta-angle, which has been measured to be smaller than $10^{-10}$ but has no explanation within the Standard Model as conventionally formulated, is exactly zero as a consequence of the substratum's structural T-symmetry.

**Scope of Stage 2.** The separate dimension, wave-rule, carrier and representation constructions do not constitute a chain of biconditionals over all reversible bounded-degree models. In particular, a nearest-neighbor condition is stronger than A3, $m=1$ is not selected by coupling-degree minimization, and the cubic/octahedral carrier is a branch-specific physical choice. With this calibration the boundary-mode count reproduces the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy $S = A/(4 \ell_P^2)$, the $1/4$ coefficient being the ratio $2\pi/8\pi$ of the KMS period to the first-law prefactor that the calibration matches.

The result is a set of mathematical consequences of that construction, not a unique substratum theorem from the original E/A inputs alone.

The significance of the result is the constrained structure of the selected cubic model, not a proof that the empirically motivated E/A assumptions alone force that model. ## 2.5 Three observational equivalences and the proposed gauge structure

The observationally irrelevant distinctions depend on *what is being observed*. Fix a factorization $S=V\times H$ and the preparation of the hidden sector. **Fixed-interface coherent-channel equivalence** compares the reduced maps $\Phi_t$ on *all* visible density matrices, at *every* time, keeping preparation and readout fixed. **Population equivalence** compares only the rooted transition counts $T_{ij}(t)$ in one visible measurement basis. The weaker population data need not fix the action of the channel on coherent superpositions. A third, distinct comparison is equality of specified observations **across all admissible partitions**; this requires correspondence of the partitions, preparations and readouts before it is well-defined. A passive change of every coordinate, readout and preparation together is always a change of description, and proves nothing about the physical invariance of a fixed readout.

**Theorem 24 (scoped fixed-interface invariances).** For a finite visible/hidden product and uniform hidden preparation, conjugating the microscopic permutation by a hidden permutation $\pi(v,h)=(v,\sigma(h))$ that is the *same in every visible fibre* leaves the complete coherent-channel family $\Phi_t$ unchanged. A larger relabelling $\pi(v,h)=(v,\sigma_v(h))$, with a potentially different hidden permutation in each visible fibre, preserves rooted *populations* $T_{ij}(t)$ at every iterate, but not in general coherent-channel data. The proof is exact: uniform hidden preparation is invariant under each fibre permutation and the visible coordinates are unchanged by the outer conjugation; coherent inputs additionally require the same hidden action on every fibre for the partial trace to cancel.

An arbitrary relabelling of all states is **not** gauge at a fixed readout. For example, on visible and hidden bits let $\varphi(v,h)=(h,v)$, then conjugate by the permutation exchanging $(0,0)$ with $(1,0)$ while fixing the other states. The original one-step visible-population matrix has every entry $1/2$, while the conjugate's one-step matrix is the identity. These are physically distinguishable at the chosen interface, even though the total-state permutations are conjugate. If the readout and preparation are transported simultaneously, the resulting equality is only passive relabelling.

**Exactly decoupled enlargement.** Adjoin an independently prepared hidden ancilla with normalized state and a unitary evolution that never couples to the original system. The tensor-product evolution and partial trace leave the complete visible $\Phi_t$ unchanged for every $t$. This is true for finite ancillary spaces and for suitably defined infinite-dimensional spaces with a normalized trace-class state. A coupled, even slowly coupled, deep sector does not satisfy that exact identity; its accessible-time approximation is a separate statement. Nor does finite recurrence transfer to an infinite enlargement.

**Alphabet and graph comparisons.** Chapter 5 specifies which structural results hold for every alphabet size $q$ *without* an additional hypothesis, which carry $q$ dependence, and where equality of transition probabilities across $q$ is a separate hypothesis. Theorems 1–6 retain their particular emergent-QM, dimensional and gravitational *structural* consequences under their stated coupling-graph assumptions. Neither unconditional $q$-insensitive results nor a coarse graph quasi-isometry alone implies that complete coherent channels agree. The Standard Model's cubic $(3,2,1)$ decomposition additionally requires the appropriate octahedral representation; general statistical isotropy cannot replace it.

**Scope of dilation uniqueness.** Stinespring's minimal dilation theorem applies to a prepared channel/isometry, not arbitrarily to the entire family generated by one microscopic bijection. Equality of the all-time coherent-channel family does not force a single hidden-unitary conjugation of its generating microscopic dynamics: the exact six-state Pair C result establishes the obstruction even with trivial hidden commutants. The stronger **Proposition 24.2** remains valid: if the visible blocks of a fixed-carrier pair span the full hidden matrix algebra and *all corresponding block-word traces* agree, a single hidden unitary conjugates the pair; conversely, that conjugation preserves the word traces. Its forward and reverse directions have Lean witnesses. The channel-family observations alone do **not** supply that stronger premise.

**Recovery from complete multi-time records.** [Substratum, Propositions 24.3–24.4] gives two conditional repairs. Complete freely composable word probabilities determine a finite reachable and observable linear predictor up to similarity; competing dimensions $n,m$ require words only through $n+m-1$, and known minimal rank $d$ admits a Hankel construction through $2d-1$. Similarity alone need not be a physical state relabelling. A stronger result holds for one fixed finite permutation with a passive deterministic readout: if each compared model's initial law has full support on its own future-trace quotient and a bound $B$ on both quotient sizes is known, words through length $B$ identify that labelled quotient and its prior up to a unique permutation ($B\ge2$; one-letter data for $B=1$). The prior need not be stationary. Common-length block events isolate quotient states, so the quotient's Hankel rank equals its state count. This recovers predictive structure; it does not separate microscopic states with identical future readouts or establish hidden-factor/coherent-channel gauge completeness. A single rooted visible preparation does not automatically supply the needed full-support ensemble.

**Completeness status.** For the proposed *restricted* coherent-channel generators—uniform hidden relabelling and exactly decoupled hidden enlargement—Pair C proves **non-exhaustiveness**: two generators of the same fixed-interface channel family are separated by a balanced block-word trace invariant that these moves cannot change, even when composed and with the allowed matching visible relabellings controlled. For population equivalence, fibre-dependent hidden relabellings give a larger symmetry and the same Pair C obstruction does not establish non-exhaustiveness. Completeness across *all* admissible observer partitions remains **open**. These verdicts refer to different observables, and none should be substituted for another.

**Time reversal and global phase.** The chosen wave-rule branch can admit a layer-swap $T$ relating $\varphi$ to $\varphi^{-1}$, but because $T$ changes the visible temporal layers this need not be a hidden-only coherent-channel gauge generator. A global phase of an emergent unitary introduces no extra finite-state bijection. Neither point restores the refuted completeness inference.

**The proposed gauge hierarchy.** The substratum's observer-relative equivalences, the selected cubic branch's Standard Model field-gauge algebra, and the emergent Hamiltonian's diagonal-unitary freedom are distinct mathematical structures. The Standard Model representation result can be retained under its own assumptions without asserting that its gauge group is already proved to be the *complete image* of a full substratum gauge group under trace-out. Nor are these groups proved to nest as an inclusion chain; their observational carriers and comparison maps must be specified first.

**Structural realism at this scope.** Different representatives that are genuinely indistinguishable under a *specified* observer interface contain surplus information relative to that interface. This supports an observation-relative realist interpretation of their common structure. It does not imply that the selected cubic branch is the only realization of E1–E7 and A1–A6, that every different microscopic construction is gauge-equivalent, or that a unique Bell-complete ontology has been determined. Those are separate research questions.

## 2.6 The three projections

The preceding sections have identified conditional cubic-branch outputs and a set of exact observer-relative invariances, together with limits and counterexamples to their completeness. They do **not** uniquely reconstruct $(S,\varphi)$ or classify every observational gauge transformation. The following projection discussion is a proposed common-substratum interpretation with independently stated operational, Bell and gravitational premises.

The framework's claim is that quantum mechanics, general relativity, and the arrow of time are not three independent theories. Conditional on H-Bell at the Bell/local-lattice interface, they are three projections of the same finite deterministic construction. Each projection applies the same trace-out machinery to the same $(S, \varphi)$ along a different axis, and each produces a distinct emergent structure as the observer sees the substratum from inside.

**The visible-sector projection: quantum mechanics.** At a finite observable horizon, the reduced stochastic law, finite reversible realization and fixed-basis unitary/Born representation are exactly equivalent: $S\iff D\iff Q_{\rm fb}$. C4 readback forces hidden predictive memory, and finite-range graph dynamics gives its stated causal cones. The simultaneous coherent instrument/composite lift and H-Bell are additional physical obligations. A measurement-independent response-complete deterministic completion with parameter independence is Bell-local; the proposed violating branch is ontically parameter-dependent with operational no-signaling imposed separately.

For the selected local cubic branch, the exact link decomposition and conditional condensate stabilizer support the charged quotient construction of Chapters 5–6. Physical gauge action, chiral generations, Higgs interpretation and light-spectrum selection retain their individual premises. They are not consequences of the trace-out formula alone. **The horizon-clock projection: the arrow of time.** A third projection applies the trace-out machinery to the horizon-growth parameter along an observer worldline. The cosmological horizon entropy provides a structural clock. Every observer in the accelerating phase agrees that $S_{\text{dS}}$ increases — the monotonicity is a feature of the causal partition rather than of any local frame — and other arrows of time align with this structural clock through a cascade. The thermodynamic arrow descends from Jacobson's coupling between boundary entropy and matter entropy. The memory arrow descends from C2 — persistence, realized by the slow bath — which preserves correlations forward along the clock rather than backward. The measurement arrow — the observer records past outcomes and prepares future states — descends from the memory arrow. The horizon-clock construction proposes a monotonic parameter on an appropriately ordered observer worldline, with the thermodynamic and memory arrows subject to the stated horizon, mixing and readback assumptions. **The common structure.** The three projections share three features. Under H-Bell they apply to the same total substratum $(S, \varphi)$; without H-Bell, the local SM/GR lattice sector and the Bell-violating completion have not been shown to be one object. They use the same trace-out machinery — marginalization over the hidden sector under conditions C1–C4. They produce derived rather than fundamental structures: nothing in quantum mechanics, general relativity, or the arrow of time is a feature of $(S, \varphi)$ in itself; each is a feature of how the embedded observer sees the substratum from inside. The differences among the three projections lie entirely in which structure of the embedded observer's description they extract. The visible-sector projection works on the bulk dynamics and produces the reduced finite law — universally admitting the quantum representation — with the Standard Model as its specific content. The boundary-thermodynamic projection works on the classical thermal data at the partition boundary and produces general relativity with its gravitational and cosmological predictions. The horizon-clock projection works on the horizon-growth parameter along observer worldlines and produces the cascade of emergent arrows.

The proposed common-substratum account has a concrete mathematical architecture, but a complete physical assembly remains to be established. The selected cubic carrier supports the indicated representation-theoretic calculations; the boundary thermodynamics uses separate calibration and coupling hypotheses. H-Bell is one additional compatibility requirement for combining the reference lattice with Bell-violating composites, and the physical matter-to-boundary gravitational response remains a further open bridge. The substratum equivalences of §2.5 do not yet classify the transformations preserving every observation across all partitions, and the three projections should not be presented as an unconditional co-derivation from observation alone.

## 2.7 The QM-GR incompatibility as a category error

The conventional formulation of the QM-GR unification problem treats quantum mechanics and general relativity as two competing theories in the same logical category — two attempts to describe the same regime, awaiting reconciliation in a deeper unified theory. Every program of unification proceeds from this assumption. Quantum gravity programs treat the metric as a quantum field to be quantized. Geometric programs treat the wave function as a structure on the spacetime manifold. Emergent programs treat one of the two as derived from a deeper substrate that recovers the other. The persistent failure of all three approaches to produce a quantitatively confirmed unification — half a century of intensive work without an empirical test of the unification regime — suggests that the underlying assumption may be wrong.

The framework's claim is that quantum mechanics and general relativity are not two theories of the same regime at all. They are two projections of the same construction, viewed at different levels and through different machinery. The visible-sector projection that produces the memory-bearing dynamics quantum mechanics represents and the boundary-thermodynamic projection that produces general relativity refer to *different objects* in the construction. The wave function is a property of the embedded observer's compressed description of the visible sector — the bookkeeping device that tracks what the observer knows and does not know about a system. The metric is a property of the classical thermodynamics of the partition boundary — the geometric content of the surface across which the trace-out is performed. These are not two descriptions of the same physical system at different levels of approximation. They are descriptions of two different substructures of the same total object $(S, \varphi)$, derived by different projection maps from a single source.

The structural analogy that captures the relationship most clearly is the relationship between thermodynamics and statistical mechanics. Thermodynamics deals with pressure, temperature, and entropy. Statistical mechanics deals with particle positions, momenta, and microstate counting. These are not two competing theories of the same gas, and the question "how do we unify thermodynamics and statistical mechanics?" is not a coherent question. They describe different things about the same system. The relationship between them is one of projection — statistical mechanics produces thermodynamic quantities by averaging over microstates — rather than unification. Asking how to unify thermodynamics and statistical mechanics into a deeper theory mistakes the structure of their relationship.

The OI proposal interprets quantum and gravitational descriptions as different reductions of a common candidate substratum: reduced observable laws on one hand, partition-boundary thermodynamics on the other. This interpretation explains why it is useful to distinguish their mathematical levels. It does **not** by itself derive a single coherent quantum instrument/composite hierarchy, the gravitational field equations with physically justified matter coupling, or the complete compatibility of the two theories at a common microscopic realization.

This is not the same as saying that quantum mechanics is more fundamental than general relativity, or vice versa. Both are emergent. Both depend on the trace-out and the partition. Both are derived structures rather than features of the underlying reality. The fundamental object is $(S, \varphi)$ — a finite set with a deterministic bijection on it — and neither quantum mechanics nor general relativity is a feature of $(S, \varphi)$ itself. They are features of how an embedded observer or a horizon-bounded thermodynamic regime sees $(S, \varphi)$ from inside. (Nor is the ranking question recoverable from inside: by the factoring argument of Chapter 7, no criterion grounded in the accessible observations can separate two descriptions faithful to the same data — the fundamentality question is answered at the level of $(S, \varphi)$, not adjudicated between its projections.)

The proposed reinterpretation may change how the quantum-gravity problem is formulated, but it has **not** proved that the QM–GR incompatibility is resolved or that further quantum-gravity research is unnecessary. A consistent common realization still requires the operational quantum lift, H-Bell compatibility, matter-to-boundary coupling and the correct semiclassical/quantum-gravitational regime. These are active scientific obligations. The value of the structural picture should be judged by its successful derivations and independently discriminating predictions, not by declaring the traditional problem a category error already settled.

The framework's synthesis programme connects the selected cubic carrier and horizon constructions, but their exact mathematical steps retain different physical premises. The six-link decomposition and conditional generic condensate stabilizer are followed by a constructive correlated quotient and a specified faithful charged-family image [SM Propositions 14a and 15a]; the physical action and light spectrum are not selected by the coupling-matrix commutant. Boundary mode counting and horizon thermodynamics retain the assumptions of [GR §3]. A shared substratum motivates studying their connection without completing the matter/gravity coupling or a unique physical reconstruction.
