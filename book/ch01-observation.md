## 1.1 The problem and the theorem

An observer inside a physical system cannot condition on the complete state of that system. The central question is therefore not whether incomplete observation produces probabilities — it obviously can — but what class of laws the observer's reduced description belongs to.

The answer has two parts. First, at every finite observable horizon there is an exact representation equivalence:
$$
S\iff D\iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}.
$$
A finite stochastic law, a finite reversible hidden-state realization, and a fixed-basis unitary/Born representation are three descriptions of the same finite observable object. Conversely, every finite quantum record has a finite reversible realization. Second, history readback identifies the nontrivial sector: it forces hidden predictive memory in every faithful completion.

This chapter proves those statements, then adds their spatial scope. The coupling graph of the underlying dynamics gives exact causal cones for finite-range couplings. At the response-complete deterministic level, on a finite site set and for the nearest-neighbour cubic update with finite wing and readout regions on a site set of any cardinality, setting interventions, applied at one step, that each read and write only their own wing, readouts outside the other wing's graph cone, a $\pm1$ outcome recorded in every run, and one setting-independent pre-setting ensemble give a Bell-local model and $|S_{\rm CHSH}|\le2$. The adopted quantum-Bell branch therefore retains measurement independence and relinquishes ontic parameter independence, with operational no-signaling required. The one boundary not folded into the theorem is the full coherent instrument/composite category of operational quantum mechanics. Keeping that boundary explicit lets the chapter recover the original equivalence claim without confusing a universal representation theorem with a uniqueness theorem.

## 1.2 The starting point

The framework begins from a starting point usually put in two words: *observation occurs*. An observer records distinguishable outcomes of interactions with a system not wholly under its own control. This is the *cogito* of Descartes made mathematical, without its thinking subject — the framework's one concession to a foundation outside its own theorems.

That starting point, examined closely, is two things. What genuinely cannot be doubted is thin: there is differentiated content — some this-rather-than-that. Call this the first axiom. It is genuinely indubitable, because to doubt it is already to tell one thing from another, which is an instance of it. But notice what a single such fact does *not* contain: time. A single differentiated content is like a photograph — a frozen frame can be full of difference, light here and dark there, without anything in it being before or after anything else. To get from the photograph to a *movie* — to a world with succession, change, one moment after another — something must be added: that the differentiation *recurs*, that it is instantiated more than once. Call this the second axiom. It cannot be squeezed out of the first; a frozen frame, however rich, never amounts to a sequence of frames. The framework therefore rests not on one fact but on two: one that cannot be doubted, and one that must be assumed. This is a strength, not a hedge. A foundation that can point to its single assumed ingredient — and that comes with a theorem showing the assumed ingredient genuinely cannot be derived from the indubitable one — is sturdier than a foundation whose assumptions are scattered, unnamed, through a definition.

One qualification, stated here and developed in the companion paper. Counting the framework's foundational commitments is partly a matter of how the foundation is *presented* rather than a fact about the world: the same theory can be axiomatized in inter-derivable ways, and which structural feature is named an "axiom" and which is derived as a consequence is, within limits, a presentational choice — a notational variance, in Quine's sense, that leaves all observable physics untouched. In particular, whether the embedded observer's status as a *proper part* of a larger whole follows from the first axiom or is a further foundational posit in its own right turns on how the first axiom is read. The framework adopts the reading on which the registering occurs from a locus against a remainder, so that embeddedness follows; on a thinner reading it would be a third posit. The choice bears on how the framework counts its assumptions, not on any prediction it makes. The technical development of the foundational structure, and the no-go theorem behind it, is given in Part I of the companion paper *Physics Modulo Gauge*; the chapters that follow build on the structure without further reference to it.

**Definition.** An *observation* is a triple $(S, \varphi, V)$: a total system $S$, a deterministic dynamics $\varphi: S \to S$, and an observer $V \subsetneq S$ — a proper subsystem with finitely many distinguishable internal states, coupled to the complement $H = S \setminus V$ through $\varphi$.

The definition is weaker than classical mechanics. A shuffled deck of cards satisfies it. A finite cellular automaton satisfies it. So does a detector or a protein interior: nothing in the definition requires that anyone be aware of an outcome. What cannot be doubted is that *some* differentiated content is registered — that much is secured from within whatever locus raises the doubt. That a particular deck, automaton or detector registers anything is not indubitable but a physical fact, and the definition covers it all the same. Three lemmas extract its structural content. The dynamics uses ordered successive moments and a locus that persists across them. In the discrete model, the separate assumption that evolution composes makes the update over several ticks the iteration of the one-tick map $\varphi$ (Engel & Nagel, 2000). The phase flow of classical mechanics gives a continuous-time example of composition (Arnold, 1989). What *is* distinctive here is not that such a map exists, but finiteness: the observer distinguishes finitely many visible configurations (Lemma 1), and on the bijective representative of the finiteness posit A1 (Chapter 2 §2.4) the map acts on a finite system; finiteness of the visible sector (Lemma 1), not the dynamics, is the load-bearing commitment.

**Lemma 1 (Finiteness).** *The observer's finite capacity for distinguishable states makes $\mathcal{C}_V$ finite.*

A physical cutoff or mode-count hypothesis is needed to infer finitely many modes at a finite-area boundary. Holographic entropy bounds motivate a finite effective state count (Bao, Carroll, and Singh 2017). Chapter 7 relates the scale to the Planck length under its horizon calibration and counting hypotheses. At one nat of entropy per boundary mode the horizon area per mode is $4\ell_P^2$, and with the single-link coupling and the area-averaged count on a large spherical horizon in the lattice frame the lattice spacing is $\sqrt6\,\ell_P$.

**Lemma 2 (Causal partition).** *The product model uses $\Gamma = \Gamma_V \times \Gamma_H$ and $H_{\text{tot}} = H_V + H_H + H_{\text{int}}$. Cross-partition interaction terms belong to $H_{\text{int}}$. The preparation can already correlate the two sectors.*

$\Gamma_V$ is the visible sector; $\Gamma_H$ is the hidden sector; $H_{\text{int}}$ couples them. The observer definition supplies the proper-part distinction. The visible law uses the specified hidden preparation in addition to the dynamics and partition.

**Lemma 3 (Determinism and selected measure).** *$\varphi$ is a bijection on $S$. On each finite cycle of the reversible representative, the invariant probability measure is unique and uniform; globally the counting measure is selected among the invariant measures as the one of maximal entropy, becoming the Liouville measure on $\Gamma$ in the continuum limit:*
$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = \{H_{\text{tot}}, \rho\} \equiv \mathcal{L}\rho.$$

The observer uses standard Kolmogorov probability. The injectivity requirement is structural: dynamics that merged states differing in registered content would erase the observer's records of past outcomes — contrary to what observation is. On a finite set, injectivity implies surjectivity. This is worth putting in context: the reversibility of the constructed realizations — equivalently, that they conserve information — mirrors the standard assumption of mainstream physics rather than a peculiar demand of this framework. Unitary evolution in quantum mechanics *is* information conservation, and its classical shadow is Liouville's theorem, the conservation of phase-space volume recovered here. Physics treats this principle as effectively non-negotiable: the black-hole information paradox is called a *paradox* precisely because the apparent loss of information under Hawking evaporation would violate it, and the no-hiding theorem (Braunstein & Pati, 2007) makes the conservation statement rigorous. The framework's realizations are bijective by construction, and reversibility of nature's dynamics is not adopted as a postulate; what is distinctive, developed below, is what emerges from them.

These lemmas contain no quantum postulates. The memory-bearing description — with its admitted quantum representation — emerges under three further conditions.

## 1.3 The observer's blind spot

Light travels at finite speed; the universe has finite age. Every observer therefore has a horizon — a boundary beyond which no signal has had time to arrive. Beyond it lies ordinary physics, but it is structurally inaccessible. The laws of physics written for what the observer can see must average over everything they cannot. The hidden sector must be traced out.

The trace-out produces a stochastic process on $\mathcal{C}_V$. The visible state $x$ can be compatible with several hidden states $h$, and different $h$ may give different visible successors $x'$. Averaging over the hidden law conditional on the visible starting state in the specified preparation produces transition probabilities $T_{ij}(t)$ — the chance that $x_i$ at $t_1$ becomes $x_j$ at $t_2$. The deterministic underlying system has become, from the observer's perspective, a stochastic process.

In the Markovian regime — a fast-mixing bath, system slow relative to environment — under the mixing hypothesis the dynamics approaches the homogeneous Markov benchmark, its departure bounded in terms of the residue of mixing ([Main §3.4]). This is the standard setting of open quantum systems and produces approximately classical noise. The exact fixed-basis representation applies to a supplied law on a finite alphabet through a finite horizon, including Markov laws ([Main §3.4]). The separate history-memory result uses a C4 witness, the associated hidden influence (C1) and the capacity bound (C3). A physical slow bath is one conditional route to record persistence.

**Condition C1 (Non-zero coupling).** $H_{\text{int}} \neq 0$: hidden degrees of freedom affect the visible evolution at some accessible step — a non-permutation one-step matrix is the cheapest witness, though influence can also hide at step one and surface later. The horizon constraint correlations motivate a candidate coupling. Establishing C1 there requires showing that hidden variation affects an accessible visible conditional under a specified preparation. The proposed bidirectional flow requires a separate physical demonstration ([Main §3.4], Remark at the non-permutation-witness lemma).

**Condition C2 (Memory persistence).** A hidden record used in readback must persist until that readback. Slow uniform erosion is one sufficient mechanism when $\tau_S \ll \tau_B$, with $\tau_B$ the effective mixing time ([Main §1.3]). The cosmological application proposes Hubble-scale retention. What a laboratory partition retains still depends on its preparation and effective coupling to the putative reservoir.

**Condition C3 (Sufficient memory capacity).** The hidden sector needs enough distinguishable states for the process's observed conditional past–future information, $\log_2 |\mathcal{C}_H| \geq I^*$. The cosmological model estimates a boundary count of order $10^{122}$ modes at one nat per mode. Its use as laboratory memory requires a physical coupling and readout mechanism.

**Condition C4 (History readback).** History-sensitive hidden mediation: hidden degrees of freedom carry information about the visible past into later visible steps — an operational condition, satisfiable even by a pre-sampled hidden variable revealed by the history. The cosmological proposal uses a causal read-write cycle. That cycle is a property to be demonstrated there rather than assumed. Its witness needs a matched-seed write and later readback through the boundary (Chapter 7 §7.2). Without it, influence plus storage is mere noise: a hidden sector that records history on one set of components while a disjoint set drives the visible dynamics produces exactly Markovian statistics for as long as one cares to look.

The conditions identify different effects. C1 is influence at an eligible step, C3 is the capacity bound, and C4 is the operational witness of history dependence. C2 is persistence until readback. A fast bath under the uniform-erosion hypothesis suppresses readback, whereas the tape-and-ledger control in [Main §2.3] has influence, retention and capacity with zero conditional memory. In the storage-and-readback model, a register that is coupled, persistent, sufficiently capacious, and read back sustains the memory-bearing law when the readback carries the C4 contrast. At the lattice cut of [SM §8], C1 is verified structurally under the stated update and cut, and the C3 capacity floor holds for the realized process by data processing. C2 and C4 remain hypotheses of Theorem 22. The cosmological proposal still requires physical readback (Chapter 7 §7.2).

**A clarification on what "slow" means.** C2 is not a claim that physics fundamentally proceeds at different rates in different regions of the universe — it does not. The underlying substratum bijection proceeds at one unified rate, with no preferred clock and no "slow region." Rather, C2 is a claim about *correlation timescales*: how long correlations persist in the hidden sector before relaxing, relative to how quickly the visible sector probes those correlations. The asymmetry between the visible-sector and hidden-sector timescales comes from the partition's structure, not from any local variation in physical law.

In the cosmological application, the calibrated horizon area motivates a boundary count $N_H \sim 10^{122}$ at one nat per mode, and the horizon scale motivates $\tau_B \sim H_0^{-1} \sim 10^{17}$ s. These estimates use the specified horizon model.

Applying those estimates to C2-slow and C3 for a laboratory partition requires a relation between the laboratory preparation and coupling, its effective mixing rate and the available boundary states. That relation remains open.

For other partitions, however, C2 and C3 can be tuned independently. In biological systems (Chapter 13), the protein conformational landscape has a hidden sector substantially smaller than cosmological ($N_H \sim 10^{10}$ conformational states), with slow dynamics set by folding-landscape physics rather than horizon thermodynamics. Each condition has a different source: C3 from the configurational entropy of the conformational space; C2 from the kinetic barriers separating metastable conformations. In engineered quantum systems (Chapter 15), C2 and C3 can be tuned independently by choosing the substrate material (which controls $N_H$ through the TLS bath density) and the operating temperature (which controls $\tau_B$ through bath equilibration rates). The BEC analogue-gravity experimental program (Chapter 15 §15.8) specifically tests the framework's predictions in the regime where C3 is marginal while C2 remains satisfied — an experimental design that depends on the independence of the two conditions.

The conditions have distinct roles in the history-memory result of [Main §3.4]. A C4 witness implies hidden influence in every faithful realization, C3 bounds capacity for each supplied law, and C2-slow has the stated conditional mixing scope. The framework's empirical content (Chapters 13, 15) and forward predictions (Chapter 18) rely on the separate physical sources to make distinct quantitative claims about regimes where one condition is marginal and others are satisfied.

The finite-law representation and the history-memory characterization use separate hypotheses. The remainder of the chapter develops the proof in the stages identified in §1.1: partition-relativity (§1.4), P-indivisibility (§1.5), the two routes to unitary quantum mechanics (§1.6), Bell-inequality violations (§1.7), the characterization theorem in its current per-horizon form (§1.8), and the algorithmic reading of what the theorem establishes (§1.9).

## 1.4 Partition-relativity

The marginalized transition probabilities of the previous section depend on three inputs: the dynamics, the partition, and the measure. The framework's lemmas fix the dynamics. The measure is fixed only by adding a selection principle: Lemma 3 takes the invariant measure of maximal entropy as the canonical one. Under the canonical measure only the partition remains as a free input, and the following result establishes that this is the entire freedom: the emergent description is determined by the partition alone.

**Lemma.** *Under the canonical measure of Lemma 3, the emergent description is uniquely determined by the partition.*

*Proof.* By Lemma 3 the Hamiltonian flow $\phi_t$ is unique. By Lemma 2 the partition is fixed. An embedded observer with incomplete knowledge of the total energy averages over energies with a smooth prior, recovering the Liouville measure $\mu$ on a phase-space band — the continuum form of Lemma 3's maximal-entropy selection. Letting $\pi_V$ project onto the visible sector, the marginalized transition probabilities are
$$T_{ij}(t_2, t_1) = \int_{\Gamma_H} \delta_{x_j}\!\bigl[\pi_V\bigl(\phi_{t_2-t_1}(x_i, h)\bigr)\bigr] \, d\mu(h),$$
determined by dynamics, partition, and measure. With the dynamics fixed and the measure set to the canonical one, only the partition remains as input. $\square$

Three statements about the measure are distinct, and the lemma above is stated under the second. *Invariance.* Invariance alone does not single out a measure: on each orbit the invariant measure is unique and uniform, but a bijection with two or more cycles carries distinct invariant measures, the uniform measure on each cycle (kernel: `invariance_does_not_select` in `OIBridge/CanonicalMeasure.lean`), so the deterministic evolution by itself does not fix the measure. *Canonical selection.* Among the invariant measures, Lemma 3 selects the counting measure as the one of maximal entropy compatible with the observer's records; its continuum form is the Liouville measure. The selection is a principle the framework adopts for constructed realizations — a standard but substantive epistemic postulate, carried in the posit ledger of [Main §4.5] — not a consequence of invariance. *Realization datum.* The canonical measure is the default, not a constraint on representations. A particular realization may carry its own fixed hidden prior $\mu_H$ as part of the realization datum — a structured preparation — and the emergent law is then a function of the dynamics, the partition and $\mu_H$. The same bijection and partition under different priors generally give different visible laws; the two-state swap under a uniform and under a point-mass hidden prior is the simplest case ([Main §1.2]). Partition-relativity is therefore relative to the declared measure, and the framework's baseline transition matrices use the canonical one.

Different partitions of the same $(S,\varphi)$ can give different reduced laws for a common preparation. Chapter 7 relates $\hbar$ to the counting scale under H-slope, H-count and the stated horizon conditions. The $1/4$ entropy coefficient is a consistency relation of that calibration. The September 2025 analysis of GW250114, observed on January 14, used inferred Kerr horizon areas to test the classical area theorem. The entropy coefficient itself has no direct test.

Partition-relativity establishes that the emergent description is well-defined. The next question is what kind of description it is — whether the marginalization produces a Markovian process, a generic non-Markovian one, or something more specific. The result of this section is that, in the recurrence and non-permutation regimes established there, the process satisfies a precise structural property with a name in the open-systems literature — P-indivisibility — while C1–C4 by themselves deliver accessible non-Markovianity ([Main §3.4]). The two sections following establish how the imported correspondence supplies the quantum representation across its class, under the translation hypothesis (T) — with P-indivisibility governing whether that representation is nontrivial ([Main §3.1]), which is a claim about the two-time marginal family and does not transport the multi-time content of (C4).

## 1.5 P-indivisibility

The central technical result of the chapter is that, on a finite reversible representative, the marginalized process is P-indivisible across the recurrence cycle whenever, under the uniform hidden prior, its one-step visible matrix is not a permutation, as non-trivial coupling acting at the first step makes it. On accessible windows the proved statement is conditional memory: a (C4) readback gap forces $I(X_{<k}; X_{k+1} \mid X_k) > 0$, which does not by itself make the process P-indivisible there ([Main §2.3]).

**Theorem (P-indivisibility).** *Let $\mathcal{C}_V$ and $\mathcal{C}_H$ be finite sets with $|\mathcal{C}_V| \geq 2$, and let $\varphi: \mathcal{C}_V \times \mathcal{C}_H \to \mathcal{C}_V \times \mathcal{C}_H$ be a bijection with*
$$T_{ij} = \frac{|\{h \in \mathcal{C}_H : \pi_V(\varphi(x_i, h)) = x_j\}|}{|\mathcal{C}_H|}.$$
*With the common uniform hidden prior, if $T$ is not a permutation matrix, the rooted family fails P-divisibility by the return.*

P-indivisibility is a precise property of stochastic processes that requires unpacking. For a fixed preparation at the root time $t_1$, the family is *P-divisible* when every pair $t_1 < t_2 < t_3$ admits the row-kernel factorization
$$T(t_3, t_1) = T(t_2, t_1) \cdot \Lambda(t_3, t_2),$$
where $\Lambda(t_3, t_2)$ is a valid stochastic matrix — non-negative entries, rows summing to one. P-divisibility expresses the natural intuition that probabilistic dynamics should compose: the probability of going from $x_i$ to $x_k$ over the long interval $[t_1, t_3]$ should be obtainable by chaining the probability of going from $x_i$ to some intermediate state $x_j$ over $[t_1, t_2]$ with the probability of continuing from $x_j$ to $x_k$ over $[t_2, t_3]$. Classical Markov processes satisfy this property trivially because the future depends only on the present state, not on history. P-indivisibility means some such pair has no row-stochastic divisor. When the earlier matrix is invertible, a negative entry of the unique candidate proves the failure.

Breuer, Laine, and Piilo (2009) introduced a more directly interpretable signature of non-Markovian dynamics: *information backflow*. In a P-divisible process, the distinguishability of any two initial conditions can only decrease over time — the future loses information about the past monotonically, as in any classical Markov chain reaching equilibrium. In a P-indivisible process, distinguishability can rebound: information about the initial condition that appeared lost can return to the visible sector at a later time. A revival concerns the rooted transition matrices. The link between the finite-return criterion and history memory within the recurrence cycle uses a full-support visible prior ([Main §2.3]). In the coin–die example below, conditioning on the history reveals information about a preloaded hidden selector. The signature runs one way: a rebound certifies P-indivisibility, but a P-indivisible process need not produce one — some keep the distinguishability of every pair of initial distributions non-increasing ([Main §2.3]) — so the absence of backflow does not establish P-divisibility.

The proof of the theorem uses the total variation distance between probability distributions,
$$d(p, q) = \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_k |p_k - q_k|.$$
This quantity ranges from $0$ (identical distributions) to $1$ (distributions with disjoint support) and measures how distinguishable two probability distributions are by any measurement. For a P-divisible process, $d$ is non-increasing along trajectories: starting from $\delta_i$ (the point mass at $x_i$) and $\delta_j$ (the point mass at $x_j$), the quantity $d(\delta_i T^{(t)}, \delta_j T^{(t)})$ — where $T^{(t)}$ denotes $t$ steps of the dynamics — cannot grow with $t$. P-indivisibility is therefore established by demonstrating non-monotonic behavior of $d$ along some trajectory.

*Proof of P-indivisibility.* The proof exhibits non-monotonicity through a three-step construction.

*Step 1 (Recurrence to maximal distinguishability).* The dynamics $\varphi$ is a bijection on the finite total set $\mathcal{C}_V \times \mathcal{C}_H$. Every bijection on a finite set is a permutation, and every permutation generates a finite cyclic group: there exists an integer $N$ (the least common multiple of the cycle lengths in $\varphi$'s cycle decomposition) such that $\varphi^N = \mathrm{id}$. Applied to the marginalized dynamics, this means $T^{(N)}$ equals the identity matrix $I$. Starting from $\delta_i$ and applying $T^{(N)}$ returns $\delta_i$ unchanged; starting from $\delta_j$ returns $\delta_j$. The distinguishability is $d(\delta_i T^{(N)}, \delta_j T^{(N)}) = d(\delta_i, \delta_j) = 1$ for any $i \neq j$. After $N$ steps, the two initial conditions are again perfectly distinguishable.

*Step 2 (Strict contraction at intermediate times).* If $T$ is not a permutation matrix, then there must exist visible states $x_i$, $x_j$, and $x_l$ with both $T_{il} > 0$ and $T_{jl} > 0$. (If no such triple existed, each row of $T$ would have a single nonzero entry equal to one, making $T$ a permutation.) The existence of $x_l$ as a common destination from both $x_i$ and $x_j$ means the post-step distributions $\delta_i T$ and $\delta_j T$ both assign positive weight to $x_l$ — they overlap. For overlapping distributions $p, q$ with $p_l \wedge q_l > 0$, the total variation distance satisfies
$$d(p, q) = \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_k |p_k - q_k| < 1,$$
the inequality strict because of the overlap. So $d(\delta_i T, \delta_j T) < 1$.

*Step 3 (Non-monotonicity).* Combining the previous two steps: at $t = 0$, $d(\delta_i, \delta_j) = 1$. At $t = 1$, $d(\delta_i T, \delta_j T) < 1$. At $t = N$, $d(\delta_i T^{(N)}, \delta_j T^{(N)}) = 1$. The trajectory of distinguishability is therefore $1 \to (\text{something less than } 1) \to 1$ — non-monotonic. A P-divisible process cannot produce this trajectory because $d$ would be non-increasing throughout. The process is P-indivisible. $\square$

The proof uses the finite bijection, the common hidden prior and the non-permutation first-step matrix that gives overlap. Recurrence and condition C1 alone do not guarantee that first-step witness. C1 can be witnessed later than the first step. The general overlap-and-return criterion in [Main §2.3] permits such later witnesses. In continuous time, for a Hamiltonian flow on compact energy surfaces, the same argument needs the flow to return near-totally at a common time after the rooted maps have left the permutation class — the counterpart of $\varphi^N = \mathrm{id}$. Poincaré recurrence does not supply this for a general flow: it returns almost every state at its own time, and a mixing flow never again returns a whole visible cell at once at late times. The extension therefore holds for flows with such a common return and is open for general Hamiltonian flows ([Main §2.3]).

*Corollary (near-return).* *Let $\mathcal{C}_V$ be finite with $|\mathcal{C}_V| \geq 2$, let $\rho$ be a probability on a measurable space $H$, shared by every root, let $F$ be a measurable map of $\mathcal{C}_V \times H$, and let $\Gamma_t(a, b) = \rho\{h : \pi_V F^t(a, h) = b\}$. If $\Gamma_s$ is not a permutation matrix, some roots $a \neq b$ and value $x$ have $m = \min(\Gamma_s(a, x), \Gamma_s(b, x)) > 0$. Assume, as a hypothesis of this result and not adopted, the near-return condition (NR) for such a pair: some $n > s$ has $(1 - \Gamma_n(a, a)) + (1 - \Gamma_n(b, b)) < m$. Then the rooted family is not P-divisible on $\{0, \ldots, n\}$.* *Proof.* Each $\Gamma_t$ is row-stochastic because $\rho$ is a probability. If the $|\mathcal{C}_V|$ rows of $\Gamma_s$ had pairwise disjoint supports, the supports would partition the $|\mathcal{C}_V|$ columns into singletons and $\Gamma_s$ would be a permutation matrix; so two rows $a \neq b$ share a column $x$, and $m > 0$. Then $d(\Gamma_s(a), \Gamma_s(b)) \leq 1 - m$, while testing the event $\{a\}$ gives $d(\Gamma_n(a), \Gamma_n(b)) \geq \Gamma_n(a, a) - \Gamma_n(b, a) \geq 1 - (1 - \Gamma_n(a, a)) - (1 - \Gamma_n(b, b)) > 1 - m$. A row-stochastic $\Lambda$ with $\Gamma_n = \Gamma_s \Lambda$ would not increase the total variation distance between rows (every stochastic matrix contracts total variation), so none exists. $\square$ On a finite representative Step 1 of the proof above supplies the condition (NR) at every multiple of $N$ exceeding $s$. The condition (NR) needs no finite hidden space, invariant law, bijection or period $N$; $n$ is any time after $s$ at which its inequality holds. (NR) concerns a time that need not be accessible and then has no finite-window test; for the selected update of Main §3.2 on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ the only laws known to satisfy it are supported on periodic configurations.

**The accessible-timescale lemma.** A potential objection to the proof as stated is that the recurrence time $N$ can be astronomically large. $N$ is the order of $\varphi$ as a permutation of the $n = |\mathcal{C}_V \times \mathcal{C}_H|$ total configurations. For any bijection it is at most Landau's function $g(n)$, the largest order of a permutation of $n$ points, with $\ln g(n) \sim \sqrt{n \ln n}$, and it divides $\mathrm{lcm}(1, \ldots, n) = e^{(1 + o(1))\,n}$; under the exactly linear update rule (A5) it is below $n$ (Chapter 18 §18.7). The framework bounds $N$ but does not compute it, and $n$ is not fixed by visible data. For $N$ of astronomical size the distinguishability restoration at $t = N$ is not directly observable. What an observer can measure is memory on *accessible* timescales — times short compared to $\tau_B$, the hidden sector's intrinsic memory time — not at recurrence. The accessible-timescale lemma supplies that memory, as conditional mutual information, wherever the stored record is read back (C4). It does not supply P-indivisibility on those windows: a readback gap is compatible with a population law that is divisible on every window short of recurrence ([Main §2.3]).

For an illustration of retention, suppose that uniform exponential erosion in the iterated coarse channel gives a per-step retained fraction $r=e^{-\tau_S/\tau_B}$. A record that undergoes $k$ such steps retains the fraction $r^k\approx 1-k\tau_S/\tau_B$ when $k\tau_S\ll\tau_B$. This is a model estimate for retention of the record.

The quantitative observable bound uses readback. If two histories with the same present state each have probability at least $p_0$ and next-step laws separated by total variation at least $\delta$, then $I(X_{<k};X_{k+1}\mid X_k)\ge p_0\delta^2/\ln 2$ ([Main §2.3]). The probabilities and gap depend on the history pair and its readout.

For illustration, inserting $\tau_B\sim H^{-1}\sim 10^{17}$ s and $\tau_S\sim 10^{-15}$ s into the retention model gives $k\tau_S/\tau_B\sim 10^{-12}$ at $k=10^{20}$. The calculation assumes the proposed cosmological retention time applies to the laboratory coupling. The physical preparation, rate and readout needed to test that assumption remain open. In the separate horizon count, $e^{S_{\text{dS}}}$ modeled boundary states would have capacity $S_{\text{dS}}/\ln 2$ bits.

The witnessed readback gap gives conditional memory inside its accessible window. A non-permutation rooted map followed by the finite return gives P-indivisibility across the recurrence cycle.

**A worked six-hidden-state model.** A concrete example makes the mechanism tangible. Take a two-state visible sector $\mathcal{C}_V = \{0, 1\}$ — call it a coin with states Heads and Tails — and a six-state hidden sector $\mathcal{C}_H = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ — call it a die. The dynamics is the permutation
$$\sigma = (0{,}1 \leftrightarrow 1{,}1)\,(0{,}2 \leftrightarrow 1{,}2)\,(0{,}3 \leftrightarrow 0{,}4)\,(0{,}5 \leftrightarrow 0{,}6)\,(1{,}3 \leftrightarrow 1{,}4)\,(1{,}5 \leftrightarrow 1{,}6).$$
Each pair $(\cdot \leftrightarrow \cdot)$ is a transposition that swaps two total states; all six swaps together are independent (act on distinct states), so the full dynamics is a product of disjoint transpositions and satisfies $\sigma^2 = \mathrm{id}$.

The die is a preloaded selector. C1 holds because the coin flips for $h=1,2$ and stays for $h=3,4,5,6$. The die's successor is the same at a matched hidden seed whichever initial coin value is used. The selector survives the update and the uniform preparation gives differing next-step laws for histories that meet at the same coin value, a C4 witness. The six hidden states satisfy the capacity bound for this law.

Compute the one-step transition matrix starting from $x = 0$ (Heads) with the die uniformly distributed. The dynamics sends $(0, 1) \to (1, 1)$ and $(0, 2) \to (1, 2)$ — Heads becomes Tails for these die values. It sends $(0, 3) \to (0, 4)$, $(0, 4) \to (0, 3)$, $(0, 5) \to (0, 6)$, $(0, 6) \to (0, 5)$ — Heads stays Heads for these die values, though the die value changes within the hidden sector. So $P(0 \to 0) = 4/6 = 2/3$ and $P(0 \to 1) = 2/6 = 1/3$. Symmetric reasoning for $x = 1$ gives
$$T(1, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 2/3 & 1/3 \\ 1/3 & 2/3 \end{pmatrix}.$$
The distinguishability after one step is $d(\delta_0 T, \delta_1 T) = \tfrac{1}{2}(|2/3 - 1/3| + |1/3 - 2/3|) = 1/3$. The two initial conditions, perfectly distinguishable at $t = 0$ with $d = 1$, have become much less distinguishable: $d$ has dropped to $1/3$.

A time-homogeneous Markov chain with this one-step matrix would have $T(2, 0) = T(1, 0)^2$. Computing,
$$T(1,0)^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 5/9 & 4/9 \\ 4/9 & 5/9 \end{pmatrix},$$
with distinguishability $1/9$ in that homogeneous example. But the actual second-step matrix is $T(2, 0) = I$ because $\sigma^2 = \mathrm{id}$ sends every total state back to itself, so the visible-sector marginals are unchanged from the starting point. The distinguishability at $t = 2$ has returned to $1$. The two initial conditions are perfectly distinguishable again. Complete un-mixing — impossible for any Markov process.

The signature of P-indivisibility shows up as negative entries in the would-be intermediate propagator. Invertibility of $T(1,0)$ makes the right divisor in $T(2, 0) = T(1, 0) \cdot \Lambda(2, 1)$ unique. It is
$$\Lambda(2, 1) = T(1, 0)^{-1} \cdot T(2, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \cdot I = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
The off-diagonal entries are $-1$. No valid stochastic matrix has negative entries — the factorization through $t = 1$ fails by direct computation. The process is P-indivisible.

Starting with a uniform die, observing the coin flip tells the observer that the seed was in $\{1,2\}$. Observing it stay tells the observer that the seed was in $\{3,4,5,6\}$. Two such histories may end at the same present coin value, yet one predicts a flip and the other predicts a stay. This is posterior learning about the hidden selector. The second step returns the initial coin with certainty.

A causal write has an extra requirement: varying the visible input at a fixed hidden seed must change the later hidden state ([Main §2.3]). The two-card SWAP in §1.11 supplies that witness. The coin–die example supplies history readback from a preloaded selector. The next section takes up how these finite records are represented.

## 1.6 The two routes to quantum mechanics

There are two complementary routes from the observer's stochastic law to quantum form.

With the hidden register prepared in the corresponding prior, fixed-basis Born readout reproduces the visible stochastic law exactly, including its finite multi-time trajectory statistics. Combined with the finite process-dilation theorem in the reverse direction, this yields, for a supplied law on a finite alphabet through a finite horizon, the exact equivalence $S\iff D\iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}$.

The second route is the stochastic–quantum correspondence of Barandes. It supplies a compressed quantum representation and the associated interpretive dictionary for the source's finite-configuration class. The important logical point is that the existence of a Hilbert/unitary/Born representation does not require P-indivisibility — every finite stochastic law has such a representation. ## 1.7 Bell-inequality violations

The Bell claim must separate the deterministic completion from the reduced stochastic description. For a Bell-relevant substratum whose coupling range does not connect the wings before readout, the dependency graph gives exact pointwise separation. If each setting intervention, applied at one step, also reads and writes only its own wing, every run records a $\pm1$ outcome at each wing, and the response-complete pre-setting ensemble is independent of the settings, determinism yields an ordinary Bell-local hidden-variable model and $|S_{\rm CHSH}|\le2$. Hidden memory and indivisibility do not alter that ceiling.

The framework therefore declines the measurement-dependent/superdeterministic horn and takes **ontic parameter dependence**. In the specific SM/GR realization the target mechanism is preparation-indexed adjacency in the already state-dependent graph $G(x)$ (or an equivalent ontically nonlocal response): the vacuum/uniform reference graph remains geometric and bounded-range, while an entangling preparation may supply Bell-relevant long adjacency so a microstate response at one wing can depend on the remote setting. H-Bell is the open requirement that those prepared graphs preserve operational no-signaling and the metric/Ollivier--Ricci continuum limit. The curvature condition is downstream of a harder one. The cited convergence theorem is arbitrary-dimensional only for the manifold-distance-weighted graph metric, and for the degree-$6$ cubic reference graph the intrinsic hop metric is exactly $\ell_1$ — a $\sqrt2$ diagonal stretch that does not decay at any scale — so the hop-metric curvature route fails for the reference family, before any Bell edge is added, and no $D=3$ extension of that theorem would rescue it. The emergent geometry itself is not in question: the substratum's dispersion relation is isotropic at leading order. What must be re-founded is the curvature functional, on the propagation/Laplacian geometry rather than on shortest-path counts [SM §3.2]. The setting-dependent alternative is recorded but not adopted; Main §3.3 gives the model-independent necessary lower bound $I_{\rm ont}\ge0.0309$ bits for the quantum value. Hall--Branciard's optimized causal CHSH model uses about $0.080$ bits, while the smaller $\sim0.046$-bit benchmark is retrocausal.

The cited causal-local indivisible **stochastic** construction separately obeys Tsirelson's bound because its causal-independence notion is weaker than Bell factorization. That theorem is not the hidden-variable locality structure of the deterministic completion. If the spatial/intervention restriction is removed, the general finite OI response construction can realize PR-box behavior, showing that representability, no-signaling, hidden memory, and indivisibility do not by themselves select the quantum Bell set.

## 1.8 The characterization theorem

The characterization is best read as a layered biconditional rather than a single slogan.

**Finite representation theorem.** On any finite horizon, $S\iff D\iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}$. Every finite stochastic process has a finite deterministic reversible realization, and every such realization has an exact fixed-basis unitary/Born representation. This supplies both directions of the finite observable-law equivalence.

**Readback theorem.** C4 is the condition that makes the OI sector memory-bearing. It forces different hidden posteriors for visible histories that share a current state but have different future laws, a persistence/readback requirement, and the capacity lower bound of C3. Without A1, the near-return condition of the corollary in §1.5, assumed as a hypothesis and not adopted, is the stated sufficient condition for the indivisibility conclusion; it concerns a time that need not be accessible and then has no finite-window test; for the selected update of Main §3.2 on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ the only laws known to satisfy it are supported on periodic configurations. Under A1 the physical representative is finite and reversible, so it also recurs: its rooted visible maps, defined for every root under one hidden prior, return to the identity, and a non-permutation stochastic matrix has no stochastic inverse, so any non-permutation rooted map makes the rooted law indivisible somewhere in the recurrence cycle. A C4 gap supplies such a map, and so does every non-deterministic visible step, including in laws that are Markov on every accessible window. With a full-support visible prior, that indivisibility is equivalent to memory at some order below the recurrence time; an observer sees the memory only when it appears at an accessible order, which is what C4 asserts.

**Spatial extension.** The dependency graph supplies finite causal cones exactly for finite-range substrata. Bell adds a separate constraint: for a response-complete deterministic completion that records a $\pm1$ outcome in every run, measurement independence plus parameter independence would imply $|S_{\rm CHSH}|\le2$. Section §1.7 therefore takes the measurement-independent, ontically parameter-dependent branch for Bell-violating substrata and keeps operational no-signaling separate.

**Operational boundary.** These theorems do not prove that the entire family of coherent instruments and composites equals the standard quantum operational theory. On a finite carrier, within complex matrix kinematics taken as given, that equality holds exactly under the five completion conditions of [GR §3.3]. The substratum's stated interventions do not supply one of them, full reversible control; whether an extended OI mechanism can supply it, and which of the others the substratum supplies, are open; this is not a qualification on the exact finite stochastic/representation equivalence itself.

A second boundary sits alongside it, and it concerns which unitary evolution relates two times. The equivalence says which visible laws admit a fixed-basis unitary representation; it does not single out the evolution connecting one time to the next. For dilated processes one visible family can admit distinct coherent unitary lifts — each reproducing the visible law at every time, each composing exactly across times — whose relating evolutions differ, because a lift may be deformed by a time-dependent right action that preserves everything the visible law sees. A deformation constant in time cancels from every relating evolution, so time dependence is what the nonuniqueness needs. Making the relating evolution unique therefore requires a selection condition beyond visible-law representability and ordinary coherence, and this book does not supply one or say what form one must take. The invisible freedom itself has two sides — a right action by the anchored class and a left action within each visible output's hidden fibre, each maximal among uniform actions of its kind — and it does not exhaust the freedom: two lifts can fail to be related by any uniformly invisible right deformation and still agree on every relating evolution, and a pair outside that right class can differ in relating evolution while being related by a left in-fibre move. At one time, what the visible law leaves free after both actions is known exactly: it is the off-diagonal part of the fibre-Gram data — for each visible output, the overlaps among the hidden states into which the lift sends the visible inputs — modulo phases, with the visible law as its diagonal; and that residue is not inert, since two lifts of one visible law can differ in it and differ in relating evolution. That residue does not reach the fixed-basis layer. With a trivial ancilla, the fixed-basis datum built from a lift's unitary at one time, with uniform initial law and identity readout, has finite-horizon trajectory laws fixed by the visible law alone, also after contractively scaled partial permutations on either side, and two such data have equal laws exactly when their visible laws are equal; the fixed-basis correspondence acts on the quotient by visible equality. The trivial ancilla is part of that statement: an ancilla attached as a tensor factor with its own unitary leaves the rooted family unchanged, while an ancilla carried between steps as hidden basis states can separate two lifts that share every column entering the visible law, already in the two-step law. What remains open is two-part: what selects the trajectory of that data through time, and what fixes the relating evolution within a trajectory — the latter not fixed even by the complete trajectory. No selection principle is named or excluded. None of this weakens the finite observable-law equivalence, and none of it makes embedded observation and quantum mechanics inequivalent.

## 1.9 What the theorem says

The theorem says something stronger than “quantum mechanics can be simulated by a classical hidden model” and something more precise than “C1–C4 uniquely force all of quantum mechanics.”

It says that, for finite observable laws, the stochastic, reversible-deterministic, and fixed-basis quantum descriptions are mathematically intertranslatable. A finite embedded observer therefore does not need quantum mechanics added as a separate microscopic postulate in order for a Hilbert-space/unitary/Born description to exist. History readback then supplies the physical distinction between a trivial Markov member of that representation class and the memory-bearing sector characteristic of OI.

The converse is equally important: every finite quantum record has a finite reversible OI realization. Given the quantum conditional probabilities for a fixed preparation and finite instrument menu, one finite response table serves all protocols through the chosen horizon. The exact continuum operational theory is a mathematical idealization rather than a requirement that the physical carrier itself be infinite.

The remaining qualification is localized. To identify the complete local operational theory of complex quantum mechanics, rather than its exact stochastic representation, one must lift the whole coherent intervention and composite hierarchy into one compatible quantum instrument theory, which on a finite carrier, within complex matrix kinematics taken as given, happens exactly under the five completion conditions of [GR §3.3]. The Bell fork is logically separate: §1.7 specifies the adopted measurement-independent, ontically parameter-dependent completion and its no-signaling requirement rather than treating Bell screening as one of those completion conditions. The book keeps both boundaries explicit rather than weakening what is already exact.

## 1.10 The four structural limits

The exact equivalence $S \iff D \iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}$ applies to supplied laws on finite alphabets through finite horizons. On a horizon containing a C4 witness, every faithful deterministic realization has hidden influence (C1) and obeys the quantitative capacity bound (C3). Reversibility retains the record in exact realizations, including realizations of memoryless laws. A physical mixing-time bound for C2-slow uses the conditional-mixing hypothesis ([Main §3.4]). The following limits illustrate particular mechanisms and preparations.

**The C1 limit: the isolated observer.**

C1 requires non-trivial coupling between visible and hidden sectors. In the limit of complete failure — no coupling at all — the visible sector evolves as a *closed* deterministic system. The bijection $\varphi$ restricted to $\mathcal{C}_V$ is a permutation; the dynamics on $V$ is just the corresponding classical evolution. No information ever crosses the partition boundary. The hidden sector exists but is causally disconnected from the visible sector for all purposes.

The dynamics in this regime is fully classical in character: memoryless. The reduced description still admits the fixed-basis quantum representation — every finite law does, trivially here ([Main §3.4]) — but there is nothing memory-bearing in it: no coupling-induced non-Markovianity for a nontrivial representation to encode, hence none of the interference phenomenology the framework attributes to hidden-sector memory. No P-indivisibility arises from a partition with no coupling, since the visible sector then evolves autonomously by a permutation; Bell violation is a separate question, governed by ontic parameter dependence (§1.7), not by P-indivisibility. The observer sees a classical Newtonian world with deterministic trajectories and well-defined values of every observable at every time.

This is the regime closest to the historical picture of classical mechanics: a clockwork universe where every particle has a definite position and momentum and the equations of motion fully determine the future from the present. The framework's content is that this picture is correct for the limit $C1 \to 0$ — but no laboratory observer occupies this limit. Gravitational coupling between visible and hidden sectors is unavoidable in our universe; stress-energy conservation forces correlations across any horizon; the cosmological horizon is not an impermeable wall. Real observers are coupled, and what coupling produces is the memory-bearing quantum phenomenology — the representation itself is universal; coupling supplies the nontrivial sector.

The C1 limit therefore illuminates a structural point: classical mechanics is what *uncoupled* systems do. The framework's proposal is that the apparent quantumness of the world is a signature not of intrinsic randomness in the underlying reality but of our *non-isolation*. Quantumness here means the phenomenology, not the bare representation, which the uncoupled limit keeps; what is proved is that memory in the observer's record cannot arise without the coupling. The substratum is deterministic; what makes physics look probabilistic is that the observer is coupled to something they cannot directly see. An observer who could isolate themselves completely from everything outside their measurement apparatus would see classical mechanics. No such observer exists in our universe, and the framework's content is that no such observer can exist — gravitational coupling is universal.

The proposal has a striking consequence: the project of "explaining quantum mechanics" by finding the right deterministic substructure is well-defined within the framework, but the dynamics an uncoupled observer would see in that substructure is memoryless classical mechanics. Quantum mechanics, on this proposal, is the dynamics that *embedded* observers see when they are coupled to the substructure; the substructure itself, viewed from a hypothetical uncoupled vantage point, would be classical. The framework's reframing is that the persistent intuition "there must be a deterministic story underneath" is correct, and the deterministic story is classical, but it is structurally inaccessible to any actual observer.

**The C2 limit: the thermalized observer.**

C2 requires persistence of the record used at readback. The slow-bath mechanism uses $\tau_S / \tau_B \ll 1$ with $\tau_B$ an effective mixing time ([Main §1.3]). In the limit of complete failure — the record is erased instantly, $\tau_B \to 0$ — the hidden sector equilibrates between every visible-sector measurement. No correlation written into the hidden sector during one measurement persists to influence the next. History readback cannot occur because there is no memory in the bath for the visible sector to read back.

The dynamics in this regime is *fully Markovian*. The reduced description on the visible sector is a classical stochastic process with transition probabilities depending only on the current state. The observer sees probabilistic dynamics, but the probabilities are memoryless: the memory-bearing/interference mechanism is absent, while the fixed-basis representation survives, trivially diagonal-preserving ([Main §3.4]). This temporal statement must not be turned into a Bell claim: Bell violation in the deterministic completion is governed separately by ontic parameter dependence (§1.7), not by P-indivisibility. The Born rule reduces to the classical probability assignment for transitions between well-defined states. A Schrödinger-form unitary representation still exists — $Q_{\mathrm{fb}}$ supplies one for every finite law — but here it encodes no temporal correlations: what the regime lacks is the memory-bearing content, not the equation's existence ([Main §3.4]).

This regime corresponds to what physics calls the *thermal limit* — systems in contact with a fast equilibrating bath, where the bath's state at any moment is independent of the system's history. The classical statistical mechanics of thermal systems is exactly this limit. Brownian motion, chemical reaction kinetics in well-stirred solutions, ordinary diffusion processes — these are all Markovian dynamics described by classical stochastic processes, and the framework's content is that this is what C2 failure looks like.

A cosmological estimate uses the proposed time $\tau_B \sim H^{-1}$. Applying it to laboratory records requires the effective-channel and preparation link described in §1.3. Under effective mixing and uniform erosion, the coarse-grained dynamics generated by iterating the one-step channel suppresses the readback gap when $\tau_{\rm mix}\ll\tau_S$ ([Main §§2.3, 4.6]).

The C2 limit therefore illuminates a different structural point: classical stochastic mechanics is what *thermalized* systems do. The framework gives a structural account of why decoherence and the classical limit emerge in systems coupled to fast environments — a familiar phenomenon in quantum mechanics. Decoherence theory describes how quantum coherences are destroyed by coupling to a fast bath; the framework's reading is that decoherence is the transition from the memory-bearing regime back toward the C2 failure limit. Systems with engineered fast baths approach classical stochastic behavior; systems isolated from fast baths retain quantum coherence. The conventional thermal-classical-limit story is the framework's C2 limit told from inside quantum mechanics.

A corollary, stated carefully: what the framework requires is a hidden sector that RETAINS the record across the window, which is not the same as a frozen one. Fast hidden dynamics that conserve and route the record satisfy the condition — a shift-register bath rotating five cells per visible step supports a maximal readback gap (`fastbath_probes.py`) — so persistence, not slowness, is doing the structural work beyond merely providing some unobserved degrees of freedom. An observer whose fast hidden bath scrambles the record sees statistics with suppressed readback; one whose fast bath conserves and routes it retains the full memory-bearing description (`fastbath_probes.py`). Small $\tau_S/\tau_B$ is a proposed mechanism for persistence in the cosmological model and a parameter of the conditional channel approximation. The finite-law and history-memory theorems use their own premises ([Main §3.4]).

**The C3 limit: the universe-sized observer.**

C3 requires sufficient hidden memory capacity — $\log_2|\mathcal{C}_H| \geq I^*$, with $N_H \gg N_V$ the comfortable realization regime. A capacity shortfall caps the amount of memory that can be represented. The extreme case considered here is a trivial hidden configuration space, where the visible sector $V$ covers the total system $S$. There is no trace-out because there is nothing to trace over. The observer is *coextensive* with the universe.

The dynamics in this limit is fully deterministic and fully classical. The bijection $\varphi$ on the full system $S$ is just a permutation of classical states; the observer who is all of $S$ sees its complete deterministic evolution. No memory-bearing sector — the fixed-basis representation survives, trivially, as always ([Main §3.4]) — and no interference phenomenology, but for a different reason than in the C1 case. In the C1 limit, the visible sector is closed but the framework still applies to it as a system; in the C3 limit, the framework's content has no purchase because there is no embedded observation taking place. The "observer" is not observing anything; they are the system being observed, with no separation between knower and known.

This is the regime that superficially resembles a *God's-eye view*. The traditional theological framing imagines an observer who stands outside the universe and sees everything at once with no uncertainty. The framework's C3 limit is similar in eliminating quantum indeterminism, but it differs structurally: there is no "outside" in a deterministic universe. Such an observer would be coextensive with the universe, not transcendent of it. They would not be viewing the universe; they would be the universe describing itself.

Three structural features of this regime are worth noting.

*Observation breaks down entirely.* In the framework's account, observation is a *partition-relative* concept. The observer reads the visible sector through measurements that depend on the partition's coupling. Without a partition — without anything in the hidden sector to be coupled to — measurement has no meaning. The observer cannot perform observation in the framework's sense. The God's-eye view is therefore not a limit case of observation but a category outside it.

*Limits of prediction from a record.* A proper observer may identify the state, or its visible future, on some supported records. Where compatible hidden states lead to different visible futures, the available record does not fix which future occurs. Substratum Proposition 24.5 gives the corresponding reversible examples.

Gödel's, Turing's and Wolpert's results concern specified formal, computational and inference-device tasks (Chapter 12 §12.8). They illustrate the limits of universal inference. Which states or futures a particular observer can identify depends on the support and dynamics of their model.

The coextensive case lies outside the definition of a proper observer. Within a proper partition, predictive limits depend on the available records and the dynamics.

**The three limits together.**

The three failure modes are structurally distinct.

| Condition | What fails | Resulting dynamics | Resulting situation |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| C1 | Coupling | Closed classical | Isolated subsystem |
| C2 | Timescale separation | Markovian stochastic | Thermalized observer |
| C3 | Capacity hierarchy | No observation | Coextensive with system |

In these limit models, zero hidden influence gives autonomous visible evolution and full erasure gives a Markov law for the stated preparation. A hidden-sector capacity shortfall bounds accessible memory. A coextensive description is the extreme case of zero hidden capacity.

Together the limits illustrate different roles for C1–C4. A physical model of a laboratory observer must specify its preparation, hidden coupling, retention and readout. The proposed cosmological application has yet to provide that link.

The finite-law representation applies across these regimes. The distinctive history-memory result concerns observations with differing next-step conditionals from the same present value. The physical conjecture is that the relevant hidden processes of our world generate the observed quantum statistics under a common operational interface. The named control and readout questions in [Main §3.4] remain open.

This reframing has consequences for the framework's empirical content beyond what is already developed in Chapters 5 through 19. Each of the four conditions is independently testable through the restricted quantum-statistical regime — engineered systems approaching the marginal-failure boundary. Chapter 15 §15.7 develops the restricted quantum-statistical regime in engineered quantum systems with the BEC analogue-gravity test as the framework's cleanest experimental probe of marginal hidden capacity. Chapter 13 §13.7 develops the proposal's conjecture for biological systems. Chapter 18 §18.3 develops the restricted quantum-statistical regime as a forward prediction of the mechanism proposal. The four structural limits are therefore not just conceptual content but a framework of empirically accessible regimes, with current experiments now beginning to probe the marginal-failure boundaries.

## 1.11 The card table and the chessboard

Chess is a game of complete information: every piece stands in the open, and in principle the game is exhaustively analyzable — a sufficiently large lookup table settles every position. Poker is a game of incomplete information, and that difference is not cosmetic. When von Neumann founded game theory he took poker, not chess, as the interesting case, precisely because hidden cards force probability into rational play even though nothing in the rules is random. The mixed strategy is not ignorance dressed up; it is the correct mathematical form of play from inside an information set. The framework's claim can be stated in exactly these terms: the substratum is a chess game, and physics is the rulebook a piece would rationally write from inside it. What this section shows is that ordinary poker is not a rich enough interior view — and that the specific way it falls short, and the specific modification that repairs it, together reproduce the anatomy of Chapter 1 in miniature.

The comparison game uses a static hidden deal. For a visible record that screens earlier play from the future, its odds factor through the intermediate record. The modified table below gives a finite example in which the observed record fails that screening condition.

Three modifications close the gap, and they are conditions C1–C4 of §1.3 in card form. First, the shuffle between streets is a deterministic, reversible permutation of the deck — a faro shuffle, say — rather than a randomization: bijective dynamics on the full state. Second, each street deals burn cards, face down and never shown, which the shuffle correlates with the live cards: a hidden sector coupled to the visible one (C1), whose configuration persists between streets (C2), with capacity to store a record of the visible play (C3). Third, revealing a card is an intervention, not a passive glance: a revealed card is burned and replaced under a fixed rule, so revelation physically breaks the card's correlation with the rest of the deck.

A small such game gives a causal write and readback through a burn card. The finite return illustrates the same kind of revival as the coin–die example of §1.5. The player holds one hole card, red or black — the coin. One burn card, red or black, lies face down — the memory register the die provided. The shuffle between streets is the simplest reversible move available: hole card and burn card trade places. Then, writing the odds that the hole card at showdown matches the hole card as dealt:

| protocol | matching odds |
|---|---|
| one shuffle, then showdown | 1/2 |
| two shuffles, then showdown | 1 (certain) |
| two shuffles, odds chained through the unobserved middle | 1/2 |

Each line is checkable by hand — there are sixteen cases in the longest protocol. A single shuffle randomizes the visible card completely. **Schrödinger's cat, played as cards.** Every reader arrives carrying one thought experiment, and the game just built is the right table to play it on. Schrödinger (1935), in the same paper that coined *entanglement*, sealed a cat in a chamber with a radioactive atom, a Geiger counter, a hammer, and a flask of poison: within the hour, the formalism's entanglement chain has amplified one atom's superposition, link by link, into a description assigning the cat itself equal parts alive and dead until the box is opened. He offered it as a "quite ridiculous case" — a reductio, not a report. If the wavefunction is a complete description and "measurement" an undefined primitive, nothing in the theory says where along the chain atom → counter → hammer → cat a definite fact appears. Ninety years of interpretation dispute is the residue of that question.

Played on this table, the paradox takes four sentences. The sealed hour is an unobserved middle street. "Alive $+$ dead" is not a state of the cat but a line on the odds sheet — the indivisible pricing any player must use for a hand whose history routes through the burn pile. Opening the box is the peek with its forced re-burn: an intervention that severs a correlation, not the creation of a fact. And the deal was definite throughout — the substratum holds exactly one configuration at every moment; the superposition is bookkeeping for where the record resides, in cards the observer has not turned. What dissolves the *cat* specifically is a further observation: a cat's middle street is never unobserved. In the analogy, forcing a re-burn removes a particular correlation. Environmental interactions in a physical cat experiment can degrade coherence, with the extent and recoverability depending on the interaction and preparation (§1.6).

Schrödinger's instinct was therefore sound — no cat is ever alive-and-dead — but the framework's repair is subtraction where the objective-collapse programs (GRW/CSL, Diósi–Penrose) offer addition. They make the cat definite by appending a stochastic law; the framework makes it definite by deleting a premise — the superposition was the observer's ledger, not the chamber's state, so there was never anything to collapse (and Chapter 18 records that additions of the Penrose type are structurally forbidden here). The two repairs part company at exactly the frontier of §1.6: engineered "cat states," superpositions of macroscopically distinct configurations held genuinely unobserved, for which the laboratory tests concern specific physical models and their predicted decoherence or collapse rates (Chapter 4 §4.5). The finite-record theorem gives an exact representation of the supplied records. The deterministic model, under its stated hypotheses, has no intrinsic collapse mechanism. The boundary drawn above remains load-bearing: a cat in one box is a single-system story, the toy's home turf; it is Bell's two boxes, not Schrödinger's one, where the cards must be surrendered to the full construction.
