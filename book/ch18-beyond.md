## 18.2 The Born rule as dynamical equilibrium

The Born rule — that the probability of measuring a quantum system in state $|x\rangle$ given wave function $|\psi\rangle$ is $P(x) = |\langle x | \psi \rangle|^2$ — is one of the most empirically successful predictions in physics. It is also one of the most foundationally puzzling. In standard quantum mechanics, the Born rule is an *independent postulate*: it holds always, for all systems, at all times, with no underlying mechanism producing it. The various interpretive programs (many-worlds, QBism, Bohmian mechanics, dynamical collapse) take different positions on what the Born rule means, but they share the structural feature of treating it as fundamental rather than derived.

The framework's account is layered. At the representation level the Born rule is part of the exact equivalent description: every finite-horizon law has a unitary representation with Born-rule fixed-basis readout ($S \iff D \iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}$, [Main §3.4]). This representation exhibits the Born form without selecting its quadratic exponent; the physical origin of the quadratic probability rule remains open ([Main §3.1]), as does the common coherent operational extension to arbitrary noncommuting instruments and composites. On top of that sits the cosmological-realization *mechanism proposal*: the equilibrium reading of the read-write cycle between the visible and hidden sectors — the observer reads the visible sector through measurement, writes correlations into the hidden sector through the coupling $H_{VB}$, and reads back those correlations on subsequent measurements — with Born-rule probabilities as the stationary statistics of the cycle for the equilibrated hidden prior. That stationary-distribution reading is the account's load-bearing step, and it is a structural commitment of the realization, not a proved theorem of the foundational paper.

On this mechanism proposal, the Born rule would be a *dynamical* equilibrium rather than a fundamental postulate. The system would approach Born-rule statistics on a characteristic timescale set by the hidden sector's equilibration time $\tau_{\text{eq}} \sim \tau_B$, where $\tau_B$ is the hidden sector's correlation time from condition C2. For the cosmological partition $\tau_B$ is the horizon time, $\tau_B(t) \sim H(t)^{-1}$: about $10^{17}$ seconds today and far shorter in the early universe. Two quantities must be kept apart. The ratio $\tau_S/\tau_B \sim 10^{-32}$ is the per-step loss of a stored record under C2; it sets how long a stored record survives, not the size of memory effects or the distance from equilibrium. The quantity that tracks the approach to equilibrium is instead the accumulated number of relaxation times, $\int dt/\tau_{\text{eq}} \sim \int H\,dt = \ln(a/a_i)$, but that count does not by itself fix how a deviation decays. If the observed deviation $\delta$ obeys a closed relaxation law $\dot\delta = -\lambda H\delta$, a deviation present when relaxation began at scale factor $a_i$ decays as $(a_i/a)^{\lambda}$; the identification $\tau_{\text{eq}} \sim H^{-1}$ fixes neither that closed law nor the coefficient $\lambda$, which it constrains only to be of order one. Under such a law the epoch-local reading is required: with $\tau_{\text{eq}}$ fixed at today's value, of order $H_0^{-1}$, the age of the universe would amount to about one relaxation time and would leave a fraction of order $e^{-\lambda}$ of any initial deviation in place today, a departure that laboratory tests of the Born rule exclude unless the initial deviation was itself negligible. On the epoch-local reading the same law suppresses the residual in any current or foreseeable laboratory experiment by the factor $(a_i/a_0)^{\lambda}$, and the Born rule is exact for all practical purposes. If the hidden sector began at equilibrium there is no transient to relax, and the account reduces to the maximal-entropy measure of a finite representative posited in [Main §4.5]. (Status of this estimate: the identification $\tau_{\text{eq}} \sim \tau_B$ and its epoch-local reading are heuristic content of the mechanism proposal, and the closed relaxation law and its coefficient $\lambda$ are further assumptions the proposal does not derive; none of these is a theorem of the foundational paper.)

**Transient violations in the very early universe.** If the hidden sector began away from its stationary distribution, the Born rule was *not* exact in the very early universe: until the hidden sector equilibrated, measurement statistics deviated from $|\langle x | \psi \rangle|^2$. Whether it began away from equilibrium is a question about the cosmological initial conditions that the account does not settle; if it began at equilibrium, there were no such violations. Any deviations are parametrized by
$$P(x, t) = |\langle x | \psi(t) \rangle|^2 + \sum_k c_k(t) \langle x | \hat{O}_k | \psi(t) \rangle,$$
where the operators $\hat{O}_k$ encode the measurement history and the coefficients $c_k(t)$ parameterize possible history-dependent departures. Their decay would require an additional effective relaxation law, such as the closed law considered above, applicable over a specified finite epoch. Neither asymptotic convergence to zero nor a universal decay rate follows from the finite reversible substratum dynamics, which is recurrent under A1 (§18.7); microscopic recurrence and a possible effective relaxation over a finite epoch are distinct. (Status: the expansion is the proposal's *parametrization* of the deviation — the form an eventual derivation would have to fill in — not a derived spectral decomposition.)

The structural source of any transient violations is the hidden sector's initial state. In the framework's substratum dynamics, the hidden sector begins in a specific initial configuration set by the cosmological initial conditions. If that initial state is away from the stationary distribution, then before the visible-hidden coupling has driven the hidden sector there, the read-write cycle's input statistics differ from the Born rule, and the observer reads visible-sector outcomes with probabilities that deviate from $|\langle x | \psi \rangle|^2$ in ways that depend on the specific initial state.

This connects to Valentini's *quantum relaxation* hypothesis in de Broglie-Bohm theory, where Valentini proposed that Born-rule violations in the early universe would leave observable imprints. The mechanism proposal offers a structural mechanism for what Valentini's program postulates: hidden-sector equilibration would drive relaxation toward the Born rule on the heuristic timescale $\tau_{\text{eq}} \sim \tau_B$. Valentini's program postulated the relaxation; the account locates a candidate mechanism for it in the partition's read-write architecture. Both take the initial departure from equilibrium as an assumption about the cosmological initial conditions.

**Observational signatures in the CMB.** If primordial Born-rule violations were present during inflation, their empirical signature would be modified statistics of the primordial fluctuations. A change in the variance alone alters the primordial power spectrum without entailing any non-Gaussianity — a Gaussian distribution with a changed variance has no connected moments beyond the second — so a non-Gaussian signature arises only if the departure also carries higher connected moments. Standard inflationary models predict approximately Gaussian primordial perturbations, and the Planck 2018 analysis detects no primordial non-Gaussianity: for the local shape, $f_{\text{NL}} = -0.9 \pm 5.1$ (68% CL). The framework does not yet supply an amplitude to compare with this bound or with the measured power spectrum.

Standard local, equilateral, and folded non-Gaussianity shapes arise from specific inflationary mechanisms (multi-field inflation, non-canonical kinetic terms, modified initial vacuum states). Which moments a Born-rule departure would modify, and with what scale dependence or bispectrum shape, depends on the source dynamics of the relaxation, the family of initial deviations and the map from the hidden-sector deviation to the observed fluctuations, none of which the proposal specifies; whether such a departure produces any non-Gaussianity, and whether its shape would differ from the standard ones, is open.

The empirical accessibility of the proposal depends on inputs it does not yet fix: the source dynamics of the relaxation through inflation, the family of initial deviations, the rule for a mode's evolution after horizon exit, and the map from a residual deviation to the measured power spectrum or non-Gaussianity. The residual on a given scale is set by the initial deviation and by the relaxation accumulated between the onset of relaxation and the imprinting of that scale, not by the relaxation timescale alone: even rapid relaxation leaves almost the entire deviation on a scale imprinted shortly after relaxation begins, and no relaxation history leaves a residual if the initial deviation is zero. Once the observable map is derived, the Planck measurements would constrain the combination of initial deviation and relaxation history; no bound on $\tau_{\text{eq}}$ follows before then. The epoch-local reading above places $\tau_{\text{eq}} \sim H_{\text{inf}}^{-1}$. Under a closed relaxation law, if a mode's relaxation stops at its horizon exit, the residual on observable scales depends on how many e-folds of relaxation preceded that exit and, for scale-independent initial deviations, is largest on the largest scales; if relaxation continues to a common later epoch, that scale dependence cancels; and scale-dependent initial deviations can cancel or mimic either trend. The account fixes none of these.

**Status ledger for §18.2.** Layer by layer: the representation-level Born status is a theorem ([Main §3.4]: admitted universally, coherent operational extension characterized by five named conditions in [GR §3.3]); the read-write equilibrium reading is a mechanism *proposal* whose stationary-distribution step is a structural commitment; the equilibration timescale, its epoch-local reading and the deviation parametrization above are the proposal's heuristic quantitative content, and the closed relaxation law and rate coefficient on which a decay law rests are further assumptions it does not derive; the observable form of a departure — a power-spectrum change, non-Gaussianity, or both — is not derived; and the F7 entry — transient deviations if the hidden sector began away from equilibrium, observed through the CMB's primordial statistics — is a conditional prospect: it becomes a testable prediction only once the source dynamics, the family of initial deviations and the map to observables are specified, and it is detectable only if a nonzero residual survives on observable scales when they are imprinted. No entry in this section is a theorem of the foundational paper.

CMB-S4 (the upcoming ground-based experiment) and LiteBIRD (the planned space-based experiment) will tighten the non-Gaussianity constraints by roughly an order of magnitude over the next decade. Whether these experiments bear on the proposal depends on the source dynamics, the family of initial deviations and the map to observables, none of which the proposal yet specifies. Once they are specified, a predicted departure above the new sensitivity would be tested by these experiments, and a smaller one would leave the proposal unconstrained by them.

**History-dependent corrections at late times.** Even after the hidden sector has equilibrated, the Born rule receives history-dependent corrections. Their size is not fixed by the timescale ratio: $\tau_S / \tau_B \approx 10^{-32}$ for the cosmological partition sets how long the hidden record survives, while the size of the correlations is set by the coupling and the readback, which the framework does not quantify for that partition. The bath's motion contributes an absorbable Hermitian drift at first order in the ratio and genuine non-unitarity at second order ([Main §3.2]). The *structure* of the corrections is predicted: successive quantum measurements are not exactly independent samples from the Born distribution. They carry correlations encoding the deterministic structure of the substratum bijection $\varphi$.

The conceptual content of the late-time correction is significant. Standard quantum mechanics treats measurement outcomes as fundamentally independent random samples from the Born distribution. The framework predicts that this independence is approximate — there exist correlations between successive measurements, generated by the deterministic substratum dynamics, that persist in the frozen-bath limit $\tau_S / \tau_B \to 0$, where the hidden record is retained longest. The framework's substratum is deterministic; the apparent randomness of quantum mechanics is the observer's incompleteness rather than fundamental indeterminism. The late-time corrections are the structural signature of this underlying determinism; their magnitude is not fixed by the timescale ratio.

## 18.3 The quantum-classical boundary and the restricted quantum-statistical regime

The quantum-classical boundary is one of the most pragmatically important features of physics and one of the most foundationally ambiguous. Standard quantum mechanics applies universally in principle: a rock, a cat, and an electron are all described by wave functions, with the rock's wave function being too entangled with environmental degrees of freedom to show interference effects. The pragmatic boundary between "quantum" and "classical" is set by decoherence rather than by any structural distinction — macroscopic objects appear classical because they are quantum-but-decohered, not because they obey different physics.

The framework's account is structurally different. The boundary it draws is *determined by whether the framework's conditions C1, C2, C3, C4 are satisfied* — and it is a boundary in the memory currency, not in representability, which is universal ($S \iff D \iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}$, [Main §3.4]). A system satisfying all four carries the memory-bearing sector — accessible non-Markovian statistics, $M_t > 0$, with unavoidable hidden predictive memory in every completion; a system failing C1, C4 or the persistence form of C2 has that sector empty — not "quantum but decohered" but genuinely described by memoryless classical stochastic dynamics, its quantum representation trivial; a system failing C2 only in its timescale form has it suppressed, not shown to be empty, and limited capacity caps it (below). The boundary drawn by C1, C4 and the persistence form of C2 is structural rather than approximate.

**The four conditions as criteria.** Recall from Chapter 1 the framework's four conditions for an embedded observer's reduced description to carry the memory-bearing sector — the discriminating content, the representation itself being universal ([Main §3.4]):
- C1 (coupling): the subsystem must interact non-trivially with a hidden sector;
- C2 (memory persistence): the hidden sector must retain a record of the subsystem's past across the accessible window, $\tau_B \gg \tau_S$ with $\tau_B$ the coarse-grained mixing time — slow hidden dynamics is one sufficient mechanism, not the condition itself ([Main §1.3]);
- C3 (capacity): the hidden sector must have capacity sufficient for the process's conditional memory — the capacity floor $\log_2 |\mathcal{C}_H| \geq I^*$ ([Main §3.4]); $N_H \gg N_V$ is the comfortable physical regime, not the condition;
- C4 (history readback): hidden degrees of freedom must carry the visible past into future visible conditionals — the readback gap that makes the stored memory accessible ([Main §1.3]).

A system implementing C1–C4 with all four conditions strongly satisfied has a memory-bearing reduced description — the framework's identification applies in full at its stated layer — the memory-bearing description with its quantum representation, correctly given by unitary evolution on a Hilbert space at the transition-statistics layer, the coherent operational lift still to be established ([Main §3.4]). A system failing C1 (no hidden-sector coupling) has memoryless classical stochastic dynamics — the memory-bearing sector is empty, the quantum representation trivial. A system failing C2 in its timescale form (a hidden sector that equilibrates faster than the visible-sector dynamics, its records eroded before readback) has emergent dynamics whose accessible memory effects are suppressed. A hidden sector of limited capacity caps accessible memory — the capacity floor $M_t \le \log_2|\mathcal{C}_H|$ ([Main §3.4]); the mechanism proposal hypothesizes that marginal capacity produces partial distinguishability revival, the restricted quantum-statistical regime of Chapter 15 §15.7, with memoryless operational modeling failing while the transition-statistics layer remains representable ([Main §3.4]).

**The restricted quantum-statistical regime.** The mechanism proposal adds a revival scale to the distinction between memoryless and memory-bearing visible laws, and names one range of it. The scale is the revival measure $\mathcal{N}$. On an accessible window $W = \{0, \ldots, K\}$, let $d_t$ be the distinguishability at time $t$ of two initial preparations under the visible dynamics — the total-variation distance of their visible population laws, equivalently the trace distance of the corresponding diagonal states — and let $\mathcal{N}$ be the largest increase $d_t - d_s$ over pairs of preparations and times $s < t$ in $W$, with $\mathcal{N} = 0$ when distinguishability never increases. $\mathcal{N}$ is the peak revival on the window, a single increase, so $0 \le \mathcal{N} \le 1$. It is not the Breuer–Laine–Piilo measure, which sums every increase over the window. That cumulative backflow is kept separate: on a finite reversible system the rooted laws are periodic ([Main §2.3]), so a revival in one recurrence period recurs in every later one, and the sum grows without bound as the window lengthens. $\mathcal{N} > 0$ on a window certifies P-indivisibility there, since a P-divisible family never increases distinguishability; the converse fails ([Main §2.3]). Memory does not fix $\mathcal{N}$: the XOR control of [Main §2.3] carries memory on a window on which its family is P-divisible, so $\mathcal{N} = 0$ there, and $\mathcal{N}$ measures revival, not memory. Three ranges are named on a window: no revival ($\mathcal{N} = 0$), partial revival ($0 < \mathcal{N} < 1$) and complete revival ($\mathcal{N} = 1$). The mechanism proposal calls partial revival the *restricted quantum-statistical* regime. The name records that the quantum description supplied there is the representation at the transition-statistics layer, with the operational layer built on it not supplied; that restriction holds at every value of $\mathcal{N}$, so the regime is fixed by its revival range, and the name marks neither a partial nor a complete quantum theory and is not a quantum-membership scale: every finite law at every $\mathcal{N}$ admits the quantum representation and a deterministic hidden realization ([Main §3.4]).

By definition the regime lies strictly between no revival ($\mathcal{N} = 0$) and complete revival ($\mathcal{N} = 1$). In standard quantum mechanics, a decohered system is still described by a density matrix — it is quantum but mixed. In the framework the regime is a statement about the DYNAMICS, not about representability: every stochastic transition family admits the one-step ancilla dilation ([Main §3.1]), so no process lacks a Hilbert-space representation at the transition-statistics layer. What varies across the regime is the size of the largest distinguishability revival on the window; a positive revival certifies indivisibility, while indivisibility without revival is possible ([Main §2.3]). What a system in the regime may lack is the stronger OPERATIONAL structure built on top of that representation: coherent preparations of the represented states, the instrument algebra governing interventions, and representation-consistency across horizons — each of which the framework leaves open or bounded rather than delivered ([Main §3.4]). The quantum description is restricted in that precise sense: the transition-statistics layer is available throughout the regime, as at every value of $\mathcal{N}$, while the operational layer is available only to the extent those questions are settled, so the representation is not a complete operational quantum theory; and $\mathcal{N}$ records the largest revival, not a distance from the divisible families.

Partial distinguishability revival itself is standard open-systems phenomenology — the Breuer–Laine–Piilo measure quantifies trace-distance revival, and the Rivas–Huelga–Plenio measures quantify departures from complete-positivity divisibility, a different property; what is distinctive here is the proposed capacity-controlled origin and the specific scaling law. Standard accounts treat quantum and classical as endpoints of a spectrum (with decoherence interpolating between them), but the framework separates memoryless from memory-bearing visible laws ([Main §3.4]), and the mechanism proposal adds a revival scale inside the memory-bearing case — the restricted quantum-statistical regime for partial revival — all of it quantum-representable ([Main §3.4]).

**Where to look for the restricted quantum-statistical regime.** The mechanism proposal, layered on the capacity theorem, conjectures where partial revival occurs: in systems whose hidden sector has capacity comparable to the visible sector's, with protein conformational dynamics and single-enzyme kinetics as the biological candidates Chapter 13 conjectures, which the capacity ratio does not by itself place there. The timescale ratio $\tau_S/\tau_B$ does not by itself place a system on the revival scale. A fast bath that erodes the stored record uniformly suppresses the readback gap exponentially in $\tau_S/\tau_B$ at every order $k \geq 2$ ([Main §2.3]); a slow bath does not by itself give memory or revival — a static bath whose record is never read back carries no memory ([Main §2.3]).

The framework's content is not "quantum effects in biology" — standard quantum mechanics allows quantum effects at any scale, and the literature documents many examples. The framework's content is the distinction between memoryless and memory-bearing visible laws, all quantum-representable ([Main §3.4]), together with the mechanism proposal's conjecture that partial revival occurs at marginal hidden capacity.

**Observational signatures.** Single-molecule enzyme experiments already show non-Markovian kinetics — history-dependent reaction rates, stretched-exponential waiting-time distributions, transition statistics that cannot be fitted with a Markovian rate equation. The framework's prediction is that these signatures are not artifacts of poorly-characterized rate constants but features the mechanism proposal attributes to partial revival. Two tests are distinct. Whether the multi-time statistics admit a memoryless (Markov) description at the accessible horizon decides memory; whether the distinguishability of two preparations revives on that horizon decides $\mathcal{N} > 0$, which certifies indivisibility. A memory-bearing system can show no revival ([Main §2.3]); the mechanism proposal predicts memory together with partial revival — a proposal-level claim ([Main §3.4]).

The distinction is empirically testable. A memoryless (divisible) density-matrix description requires specific algebraic constraints on the transition probabilities — positivity, completeness, certain inequality relations between multi-time correlators. The mechanism proposal predicts that protein conformational dynamics and single-enzyme kinetics violate at least some of these constraints. The experimental program would require multi-time correlation measurements with sufficient statistics to constrain the algebraic structure, which is within reach of current single-molecule techniques.

The restricted quantum-statistical regime is the mechanism proposal's testable conjecture, with experimental targets in the biological sciences that the framework's quantum-biology chapter (Chapter 13) develops in detail.

## 18.4 The epistemic character of quantum randomness

The framework's commitment to a deterministic substratum has a striking consequence for the foundations of quantum mechanics: quantum randomness is *epistemic* rather than ontic. The apparent indeterminism of quantum measurement outcomes is the observer's incompleteness, not a fundamental feature of physical reality. The substratum bijection $\varphi$ is deterministic; the memory-bearing description — with its admitted quantum representation — emerges from the framework's structural conditions applied to embedded observation of this deterministic dynamics ([Main §3.4]); the randomness the observer sees is the result of marginalizing over the hidden sector.

This is a substantive position, and it has consequences both for the foundations of quantum mechanics and for what kinds of accounts of physics are compatible with the framework.

**Deterministic but inaccessible.** The framework's substratum dynamics is given by the bijection $\varphi$ acting on the configuration space $S$. Each state $(x, h)$ — visible coordinates $x$ paired with hidden coordinates $h$ — maps to a unique successor $\varphi(x, h) = (x', h')$. There is no randomness in the dynamics; the substratum is strictly deterministic.

The apparent randomness arises because the observer sees only $x$, not $h$. Different hidden states $h_1, h_2, \ldots$ compatible with the same visible state $x$ lead to different successor visible states $x_1', x_2', \ldots$ under the bijection. From $x$ alone the observer cannot distinguish among the hidden states; they appear as a probability distribution over the possible successors. The probabilities are *not* features of the dynamics — the dynamics is deterministic — but features of the observer's reduced description of the dynamics, given the observer's incomplete information about the hidden-sector configuration.

This is what "deterministic but inaccessible" means in the framework: the outcomes are fully determined by the substratum dynamics, but the observer cannot in principle access the complete state that determines them. The randomness is unavoidable for any embedded observer, but it is not fundamental — it is the cost of partial observation.

**Bell's theorem and the chosen nonlocality.** Bell does not rule out determinism by itself; it rules out reproducing the observed correlations with a response-complete deterministic completion that records a $\pm1$ outcome in every run and is both measurement-independent and parameter-independent. Chapter 1 proves the corresponding Bell ceiling: if the setting interventions, applied at one step, each read and write only their own wing, the Bell-relevant coupling range does not connect the wings before readout, every run records a $\pm1$ outcome, and one setting-independent pre-setting ensemble is used, then $|S_{\rm CHSH}|\le2$. The framework therefore declines the measurement-dependent/superdeterministic horn and takes **ontic parameter dependence**. In the specific SM/GR realization the target mechanism is preparation-indexed adjacency in the already state-dependent graph $G(x)$ (or an equivalent ontically nonlocal response), so a prepared microstate response may depend on the remote setting while the reference vacuum/uniform graph remains geometric and bounded-range. Whether those prepared graphs keep the operational distribution no-signaling and preserve the continuum-curvature limit is the open hypothesis H-Bell. The $2\sqrt2$ theorem for the causal-local indivisible stochastic description is a separate weaker-layer result, not a locality theorem for the deterministic completion.

One proposed physical realization of that preparation-indexed parameter dependence uses non-localized cosmological-boundary modes. This is a candidate realization, not an established Bell mechanism: any such prepared long-edge/response structure must satisfy H-Bell, i.e. preserve operational no-signaling and the metric/Ollivier--Ricci continuum limit used by the gravitational reconstruction. What distinguishes it from a superluminal signaling mechanism is operational no-signaling, not ontic parameter independence.

**Practical consequences are absent.** The distinction between epistemic and ontic randomness has no consequence observed at current experimental precision — quantum random number generators, quantum key distribution protocols, and other applications that rely on "true randomness" function identically under either interpretation. What these applications rely on is unpredictability to the parties who can observe or act on the source, not the ontic or epistemic status of its randomness. Determinism does not by itself guarantee that unpredictability: an embedded observer whose actions reach the hidden state driving a deterministic source can, for some sources, predict its output better than chance. Whether a source stays unpredictable to observers of bounded memory, whether they act on it or only read it, depends on the source, and the framework does not establish it for the hidden sector of the physical realization.

The framework's content here is therefore conceptual rather than empirical at near-term experimental scales. The framework's commitment to deterministic substratum changes what physics-based accounts are compatible with the framework — but it does not change near-term experimental predictions in any observable way.

**Conceptual consequences.** The deeper conceptual consequence is significant. The universe is deterministic in the framework. The observer's indeterminacy is the cost of partial observation, not a feature of the substratum; whether partial observation yields the specifically quantum statistics is open, since embedded observation by itself does not select quantum mechanics (Chapter 19 §19.2.12). At the fixed-basis level the measurement problem receives an observer-relative account: no collapse, no branching and no objective randomness in the substratum; which operational protocols the completion settles is open (Chapter 19 §19.2.5). There is a bijection, a partition, and an observer who cannot see the whole.

This has implications for what classes of physics-based accounts of consciousness, the foundations of probability, the relationship between determinism and free will, and various other foundational questions are compatible with the framework. Accounts that require fundamental indeterminism in physical reality — Penrose's orchestrated objective reduction is the most prominent example in consciousness research — are incompatible with the framework's deterministic substratum. Accounts that treat quantum probabilities as derivable from incomplete information rather than as fundamental are compatible with and supported by the framework.

The framework does not claim to settle these foundational questions; it makes structural commitments that constrain which accounts are compatible. Sections 18.9 and 18.10 develop two of these constraints in detail — what the framework says about cosmic spatial topology and what it says about consciousness — but the broader pattern is that the framework's commitment to deterministic substratum is a substantive structural feature that constrains the foundational landscape.

The framework's quantum randomness is therefore epistemic, exact in its predictions for any embedded observer's empirical access, and consequential for the classes of foundational accounts compatible with it. The empirical record is silent at the $10^{-32}$ level; the conceptual record is substantial.

## 18.5 Quantum gravity: the cumulative case at the framework level

The framework gives a geometry-first reading of three quantum-gravity problems: the cosmological constant problem, the black hole information paradox, and the non-renormalizability of perturbative quantum gravity. For the cosmological constant, the two levels of description can be distinguished, and the gravitational coupling remains open. Bijectivity preserves substratum information, and the Page curve is conditional. The proposed lattice supplies a microscopic cutoff, with recovery of the covariant effective theory open at G3. This section brings those readings together.

**The conventional formulation.** Standard treatments of quantum gravity attempt to quantize the gravitational field by analogy with the quantization of the Standard Model forces. The metric tensor $g_{\mu\nu}$ is treated as a quantum field, decomposed into a classical background plus fluctuations, and the fluctuations are quantized through canonical or path-integral methods. Three obstacles arise.

The cosmological constant problem: the QFT vacuum energy summed over all field modes up to the Planck cutoff is of order $M_{\text{Pl}}^4 \sim 10^{113}\,\text{J/m}^3$. The observed vacuum energy density is $\sim 10^{-9}\,\text{J/m}^3$. The ratio $10^{122}$ is treated as a fine-tuning problem requiring an unknown cancellation mechanism.

The black hole information paradox: Hawking's 1975 semiclassical calculation produces thermal radiation that appears to carry no information about the black hole's initial state. If the radiation is exactly thermal, information is destroyed when the black hole completes evaporation — in tension with quantum mechanics' commitment to unitarity.

Non-renormalizability: perturbative quantum gravity treated as a quantum field theory in flat spacetime is non-renormalizable. Infinitely many independent counterterms are required to absorb divergences at successively higher orders, with no UV completion within the standard quantization framework.

The framework starts with causal geometry in the classical substratum. In the proposed description, an embedded observer traces out degrees of freedom across a boundary defined using that geometry. This ordering is needed for that trace-out construction.

**The first component: the cosmological constant.** Chapter 7 §7.7 distinguishes two scales. The Friedmann equation sets the critical-density scale $\rho_{\text{crit}} \sim c^2 H^2/G$. The observed vacuum density is a supplied fraction of that scale. Summing QFT zero-point energies up to a Planck cutoff gives $\rho_{\text{QFT}} \sim M_{\text{Pl}}^4$ in natural units. Their large ratio is of the order of the de Sitter entropy $S_{\text{dS}} = A/(4 l_p^2)$ under these scale estimates. The framework reads this comparison as an information compression ratio between the substratum and the observer's emergent description.

The gravitational comparison requires a native coupling at G3. A common offset cancels in a difference of complete conserved stress tensors evaluated on the same metric with a fixed reference. The native stress or flux map and the gravitational response to physical vacuum changes remain open.

**The second component: the black hole information paradox.** Chapter 7 §7.6 developed the framework's account of the information paradox. A bijective substratum preserves information. Whether the radiation carries it is a separate question. For a common dilation and initial state, the nested-partition formalism gives the same reduced channel from the direct and sequential trace-outs. The Page curve additionally needs H-scramble and H-decouple. The paper rules out that condition for a definite initial configuration with the bipartition aligned to the configuration coordinates (Chapter 7 §7.6).

The derivation runs through a mechanism substantially different from the quantum-extremal-surface and entanglement-island computations — cycle typicality on the configuration space rather than replica wormhole computation in the gravitational path integral — and the correspondence between the two is conjectural. For a mass-independent emission coefficient (Chapter 7 §7.6), its Page time is a fixed fraction of the evaporation time set by the entropy the radiation carries per unit of horizon entropy lost: $t_P \approx 0.646\, t_{\text{evap}}$ when the two are equal, and earlier when the radiation carries more, as it does in the semiclassical emission regime defined in Chapter 7 §7.6.

The two-level description makes it possible for information preserved by bijectivity to reside in sectors that the emergent description traces out. Whether and when this information becomes accessible in the radiation requires the separate dynamical argument.

**The third component: non-renormalizability.** In the usual continuum effective field theory of gravity, perturbation theory about the Einstein–Hilbert action requires new counterterms at successive orders. The effective theory is predictive order by order at low energy. [Donoghue](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.50.3874)

The framework proposes a physical lattice with spacing $\sqrt{6\sigma}\,l_p$ under the link count and horizon calibration of Chapter 7 §§7.3–7.4. That spacing would set the microscopic cutoff. The covariant effective description would apply at wavelengths large compared with that spacing.

On this proposal the lattice dynamics would take over at scales approaching $\sqrt{6\sigma}\,l_p$. Matching those dynamics to covariant field equations with their sources remains open at G3 ([GR §8.7]).

**The shared structural feature.** In each case the geometry-first proposal distinguishes the substratum from the observer's description. For the cosmological constant it distinguishes the horizon scale from the zero-point estimate. For the information paradox it distinguishes preservation by bijectivity from accessibility in the radiation. For perturbative gravity it distinguishes the proposed microscopic cutoff from its open matching to the long-wavelength covariant theory.

These readings motivate a common substratum model $(S,\varphi)$ for quantum records and gravity. G3 remains open (Chapter 19 §19.2.1). The framework's treatment carries conditional and consistency relations, including the horizon calibration with its $1/4$ ratio, the descriptive vacuum-energy distinction, the dark-sector reading, and the MOND scale $a_0 = cH/6$ conditional on the open coupling map with its factor $1/6$ imported. Its forward exposure is listed in Appendix A.5.

## 18.7 Finite information and exact finite recurrence

A finite substratum has structural consequences that extend beyond the empirical predictions of fundamental physics. It is described by a finite configuration space $S$ with bijection dynamics $\varphi: S \to S$. Finiteness is then not a calculational convenience but load-bearing: it guarantees exact recurrence of the substratum dynamics — the finite-bijection form of recurrence, stronger than what Poincaré's theorem gives for a finite-measure system — which, together with a non-permutation rooted map, produces P-indivisibility at the embedded-observer level; a fixed-basis unitary quantum representation is internal and universal ([Main §3.4]), the compressed form supplied across the correspondence's class under (T), with P-indivisibility fixing its nontriviality. In this exact-return form the recurrence route in the framework's logic chain uses finite $S$; the near-return condition of Chapter 1 §1.5, assumed as a hypothesis and not adopted, also gives the indivisibility conclusion (it concerns a time that need not be accessible and then has no finite-window test; for the selected update of Main §3.2 on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ no law that is not supported on periodic configurations is known to satisfy it).

**Finite information content.** The substratum's information content is $\log_2 |S|$ bits — finite for any finite $S$. This is distinct from the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy $S_{\text{BH}} = A/(4 l_p^2)$, which bounds the *accessible* information at any given partition (the capacity of the visible sector and the boundary layer). The total information content of $S$ is at least that of the boundary layer at the cosmological horizon; whether it exceeds the Bekenstein-Hawking bound is not fixed by visible data, and for a finite substratum it is finite.

A clarification is essential. The cardinality $|\mathcal{C}_D|$ of the deep hidden sector is *gauge* in the framework: different values of $|\mathcal{C}_D|$ produce the same visible transition data exactly for a deep sector beyond the reach of the dynamics over the times considered, and at leading order on accessible timescales under the posited influence bound H-BD of [GR §3.2]. The "total information" of the universe is therefore not fixed by those data. But the *finiteness* of $|\mathcal{C}_D|$ — for the recurrence route specifically, that there exists some finite value, even if its specific magnitude is not observable — is load-bearing for that route's exact-return form. Infinite hidden sectors would break the finite-order argument that drives that form. P-indivisibility itself does not require finite $S$: any revival of distinguishability between the visible laws of two roots implies it, whatever the size of the hidden sector. For the selected linear update on the infinite lattice, with prime $q$, a nonzero coupling coefficient, a uniform product prior on the hidden sites and lattice-convex visible windows, no such revival occurs, and the process is P-divisible at every horizon. Accessible non-Markovianity would survive through readback, and the imported representation is gated by (T) rather than by recurrence, so for that update and prior an infinite substratum removes this route rather than the framework's quantum representation.

Finite information is therefore neither a quantitative claim about the specific cardinality nor a metaphysical preference for finitism. For a finite substratum it is a sufficient condition for one particular route — the recurrence-based P-indivisibility result in its exact-return form, which needs every root's visible law to return to certainty at a common time, $\Gamma_n = I$, almost surely over the hidden prior. A bijection of a finite set supplies that return at its order ($\varphi^N = \mathrm{id}$ gives $\Gamma_N = I$); the Poincaré recurrence theorem for a finite-measure system does not, since it returns almost every state at its own time. Finiteness is what makes this form of the route to P-indivisibility available.

**Exact finite recurrence.** A bijection $\varphi$ acting on a finite set $S$ is a permutation. Every permutation on a finite set has finite order: there exists a smallest positive integer $N$ such that $\varphi^N = \mathrm{id}$, the identity map. After $N$ time steps, it returns to its exact initial state. A bijection of a finite set is therefore strictly periodic, with period $N$ determined by the bijection's cycle structure.

The recurrence period can be astronomically large. For an arbitrary bijection of $S$, two upper bounds with different growth rates bear on it. *A bound valid for every permutation:* $N$ is the least common multiple of the cycle lengths, each at most $|S|$, so $N$ divides $\mathrm{lcm}(1, 2, \ldots, |S|)$. Its logarithm is Chebyshev's function, $\ln \mathrm{lcm}(1, \ldots, n) = \psi(n)$, with $\psi(n) \sim n$ by the prime number theorem. *The largest period any permutation attains:* this is Landau's function $g(n)$, the maximum order of a permutation of $n$ points, with $\ln g(n) \sim \sqrt{n \ln n}$ (Landau 1903). Since $\sqrt{n \ln n}$ is $o(n)$, $g(n)$ lies far below $\mathrm{lcm}(1, \ldots, n)$, and no permutation has order near $e^{n}$. Typical-permutation order estimates require a specified probability law for $\varphi$, which the framework does not adopt; Chapter 3 §3.2 holds that random permutations of finite sets typically lack the structure C1–C4 require.

The substratum's cardinality is not fixed by visible data. [Substratum §4] bounds only the visible-plus-boundary sector, $|\mathcal{C}_V \times \mathcal{C}_B| \le e^{10^{122}}$ at the de Sitter horizon; the deep sector's cardinality is gauge (above). For illustration only, if $|S|$ were of order $e^{10^{122}}$, the first bound would give
$N \le \mathrm{lcm}(1, 2, \ldots, |S|) = e^{\psi(|S|)} \sim e^{e^{10^{122}}},$
in the sense $\ln N \le \psi(|S|) = (1 + o(1))\,|S|$. The second is sharper: no bijection of that set has period above $g(|S|)$, with $\ln g(|S|) \sim \sqrt{|S| \ln |S|} \sim 10^{61}\, e^{5 \times 10^{121}}$.

The exactly linear rule on a fixed coupling graph is far more constrained than an arbitrary bijection. Under linearity (A5, Chapter 2 §2.4) the update is an invertible linear map of $S = (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^D$, where $D$ counts every stored component, including the previous-time copy that a second-order rule carries. Its powers $\varphi^0, \ldots, \varphi^{N-1}$ are distinct and nonzero, and they lie in the ring generated by $\varphi$, which by the Cayley–Hamilton theorem has at most $q^D$ elements. Hence
$N \le q^D - 1 = |S| - 1$
for every alphabet size $q \ge 2$. For prime $q$ some linear maps attain $q^D - 1$, but the wave rule falls far short of it: on a ring of $L$ sites, with $q$ prime and $\gcd(L, q) = 1$, its period is exactly $qL$, against $|S| = q^{2L}$ (Appendix B, Theorem B.3.1). The bound does not cover the other maps the corpus uses: the state-dependent-geometry map $F(u, v) = (v, \mathrm{WE}_{G(v)}(v) - u)$ of [SM §3.1], which carries the gravitational sector, and the rounded bijection of [SM §4.2], whose proposed stationary law is meant to support full-shell sampling, are bijections but not linear maps, and for them only $N \le g(|S|)$ and $N \mid \mathrm{lcm}(1, \ldots, |S|)$ hold. The period of the three-dimensional rule at cosmological size is not computed in the corpus.

The return itself is definite: a finite model returns to its exact initial state after $N$ substratum time steps, where $N$ is fixed by the cycle structure of $\varphi$. It is at most $g(|S|)$ for any bijection and below $|S|$ for the exactly linear rule. The framework bounds $N$ rather than computing it, and it supplies no lower bound on $N$.

A finite model is periodic with period $N$, the least common multiple of the bijection's cycle lengths. For a rule fixing the zero configuration on a nontrivial state space, no single cycle passes through every configuration. The exact linear rule of [SM §4.2] has this property and therefore at least two cycles. The cycle-ergodicity question that [SM §4.2] treats concerns sampling within an energy shell. Within a single cycle no state function increases throughout; a direction of the observer's experience is fixed by boundary conditions on a stretch of the cycle (the arrow of time discussed in the next section), and at the substratum level the dynamics has no preferred direction.

**Implications for the framework's logical structure.** The recurrence theorem connects three structural features of a finite model: substratum finiteness ($|S| < \infty$), bijection dynamics (the bijectivity premise of the framework's models), and P-indivisibility at the recurrence scale. The connection runs in three steps. Finiteness of $S$ guarantees exact recurrence ($\varphi^N = \mathrm{id}$). Recurrence together with C1 acting at the first step — which makes the one-step matrix a non-permutation under the uniform hidden prior, the cheapest non-permutation rooted map — gives returns of information from hidden to visible over the recurrence timescale (P-indivisibility across the recurrence cycle, not on accessible windows); the coupling is load-bearing rather than decorative, since for an uncoupled partition $\varphi = \varphi_V \times \varphi_H$ the visible sector evolves autonomously, its two-time family is divisible, and no distinguishability is ever restored — recurrence notwithstanding. With a full-support visible prior this indivisibility is equivalent to memory at some order below the recurrence time; the memory-bearing sector is the one in which C4 places that memory at an accessible order, and every finite law, memory-bearing or not, admits the quantum representation.

Finite information is therefore load-bearing for the exact-return form of the recurrence route specifically, not for representability or for accessible non-Markovianity: readback at order $k$ has its own finite-horizon route, and the imported representation is gated by (T) rather than by recurrence, so those routes do not exclude an infinite deep hidden sector, which would need its own normalized state or measure and well-defined dynamics ([Main §2.3], §3.1; Chapter 2 §2.3). Removing finiteness — replacing $|S| < \infty$ with $|S| = \infty$ — would remove that exact-return form rather than the framework's quantum representation; the route then rests, as a sufficient condition, on the near-return condition of Chapter 1 §1.5, assumed as a hypothesis and not adopted.

## 18.8 The arrow of time

The framework's substratum dynamics is given by the bijection $\varphi: S \to S$. The bijection has *no arrow of time*. Both $\varphi$ and its inverse $\varphi^{-1}$ are equally valid maps on the configuration space, and the substratum dynamics is unchanged under the exchange $\varphi \leftrightarrow \varphi^{-1}$. The framework's commitment to T-symmetric substratum dynamics is the same commitment that gives reciprocal transition counts under the counting hidden prior of a finite model in Chapter 6 §6.3 (and narrows $\theta$ to $\{0,\pi\}$ without fixing $\bar\theta$) — T-invariance at the substratum level is a structural feature that propagates through the framework's emergent descriptions.

This raises an obvious question. The universe we observe has an unambiguous arrow of time: entropy increases, biological systems age, memories form of the past but not the future, the universe expands rather than contracts. If the substratum dynamics is T-symmetric, where does the observed arrow come from?

**The arrow as observer-emergent.** The framework's answer is that the arrow of time is *emergent from the observer's coarse-grained description*, not from the substratum dynamics. The substratum dynamics is T-symmetric; the observer's reduced description of a non-equilibrium initial condition is not. The asymmetry enters through the trace-out of a non-equilibrium initial condition — the marginalization over the hidden sector that produces the embedded observer's reduced description; under the invariant measure the trace-out alone produces none. The arrow of time is therefore a feature of embedded observation of such a condition, in the same way that quantum mechanics, the dark sector, and the Born rule are.

The mechanism is the standard Boltzmann argument applied to the framework's partition. The visible sector starts in a particular configuration that is, in some sense, "low entropy" relative to the typical configurations accessible at later times. The hidden sector starts in a configuration that contains all the correlations needed to specify the visible sector's future evolution. As the dynamics evolves, correlations flow from hidden sector to visible sector and back, with the time-reversibility of the underlying dynamics fully preserved at the substratum level. But the observer, who sees only the visible sector, sees an irreversible flow: information about the past is encoded in the visible sector's current state (entropy increasing), while information about the future is encoded in the hidden sector (inaccessible to the observer).

The arrow of time is therefore *the observer's incompleteness* expressed temporally. The same partition that produces quantum randomness (by hiding the substratum information that determines outcomes) produces the arrow of time (by hiding the substratum information that would let the observer reverse the dynamics). Both are aspects of the same structural feature: the observer's reduced description is asymmetric in ways that the substratum dynamics is not.

**Detailed balance and the thermodynamic arrow.** The framework's content on detailed balance from Chapter 6 §6.3 has a direct implication for the arrow of time. For a time reversal that preserves the partition, and the counting prior on hidden states, the visible transition probabilities satisfy reciprocity, $T_{ij} = T_{\theta j, \theta i}$ at all timescales, with $\theta$ the action of time reversal on visible states; when the visible states carry no T-odd data this is detailed balance, $T_{ij} = T_{ji}$ — the rate of transition from $i$ to $j$ equals the rate of transition from $j$ to $i$. This follows from *substratum-level* T-symmetry: the dynamics has no preferred direction.

The thermodynamic arrow of time emerges not from any asymmetry in the transition probabilities but from the partition's *initial conditions*. The universe starts in a configuration that is far from equilibrium relative to the substratum's full state space, with most of the substratum's degrees of freedom on the hidden side of the partition and the visible sector occupying a small, structured region. As the dynamics evolves, the system moves toward equilibrium relative to the substratum's state space — entropy increasing in the observer's coarse-grained description — until the entire substratum is fully mixed. The arrow of time runs in the direction of this equilibration.

The relation to detailed balance is that the H-theorem — the standard derivation of entropy increase from microscopic dynamics — is a theorem about the *coarse-grained* description, not about the underlying transition probabilities. The H-theorem assumes a particular kind of initial condition (low entropy at the coarse-grained level) and derives entropy increase from there. Detailed balance at the microscopic level is fully consistent with the H-theorem at the coarse-grained level, because the H-theorem is about how the observer's information about the system evolves rather than about the system's underlying dynamics.

**The cosmological arrow.** Whether the thermodynamic arrow and the cosmological arrow — the direction in which the universe expands — are aligned is open in the framework: it is neither established nor refuted. The cosmological partition has a specific initial condition: the universe begins in a low-entropy configuration with most degrees of freedom on the hidden side of the cosmological horizon. On an expanding FRW background satisfying the null energy condition, the apparent-horizon area is non-decreasing. Its assigned entropy has that interpretation under the stated calibration assumptions; whether this behavior aligns with boundary correlations or hidden-sector disorder remains open.

Both arrows are traced to the same initial conditions of the cosmological partition. That is a common boundary condition, not a derived mechanism: that the thermodynamic arrow within the visible sector tracks the cosmological arrow is not established. The universe started with low entropy at the cosmological level, as an initial condition; at the cosmological boundary, coupling C1, persistence C2 and capacity C3 are verified, while history readback C4 is a named realization condition not presently discharged (Chapter 7 §7.2); the cosmological boundary is the primary partition for any embedded observer.

**Why the framework reframes rather than explains.** The framework's content on the arrow of time is not an explanation of *why* the universe has a preferred direction; it is a *reframing* of the question. Standard accounts of the arrow of time treat it as an asymmetry that requires explanation — why is entropy increasing in the direction we call "future" rather than the direction we call "past"? The framework's answer is that there is no asymmetry in the substratum dynamics; the asymmetry is in the observer's reduced description, which inherits the initial conditions of the partition.

The reframing places the explanatory burden on a different question. Instead of asking "why does the universe have a preferred direction of time?", the framework asks "why does the universe start in a low-entropy partition?" The latter question is a question about *initial conditions* — addressable by inflationary cosmology, anthropic arguments, or other initial-condition mechanisms — rather than a question about *dynamics*. This is the same kind of reframing the framework applies to the cosmological constant problem (Chapter 7 §7.7): the conventional formulation treats the small observed value as a fine-tuning problem in the dynamics; the framework treats it as a feature of the partition's structure that does not require dynamical explanation.

The arrow of time is therefore one more case where the framework's substratum-emergent distinction does substantive work. The substratum is T-symmetric (the dynamics has no arrow). The emergent description is not T-symmetric (the observer sees an arrow). The asymmetry is structural — inherited from the partition's initial conditions — rather than dynamical. The framework's content here is not to solve the arrow-of-time problem in the conventional sense but to locate it precisely: the arrow is at the level of partition initial conditions, not at the level of substratum dynamics.

## 18.9 Cosmic spatial topology

The framework's structural commitments determine the *local* structure of the substratum but are silent on its *global* structure. The substratum is a cubic lattice of spacing $\sqrt{6\sigma}\,l_p$ (Chapter 7 §§7.3–7.4). The local cubic structure produces the gauge group, the matter content, and the framework's quantitative predictions across Standard Model and gravitational sectors. But whether the lattice extends globally as the infinite discrete lattice $\mathbb{Z}^3$, the standard construction of A4, which is distinct from a continuous $\mathbb{R}^3$; identifies as a 3-torus $\mathbb{Z}^3/P$, a finite periodic quotient that A4 admits as a qualified case; has a Klein-bottle-type identification, which is not a quotient by translations alone; or admits some other global identification structure — that is not determined by the framework's structural commitments. The framework is locally specific and globally agnostic.

The framework's local calculations use the properties of the partition at scale $\epsilon$. A global identification can affect what enters an observer's causal neighborhood. The finite-window comparison below specifies when two models give the same local records.

**Janna Levin's compact-universe proposal.** Levin developed in the late 1990s and early 2000s the proposal that the universe could have a non-trivial spatial topology — specifically, that spatial sections could be *compact* (a 3-torus, a Klein-bottle quotient, whose identification includes a parity-reversing map and so is not a quotient by translations alone, or other compact identifications) rather than infinite. The motivation was partly cosmological (compact topology produces specific signatures in the CMB power spectrum at the largest angular scales) and partly philosophical (an infinite universe creates conceptual difficulties around uniqueness and the anthropic principle, addressed at book length in Levin's *How the Universe Got Its Spots* in 2002).

The Klein-bottle specifically refers to non-orientable compact spatial topologies in which a parity-reversing identification connects opposite "sides" of the universe. The proposal is one example within the broader family of compact-universe possibilities — others include the 3-torus (orientable, no parity reversal), the Poincaré dodecahedral space (constant positive curvature, no parity reversal), and various lens-space identifications. Levin's work, with collaborators Cornish, Spergel, Starkman, Weeks, and others, developed the empirical program for testing compact-universe proposals through their characteristic CMB signatures.

**The empirical program: matched-circle signatures.** A topological image of the observer can have a last-scattering sphere that intersects ours. In that case the same region of the last-scattering surface can be seen in two directions, producing correlated temperature patterns along a pair of circles.

Cornish, Spergel, Starkman, and Komatsu (2004) developed a statistical method for detecting matched circles. WMAP and Planck temperature maps have been searched for these patterns.

Planck found no statistically significant matches in the configurations it searched. The resulting bounds constrain suitable topological loops through our position on scales comparable to the diameter of the last-scattering surface. A topology can have shorter loops elsewhere and still evade this bound. [Planck 2015 results XVIII](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201525829) · [COMPACT, Part I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02603)

**The framework's compatibility with Levin's proposal.** Levin's compact-universe proposal is *consistent with* but *not predicted by* the framework. Three structural points clarify this.

First, a compact identification by translations can give a finite periodic quotient $\mathbb{Z}^3/P$, which A4 admits as a qualified case under the conditions on $P$ stated with each result that uses it; a Klein-bottle-type identification is not a quotient by translations alone. With finitely many states per site and the specified finite-carrier dynamics, a finite periodic quotient is one way to obtain a finite substratum in a model.

Second, a finite substratum can also be obtained on a finite lattice region with prescribed boundary conditions and finitely many states per site.

Third, the local finite-window laws agree across two topology choices if the local updates agree, the relevant dependency cones are identified, and the joint law of their inputs agrees (GR Appendix B). This gives a test for when a global identification can be ignored in a local comparison.

The framework leaves global topology open. Consequences of a particular identification depend on the model and the observer's causal neighborhood.

**The CMB as joint testing ground.** Both Levin's matched-circle program and the framework's Born-rule transient violations (§18.2) are CMB-data-driven empirical programs. They are structurally distinct — matched-circles tests global identification, Born-rule transient violations tests boundary thermodynamics at inflation — but they use overlapping observational data. Planck 2018 constrains the standard non-Gaussianity shapes, and CMB-S4 and LiteBIRD will tighten those constraints; whether they bear on the Born-rule proposal depends on the quantities §18.2 does not yet fix.

The framework's CMB content does not compete with Levin's. The matched-circle test depends on the spatial extent and structure of topological identifications. The framework's Born-rule transient violations, if present, depend on the temporal evolution of the visible-hidden coupling during inflation. The two tests probe orthogonal physical content using the same data sets — analogous to how the JUNO experiment probes neutrino mixing and the same reactor antineutrino flux probes other oscillation parameters.

**Open question: non-orientability and chirality.** On Chapter 5's H-link/H-cust branch and under the physical identification H-spin', the staggered trace-out gives a vector-like $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ coupling; chiral $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ additionally requires the open taste-selectivity hypothesis H-χ' (SM §4.8). A separate question is whether a non-orientable global topology (Klein-bottle specifically, or other non-orientable compact identifications) interacts non-trivially with the framework's local-lattice chirality.

On the stated local lattice branch, the chirality scale is microscopic. A global identification on a cosmological scale would be separated from it by roughly 60 orders of magnitude at $\sigma = 1$. Whether global phase or sign factors can affect the local chirality structure remains open.

This is an open question the framework has not addressed. Resolving it would require a calculation: starting from a specific non-orientable compact topology (Klein-bottle or otherwise), determining whether the framework's local chirality derivation acquires global corrections, and computing the observable signatures of any such corrections. The framework does not currently have the calculation; the question is pre-registered as an open theoretical task.

The framework leaves cosmic spatial topology open. Levin’s compact-universe proposals illustrate one way to model a finite substratum under the conditions above. The effect of non-orientability on the local chirality argument requires a separate calculation.

## 18.10 The framework's position on consciousness

The framework's mathematical object — the partition with C1–C4 structure — defines an *observer* in a structural sense. The observer is not yet a *conscious entity*. Whether the structural conditions for observation are also sufficient conditions for consciousness is a separate question, with the framework's commitments having implications for what answers are possible.

This section develops the framework's substantive position on consciousness: observation is *necessary but not sufficient* for consciousness. The framework provides structural necessary conditions for conscious systems and imposes constraints on what physics-based consciousness theories are compatible with its commitments. The hard problem of consciousness proper — the explanatory gap between physical/structural facts and phenomenal experience — is not addressed, and the framework is explicit about this scope limitation.

**Mathematical observer is not equivalent to conscious entity.** The framework's observer is a partition $V$ of the substratum's configuration space combined with the conditions C1 (coupling to hidden sector), C2 (record persistence — slow-bath separation the route; a physical-regime premise in [Main]'s typing), C3 (sufficient hidden-sector capacity), and C4 (history readback — the load-bearing memory condition). The partition is a mathematical object that has dynamical consequences (the trace-out producing the reduced law whose representation carries the emergent QFT), thermodynamic consequences (entropy displacement producing dark matter phenomenology), and empirical consequences (the JUNO prediction, the BH entropy, the dark sector).

The partition is not, by itself, a conscious entity. A thermostat coupled to room temperature implements a partition in a degenerate sense (the thermostat's internal state interacts with the room's thermodynamic state). A cosmological horizon implements a partition with $\tau_S/\tau_B \sim 10^{-32}$. A protein interior implements a partition. Nothing in the framework attributes phenomenal experience to any of them. The framework's mathematical observer is therefore a structural object, not a phenomenal one, and the framework does not claim equivalence between mathematical observation and conscious experience.

**The asymmetric position.** The framework's substantive content on consciousness is the asymmetric claim: any conscious system must implement a partition with C1–C4 structure, but not every C1–C4 partition is conscious. The framework provides structural necessary conditions for consciousness without specifying sufficient conditions. The two directions below differ in standing: Direction 2 follows from the definition of observation, which detectors and horizons satisfy; Direction 1 is a conjecture about consciousness, motivated by the role of memory in conscious information processing, and not a theorem of the framework. Evidence in the framework's sense is perspective-neutral — a record bears its evidential relations through its correlations at the partition, whether or not a conscious system takes it up; whether evidence as experienced requires a conscious perspective is an epistemological question the framework does not address.

The claim has two directions worth distinguishing.

*Direction 1: Consciousness requires observation.* Any conscious system must implement a partition with substantial C1–C4 structure. A system that fails C1 (no coupling to a hidden sector) cannot be conscious because it has no environment to be aware of. A system that fails C2 (hidden sector equilibrates faster than visible-sector dynamics) cannot be conscious on the framework's account with C2 read in its timescale form: the memory it retains, which would support integration over time, is suppressed. A system that fails C3 (insufficient hidden-sector capacity) cannot be conscious because it cannot store the information that conscious experience would require. The C1–C4 architecture is necessary for the non-Markovian information processing that conscious systems must exhibit.

*Direction 2: Observation does not require consciousness.* The cosmological horizon, proteins, and engineered C1–C4 systems implement observation structurally, and nothing in the definition requires that they be conscious. The framework's observation has structural consequences (the memory-bearing quantum representation, dark sector phenomenology) at the level of the partition's emergent dynamics, but it does not entail phenomenal experience. Whatever distinguishes the conscious C1–C4 systems from the non-conscious ones is some *additional feature* beyond the bare C1–C4 architecture — biological substrate, neural architecture, integrated information $\Phi$, global workspace dynamics, recurrent processing, or some combination — that the framework's structural commitments do not specify.

The asymmetric position is more conservative than alternatives like dual-aspect monism (where structural observation and phenomenal consciousness are necessarily co-occurring) and panpsychism (where every C1–C4 system is conscious to some degree). The framework's content allows but does not require these stronger positions.

**Substantive constraints on consciousness theories.** The framework's commitments constrain what physics-based theories of consciousness are compatible with it.

*Deterministic substratum rules out fundamental-indeterminism accounts.* The framework's substratum is deterministic: the bijection $\varphi$ maps each state to a unique successor. Quantum randomness is epistemic, not ontic (§18.4). Theories of consciousness that *require* fundamental indeterminism in physical reality are incompatible with the framework. The most prominent example is Penrose's orchestrated objective reduction (Penrose-Hameroff), which posits that conscious experience arises through gravitationally-induced quantum collapse in microtubules — a specific form of fundamental indeterminism that the framework's deterministic substratum forbids. The framework's commitments make Penrose-Hameroff structurally incompatible.

*C1–C4 architecture is necessary for non-Markovian information processing.* Conscious systems must exhibit memory effects, history-dependent dynamics, and readback of stored correlations between subsystems — the structural signatures of non-Markovian dynamics that emerge from the framework's C1–C4 architecture. Theories of consciousness that treat conscious systems as fundamentally Markovian (instantaneous, memoryless processing) are incompatible with the framework's account of how cognitively non-trivial information processing must work. Strict feed-forward computational accounts of consciousness face this constraint.

*A finite substratum bounds information content.* A finite substratum (§18.7) would constrain theories of consciousness that posit infinite information content for conscious experience. Theories requiring genuinely infinite degrees of freedom (some forms of mathematical Platonism applied to consciousness, for instance) face this constraint.

These are real constraints rather than vacuous ones. The framework rules out a substantial class of physics-based consciousness theories while remaining compatible with the broader landscape of accounts that supply additional sufficient conditions for consciousness beyond the framework's necessary ones.

**Integrated information theory as adjacent content.** Integrated information theory (IIT), developed primarily by Giulio Tononi, is the most prominent physics-adjacent consciousness theory and has substantive content overlap with the framework. IIT proposes that consciousness corresponds to integrated information $\Phi$, a quantitative measure of how much a system's parts inform each other beyond what their individual contributions predict. Systems with high $\Phi$ are conscious; systems with low $\Phi$ are not.

There is a real structural relationship between the framework's C1–C4 architecture and IIT's $\Phi$. C1 (non-trivial coupling) is necessary for non-zero $\Phi$ — without inter-part coupling, integrated information cannot be non-trivial. C2 (record persistence — realized here by slow-bath separation) is necessary for IIT's information integration over time — the framework's persistence requirement, met via $\tau_B \gg \tau_S$, provides the memory architecture that integration requires. C3 (sufficient hidden-sector capacity) is necessary for the system's state space to be large enough to support meaningful integration. A system satisfying C1–C4 with substantial margins (large $\tau_B/\tau_S$, large $N_H/N_V$) is a system with substantial $\Phi$; a system satisfying C1–C4 marginally has low $\Phi$.

The relationship is not exact identity. C1–C4 are *necessary conditions* for non-Markovian dynamics in the framework's specific sense, while $\Phi$ is a *quantitative information-theoretic measure* in IIT's specific formulation. But the conditions overlap substantially. The framework's content here is compatible with IIT as a candidate sufficient-condition theory: the C1–C4 architecture provides the necessary structural conditions for the framework, and IIT's $\Phi$ provides the additional sufficient content for consciousness within that architecture. The framework does not endorse IIT's identification claim (that $\Phi$ *is* consciousness), but it provides structural foundations that IIT-like accounts can build on.

The relationship runs in both directions. The framework's structural account of subjecthood — what makes a system a subject of observation in the framework's mathematical sense — is compatible with IIT's account of what makes a system conscious. Conversely, IIT's predictions about which systems are conscious provide quantitative content that complements the framework's structural necessary conditions. The two accounts engage at different levels and are not in tension.

**The hard problem is not addressed.** The framework's content on consciousness is necessary-condition content. The framework provides structural conditions that conscious systems must satisfy; it provides constraints on what theories of consciousness are compatible with the framework's commitments; it provides empirical predictions about the structural features conscious systems must exhibit. None of this addresses the hard problem of consciousness proper — the question of *why* phenomenal experience accompanies physical processes at all.

The hard problem is a question about the relationship between structural/physical facts and phenomenal facts. The framework provides a structural account (the partition with C1–C4 structure produces the emergent dynamics that conscious systems must satisfy). What it does *not* do is bridge the explanatory gap from such a structural account to phenomenal experience. The framework predicts which systems should be conscious as a necessary-condition claim, but it does not explain *why* implementing those necessary conditions should produce subjective experience rather than functional information processing "in the dark."

This scope limitation is honest rather than evasive. No physics-based framework currently bridges the explanatory gap; claiming that the framework does so would be overreaching. The framework's value here is its discipline: necessary-condition content with substantive constraints on theories, combined with explicit acknowledgment that the hard problem is not addressed. This is more than the strict scope often adopted in physics frameworks (which decline to engage consciousness at all) and less than the overclaiming sometimes seen (where physics frameworks announce solutions to the hard problem they cannot actually deliver).

**Empirical predictions and constraints.** The framework's necessary-condition conjecture implies structural expectations about the features conscious systems should exhibit; they are consistency checks on the conjecture, not evidence for the framework.

Conscious systems should exhibit non-Markovian information processing — memory effects, history-dependent dynamics, readback of stored correlations between subsystems. The C1–C4 architecture is empirically detectable in neural systems; the prediction is that systems we identify as conscious (humans, mammals, possibly other complex biological systems) should exhibit these signatures at their relevant timescales. Neuroscience evidence — synaptic plasticity, neuromodulator gradients, glial dynamics, slow oscillations supporting cognitive integration — is consistent with this prediction. The framework's content is structurally aligned with the broader neuroscience consensus on the architecture of conscious information processing.

Conscious systems should *not* be possible in architectures that strictly fail C1–C4. The framework predicts that strictly feed-forward neural network architectures (without recurrent connections, without memory states, without integration over time) cannot produce consciousness — they fail C2 by lacking the temporal integration architecture. This is consistent with the empirical finding that consciousness in humans is associated with recurrent processing rather than purely feed-forward processing. The framework's content here is a structural constraint that aligns with empirical neuroscience.

The framework's bioinformatics content from Chapter 17 is structurally related. The molecular clock overdispersion, rate autocorrelation across the tree of life, and LTEE power-law fitness trajectory are all empirical signatures of non-Markovianity in evolutionary biology systems. The framework predicts that similar non-Markovian signatures should be detectable in conscious systems at their relevant timescales — the brain's analog of the molecular clock's overdispersion would be the variability of neural processing rates across timescales, predicted by the framework to be non-Markovian rather than Markovian. The empirical program connecting these structural predictions to consciousness research is open.

The framework's content on consciousness is therefore disciplined necessary-condition content with substantive empirical predictions, real constraints on theory choices, and explicit honesty about the limits of its scope. The hard problem of consciousness proper is not solved or dissolved by the framework. The framework's value in consciousness research is structural — what makes a system the kind of system that *could* be conscious — rather than phenomenal.

## 18.11 Chapter close

This chapter has developed the framework's distinctive forward content beyond standard quantum mechanics and general relativity. The empirical record of Chapters 5 through 17 establishes the framework's content where it agrees with current physics at high precision; this chapter has developed the framework's content where it makes predictions current physics does not yet test.

**Four categories of forward content.** The chapter's content sorts into four categories distinguished by their empirical accessibility.

*Testable forward predictions.* The restricted quantum-statistical regime (§18.3) is testable through distinguishability-revival measurements in single-molecule kinetics, accessible to current laboratory techniques. The BQP computational ceiling (Chapter 14) is exposed to any demonstrated super-BQP computation — which would falsify the global-coverage premise first, the framework-plus-model conjunction only with further argument. The framework's content in this category is empirically engaged at near-term experimental scales.

*Conditional prospects.* The Born rule's dynamical equilibrium (§18.2) is a proposed mechanism without a specified observable response. It would become testable through CMB measurements of the primordial power spectrum and non-Gaussianity, such as CMB-S4 and LiteBIRD, only once the source dynamics, the family of initial deviations and the map to observables are specified; until then it carries no quantitative prediction.

*Structural commitments with limited empirical access.* The epistemic character of quantum randomness (§18.4) is consequential for foundations but has no consequence observed at current experimental precision. The cumulative case on quantum gravity (§18.5) reframes three problems but has no direct empirical signature beyond the predictions already developed in the cosmological constant, BH information paradox, and renormalization treatments. The arrow of time (§18.8) is observationally manifest but is not directly testable as a framework prediction because all physics-based accounts of the arrow generate the same empirical signature. The framework's content in this category is structurally consequential without producing additional empirical handles beyond those already developed in Chapters 5-17.

*Framework's scope limits.* Cosmic spatial topology (§18.9) remains open. Local comparisons between models of different topology require matching causal-window inputs. The hard problem of consciousness (§18.10) is explicitly outside the framework's scope; the framework provides necessary-condition content on consciousness but does not bridge the explanatory gap from structural to phenomenal facts. The framework's content in this category is honest acknowledgment of what the framework does not address.

**Forward pointers.** Chapter 19 develops the framework's content against the standard list of open problems in fundamental physics. The chapter is a systematic inventory: ten problems the framework addresses with a resolution, dissolution, conditional construction or partial treatment, its table giving each one's status (quantum gravity in three components, covariant unification open, the cosmological constant, dark matter, dark energy, the measurement problem, BH information, hierarchy, gauge group origin, generation puzzle, dark sector budget), two split-status entries (the Born-rule origin — representation level settled, coherent operational extension characterized by five named conditions; strong CP — open under two named conditions) and five scope limits where the framework does not provide a derived value (the flavor problem with structured open task; baryogenesis, which §19.3.3 reclassifies as solution-specific — the baryon asymmetry is downstream of the framework's flavor-sector CP input; inflation and initial conditions; the Hubble tension; the cosmological initial state). Each entry cross-references where in the book the relevant content is developed, with Chapter 19 functioning as a reference resource for readers wanting to understand how the framework positions itself relative to the recognized hard problems of fundamental physics.

**Cumulative case.** The framework's content across the empirical chapters (5-17) and this forward-predictions chapter (18) forms a cumulative case for the framework's structural commitments. Of the twenty-two Standard Model observables, two are parameter-free structural retrodictions and five are conditional structural ones, and the remainder are layered, chained or fitted; the seven gravitational-sector entries are conditional relations and estimates, two of them compared with measured values (G6, G8) and one with upper bounds (G12); the seven neutrino-sector entries include retrodictions and bound-only comparisons. The framework's content in biology (Chapters 11-13 and 16-17) makes specific predictions that align with the empirical record across non-Markovian dynamics, evolutionary signatures, and clinical pharmacology. The forward content of this chapter extends the framework's reach to phenomena standard physics has no specific commitments on — Born-rule equilibrium, the capacity-controlled origin of partial revival, computational ceilings, and the arrow of time. The consciousness conjecture of §18.10 is not part of this case.

The framework's evidential standard is parameter-free structural prediction matching observation. The cumulative case across the book is retrodictive — exact structural values matching existing data across physics, biology, and computation — and the pre-registered standard properly applies to the forward content of this chapter, which awaits its data. The framework's physical content beyond conventional physics is structural extension of the same framework that produced the empirical predictions of Chapters 5-17, not speculative addition to an empirical base.

The chapter is honest about what it does not address. The hard problem of consciousness is not solved. The cosmological initial state is not explained. Several other open problems remain in the framework's residue. Chapter 19 develops these residues in detail, alongside the resolved-problem inventory that makes the framework's cumulative empirical reach explicit.
