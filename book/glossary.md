# Glossary

This glossary defines the framework's distinctive terminology and the technical vocabulary used throughout the book. Entries are organized alphabetically. Cross-references to chapter and appendix sections point to where each concept is most fully developed.

**Note on convention:** terms in *italics* within definitions indicate other glossary entries. Where the framework's usage differs from standard physics or mathematics usage, the difference is noted explicitly.

***

**Anomaly cancellation.** A condition in quantum field theory requiring that the sum of contributions from chiral fermions to certain triangle diagrams vanishes. In the Standard Model, anomaly cancellation between fermion generations is a structural consistency requirement that constrains the allowed hypercharge assignments. The framework derives the *unique* hypercharge assignment $(Y_Q, Y_u, Y_d, Y_L, Y_e) = (1/6, 2/3, -1/3, -1/2, -1)$ from the six anomaly conditions of $\mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$ on the nonzero-$Y_Q$ branch, with no free parameters. Developed in Chapter 6 §6.7 and Appendix B §B.5.

**A1 (Finiteness).** The open hypothesis, not adopted, that the substratum has a finite total configuration space (Chapter 2 §2.4), as assumed in the minimal finite representative. Motivated physically by the Bekenstein bound at the cosmological horizon (an entropy-based bound, under the physical assumptions of input E3, of at most $e^{S_{\text{dS}}}$ effectively distinguishable states of the visible sector and boundary layer, $S_{\text{dS}} \sim 10^{122}$). Discussed in Chapter 2 §2.4, with defense in Appendix C §C.6.

**Background independence (A6).** The sixth of the framework's structural assumptions A1–A6: the substratum dynamics is covariant under spatially varying internal-index transformations, a site-dependent transformation being a symmetry provided the coupling data carried on the links are transformed with it. The content of the assumption is the covariant interface itself — the coupling is link-valued data the transformations act on — and once that is in place the local transformation law imposes no further condition on the rule. It is distinct from the stronger fixed-background condition that the same transformations leave the dynamics invariant with the coupling held fixed, which for an invertible coupling forces agreement across every coupled pair of sites; from the global commutant symmetry, its specialization to transformations constant across the lattice that leave the coupling unchanged, from which the local gauge reading proceeds; and from the state-dependent coupling graph, a separate principle sharing the name, under which the graph itself evolves with the state. Where the framework says it has no fixed spacetime background, with spacetime emergent, that is a third sense of the words and not this assumption. Developed in Chapter 2 §2.4 and Chapter 5 §5.5.

**Barandes correspondence.** The mathematical result establishing that stochastic processes in its own indivisible class correspond structurally to unitary quantum dynamics under its hypotheses. The class is a tuple structure — configuration space, target and conditioning times, transition maps, prior, random variable — and divisibility failure is generic within it rather than definitional, so Markov chains are included. The framework's link to it is the explicit translation hypothesis *(T)*: that the framework's per-horizon transition family instantiates that tuple ([Main §3.1], `tuple_probes.py`). *P-indivisibility* does not gate the representation's existence; it determines whether the representation is nontrivial. This route supplies a compressed transition-statistics representation. The internal reversible construction and permutation unitary independently give the exact fixed-basis representation of a supplied law on a finite alphabet and horizon. A physical operational instrument algebra requires its own assumptions. Developed in Chapter 1 §1.6 and Appendix B §B.2.

**Bekenstein-Hawking entropy.** The entropy $S = A/(4 l_p^2)$ assigned to a horizon of area $A$ in units of the Planck length $l_p$. The factor $1/4$ follows from the Hawking temperature and the first law. In the framework it is the ratio $2\pi/8\pi$ fixed by the horizon calibration of Chapter 7 (conditional on H-slope); the mode count reproduces it for any cell size once the calibration is imposed. The September 2025 analysis of GW250114, observed on January 14, supports the classical area theorem from inferred Kerr areas; the $1/4$ coefficient has no direct empirical test. Developed in Chapter 7 §7.5.

**Bell-inequality violations.** Empirical correlations exceeding bounds derivable from local hidden-variable theories. For a response-complete deterministic OI completion, measurement independence plus ontic parameter independence, with a $\pm1$ outcome in every run, gives Bell factorization and $|S_{\rm CHSH}|\le2$. The framework therefore retains measurement independence but relinquishes **ontic parameter independence** for Bell-violating substrata, with operational no-signaling required. The measurement-dependent alternative is recorded rather than adopted: the manuscript proves the model-independent necessary lower bound $I_{\rm ont}\ge0.0309$ bits for the quantum CHSH value, while an optimized causal model in Hall--Branciard uses about $0.080$ bits (the smaller $\sim0.046$-bit benchmark is retrocausal). Developed in Chapter 1 §1.7 and Appendix C §C.2.

**Born rule.** The quantum-mechanical prescription that the probability of measurement outcome $i$ on state $|\psi\rangle$ is $|\langle i|\psi\rangle|^2$. Conventionally treated as a postulate of quantum mechanics. In the framework, at the finite fixed-basis observable-law layer, the Born form is part of an exact equivalent representation: $S\iff D\iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}$ ([Main §3.4]). The quadratic form is part of the representation dictionary. Its physical selection independently of that dictionary remains open. What remains is operational: on a finite carrier, within complex matrix kinematics taken as given, an operational theory's available instruments on the carrier and its composites are the standard noncommuting quantum instruments exactly when the five completion conditions of the operational-completion characterization ([GR §3.3]) hold; the substratum's stated interventions do not supply one of them, full reversible control; whether an extended OI mechanism can supply it, which of the others the substratum supplies, and a derivation of that kinematics are open. The read-write-cycle equilibrium account in Chapter 18 §18.2 is an additional physical mechanism proposal and is not needed for the finite representation equivalence.

**BQP (Bounded-error Quantum Polynomial time).** Decision problems solvable by uniform polynomial-size quantum circuits with error probability bounded by $1/3$. Chapter 14 characterizes computation for observers in a scalable uniform model with quantum preparation, gates and readout, polynomial time and precision, efficient simulation, suitable noise assumptions and operational closure with charged conditioning. Application to all physical computation requires global coverage of the upper-bound hypotheses.

**C1 (Coupling).** The hidden-influence condition in the history-memory characterization. Requires non-trivial coupling between the *visible* and *hidden sectors* of the *embedded observer*'s partition: hidden influence on some accessible visible transition. A non-permutation one-step matrix $T$ is the cheapest *sufficient witness* of C1 — not its definition and not necessary, since influence can hide at step one and surface later (the delayed-revival family, [Main §3.4]). A causal write to the hidden state is an additional property of particular realizations. Discussed in Chapter 1 §1.3.

**C2 (Memory persistence).** A record-persistence condition with a conditional physical timescale form. The history-memory characterization uses C1, C3 and C4. Stated in two forms ([Main §1.3]): (C2-structural), that a hidden record of visible history persists to the readback that uses it — supplied by reversibility in every bijective realization, so as a condition it bears on coarse-grained descriptions of the hidden sector; and (C2-slow), $\tau_S \ll \tau_B$, one sufficient mechanism, fast conserved dynamics being another (`fastbath_probes.py`). C2 is the persistence half of what C4 operationally needs (the other half is in-window routing), and, in the coupling-step model of [Main §3.4]'s physical-memory theorem — next visible state determined by the current visible and hidden states, any hidden map between couplings — observed conditional memory $M_k > 0$ at an accessible order $k$ forces some realized hidden conditional at that coupling time to stay at least $\varepsilon_{\min}(M_k, n_V)$ from its reference prior $\pi_H$, with no mixing hypothesis (the physical-memory theorem, [Main §3.4]); under the hidden-sector mixing hypothesis this becomes a lower bound on bath persistence. The proposed cosmological estimate gives $\tau_S / \tau_B \sim 10^{-32}$ for the illustrative laboratory step if the horizon scale sets the laboratory effective mixing time. When the coarse-grained hidden sector thermalizes between measurements, C2 fails in its timescale form and, under the mixing hypothesis, the conditional memory of the dynamics is bounded in terms of the residue of that mixing at the coupling time. Discussed in Chapter 1 §1.3.

**C3 (Hidden-sector capacity).** The second of the framework's three structural conditions (C1, C3, C4). Requires hidden capacity sufficient for the process's conditional memory: $\log_2 |\mathcal{C}_H| \geq I^*$, the capacity floor — the data-processing bound $I(X_{<t}; X_{>t} \mid X_t) \leq I(X_{<t}; H_t \mid X_t) \leq \log_2|\mathcal{C}_H|$ applied to the whole visible future, which holds in every faithful realization ([Main §3.4]). $N_H \gg N_V$ is the comfortable physical regime, not the condition; for our universe at the cosmological horizon, $N_H \sim 10^{122}$. C3 saturation caps accessible memory — the theorem-level content. The mechanism proposal hypothesizes that the marginal-capacity regime is the *restricted quantum-statistical* regime of partial distinguishability revival, memoryless operational modeling failing while the transition-statistics layer remains representable ([Main §3.4]); all operational-failure readings here are proposal-level until a selection theorem supplies the implication. Discussed in Chapter 1 §1.3, with the restricted quantum-statistical regime in Chapter 15 §15.7 and Chapter 18 §18.3.

**C4 (History readback).** Two positive-probability histories with the same current visible state give different next-step laws through the hidden state. A preloaded hidden selector revealed by the history can supply this witness. A causal write has the further matched-seed requirement. For a witnessed history pair, [Main §2.3] gives a conditional-memory bound in terms of its probabilities and the total-variation gap. Discussed in Chapter 1 §1.3.

**Cabibbo angle.** The CKM mixing parameter $\lambda = \sin\theta_C \approx 0.225$ governing first/second-generation quark mixing in the Standard Model. Conventionally a fitted empirical parameter. The framework obtains $\lambda = 1/(\pi\sqrt{2})$ from a single Brillouin-zone distance on the substratum lattice, under an identification hypothesis whose emergent dressing is open (conditional structural), matching observation at $0.04\%$. Developed in Chapter 6 §6.5.

**Cat state (Schrödinger's cat).** A superposition of macroscopically distinct configurations. In the deterministic model each full trajectory has one configuration at each step, and the finite-record theorem gives a fixed-basis representation of its observed law. In a physical experiment, coherence depends on the preparation and environmental interaction. Engineered cat states test particular models of coherence loss or collapse (Chapter 1 §§1.6, 1.11 and Chapter 4 §4.5).

**CaMKII (Calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II).** A neuronal kinase involved in synaptic plasticity. Its regulatory dynamics provide a candidate record for a matched-present readback test. The proposed molecular timescale changes in disease need separate evidence (Chapter 16 §16.4).

**Characterization theorem.** The per-horizon characterization of the memory-bearing sector: an embedded observer's reduced description is non-Markovian on accessible horizons if and only if, for some accessible order, every finite accessible horizon extending at least one step beyond it admits a deterministic realization satisfying *C1*, *C3* and *C4*, with *C4*'s readback at that order; *C2* lies outside the equivalence, its physical content carried by the physical-memory theorem (persistence form without a mixing hypothesis, slow-bath form conditional on it). A fixed-basis unitarily evolving quantum representation exists for every finite-horizon law — internal and universal, by the finite-horizon equivalence $S \iff D \iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}$ ([Main §3.4]); the compressed representation is supplied across the correspondence's class through the imported stochastic–quantum correspondence, nontrivially where the dynamics is P-indivisible, under the translation hypothesis *(T)* — a separate conditional bridge, not part of the equivalence. Forward direction (sufficiency): a realization with *C1*, *C3* and *C4* $\Longrightarrow$ accessible non-Markovianity, the *C4* readback gap forcing positive conditional memory. Reverse direction (necessity): accessible non-Markovianity $\Longrightarrow$ such a realization on each accessible witness horizon, by the finite-horizon process dilation, with *C1*, *C3* and *C4* each necessary and *C2*'s necessity for physical hidden sectors conditional on the quantitative mixing hypothesis in its slow-bath (timescale) form and holding without it in its persistence form. Developed in Chapter 1 §§1.8-1.9, with necessity proofs in Appendix C §C.5.

**Chk1 (Checkpoint kinase 1).** A serine/threonine kinase regulating cell-cycle checkpoint responses to DNA damage. The framework identifies Chk1 as a canonical example of regulatory-domain *memory asymmetry*: Chk1 inhibitors selectively sensitize tumor cells because tumor cells rely on non-Markovian checkpoint dynamics that normal cells with intact G1 checkpoints do not require. Discussed in Chapter 16 §16.3.

**Chirality (in the framework).** The framework's commitment to chiral SU(2) electroweak structure rests on the staggered bipartite structure on the cubic lattice combined with the trace-out operation, and is conditional on the chirality hypothesis H-χ' (SM §4.8). Propagates through molecular physics to produce the parity-violating energy difference (PVED) between mirror-image molecules, predicting L-amino acids as the slightly more stable enantiomer. Discussed in Chapter 10 §10.7.

**Chromatin.** The protein-DNA complex organizing eukaryotic genomes, including nucleosome positioning, histone modifications (acetylation, methylation), DNA methylation patterns, and three-dimensional chromatin architecture. The framework treats chromatin as the *hidden sector* of the biological *C1–C4* partition with the transcriptional machinery as visible sector. Developed in Chapter 16 §16.9.

**CKM (Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa) matrix.** The unitary mixing matrix between Standard Model quark mass and weak interaction eigenstates. Conventionally parameterized by three mixing angles and one CP-violating phase. The framework obtains the Cabibbo angle structurally, under an identification hypothesis whose emergent dressing is open, and provides partial content for the broader CKM structure. Developed in Chapter 6 §6.5.

**CHSH inequality.** The Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt formulation of Bell's inequality, with the Bell-local bound of 2 and the quantum (Tsirelson) bound of $2\sqrt{2}$. In the deterministic OI completion, measurement independence plus parameter independence, with a $\pm1$ outcome in every run, gives the bound 2; quantum violation under measurement independence therefore uses ontic parameter dependence. The cited stochastic causal-local indivisible construction separately has the Tsirelson bound. Discussed in Appendix C §C.2.

**Cosmological constant problem.** The discrepancy of approximately $10^{122}$ between quantum field theory's predicted vacuum energy ($\sim 10^{113}$ J/m³) and the observed value inferred from cosmic expansion ($\sim 6 \times 10^{-10}$ J/m³). The framework proposes to *dissolve* this problem by identifying the two quantities as properties of logically distinct descriptions: the quantum vacuum energy is the emergent QFT's prediction within a partition; the observed cosmological constant is the gravitational consequence of the substratum's full content. The descriptive distinction is established; the dissolution is conditional on the open coupling step. Developed in Chapter 7 §7.7.

**Cryptochrome.** A flavoprotein that can participate in photoinduced radical-pair dynamics. Robin cryptochrome 4 has shown magnetic sensitivity in vitro. The responsible avian receptor and mechanism in vivo and its relevant coherence lifetime remain open (Chapter 13 §13.5).

**Cubic decomposition.** On the six signed link directions the cubic group has the multiplicity-free decomposition $\mathbf{6}=T_1\oplus E\oplus A_1$ of dimensions $3,2,1$. The unitary commutant is $\mathrm{U}(1)^3$. The distinct-block condensate stabilizer and specified charged matter action under H-link and H-cust enter the separate local gauge-algebra result. Developed in Chapter 5 §§5.4–5.6.

**DSW (Danielson-Satishchandran-Wald) horizon decoherence.** A 2022 result by Danielson, Satishchandran, and Wald proving within standard QFT in curved spacetime that Killing horizons impose fundamental decoherence on nearby quantum superpositions, with rate determined by horizon geometry. External convergence with the framework's universality-class content. Discussed in Chapter 9 §9.7 and Chapter 15 §15.8.

**Embedded observer.** The framework's technical term for an observer that is a proper subsystem of the system it registers — coupled to the system, bounded in extent, unable to access the full state from outside. A detector, a protein interior or a cosmological horizon qualifies; nothing in the definition requires consciousness. The central object of the framework's analysis. Defined precisely in Chapter 1 §1.2, characterized by C1–C4 in §1.3.

**Emergent description.** The reduced description of the visible sector after marginalizing over (tracing out) the hidden sector. In the framework, every finite-horizon observable law — C1–C4 or not — admits a fixed-basis unitary quantum representation ($S \iff D \iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}$, [Main §3.4]); systems satisfying C1–C4 constitute the memory-bearing sector, where hidden predictive memory is unavoidable in every completion. Developed throughout Chapter 1.

**Epigenome.** The set of chromatin marks and methylation patterns that regulate gene expression while remaining heritable across cell divisions and (partially) generations. The biological instantiation of the framework's *substratum-emergent operator distinction* at the molecular scale. Discussed in Chapter 16 §§16.9-16.10.

**Extended Church-Turing Thesis (ECT).** Conventionally, the classical efficient-simulation conjecture — any physically realizable computation efficiently simulable by a probabilistic classical Turing machine — the thesis quantum computing challenges. Its quantum counterpart, the BQP-form (quantum) ECT, asserts efficient quantum-simulability of all physical computation. The framework derives the BQP-form (quantum) ECT as a *conditional theorem* under a global-coverage premise (every physically realizable computation falling under Chapter 14 §14.4's operational-closure and efficiency hypotheses): any computation so covered satisfies the BQP bound. Developed in Chapter 14 §14.6.

**Fibre-Gram data.** For a coherent lift of a visible law and each visible output, the Gram matrix of the hidden states into which the lift sends the visible inputs. Its diagonal is the visible law itself. Two lifts are related by the two-sided invisible action at one time exactly when their fibre-Gram data agree up to phases, so the off-diagonal part modulo phases is exactly what the visible law leaves free at that time. It is a coordinate on the space of lifts, not a physical quantity, and no statement is made about what selects its trajectory through time. Developed in Chapter 1 and §19.3.9.

**Fine-tuning.** The observation that certain physical parameters (fine-structure constant, mass ratios, cosmological constant) appear to occupy narrow ranges supporting chemistry and complexity. The framework's reading: most chemistry-supporting features are *structural* (derived from the framework's commitments) rather than parametric, dissolving the conventional fine-tuning problem. Discussed in Chapter 10 §10.8.

**Frauchiger-Renner theorem.** A 2018 multi-agent no-go theorem deriving a conflict among quantum probability-one certainty, consistency of agents' certainty inferences and single outcomes for its protocol. Partition-relative descriptions alone do not resolve that conflict; applying it to a proposed OI completion requires its operational probabilities and a separate check of those premises. Discussed in Appendix C §C.3.

**Gap equation (for $\hbar$).** The thermal self-consistency condition between the classical horizon temperature $T_{cl} = c^2\epsilon^2 \kappa/(8\pi G k_B \sigma)$ and the KMS temperature $T_Q = \hbar\kappa/(2\pi c k_B)$ of the emergent QFT, producing $\hbar = c^3 \epsilon^2/(4G\sigma)$, with $\sigma$ the entropy of one boundary mode. It relates Planck's constant to the discreteness scale, conditional on H-slope, and fixes only $\epsilon/\sqrt{\sigma} = 2\,l_p$: a self-consistency condition, not an independent determination of the value of $\hbar$. Developed in Chapter 7 §7.3 and Appendix B §B.4.

**Gauge group.** A continuous group of local symmetries under which the dynamics is invariant. The Standard Model gauge group is $\mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$. Conditionally on H-link, the framework derives this gauge group from the stabilizer of a cubic-equivariant condensate with distinct block values on the substratum lattice (the coupling matrix itself is scalar); the stabilizer computation is exact, the physical-carrier identification is the premise. Developed in Chapter 5.

**Generation (fermion).** In the Standard Model, the three sets of fermions with identical gauge-quantum-number assignments but different masses: $(u, d, e, \nu_e)$, $(c, s, \mu, \nu_\mu)$, $(t, b, \tau, \nu_\tau)$. The framework derives one singlet plus one symmetry-related triplet of candidate matter sectors from the staggered taste structure on the cubic lattice; reading the triplet as three *physical* generations is conditional on *H-spin'* (SM §4.7), whose free-kernel and 4D-staggered routes are both settled negative. Discussed in Chapter 6 §6.2.

**God's-eye view.** A hypothetical observer coextensive with the universe, with complete knowledge of all degrees of freedom. The framework identifies the God's-eye view as the *C3* failure limit and shows that it is not a limit case of observation but a category outside it — observation is partition-relative, and without a partition, observation does not occur. Discussed in Chapter 1 §1.10.

**GW250114.** A binary black-hole merger observed on January 14, 2025. The September 10, 2025 LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA paper inferred an increase in Kerr horizon area, with $4.4\sigma$ for its fiducial analysis. This classical area comparison leaves the Bekenstein-Hawking $1/4$ entropy coefficient untested. Discussed in Chapter 7 §7.5.

**Hidden sector.** The portion of the framework's partition that the embedded observer does not directly access. The framework's four conditions specify properties of this sector (coupling, slow evolution, large capacity). For laboratory observers in our universe, the hidden sector is everything beyond the cosmological horizon plus the trans-horizon degrees of freedom of any local environment. Defined in Chapter 1 §1.2.

**Higgs (in the framework).** The framework treats the Higgs as the *singlet* ($A_1$) taste of the staggered decomposition — a *composite scalar* in the framework's account rather than a fundamental field. The Higgs quartic coupling at the compositeness scale is exactly zero, with the observed value at low energies generated by RGE running. Developed in Chapter 6 §6.2.

**Hsp90.** A molecular chaperone whose role in buffering phenotypic variation has been studied in evolutionary capacitance. The matched-present readback and its kinetics remain proposed tests (Chapter 17 §17.9).

**Hypercharge.** The Standard Model U(1) quantum number of fermion multiplets, with values $(Y_Q, Y_u, Y_d, Y_L, Y_e) = (1/6, 2/3, -1/3, -1/2, -1)$. Conventionally fitted empirical inputs. The framework derives these values as the unique anomaly-free solution on the nonzero-$Y_Q$ branch. Developed in Chapter 6 §6.7 and Appendix B §B.5.

**Information backflow.** A revival of distinguishability: the trace distance between the evolved states of two preparations increases over some interval (the Breuer–Laine–Piilo signature). In a P-divisible process distinguishability never increases, so backflow certifies P-indivisibility; a P-indivisible process need not show it. It is distinct from accessible finite-horizon memory, the conditional past–future dependence that history readback (C4) supplies on accessible windows, which does not by itself produce backflow ([Main §2.3]). Discussed in Chapter 1 §1.5.

**Jordan-Chevalley decomposition.** For an invertible operator with a multiplicative Jordan-Chevalley decomposition, $F = F_{ss} \cdot F_u$ has commuting semisimple and unipotent factors, with $F_u = I + N$ and $N$ nilpotent. For the ring wave equation under the hypotheses of Appendix B §B.3, $N^2 = 0$ and $F$ and $F_{ss}$ have the same eigenvalues, so spectral invariants are independent of $N$; hidden-sector marginalization does not remove $N$ from the visible laws. Developed in Appendix B §B.3.

**JUNO (Jiangmen Underground Neutrino Observatory).** A reactor neutrino experiment that in November 2025 reported the first world-leading direct measurement of the solar neutrino mixing angle: $\sin^2\theta_{12} = 0.3092 \pm 0.0087$. The flavor-model value $1/3 - 1/(4\pi^2) = 0.3080$, with leading-order Cond 2 and the separate normalization $b_{23}=1$, matches the direct measurement at $0.14\sigma$ and the post-JUNO global fit ($0.3085 \pm 0.0073$) at $0.07\sigma$ — a retrodiction; The design-lifetime comparison tests the stated phenomenological target. Discussed in Chapter 8.

**Koide relation.** An empirical relation among the three charged-lepton masses: $Q = (m_e + m_\mu + m_\tau)/(\sqrt{m_e} + \sqrt{m_\mu} + \sqrt{m_\tau})^2$. Observed value $Q = 0.666664 \pm 0.000005$. The framework predicts $Q = 2/3$ from cubic-group quadratic Casimir; the match is better than $0.001\%$ ($0.4\sigma$). Developed in Chapter 6 §6.6.

**Markovian.** A process whose next-step conditional law given the available history depends only on the present state. The exact fixed-basis representation of a supplied law on a finite alphabet and horizon includes Markov laws ([Main §3.4]). Contrasted throughout Chapter 1.

**Memory asymmetry.** The framework's unifying therapeutic principle in medicine: when a disease process depends on non-Markovian signaling dynamics that the corresponding normal tissue does not depend on, therapies can target the memory structure rather than the catalytic function, with the framework predicting wider therapeutic windows. Developed in Chapter 16 §16.13.

**MOND (Modified Newtonian Dynamics).** A phenomenological framework for galactic rotation curves involving an acceleration scale of order $10^{-10}$ m/s². On the stated horizon and heat-input branch, the framework uses $a_0=cH/6$ with the factor $1/6$ imported from Verlinde. The proposed covariant sourcing remains conditional on G3. Developed in Chapter 7 §7.9.

**Non-Markovian.** A stochastic process whose future depends on past states beyond the present. Accessible history dependence is the content of a C4 witness. Finite-alphabet and finite-horizon representability also holds for memoryless laws, and recurrence-scale P-indivisibility uses a non-permutation rooted map and a finite return ([Main §3.4]). Physical applications require their own witnesses. Defined in Chapter 1 §1.5. Three notions are kept apart throughout and none unconditionally implies another: *accessible finite-horizon memory* (equivalently conditional past–future dependence in the visible law) is the framework's own term and is what "non-Markovian on accessible horizons" means here; *stochastic P-indivisibility* is failure of divisibility of the visible population law and rests on recurrence together with a non-permutation rooted map, not on C1/C3/C4 alone; *generic quantum non-Markovianity* is an open-system notion whose content depends on the quantum divisibility or backflow criterion adopted, and it is not what either of the first two asserts.

**Operational gluing theorem.** The framework's finite-resolution operational result ([Main §3.4]): for a fixed initial state, finite instrument family $\mathcal{F}$, horizon $K$ and grid $G$, the exact arithmetic construction requires both the initial state and instrument data in the working ring, with exact trace comparisons. At fixed outcome count the adaptive law has grid error $O(K/G)$, with one fixed mechanism per instrument, gluing by restriction across overlapping families, sequential and parallel composition, and a count of reachable hidden labels. The response-table construction uses supplied real probabilities. At fixed-point precision the initial rounding error and the open error bound for the clipped and normalized outcome law are separate. The theorem establishes finite operational **realizability** and, together with the uniform-continuum theorem, finite-test density. It does not by itself prove that the standard local quantum instrument category is the unique coherent operational completion, because the same finite reversible machinery can realize non-quantum instrument families. Which further conditions select the quantum instrument category on a finite carrier is stated by the operational-completion characterization ([GR §3.3]): five completion conditions, none supplied by bare finite OI, within complex matrix kinematics taken as given. Exact arithmetic checks in `opglue_probes.py` cover the stated finite examples.

**NV (Nitrogen-vacancy) center.** A point defect in diamond where a nitrogen atom replaces a carbon atom adjacent to a lattice vacancy. The basis for room-temperature quantum sensing with current sensitivity limited by the NV electron-spin coherence time. The framework predicts that backflow-aware sensing protocols exploiting the diamond lattice's slow nuclear-spin bath recover information Markovian protocols discard; the resulting sensitivity gain is not quantified. Discussed in Chapter 15 §15.6.

**Operator distinction (substratum-emergent).** The framework's structural distinction between operators acting at the substratum level (with specific conservation properties at the molecular or quantum-field level) and operators acting at the emergent level (with conservation properties at the coarse-grained level). The two operators can be functionally distinct even when they look identical at the emergent level. Developed in Chapter 6 §6.3 (for fundamental physics) and Chapter 16 §16.10 (for chromatin biology).

**Page curve.** The time-dependence of black-hole entanglement entropy: rising during the early evaporation phase, reaching a peak at the Page time, then falling as the black hole evaporates fully. The framework obtains a cycle-averaged Page curve on the full product space of black hole and radiation, under the scrambling condition H-scramble and the decoupling condition H-decouple (H-scramble fails for every bijection acting on an initial configuration with the bipartition aligned to the configuration coordinates, so the Page value needs an initial superposition over many configurations or a non-aligned bipartition), with a Page time that, for a mass-independent emission coefficient, is a fixed fraction of the evaporation time: $t_P \approx 0.646\, t_{evap}$ when the radiation's entropy equals the horizon entropy it carries off, earlier when it carries more. Developed in Chapter 7 §6.

**Partition-relativity.** The framework's structural feature that the same physical substratum admits multiple visible/hidden partitions, each producing its own emergent description. The emergent description depends on the partition choice; the underlying substratum does not. Does not by itself resolve a no-go theorem: the operational probabilities and each theorem's substantive premises require separate checks. Developed in Chapter 1 §1.4.

**P-indivisibility.** Failure of a stochastic transition family to factor through valid intermediate stochastic maps. In OI, a non-permutation one-step marginal on a fixed finite reversible system is a sufficient recurrence witness. More generally, any non-permutation rooted map forces indivisibility somewhere in the full recurrence cycle: recurrence returns the rooted visible map to the identity, and a non-permutation stochastic matrix has no stochastic inverse. A C4 readback gap supplies such a map, and so does every non-deterministic visible step, including in laws that are Markov on every accessible window. With a full-support visible prior the indivisibility is equivalent to memory at some order below the recurrence time, observable only when that order is accessible. This global result does not imply accessible short-time P-indivisibility in every window; the two notions are kept distinct. P-indivisibility is a statement about the visible population law and is not generic quantum non-Markovianity: inside the framework's diagonal-preserving realizations it does rule out a stochastic intermediate map and hence CP-divisibility, but outside that class the two come apart — a CP-divisible family with a unitary intermediate map can induce P-indivisible computational-basis populations, the intermediate map consuming coherences the population law never sees ([Main §2.3]).

**PMNS (Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata) matrix.** The unitary mixing matrix between Standard Model neutrino mass and flavor eigenstates. The cubic flavor model gives conditional angle values. Its solar-angle estimate $\sin^2\theta_{12} = 1/3 - 1/(4\pi^2)$ uses leading-order Cond 2 and the separate normalization $b_{23}=1$. The value matches the JUNO-era global fit at $0.07\sigma$, a retrodiction. Discussed in Chapter 8.

**Read-write cycle.** In a causal realization of history readback, varying the visible input at a fixed hidden seed changes the later hidden state, and the retained difference affects subsequent visible conditionals. The finite SWAP supplies a write witness. The coin–die example instead reveals a preloaded selector through the visible history. The cosmological and laboratory sourcing of a causal cycle remains open (Chapter 1 §§1.3, 1.5).

**Reader/writer/eraser pharmacology.** Categorizes molecular targets by their role in establishing marks, modifying or removing them, or recognizing their state. Chapter 16 proposes comparing therapeutic windows for restoration of different molecular functions.

**Restricted quantum-statistical regime.** The mechanism proposal's name for partial distinguishability revival on an accessible window, $0 < \mathcal{N} < 1$. Here $\mathcal{N}$ is the revival measure of Chapter 18 §18.3: the largest increase $d_t - d_s$, over pairs of preparations and times $s < t$ in the window, of their distinguishability $d$ (the total variation of the visible population laws, equivalently the trace distance of the diagonal states) — the peak *information backflow* on the window, kept separate from the cumulative Breuer–Laine–Piilo sum. $\mathcal{N} > 0$ certifies P-indivisibility on the window; memory alone does not give $\mathcal{N} > 0$ ([Main §2.3]). The name refers to the quantum representation at the transition-statistics layer, which every finite law admits together with a deterministic hidden realization, with the operational layer not supplied ([Main §3.4]); it marks a revival range, neither a partial nor a complete quantum theory and not a quantum-membership scale. Hypothesized at marginal hidden capacity, with the proposed model form $\mathcal{F}(r) = 2r - r^2$, $r = N_H/N_V$ (derivation not supplied). Diagnosed by distinguishability-revival measurements; a multi-time memory test alone does not separate it from complete revival. Developed in Chapter 13 §13.7, Chapter 15 §§15.7-15.8, Chapter 18 §18.3.

**Rett syndrome.** A neurodevelopmental disorder caused by pathogenic MECP2 variants. Guy et al. (2007) observed neurological rescue after reactivating Mecp2 in symptomatic adult mice. Comparative therapeutic windows and the proposed mechanism require additional evidence (Chapter 16 §16.12).

**RNA world.** The hypothesis that early life used RNA for both information storage and catalysis before DNA and proteins evolved their specialized roles. RNA's dual role as template and catalyst in a single molecular species is the hypothesis's chemical support; it is an empirical hypothesis, not a consequence of C1–C4. Developed in Chapter 11 §11.7.

**Staggered (fermion structure).** The placement of independent fermion modes at alternating cubic lattice sites, producing the chiral structure of the framework's emergent QFT and the multiple-taste structure that gives rise to the $1 \oplus 3$ candidate-sector decomposition (three physical generations only under *H-spin'*). **Standard Model.** The current best theory of fundamental particle physics: a chiral gauge theory with gauge group $\mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$, three fermion generations, one Higgs doublet, and approximately 19 free parameters. The framework reproduces twenty-two classified observables — two parameter-free structural, five conditional structural, the remainder layered, chained or fitted (Appendix A) — and the anomaly-free hypercharges on the nonzero-$Y_Q$ branch; the gauge group follows under H-link, whose single-copy premise is not itself derived, the physical generation reading under H-spin', and the chirality assignment under H-χ'. Developed in Chapters 5-6.

**Stinespring dilation.** A 1955 theorem of operator algebra establishing that any completely-positive trace-preserving map on a Hilbert space can be realized as a unitary evolution on a larger Hilbert space with subsequent tracing-out. It provides operator-algebraic context for dilated channels. The finite-law proof in Main §3.4 directly constructs a reversible realization and its permutation unitary. Developed in Chapter 1 §1.6 and Appendix B §B.2.

**Substratum.** The underlying deterministic system $(S, \varphi)$, modelled by a bijection on total configurations. Its total configuration space is finite in the representative specified under the open posit A1. With a partition and preparation, tracing out the hidden sector gives a visible law with an exact fixed-basis quantum representation for finite alphabets and horizons. The substratum is the framework's primitive ontological commitment, held at the level of the reconstructed local residue modulo the substratum gauge group rather than any particular representative (Chapter 3) — the "what is really there" beneath the emergent quantum-mechanical description. Constructed concretely in Chapter 2.

**TLS (Two-level system).** A candidate microscopic source of time-correlated noise in superconducting qubits. Device-level attribution and accessible readback require their own tests (Chapter 15 §15.3).

**Trace-out.** The mathematical operation of marginalizing over (summing or integrating out) the hidden-sector degrees of freedom, producing the reduced description on the visible sector. The framework's central technical operation. Equivalent to partial trace in quantum mechanics. Defined throughout Chapter 1.

**Tsirelson bound.** The maximum CHSH inequality violation achievable by standard quantum mechanics: $2\sqrt{2}$. In the manuscript this value is retained for the cited **stochastic causal-local indivisible** construction and for any successful lift to the standard quantum Bell set. It is not the ceiling of a measurement-independent, parameter-independent deterministic completion recording a $\pm1$ outcome in every run, whose Bell bound is 2. The deterministic OI branch reaches quantum violation by ontic parameter dependence; the measurement-dependent alternative is separately **bounded and benchmarked** in Chapter 1 §1.7. Discussed in Appendix C §C.2.

**U-parity.** The framework's grading structure in the neutrino sector, distinguishing $U^+$ and $U^-$ states in the cubic-group flavor classification. Produces the framework's distinctive sum rule and structural relation among the three PMNS mixing angles. Developed in Chapter 8 §8.4.

**Universality class.** A set of substrata that share the same emergent description at the framework's Tier 1 level. The framework's content: the substratum-to-emergent map is many-to-one, with multiple bijections producing the same observable physics. Developed in Chapter 9 §9.3.

**Vibronic coherence.** Coherence involving electronic and vibrational degrees of freedom. Spectroscopy and dynamical models constrain these contributions in light-harvesting complexes (Chapter 13 §13.4).

**Visible sector.** The portion of the framework's partition that the embedded observer directly accesses. Contrasted with the *hidden sector*. The visible sector is where measurements occur and where the emergent description applies. Defined in Chapter 1 §1.2.

**Wolpert's theorem.** David Wolpert's results (2001, 2008) on fundamental limits of inference devices embedded in the system they are trying to predict. A physical analog of Gödel's incompleteness theorem and Turing's halting problem result — logical, worst-case, and unquantified. The framework's partition-induced trace-out is the mechanism of a *parallel physical* incompleteness, with the memory-bearing dynamics quantum mechanics represents as the specific cost; Wolpert's result corroborates the pattern but is not the source of the framework's derivation. Discussed in Chapter 12 §12.8, Appendix C §C.7.

**Yamanaka factors.** The four transcription factors (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc) whose transient expression reprograms somatic cells to induced pluripotent stem cells. Particular iPSC protocols retain some cell-of-origin methylation marks. The extent depends on the protocol and assay, and the proposed operator analogy motivates comparisons of chromatin marks and cell function. Discussed in Chapter 16 §16.10.
