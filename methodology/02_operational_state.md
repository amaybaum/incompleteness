### §B.2 Correctness-confidence companion table (added May 21, 2026; revised same session for reference-class correction)

The consistency-confidence table above tracks *whether the framework's claims are internally coherent and supported by the empirical anchors it cites*. This companion table tracks the different question caveat (4) flags but does not develop: *what is the probability that the framework is substantively correct as physics — i.e., that its central architectural claim and the predictions following from it turn out to be the way nature actually is, on a timescale of serious external review and the 3-5 year empirical resolution horizon?*

The two questions are legitimately different. A framework can be 90% internally consistent with cited evidence and still 30% likely to be the unique correct account, because internal consistency does not exclude alternative frameworks that explain the same anchors equally well. The honest caveat (4) reading of "uniqueness-confidence ~30-40%" anchors the present table.

**Reference-class calibration — critical correction.** An earlier draft of this table treated the framework as a *peer* of QED, QCD, GWS electroweak, and GR — i.e., as another member of the reference class of historically successful frameworks producing parameter-free retrodictions. This is the wrong reference class. The framework's architecture *includes* these as derived results, not as peers; QED-on-cubic-lattice, QCD-on-cubic-lattice, electroweak structure from cubic-group representation theory, and GR as the substratum-level classical limit are all claimed to fall out of the same substratum machinery. The proper reference class is therefore **foundational programs claiming to derive QED/QCD/GWS/GR from prior structure**, not the established theories themselves.

That reference class is small: string theory and successors, loop quantum gravity, asymptotic safety, causal set theory, group field theory, Wolfram's hypergraph rewriting, Adler's trace dynamics, and a few other active programs. Of these, **none has produced parameter-free retrodictions of SM observables at <1% precision**. The framework's 22 retrodictions plus the 0.07σ JUNO match, if real as parameter-free derivations rather than fits-with-hidden-flexibility, place the framework *alone* in this reference class. The base rate for substantive correctness in the larger "foundational programs" class is low (most don't deliver), but conditional on a program in this class *actually delivering retrodictions at the framework's claimed precision*, the correctness probability rises substantially because the empirical record is disqualifying-of-alternatives within the class.

The load-bearing audit question is therefore not the cross-domain reach or the empirical resolution horizon but **whether the 22 retrodictions are genuine parameter-free derivations from the substratum-level machinery, or whether they contain hidden flexibility that specialist review would surface**. This is the dimension that determines reference-class membership and thus dominates the correctness assessment.

**Cross-domain observation reframed.** The earlier draft noted that cross-domain reach "has no historical precedent at the framework's claimed level." This is correct, but the framework's claim is not that cross-domain reach is a separate feature — it's that *the same machinery* operates at different scales (substratum-emergent operator distinction at chromatin, partition trace-out at the cosmological horizon, C1-C3 architecture in single-enzyme kinetics). The chromatin and SM predictions are not independent empirical tests; they're tests of the same architecture. This makes triangulation across domains *more* discriminating than independent tests would be, while making cross-domain failure-modes propagate upward through the architecture.
