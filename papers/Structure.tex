\section{Hierarchical Structural Realism and Universality Classes of
Embedded
Observers}\label{hierarchical-structural-realism-and-universality-classes-of-embedded-observers}}

\textbf{Author:} Alex Maybaum\\
\textbf{Date:} May 2026\\
\textbf{Status:} DRAFT PRE-PRINT\\
\textbf{Classification:} Theoretical Physics / Foundations / Philosophy
of Physics\\
\textbf{Target venues:} Journal of High Energy Physics (JHEP),
Foundations of Physics, Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern
Physics

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{abstract}{%
\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}}

This paper articulates the structural commitments of the Incompleteness
of Observation (OI) framework beyond its selected cubic-lattice branch,
and develops comparisons with broader unification programs. An
observation axis distinguishes the foundational axiom, observer
admission, specified observational equivalence classes and selected
model families; a gauge axis distinguishes Hamiltonian rephasing,
emergent gauge structure, proposed substratum identifications and
algebra/channel equivalence. These are levels of comparison rather than
a proved chain of group inclusions. \emph{Part I} finds structural
incompatibilities between the standard matrix-model formulations of
string theory and OI's microscopic A1--A6 model family, particularly at
A2 and A5; this does not by itself decide observational equivalence.
\emph{Part II} defines embedded observers using a Schrödinger channel
and its invariant Heisenberg adjoint restriction, and defines
universality classes by normal \emph{-isomorphism and intertwining of
those restricted pairs. Proposition 9.5 proves inclusion for two
specified fixed-interface channel-preserving transformations, while the
full substratum gauge classification remains open. Minimal
prepared-isometry uniqueness is separated into finite-dimensional kernel
results and a general separable-space proof; neither determines full
hidden dynamics from one channel. }Part III* audits shared
probability-representation, dilation, memory and symmetry mechanisms
across frameworks. Shared features motivate comparisons but do not
establish membership in one observational class. Observer admission and
the conditional Standard-Model predictions remain distinct questions,
with prediction transport requiring the observables and dynamics
relevant to each prediction to be preserved.

\hypertarget{introduction}{%
\subsection{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}}

\hypertarget{scope-and-structure}{%
\subsubsection{1.1 Scope and structure}\label{scope-and-structure}}

The framework developed in {[}Main, SM, GR, Substratum{]} supplies
\textbf{conditional outputs for a selected cubic local branch}, not a
unique local substratum modulo a completed gauge group. The original
semigroup-transfer inference is refuted and the classification of all
admissible observer partitions remains open {[}Substratum §§3--4{]}.
H-Bell separately conditions Bell-inclusive existence, whose uniqueness
also remains open.

The first layer (§2) articulates the \emph{hierarchical structure} of
the framework's structural realism. Its observation axis runs from the
foundational axiom through observer admission and specified
observational equivalence to the selected OI model branch; its gauge
axis distinguishes Hamiltonian rephasing, emergent gauge structure,
proposed substratum identifications and algebra/channel equivalence.
Different predictions use different carriers and hypotheses. The diagram
organizes those dependencies without asserting a complete nested gauge
classification.

The second layer (Parts I, II, III) develops the framework's
relationship to broader unification programs through two related but
distinct questions:

\emph{Question 1: Substratum-level equivalence.} Are SM-reproducing
string compactifications gauge-equivalent representatives of OI's
substratum equivalence class? An affirmative answer would dissolve the
landscape multiplicity problem. Part I (§§3--7) develops this through
technical analysis of matrix-model formulations, with negative outcome
--- OI's structural conditions A2 (determinism) and A5 (linearity) fail.

\emph{Question 2: Cross-framework structural realism.} If the
microscopic model families differ, can specified embedded observers
nevertheless be observationally equivalent? Part II (§§8--13) defines
this through normal *-isomorphism and intertwining of the restricted
Heisenberg pairs. Proposition 9.5 supplies a restricted inclusion of
established channel-preserving OI transformations; comparison with the
full proposed \(\mathcal G_{\rm sub}\) remains open.

Part III (§14) synthesizes both questions through a four-feature audit
(Born rule, channel-level unitarity, memory/divisibility, commutant
gauge-invariance) characterizing the OI-string universality-class
relationship.

The two parts have different epistemic statuses. Part I produces a
\emph{definitive structural result} (negative --- matrix-model
formulations fail OI's A2 and A5). Part II is \emph{programmatic} --- it
articulates the universality-class proposal precisely, formalizes it via
definitions and propositions in §9, and identifies open technical
questions whose resolution would establish or refute the framing.
Definitive identification of specific universality classes --- including
the OI-string cross-class --- requires substantial follow-up work along
the lines flagged in §§11.2 and §13.1.

\hypertarget{the-string-landscape-as-a-multiplicity-problem}{%
\subsubsection{1.2 The string landscape as a multiplicity
problem}\label{the-string-landscape-as-a-multiplicity-problem}}

For four decades, string theory has aspired to derive the Standard Model
from compactification of higher-dimensional spacetime. The state of the
art is summarized by Marchesano, Shiu, and Weigand {[}MSW2024{]}: many
compactifications produce gauge groups that include
\(SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)\) as a subgroup; some produce three
generations of chiral matter; a smaller subset produces the observed
hypercharge structure; a still smaller subset produces approximately the
observed Yukawa pattern. No compactification has been demonstrated to
reproduce the Standard Model uniquely with all parameters matching
observation, and no general scheme is known to reproduce the flavor
hierarchy {[}MSW2024, §2.3{]}.

The conventional response to this state of affairs has been the
landscape picture: string theory has approximately \(10^{500}\)
metastable vacua, each corresponding to a different low-energy effective
theory, and our universe corresponds to whichever vacuum produces the SM
with the observed parameters. The vacuum-selection problem --- why our
universe is in this particular vacuum --- has been variously addressed
by the anthropic principle, by eternal inflation populating all vacua,
or by sharper conjectures (the swampland program) attempting to
delineate which vacua are consistent with quantum gravity {[}Vafa2005,
Palti2019, OoguriVafa2007{]}.

The swampland program in particular has produced significant progress in
constraining the landscape from above {[}Lehnert2025, MSW2024{]}.
Conjectures such as the Weak Gravity Conjecture, the de Sitter
Conjecture, the AdS Distance Conjecture, and the Cobordism Conjecture
aim to identify which low-energy effective theories admit consistent UV
completion in quantum gravity. These conjectures shrink the apparent
multiplicity of vacua but do not, in their current form, single out a
unique SM-reproducing vacuum.

\hypertarget{the-substratum-picture}{%
\subsubsection{1.3 The substratum
picture}\label{the-substratum-picture}}

In a separate program {[}Main, SM, GR, Substratum{]}, the Incompleteness
of Observation (OI) framework approaches fundamental physics from a
different direction. The starting point is the observation that quantum
mechanics is the marginal-statistics description used by an embedded
observer with finite distinguishable states; the trace-out over the
hidden sector generates the reduced observable law --- universally
admitting the quantum representation --- from a deterministic classical
substratum. The framework develops two technical results:

\textbf{Conditional local reconstruction (Theorem 23, {[}Substratum
§3{]}).} The selected \(d=3\) nearest-neighbor cubic wave-rule branch,
H-link with \(m=1\), the octahedral representation, amplitude-scale
gauge and the separate {[}SM{]} matter premises give the stated local
\(K=6\), \((3,2,1)\) and representation outputs under their own
conditions. The action-scale relation \(\hbar=c^3\epsilon^2/(4G\sigma)\)
also requires H-slope and the {[}GR{]} horizon/frame hypotheses. E1--E7
and A1--A6 \textbf{alone} do not uniquely select this branch, and full
gauge uniqueness is not established. M1-B/H-Bell are independent
Bell-composite inputs.

\textbf{Substratum observational equivalences (Theorem 24, {[}Substratum
§4{]}).} The appropriate transformations depend on the observable
carrier; four proposed families are not an established complete
gauge-group classification:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\roman{enumi})}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{Hidden relabellings.} A uniform hidden permutation preserves the
  full fixed-interface coherent-channel family; a
  visible-fibre-dependent hidden permutation preserves rooted
  populations but need not preserve coherences. An arbitrary full-state
  permutation is not gauge at a fixed readout.
\item
  \emph{Alphabet-size comparisons.} Structural results proved for all
  \(q\) in {[}SM §2.7{]} need no additional hypothesis; equality of
  transition probabilities across distinct \(q\) requires its separate
  hypothesis.
\item
  \emph{Decoupled hidden enlargement.} Independent normalized ancillary
  dynamics preserve coherent channels when the partial trace is defined,
  including trace-class infinite-dimensional cases; coupled extensions
  do not have this exact invariance.
\item
  \emph{Graph structural comparison.} Theorems 1--6 retain their
  specified dimension, mode and gravitational/quantum structural content
  under their own hypotheses; quasi-isometry does not itself imply
  equality of all channels or preserve the cubic octahedral SM
  decomposition.
\end{enumerate}

The proposed structural-realism interpretation concerns structures
actually invariant under a specified observer/readout equivalence. The
complete quotient \([(S,\varphi)]/\mathcal G_{\rm sub}\) is not yet
characterized; the 22 classified SM comparisons and horizon
normalization are conditional evidence for selected mathematical
branches, not the proof of a unique substratum.

\hypertarget{question-1-the-equivalence-class-proposal}{%
\subsubsection{1.4 Question 1: the equivalence-class
proposal}\label{question-1-the-equivalence-class-proposal}}

The two pictures describe the same observational target --- SM physics
--- from very different directions. String theory begins from quantum
gravity in \(d=10\) or \(d=11\) and seeks to reach the SM through
compactification. OI begins from the embedded-observer ontology and
reaches the SM through reconstruction. Question 1 asks whether the two
descriptions are independent, complementary, or equivalent.

The core proposal investigated in Part I is:

\textbf{Proposal (observational comparison).} A string compactification
reproducing the observed Standard Model could be compared with a
selected OI realization only after specifying common observable
structures, preparations and readouts. Agreement on gauge factors,
generations or measured constants does not establish equality of the
full channel family or a transformation between the substrata. Theorem
24 does not supply a complete four-generator classification: Pair C
refutes the restricted coherent-channel proposal and all-partitions
equivalence remains open. Assigning compactification moduli or flux
choices to OI hidden freedoms therefore requires an explicit comparison
map.

This proposal has three substantive consequences if defensible:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\Alph{enumi})}
\item
  \emph{Conditional reduction of multiplicity.} If an explicit map
  proved that a specified set of compactifications produced identical
  data for the chosen carrier, those differences would be redundant for
  that carrier. Reproducing the same low-energy Standard Model structure
  is not sufficient to establish this equivalence for all physical
  observations.
\item
  \emph{Conditional transfer of predictions.} A prediction transfers
  between models only when a comparison map preserves the data that
  define that prediction and its hypotheses. Matching some Standard
  Model outputs does not transfer every OI prediction to every
  SM-reproducing compactification, or conversely.
\item
  \emph{Computational gateway.} OI's cubic-lattice substratum admits
  direct lattice-Monte-Carlo and lattice-perturbation-theory
  computation. String compactifications are mathematically rich but
  computationally demanding. Gauge-equivalence under the proposal would
  let OI's lattice serve as a computational tool for string-theoretic
  questions, with results lifted to string-theory predictions through
  the equivalence.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Pre-conditions for the proposal.} The proposal is suggestive but
requires three pre-conditions:

\emph{Pre-condition 1: Generator (ii) extension to continuum.} OI's
generator (ii) operates on the alphabet \(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\), a
discrete structure. String theory uses continuous degrees of freedom ---
string positions on Calabi-Yau, complex-structure and Kähler moduli, RR
flux quanta. The technical question is whether OI's alphabet-size
hypothesis {[}SM §2.7{]} extends to continuum theories, or whether OI's
discrete alphabet is essential to the reconstruction. If discrete nature
is essential, the bridge between OI and string theory passes through
finite-dimensional matrix-model formulations of strings (BFSS, IKKT),
not the continuum string description directly.

\emph{Pre-condition 2: Existence of SM-reproducing compactifications.}
Despite forty years of effort, no string compactification has been
demonstrated to uniquely reproduce the SM with all parameters matching
observation {[}MSW2024{]}. The ``SM-reproducing subset'' of the
landscape is conjectured to exist but has not been explicitly exhibited.

\emph{Pre-condition 3: A1-A6 compatibility under matrix-model bridge.}
Even with matrix-model finite-dimensional formulations as
intermediaries, OI's structural conditions A1 (finiteness), A2
(deterministic bijection), A3 (bounded coupling), A5 (linearity) may be
violated by string-theoretic structure. Whether matrix models satisfy
A1-A6 is not obvious and requires technical verification.

§§3-6 of Part I address these pre-conditions; §7 considers the
consequences both if they hold and if they fail.

\hypertarget{question-2-why-universality-classes-are-substantively-new}{%
\subsubsection{1.5 Question 2: why universality classes are
substantively
new}\label{question-2-why-universality-classes-are-substantively-new}}

The negative answer to Question 1 (Part I) does not rest on a complete
substratum gauge classification. It concerns the stated model-class
differences between OI and string-theoretic constructions; a conjectured
broader universality equivalence is a different question.

Question 2 asks whether distinct microscopic descriptions produce
equivalent embedded observers for a specified observational carrier.
Definition 9.3 gives the precise criterion: a normal unital
*-isomorphism intertwining the restricted adjoint dynamics. Agreement on
a list of broad features is evidence for a comparison, while equality of
the restricted pairs is the class-membership question. These classes can
cut across microscopic model families, including families specified by
A1--A6.

Part I's findings make this question concrete. The four-feature audit in
§14 compares Born-form representation, channel dilation,
memory/divisibility and symmetry restriction across OI and
string-theoretic constructions. The memory mechanism has analogs across
frameworks, while P-indivisibility is not established cross-framework
(§14.3, §14.5). These features define useful comparison families; they
do not determine a single universality class in Definition 9.4.

Part II develops the formal framework for this question. Section 8
compares categorical, operational and algebraic formalizations; §9
adopts the algebraic one, defines observational equivalence, proves the
restricted inclusion of Proposition 9.5 and separates finite
minimal-isometry uniqueness (Propositions 9.7a--9.7b) from its separable
extension (Proposition 9.7c). Observer admission is addressed by
Definition 9.9, Proposition 9.10 and Corollary 9.11. Section 10 supplies
a finite-cutoff OI construction and a conditional EFT target; §11
formulates the comparison with string constructions; §§12--13 develop
interpretation, prediction transport and open comparison tasks.

The two questions are not independent. Question 1's negative answer
motivates Question 2's broader framework: if substrata cannot be
gauge-equivalent at the substratum level, the next question is what
equivalence does hold. Question 2's universality-class framework then
provides the language for Part III's cross-framework synthesis.

\hypertarget{the-frameworks-hierarchical-structure}{%
\subsection{2. The framework's hierarchical
structure}\label{the-frameworks-hierarchical-structure}}

The framework's structural-realism stance uses two axes of comparison.
The \emph{observation axis} records the scope and assumptions of a model
or observational carrier. The \emph{gauge axis} records the
transformations shown to preserve specified data. Calling them
orthogonal distinguishes these questions; it does not supply inclusion
maps between all the equivalences or identify a microscopic model family
with one observational class.

This section articulates the two axes, their orthogonality, and the
multi-level structural realism that follows. The articulation is
canonical for the framework --- it locates where each technical result
lives and stratifies the framework's predictions by hierarchy
intersection. Subsequent sections of this paper develop content at
specific levels (Part I at Level D × Level G3; Part II at Level C ×
Level G4; Part III at the partial-trace observational features sub-class
within Level C).

\hypertarget{the-observation-hierarchy}{%
\subsubsection{2.1 The observation
hierarchy}\label{the-observation-hierarchy}}

The observation hierarchy nests by inclusion: each deeper level is a
more specific commitment.

\textbf{Level A --- The observation axiom.} The framework begins with
the fact that \emph{observation occurs} {[}Main §1{]}. An observer
records distinguishable outcomes of interactions with a system not
wholly under the observer's control. Level A commits to: there is
something to observe; some part of reality is structured to record
outcomes (observers are embedded in reality, not external to it);
observation has structural consequences. This level is shared with
mainstream observer-essentiality programs since 2022 (CLPW, Maldacena,
HUZ, DEHK, Slagle-Preskill); the framework's distinguishing feature is
producing the consequences from a finite deterministic substratum
without extra dimensions or stochasticity.

\textbf{Level B --- Observer admission.} Not every substratum admits
observation. The framework identifies four conditions on a substratum's
bijection \(\varphi\) that are necessary for an embedded observer to
exist {[}Main §3{]}:

(C1) \textbf{Coupling.} The interaction between the observer's degrees
of freedom and the rest must be substantial.

(C2) \textbf{Record persistence.} The written record must survive to
readback --- in our universe realized by a slow bath, the hidden-sector
correlation time much longer than the observer's measurement timescale
(one sufficient route).

(C3) \textbf{Sufficient capacity.} The hidden sector must have
information capacity enough to store the interaction history.

(C4) \textbf{History readback.} The interaction must read back what was
written --- the load-bearing memory condition.

A substratum \((S, \varphi)\) is \emph{observer-admitting} if there
exists a partition \(V \subset S\) such that \((S, \varphi, V)\)
satisfies C1--C4, and \emph{observer-lacking} otherwise (formal
Definition 9.9 in §9.6). Level B commits to: the space of substrata is
structurally partitioned into observer-admitting and observer-lacking
subsets; most substrata are observer-lacking (random permutations on
finite sets typically lack the slow-mixing, sufficient-capacity,
coupled-subsystem structure required); the framework's content applies
only to observer-admitting substrata.

This has structural-anthropic content. Standard anthropic reasoning
treats parameters as contingent and explained by selection: we observe a
universe with parameters compatible with us because we couldn't observe
a universe without them. The observer-admission reading sharpens this:
observer-lacking substrata are not candidates for ``our universe'' at
all --- they are mathematical objects of a different kind, without
observer-extraction structure. Within observer-admitting substrata,
anthropic reasoning operates normally.

\textbf{Level C --- Universality classes.} For each admitted embedded
observer, choose an invariant observable algebra and the channel or
channel family to be compared. Two observers are observationally
equivalent when a normal unital *-isomorphism intertwines their
restricted Heisenberg adjoint dynamics (Definition 9.3). A universality
class is the isomorphism class of
\((\mathcal A_V,\Phi^*|_{\mathcal A_V})\) in Definition 9.4; a proper
observable algebra can retain less information than the full ambient
channel.

Broad comparison families and exact observational classes serve
different purposes (§9.5). Three useful descriptions are:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{Restriction-interface family:} systems sharing
  quantum-probability representation or dilation architecture; memory
  and symmetry restriction require their additional hypotheses.
\item
  \emph{Gauge-target family:} models reproducing specified gauge and
  matter data, without an assumed isomorphism of their full
  observational carriers.
\item
  \emph{Algebra/channel class:} systems satisfying the exact
  restricted-pair equivalence of Definition 9.3.
\end{itemize}

Level C supports an observation-relative structural realism: the chosen
restricted-pair isomorphism class is the common content. Proposition 9.5
places its two established OI channel invariances inside that class
under their stated preparation and algebra-invariance hypotheses. It
supplies no inclusion theorem for every proposed substratum
transformation or complete ordering of observational quotients.

\textbf{Level D --- OI's selected model family and branch.} OI adds
microscopic structural conditions A1--A6 {[}Substratum §3{]} and then
selects a cubic local branch with the further hypotheses stated below.
These conditions organize a model family; they do not by themselves
select one class of Definition 9.4 or a unique microscopic
representative:

\hypertarget{generator-ii-and-the-discrete-to-continuum-bridge}{%
\subsection{4. Generator (ii) and the discrete-to-continuum
bridge}\label{generator-ii-and-the-discrete-to-continuum-bridge}}

This section examines the technical bridge between OI's discrete
substratum (alphabet \(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\)) and string theory's
continuum structures (real-valued matrix entries in BFSS, complex moduli
in compactifications, continuous fields on worldsheets). The bridge
question is whether OI's alphabet-size hypothesis {[}SM §2.7{]} extends
to continuum theories, or whether OI's discrete nature is essential to
the reconstruction.

\hypertarget{the-alphabet-size-hypothesis-sm-2.7}{%
\subsubsection{4.1 The alphabet-size hypothesis {[}SM
§2.7{]}}\label{the-alphabet-size-hypothesis-sm-2.7}}

OI's substratum has local state-space \(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\) for
some integer \(q \geq 2\). That the alphabet size \(q\) is a gauge
parameter --- that no observable depends on the choice of \(q\) --- is
the alphabet-size hypothesis of {[}SM §2.7{]}, posed for linear
dynamics. {[}SM §2.7{]} lists the results proved for every \(q\) and
those that carry \(q\); no proof covers every observable.

This hypothesis bears on OI's claim that A5 (linearity) is forced rather
than assumed, conditionally: the forcing runs through
\emph{amplitude-scale} gauge (the unobservability of the absolute field
scale), which {[}Substratum §4, Theorem 24, \emph{Status}{]} classifies
as an adjoined operational input rather than a theorem ({[}SM §2.7{]})
--- its independent warrant by the observer architecture being an open,
referee-grade question. Linearity is therefore forced \emph{given} that
operational principle, not unconditionally. Non-linear dynamics over
\(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\) generically produce \(q\)-dependent
observable physics (\(x^2 \bmod 3\) and \(x^2 \bmod 5\) have different
algebraic structure), violating the gauge equivalence. The hypothesis is
stated within the discrete alphabet structure; it does not address
whether \(q \to \infty\) limit produces continuum dynamics or what role
real-valued degrees of freedom play.

\hypertarget{matrix-models-as-candidate-finite-dimensional-representatives}{%
\subsubsection{4.2 Matrix models as candidate finite-dimensional
representatives}\label{matrix-models-as-candidate-finite-dimensional-representatives}}

String-theoretic matrix models --- BFSS {[}BFSS1996{]}, IKKT
{[}IKKT1996{]} --- are finite-dimensional formulations of M-theory and
type IIB string theory respectively. BFSS at finite matrix rank \(N\)
has \(9N^2\) continuous bosonic matrix entries plus fermionic
counterparts; IKKT has a similar structure with one fewer dimension.
These are finite-dimensional in the sense of bounded matrix rank; they
are not finite-cardinality in the sense of OI's \(|S| < \infty\)
requirement.

Whether matrix models can be candidate representatives of OI's
equivalence class through the bridge requires a discrete-to-continuum
identification. Two natural candidates: (a) the matrix rank \(N\)
playing the role of OI's alphabet size \(q\), with \(N \to \infty\)
corresponding to \(q \to \infty\); (b) lattice discretization of
matrix-model dynamics, producing a finite-cardinality system that
approximates the continuum matrix-model in an appropriate limit.

Candidate (a) is structurally suggestive --- both \(q\) and \(N\)
parametrize the size of the basic representation space. The disanalogy
is that OI's alphabet-size hypothesis asserts \(q\)-independence of
observables; the analogous claim for \(N\) would be \(N\)-independence
of matrix-model observables, which is \emph{not} a feature of matrix
models. Different \(N\) values give different theories (BFSS at \(N=2\)
differs from BFSS at \(N=10^9\)), and the conjectured M-theory limit is
specifically \(N \to \infty\) at fixed coupling. The \(N\)-dependence of
matrix-model dynamics is structural, not gauge.

Candidate (b) is technically tractable through lattice-discretized BFSS
{[}BFSS-Lattice2015{]}. At fixed lattice spacing and matrix rank, the
configuration space is finite-cardinality and OI's A1 holds. The
remaining structural conditions A2 (deterministic bijection) and A5
(linearity) still fail per §6's analysis --- lattice Monte Carlo is
stochastic, and the Yang-Mills commutator-squared interaction remains
nonlinear at the lattice level.

\hypertarget{possible-bridges-and-their-status}{%
\subsubsection{4.3 Possible bridges and their
status}\label{possible-bridges-and-their-status}}

Three structural possibilities for the discrete-to-continuum bridge:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\alph{enumi})}
\item
  \emph{\(q \to \infty\) limit gives continuous matrices.} Under this
  reading, OI's substratum at very large \(q\) approximates a continuum
  theory, with continuous matrix entries arising as the limit. This is
  the most direct continuum extension. The challenge is that the
  alphabet-size hypothesis is posed for linear dynamics, which the
  continuum matrix-model violates. The bridge fails at A5.
\item
  \emph{Matrix rank \(N\) as analog of \(q\).} Under this reading, the
  matrix-model parameter \(N\) plays the same gauge role as OI's \(q\),
  with \(N\)-independence of observables required. This fails
  empirically --- matrix-model dynamics is genuinely \(N\)-dependent ---
  and the conjectured M-theory limit is at fixed \(N \to \infty\), not
  at variable \(N\).
\item
  \emph{Discrete-and-continuum genuinely incompatible.} Under this
  reading, OI's discrete structure is essential to its reconstruction
  theorem, and string theory's continuum structure is essential to its
  quantum-gravity content. The two frameworks describe physics through
  structurally distinct mathematical apparatus, with no natural bridge
  between them at the substratum level. This is the conclusion supported
  by §6's analysis.
\end{enumerate}

The provisional conclusion is (c). The substratum-level bridge does not
exist in the form the equivalence-class proposal of §1.3 required. The
bridge does exist at the partial-trace observation level (per §14), but
this is a different kind of bridge --- it relates structural features of
how observation extracts information, not the underlying substratum
descriptions. The discrete-to-continuum question at the substratum level
is orthogonal to the partial-trace-features question at the
observational level.

This connects directly to the framing of §14.5: structural features of
observation transport across frameworks (Born rule, unitarity,
non-Markovian marginal); substratum-level structure does not (discrete
vs continuum, bijection vs path-integral, linear vs nonlinear). The
discrete-to-continuum bridge is part of the substratum-level layer that
does not transport.

\hypertarget{sm-reproducing-compactifications-a-catalogue}{%
\subsection{5. SM-reproducing compactifications: a
catalogue}\label{sm-reproducing-compactifications-a-catalogue}}

Three principal classes of string compactifications produce the SM gauge
group through structurally different mechanisms. \emph{Heterotic}
(\(E_8 \times E_8\) or \(SO(32)\) on Calabi-Yau threefolds): \(E_8\)
broken by CY holonomy and bundle data; three generations from CY
topology (Euler character / 2); hypercharge from bundle structure.
Specific realizations include the Schoen Calabi-Yau (Bouchard-Donagi
2006) and heterotic line-bundle models (Anderson-Gray-Lukas-Palti 2014).
\emph{Type II / D-brane} (IIA or IIB on Calabi-Yau with D-brane
configurations): gauge fields from open strings on D-brane stacks
producing \(U(N)\) factors; chiral matter from D-brane intersections or
magnetization; SM gauge group typically as a subgroup of larger
Pati-Salam or left-right-symmetric structure. \emph{F-theory} (on
elliptically-fibered Calabi-Yau fourfolds): gauge group from singularity
structure of the discriminant locus; \(SU(5)\) GUT models from \(A_4\)
singularities with hypercharge from flux configuration breaking
\(SU(5)\) to the SM. None of the three classes uniquely determines all
SM parameters {[}MSW2024{]}.

The catalogued compactification families use different gauge
constructions---bundles and holonomy, D-brane configurations or
singularity/flux data. OI's selected branch instead uses its exact cubic
link representation, conditional condensate stabilizer and physical
reduction. These constructions can reach a common low-energy gauge
target without being equivalent as complete embedded-observer systems.
The catalogue supplies candidate comparisons rather than a common
observational class.

\hypertarget{structural-compatibility-a1a6-under-matrix-model-bridges}{%
\subsection{6. Structural compatibility: A1--A6 under matrix-model
bridges}\label{structural-compatibility-a1a6-under-matrix-model-bridges}}

The equivalence-class reading proposed in §1.3 requires that string
compactifications producing the observed Standard Model be
gauge-equivalent representatives of
\([(S, \varphi)]/\mathcal{G}_{\text{sub}}\). As discussed in §1.4
(Pre-condition 3), the natural bridge between OI's discrete substratum
and string theory's continuum description passes through
finite-dimensional matrix-model formulations --- BFSS {[}BFSS1996{]} for
type IIA / M-theory, IKKT {[}IKKT1996{]} for type IIB, and their close
variants. This section examines whether matrix-model formulations
satisfy OI's structural conditions A1--A6.

The six structural assumptions A1--A6 {[}Substratum §3.1{]} constrain
the class of candidate dynamics but do not by themselves select a unique
physical branch. Theorem 23's conditional cubic result also uses
nearest-neighbor second-order dynamics, H-link with m=1, octahedral
representation, amplitude-scale gauge and separate physical/matter
premises. This distinction is crucial when comparing OI's specific
choices with string-theoretic substrata.

We address each condition in turn, taking the BFSS matrix model as the
principal example. The conclusions extend to IKKT and to BFSS variants
with appropriate modifications.

\hypertarget{a1-finiteness}{%
\subsubsection{6.1 A1 (finiteness)}\label{a1-finiteness}}

\textbf{Condition.} The configuration space \(S\) is finite.

\textbf{BFSS status.} The BFSS matrix model at finite \(N\) has nine
bosonic matrices \(X^i\) (with \(i \in \{1, \ldots, 9\}\)) and sixteen
fermionic matrices \(\psi_\alpha\), each of size \(N \times N\) with
continuous (real-valued bosonic; Grassmann-valued fermionic) entries
{[}BFSS1996, see also {[}HigherPointBFSS2025{]}{]}. The configuration
space is \(\mathbb{R}^{9N^2}\) for the bosonic sector at fixed \(N\),
augmented by a Grassmann algebra for fermions. This is not finite.

\textbf{Possible repair: lattice discretization.} Lattice-discretized
BFSS {[}BFSS-Lattice2015{]} places the matrix model on a discrete time
lattice, and additionally discretizes the matrix entries through
standard lattice gauge theory techniques. At fixed \(N\) and fixed
lattice spacing, with appropriate UV cutoff on matrix-entry values, the
configuration space becomes finite. The lattice discretization is the
standard tool for nonperturbative numerical study of matrix models and
is well-developed in the literature.

\textbf{Status.} A1 fails for continuum BFSS. A1 holds for
lattice-discretized BFSS at fixed \(N\) and lattice spacing, with the
proviso that the continuum and large-\(N\) limits --- which are believed
to recover string-theoretic content --- produce \(|S| \to \infty\) in a
manner that may or may not preserve the equivalence-class structure.

\hypertarget{a2-determinism-bijection}{%
\subsubsection{6.2 A2 (determinism /
bijection)}\label{a2-determinism-bijection}}

\textbf{Condition.} The substratum dynamics \(\varphi: S \to S\) is a
deterministic bijection.

\textbf{BFSS status.} BFSS dynamics is quantum, defined through the path
integral over matrix configurations. At the operational level,
lattice-discretized BFSS is studied through Monte Carlo simulation,
which samples from the path-integral measure stochastically. Neither the
continuum quantum dynamics nor the lattice Monte Carlo is a
deterministic bijection on the configuration space.

The classical limit of BFSS --- at \(\hbar \to 0\), equivalent to taking
large coupling \(\lambda\) at fixed \(N\) --- gives Hamiltonian
equations of motion on continuous phase space. These are deterministic
but not bijective on a finite configuration space (since the
configuration space is continuous). One could discretize phase space and
time-step the equations of motion as a deterministic update, but this
discretization is artificial and does not respect the underlying
continuum dynamics.

\textbf{Implications for the trace-out construction.} OI's emergent-QM
derivation {[}Main §3.2{]} uses the Stinespring dilation to lift
visible-sector CPTP channels to unitaries on \(\ell^2(S)\), with the
descent restriction selecting permutation matrices and hence bijections.
If the substratum dynamics is stochastic (path-integral) rather than
deterministic, the trace-out gives a CPTP channel with two sources of
randomness --- substratum-intrinsic (from the path-integral) and
observer-ignorance (from the trace-out) --- rather than the
observer-ignorance-only structure that OI's framework requires. This
breaks the framework's identification of substratum bijection with
observable QM unitarity.

\textbf{Status.} A2 fails for matrix models in their standard quantum
formulation. The failure is not a discretization artifact but a
structural feature: matrix models are quantum theories, and their
dynamics is quantum-mechanical (path-integral) rather than
classical-deterministic. Repair would require reformulating the matrix
model as a classical cellular-automaton dynamics with bijection
structure, which is not the standard BFSS conjecture and would represent
a substantial re-foundation rather than a discretization.

\hypertarget{a3-bounded-coupling-degree}{%
\subsubsection{6.3 A3 (bounded coupling
degree)}\label{a3-bounded-coupling-degree}}

\textbf{Condition.} Each site is coupled to a bounded number of
neighbors in \(\varphi\)'s action.

\textbf{Status.} Reframing-dependent. Treating individual matrix entries
as sites, BFSS's Yang-Mills interaction
\(-\frac{1}{4}\text{Tr}[X^i, X^j]^2\) couples each entry to \(\sim N^2\)
others (unbounded in the large-\(N\) limit). Treating each matrix as a
single site (9 bosonic sites, 16 fermionic in BFSS), coupling is
bounded. The matrix-as-site reframing is in tension with OI's
combinatorial substratum (where ``site'' is a single configuration
value); without independent argument that this reframing is correct
rather than a definitional convenience, A3 should be regarded as failing
on the natural reading.

\hypertarget{a4-center-independence}{%
\subsubsection{6.4 A4 (center
independence)}\label{a4-center-independence}}

\textbf{Status.} Holds. BFSS has manifest invariance under matrix
translations \(X^i \to X^i + c^i \mathbb{1}\) (the BFSS analog of
spatial translation), and matrix indices \(a, b\) have no preferred
values; the action is symmetric under index permutations. Both features
are manifest in the BFSS action.

\hypertarget{a5-linearity}{%
\subsubsection{6.5 A5 (linearity)}\label{a5-linearity}}

\textbf{Condition.} The wave equation governing \(\varphi\) is linear in
the configuration variables.

\textbf{BFSS status.} The BFSS action contains the commutator-squared
interaction \(-\frac{1}{4}\text{Tr}[X^i, X^j]^2\), which is
\emph{quartic} in the matrix entries. The equations of motion derived
from this action are nonlinear. This is not an accident or
simplification: the nonlinear matrix interaction is what produces the
gauge structure of M-theory, the emergence of D-brane dynamics, and the
noncommutative-geometry interpretation of matrix theory.

OI's {[}SM §4.1{]} necessity argument for linearity rests on
amplitude-scale gauge (§4.1 above; {[}Substratum §4{]}), an adjoined
operational input that the alphabet-size hypothesis of {[}SM §2.7{]}
does not entail. The hypothesis adds a consistency statement: nonlinear
dynamics over \(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\) generically have
\(q\)-dependent emergent physics (\(x^2 \bmod 3\) vs \(x^2 \bmod 5\) are
algebraically distinct), violating it.

In matrix models, the analog of \(q\) is the matrix size \(N\).
Different \(N\) values give algebraically distinct theories --- the BFSS
coupling structure depends on \(N\), and the \(N \to \infty\) limit
(which is believed to recover the full M-theoretic content) is
structurally distinct from finite \(N\). Whether this \(N\)-dependence
corresponds to OI's \(q\)-dependence --- or whether \(N\) is more
analogous to OI's deep-sector cardinality (generator (iii)) --- is not
obvious.

If \(N\) is analogous to \(q\), then the \(N\)-dependence of
matrix-model dynamics directly violates the OI requirement. If \(N\) is
analogous to deep-sector cardinality, then \(N\)-independence at fixed
observable physics is required for \(N\) to be gauge under generator
(iii); this is the content of the BFSS conjecture (large-\(N\) matrix
model = M-theory) but is not rigorously established and remains a
conjecture.

\textbf{Status.} A5 fails directly: BFSS has nonlinear matrix dynamics,
in contrast to OI's linear wave equation. The deeper question ---
whether the nonlinearity is fundamental or whether some reformulation of
BFSS as a linear theory in a different basis is possible --- is open but
does not have a known answer in the affirmative direction. The most
natural reading is that matrix models genuinely have nonlinear
substratum dynamics, in structural disagreement with OI's
reconstruction.

\hypertarget{a6-background-independence}{%
\subsubsection{6.6 A6 (background
independence)}\label{a6-background-independence}}

\textbf{Status.} Holds in spirit, conditional on the matrix-spatial
bridge. BFSS has manifest \(U(N)\) gauge invariance under local matrix
transformations \(X^i \to U X^i U^{-1}\), which is the precursor of the
spacetime gauge theory in the long-distance limit. OI's A6 requires
covariance under spatially varying internal-index transformations, with
the link coupling transformed alongside, but BFSS has no spatial
structure beyond the matrix index; the BFSS analog is matrix-internal
\(U(N)\) gauge invariance. Whether matrix-internal gauge invariance
plays the role A6 requires depends on the matrix-spatial bridge,
established only in specific limits (e.g., the diagonal-matrix limit
where eigenvalues correspond to D0-brane positions).

\hypertarget{what-can-be-salvaged}{%
\subsubsection{6.8 What can be salvaged}\label{what-can-be-salvaged}}

The negative conclusion in §7.8 admits five partial recoveries, ordered
from most to least plausible.

\textbf{(i) Different bridge.} Matrix models are not the only
finite-dimensional formulation of string theory. Alternative bridges
include: (a) the AdS/CFT correspondence, where the boundary CFT lives on
a finite-dimensional spacetime that may be discretized while preserving
the bulk string content; (b) tensor-network reformulations of matrix
models {[}TensorNetworkMatrix2024{]}, which translate continuous matrix
dynamics into discrete tensor structures; (c) causal dynamical
triangulations {[}Loll2019{]}, a discretization scheme for quantum
gravity with manifestly finite configuration space and combinatorial
dynamics. Each of these admits independent investigation of A1--A6
compatibility, and the equivalence-class reading might hold for one of
them even if it fails for matrix models. This paper does not pursue
these alternatives in detail; they remain open directions.

\textbf{(ii) Emergent string-theoretic features from OI substratum.} A
reformulation of the relationship asks not whether string theory's
substratum-level structures (matrix models) are equivalent to OI's
substratum, but whether OI's substratum produces string-theoretic
features at appropriate emergent scales. OI's GR §5 recovers the
Bekenstein-Hawking \(A/4\) entropy through a thermal-self-consistency
calibration that is structurally distinct from string theory's
microscopic D-brane count {[}StromingerVafa1996{]}, the coefficient
being the ratio of the KMS and first-law normalizations that the
calibration matches; the D-brane count yields the same coefficient. If
OI's substratum also produces, at appropriate emergent scales,
holographic duality structure analogous to AdS/CFT {[}Maldacena1997{]},
graviton modes from the long-distance limit of the substratum dynamics,
and dualities analogous to T-duality and S-duality, then string theory's
distinctive technical achievements would be recovered as features of
OI's emergent physics rather than as independent substratum-level
commitments. The structural barrier identified in §6 (linearity and
determinism at the substratum level vs.\textasciitilde Yang-Mills
nonlinearity and path-integral structure) does not preclude this
recovery: effective field theories with nonlinear interactions and
stochastic descriptions are routinely derived from underlying
linear-deterministic dynamics through coarse-graining and emergent-scale
phenomena. Whether OI specifically produces the right emergent-scale
features to recover string-theoretic content is an open technical
question requiring direct investigation; this paper does not pursue the
analysis but flags it as the most consequential research direction left
open by the negative result.

\textbf{(iii) Approximate equivalence.} The equivalence-class reading
might hold \emph{approximately} --- in the sense that SM-reproducing
matrix-model compactifications agree with OI's predictions to within
experimental precision, even if the underlying structural conditions
differ. This would be an empirical equivalence rather than a structural
one and would be testable by computing OI-style predictions (Cabibbo
angle, Koide ratio) within specific compactifications and comparing to
OI's results. This is an empirical research direction not pursued here.

\textbf{(iv) Reformulation of A2/A5.} OI's A2 (determinism) and A5
(linearity) might admit reformulations that accommodate matrix-model
structure. For A2: a path-integral over deterministic bijection
trajectories produces ensemble-stochastic behavior at the coarse-grained
level; if BFSS can be reformulated as such an ensemble, A2 might hold at
the trajectory level. For A5: the commutator-squared interaction is
linear in the \emph{combination} \([X^i, X^j]\), with the nonlinearity
arising from the commutator structure rather than higher-order terms; if
``linearity'' is read at the combination level, A5 might hold. Both
readings are speculative and have not been worked out in detail. The
current state of OI's framework treats A2 and A5 in the standard sense
(bijection at the configuration level; linear in single-variable
expansion), and any reformulation must be argued explicitly.

\textbf{(v) Shared observational invariants.} A further comparison asks
which observable structures distinct microscopic constructions share.
Section 14 identifies quantum-representation/dilation architecture and
candidate memory or symmetry mechanisms. Proposition 9.5 gives a
restricted OI invariance theorem, while a complete substratum quotient
remains open. Agreement on generic features motivates a cross-framework
comparison, but equality of an observable pair must be established at a
specified carrier rather than inferred from the act of observation
itself.

\hypertarget{summary}{%
\subsubsection{6.9 Summary}\label{summary}}

Matrix-model formulations of string theory do not satisfy OI's
structural conditions A1--A6 in their standard formulation. The failures
at A2 (determinism / bijection) and A5 (linearity) are deep --- they
reflect structural commitments of string theory that diverge from OI's
substratum, not artifacts of the discretization bridge. The
equivalence-class proposal of §1.3 therefore does not hold for matrix
models as currently understood.

This negative result is the central technical finding of this paper.
Section 6 develops its implications: that OI and string theory are
independent programs with the same observational target, and that the
conventional approach to combining them --- string theory as a UV
completion of the SM, with OI as a foundational reformulation --- is
structurally distinct from the equivalence-class reading that this paper
investigated.

\hypertarget{implications-and-discussion}{%
\subsection{7. Implications and
discussion}\label{implications-and-discussion}}

The structural analysis of §6 finds that the standard matrix-model
formulations examined there fail OI's stated microscopic A1--A6
conditions, with A2 and A5 reflecting substantive differences in
description. Thus it does not provide a direct realization of those
matrix models as members of the selected OI model family. Observational
equivalence is a separate question: different microscopic structures can
still induce equivalent restricted observable pairs. No complete
\(\mathcal G_{\rm sub}\) classification or cross-framework comparison
map has been supplied to decide that question.

\hypertarget{oi-and-string-theory-as-independent-programs}{%
\subsubsection{7.1 OI and string theory as independent
programs}\label{oi-and-string-theory-as-independent-programs}}

OI and string theory remain independent research programs using
structurally distinct microscopic formulations to address overlapping
observable targets. Section 6 does not identify them as gauge-related
formulations or as stages of one derivation. It also does not rule out
equivalence of specified observable pairs; that would require the
comparison proposed in Part II.

This independence has methodological consequences. A successful
prediction of OI (e.g., the Cabibbo angle \(1/(\pi\sqrt{2})\), the Koide
ratio \(2/3\)) is not automatically a prediction of string theory;
conversely, the swampland program's identification of consistent UV
completions of the SM does not automatically constrain OI's substratum.
Each program is responsible for its own empirical match, with no
cross-attribution beyond what shared empirical inputs (E1--E4 in OI's
notation) provide.

The independence is also informative about the foundational landscape of
fundamental physics. OI and string theory are two of several active
programs (alongside loop quantum gravity, causal sets, asymptotic
safety, and others) that propose substratum descriptions for the SM. The
structural divergence between OI and string theory at A2 and A5
indicates that these are deep ontological choices, not technical
details: a substratum framework either commits to
deterministic-bijection / linear-wave-equation structure (OI's choice,
with the foundational economy this implies) or to path-integral /
gauge-Yang-Mills structure (string theory's choice, with the
gravitational-unification this provides). The choice cannot be made on
technical grounds alone; it requires foundational arguments.

The structural divergence leaves several possibilities: distinct
descriptions may be empirically distinguishable on a sufficiently rich
carrier; they may agree on a restricted carrier despite different
microscopic assumptions; or a further construction may relate them as
limits or effective descriptions. The present audit does not select
among these possibilities. Conditional prediction tests and explicit
algebra/channel comparisons can distinguish them without assuming that
microscopic difference alone settles observational equivalence.

\hypertarget{the-string-landscape-multiplicity-problem-is-not-dissolved-by-ois-gauge-group}{%
\subsubsection{7.2 The string landscape multiplicity problem is not
dissolved by OI's gauge
group}\label{the-string-landscape-multiplicity-problem-is-not-dissolved-by-ois-gauge-group}}

The proposed landscape reduction would require a map identifying the
selected compactifications' observational data. The matrix-model audit
supplies no such map, and the restricted OI invariances do not identify
the landscape by themselves. A reduction of multiplicity therefore does
not follow from the results here.

The landscape multiplicity problem remains open. The established OI
channel invariances do not identify all compactifications with matching
low-energy targets, and Theorem 24 does not provide a complete
classification even within the selected OI model family. The A1--A6
mismatch found for the standard matrix models therefore supplies neither
a landscape reduction nor a classification of their observable
equivalence classes.

This limits OI's proposed gauge classification twice: the full
all-partitions observational equivalence within the OI class remains
open, and the broader equivalence between OI-class substrata and non-OI
candidate models is a separate question. Pair C already refutes the
proposed restricted hidden-only/decoupled-generator completeness for one
fixed coherent-channel interface. Neither result identifies all
observationally equivalent substrata outside the selected cubic branch.

\hypertarget{cross-attribution-of-predictions-does-not-follow}{%
\subsubsection{7.3 Cross-attribution of predictions does not
follow}\label{cross-attribution-of-predictions-does-not-follow}}

The second consequence flagged in §1.3 --- that OI's predictions become
string-theory predictions through gauge equivalence --- likewise does
not follow. OI's prediction set (twenty-two classified quantitative SM
relations, the sharpest matching observation at sub-percent precision)
is a feature of OI's specific substratum derivation chain, which uses
linearity of the wave equation, the alphabet-size hypothesis, and
cubic-group decomposition. None of these is present in matrix models in
the form OI requires, so the prediction chain does not transport.

This means the empirical match of OI's predictions is OI-specific
evidence, neither confirmed nor refuted by string-theoretic
considerations. If OI's predictions hold up under further experimental
scrutiny (DESI Year 5 for the cosmological prediction; JUNO for
\(\sin^2\theta_{12}\); lattice tests for additional Layer-2(a)
predictions), this is evidence for OI's substratum description
specifically. If they fail, it's evidence against OI specifically.
String theory is not implicated either way through the gauge-equivalence
route examined here.

\hypertarget{computational-gateway-via-ois-lattice-does-not-extend-to-string-theory}{%
\subsubsection{7.4 Computational gateway via OI's lattice does not
extend to string
theory}\label{computational-gateway-via-ois-lattice-does-not-extend-to-string-theory}}

The third consequence --- OI's computationally-tractable lattice as a
tool for string-theoretic computations --- also does not follow. Without
gauge equivalence, OI's lattice computations produce predictions of OI's
substratum, not of string compactifications. They cannot substitute for
direct string-theoretic computation (Calabi-Yau geometry, brane
dynamics, flux integration) within a specific compactification.

This limitation does not, however, diminish OI's own computational
tractability. OI's predictions remain computable from the cubic-lattice
substratum without needing to engage string-theoretic structures. The
framework's empirical successes are independent of string-theoretic
computation, and that independence is preserved by the negative result.

\hypertarget{connection-to-swampland-conjectures}{%
\subsubsection{7.5 Connection to swampland
conjectures}\label{connection-to-swampland-conjectures}}

The swampland program {[}Vafa2005, OoguriVafa2007, Palti2019,
Lehnert2025{]} aims to identify which low-energy effective field
theories admit consistent UV completion in quantum gravity, by
formulating conjectures (Weak Gravity, de Sitter, AdS Distance,
Cobordism, etc.) that consistent EFTs must satisfy. The program's
success would be a constructive characterization of the SM-reproducing
subset of the landscape.

OI's reconstruction theorem provides an alternative characterization of
the same SM-reproducing target, but through different methodology: OI
derives the SM from the embedded-observer ontology, whereas the
swampland program identifies which UV completions are compatible with
consistent quantum gravity. The two characterizations are independent in
the sense established by §6, but they may agree on which low-energy
effective theories are admissible. This section examines OI's
compatibility with the major swampland conjectures, identifying both the
consistency results and the cross-validation pathways the conjectures
provide for OI's specific predictions.

\hypertarget{weak-gravity-conjecture}{%
\paragraph{7.5.1 Weak Gravity
Conjecture}\label{weak-gravity-conjecture}}

The Weak Gravity Conjecture {[}ArkaniHamedMotlNicolisVafa2007{]} states
that any consistent quantum-gravity EFT containing a \(U(1)\) gauge
field must contain a particle whose charge-to-mass ratio in Planck units
is \(\geq 1\) --- equivalently, gravity is the weakest force.

OI's emergent QFT contains the SM gauge structure
\(SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)\) with the standard charged-particle
spectrum. The lightest electrically charged particle is the electron,
with charge-to-mass ratio \(q/m \sim 10^{21}\) in Planck units ---
vastly exceeding the WGC threshold. The conjecture is satisfied robustly
by OI's emergent QFT for the standard \(U(1)\) gauge sector.

The neutrino sector provides a sharper test.
{[}IbañezMartinLozanoValenzuela2017{]} uses the WGC applied to a
\(U(1)_{B-L}\) gauge field to constrain neutrino-sector physics: if
neutrinos are Dirac, the WGC requires a tower of charged states with
masses below the electroweak scale, which has not been observed; if
neutrinos are Majorana, the WGC constraint is satisfied via the Majorana
mass term itself. OI's {[}SM §8.4{]} predicts Majorana neutrinos,
putting OI's prediction in the WGC-allowed region. The cross-validation
pathway is direct: WGC + observed neutrino phenomenology → Majorana
neutrinos; OI → Majorana neutrinos. Both routes converge on the same
prediction through structurally distinct reasoning.

\hypertarget{de-sitter-conjecture}{%
\paragraph{7.5.2 de Sitter Conjecture}\label{de-sitter-conjecture}}

The de Sitter Conjecture {[}ObiedOoguriSpodyneikoVafa2018{]} in its
refined form {[}OoguriPaltiShiuVafa2019{]} states that the scalar
potential \(V\) of a consistent quantum-gravity EFT satisfies
\(|\nabla V|/V \geq c \sim O(1)\) at any de Sitter critical point, OR
\(\min(\text{Hess}\,V) \leq -c'/M_P^2 V\), where \(c, c'\) are positive
\(O(1)\) constants. The conjecture forbids stable de Sitter vacua and is
in tension with the standard \(\Lambda\)CDM cosmology with a positive
cosmological constant.

OI's {[}GR §7{]} carries dark energy in Type II running-vacuum (RVM)
form rather than as a fixed cosmological constant --- the functional
form the local expansion shared by every smooth model, the content the
Type II channel, a structural reading rather than a derived law --- with
the parameter \(\nu\) encoding the running and the framework's expected
magnitude \(|\nu| \sim 10^{-32}\). Type II RVM is parametrically away
from a static de Sitter vacuum: the cosmological ``constant'' runs with
the Hubble rate, and the late-universe phase is asymptotically de Sitter
in the limit \(H \to 0\) but not strictly so at any finite time. The
dynamical character is consistent with the de Sitter Conjecture's
structural content; the small predicted \(|\nu|\) value is consistent
with the empirical near-\(\Lambda\)CDM behavior. Strict static de Sitter
would violate the conjecture; dynamical near-de Sitter satisfies it.

The recent DESI DR2 results, rejecting the cosmological constant at
\(2.8\sigma\)--\(4.2\sigma\) in extended cosmological models with
potential phantom crossing {[}DESI\_DR2\_2025{]}, provide independent
empirical pressure on static dark-energy models --- the same direction
the de Sitter Conjecture pushes. OI's position relative to this data
must be stated carefully, consistent with {[}GR §6.2, §7.1{]}: the
framework's predicted running \(|\nu| \sim 10^{-32}\) is observationally
indistinguishable from a cosmological constant, so the DESI
phantom-crossing \emph{magnitude} is in 3--4\(\sigma\) tension with OI's
prediction even though the running-vacuum \emph{form} is favoured
qualitatively. The de Sitter Conjecture consistency claim rests on the
strict-static distinction --- OI is nominally dynamical but
observationally \(\Lambda\)-like --- and should be read at that level of
strength.

\hypertarget{distance-conjecture}{%
\paragraph{7.5.3 Distance Conjecture}\label{distance-conjecture}}

The Distance Conjecture {[}OoguriVafa2007{]} states that as one moves to
large distance in the moduli space of a consistent quantum-gravity EFT,
an infinite tower of states becomes light, with masses scaling
exponentially with the distance traveled.

OI's framework has no fundamental moduli: the substratum bijection is
fixed, and the emergent QFT's parameters are derived from the
cubic-lattice structure rather than being moduli to be selected. The
Distance Conjecture is therefore satisfied trivially in a degenerate
sense --- there is no moduli space to traverse.

This is a structural difference between OI and string-theoretic
frameworks. In string theory, the landscape's structure is the moduli
space of compactifications; the Distance Conjecture provides a
non-trivial constraint on EFTs derived from that moduli space. In OI's
framework, the analog of the moduli space is the universality-class
structure of Part II --- different substrata in different universality
classes are not connected by continuous moduli but by structural-class
membership. The conjecture's content does not directly transport, but a
weakened analog might apply: substrata ``far'' from OI's class in the
universality-class space might be connected to OI's class only through
transitions involving infinite towers of states. This is speculative and
open.

\hypertarget{ads-distance-conjecture}{%
\paragraph{7.5.4 AdS Distance
Conjecture}\label{ads-distance-conjecture}}

The AdS Distance Conjecture {[}LustPaltiVafa2019{]} states that AdS
minima of consistent quantum-gravity EFTs are accompanied by an infinite
tower of states becoming light, with masses scaling with the AdS radius.
The conjecture excludes scale-separated AdS vacua in the strict sense.

OI's emergent QFT does not predict an AdS minimum: the cosmological
structure is positive-cosmological-constant (de Sitter-like), with the
small \(|\nu|\) deviation per §7.5.2. The AdS Distance Conjecture is
therefore not directly applicable to OI's framework. If a sub-cluster of
observer-admitting compactifications reproduced OI's predictions but
admitted an AdS minimum, the conjecture would constrain that sub-cluster
--- but this would be a constraint within the universality-class space,
not on OI itself.

\hypertarget{cross-validation-pathways}{%
\paragraph{7.5.5 Cross-validation
pathways}\label{cross-validation-pathways}}

The swampland conjectures collectively define a region of EFT parameter
space compatible with consistent quantum gravity. OI's emergent QFT
lives within this region across the conjectures examined (WGC, dS,
Distance, AdS Distance). This is non-trivial cross-validation: OI's
structural predictions (Majorana neutrinos, dynamical dark energy via
Type II RVM, no fundamental moduli, no AdS minimum) align with the
swampland-allowed region without being designed for it.

The cross-validation operates at the level of structural prediction, not
numerical agreement. OI predicts Majorana neutrinos because the
substratum's structural conditions force lepton-number violation; the
WGC predicts Majorana neutrinos because Dirac neutrinos require an
unobserved tower of states. Both routes converge on the same prediction
through different reasoning. Similar structural convergence operates for
dark energy: OI predicts running cosmological constant from the GR
cluster; the de Sitter Conjecture forbids static de Sitter vacua. Both
favor dynamical dark energy.

The pathway provides empirical leverage. If LEGEND-1000 {[}LEGEND2024{]}
confirms Majorana neutrinos, both OI and the WGC-derived prediction are
confirmed. If DESI Year 5 strengthens the rejection of the cosmological
constant, both OI's Type II running-vacuum channel and the de Sitter
Conjecture are favored. The empirical tests are joint tests of OI and
the swampland-favored EFT structure --- confirmation supports both;
refutation challenges both.

This section does not resolve whether OI's specific predictions can be
derived from swampland reasoning, or whether swampland-consistent EFTs
uniquely determine OI's universality class. Those are open questions.
What this section establishes is that OI's framework is consistent with
the major swampland conjectures examined, and that cross-validation
pathways exist for OI's structural predictions through swampland-derived
constraints.

\hypertarget{partial-recoveries-and-what-would-be-required-to-revisit-the-equivalence-class-reading}{%
\subsubsection{7.6 Partial recoveries and what would be required to
revisit the equivalence-class
reading}\label{partial-recoveries-and-what-would-be-required-to-revisit-the-equivalence-class-reading}}

§6.8 identified five partial recoveries, ordered roughly from most to
least consequential. \emph{Emergent string-theoretic features from OI's
substratum} (recovery (ii)) is the most consequential if it holds: OI's
GR §5 already produces the Bekenstein-Hawking \(A/4\) entropy through
thermal self-consistency {[}StromingerVafa1996 has the microscopic
derivation{]}; whether OI similarly produces holographic-duality
structure analogous to AdS/CFT, graviton modes from long-distance
dynamics, and T/S-dualities at appropriate emergent scales is an open
technical question. The structural barrier identified in §6 does not
preclude this recovery: emergent-scale physics can have nonlinear and
stochastic features arising from underlying linear-deterministic
dynamics through coarse-graining. \emph{Shared observational invariants}
(recovery (v)) is structurally distinct: it asks whether OI and string
theory agree at the observable level because both respect invariants of
the observation process, identifying invariants across structural
classes rather than within OI's class. \emph{Alternative bridges}
(AdS/CFT with discrete boundary, tensor networks, causal dynamical
triangulations) is tractable but would constitute substantial follow-up
work. \emph{Approximate-equivalence via empirical agreement} is
testable: computing OI's predictions (Cabibbo, Koide) within a specific
SM-reproducing compactification and comparing. \emph{A2/A5
reformulation} is the most speculative --- it would require substantial
reworking of either OI's conditions or string-theoretic structure.

The two most consequential --- emergent features and shared invariants
--- are not in competition. They address structurally distinct
questions: the first treats OI as a candidate foundational description
from which string-theoretic content emerges; the second treats
observation itself as the locus where different substratum descriptions
are forced into agreement. Both extend OI's structural-realism stance
--- that what is real is invariant structure, not specific
representatives --- to the comparison with other unification programs.

\hypertarget{recovery-ii-developed-emergent-string-theoretic-features-from-ois-substratum}{%
\subsubsection{7.7 Recovery (ii) developed: emergent string-theoretic
features from OI's
substratum}\label{recovery-ii-developed-emergent-string-theoretic-features-from-ois-substratum}}

Recovery (ii) of §7.6 --- that OI's substratum produces emergent
string-theoretic features at appropriate emergent scales --- is the most
consequential of the five partial recoveries if it holds. This section
develops what is currently tractable in this direction, distinguishing
structural features of string theory that plausibly emerge from OI's
substratum from features that do not, with honest scoping of which
features are open research questions.

The structural barrier identified in §6 (A2 and A5 violations in
matrix-model formulations) does not preclude this recovery. The barrier
operates at the level of substratum descriptions: OI's substratum is
deterministic-bijection-on-cubic-lattice; matrix models' substrata are
Hermitian-matrix-path-integral. These differ as substratum descriptions.
However, emergent-scale physics derived from each substratum can have
shared features, in the same way that two different microscopic theories
can share long-distance behavior through RG universality. The question
is which string-theoretic features are robust at the emergent scale and
which are tied specifically to string-theoretic substratum content.

\hypertarget{horizon-holography-vs-full-adscft}{%
\paragraph{7.7.1 Horizon holography vs full
AdS/CFT}\label{horizon-holography-vs-full-adscft}}

The strongest emergence claim is for \emph{horizon holography} --- the
Bekenstein-Hawking \(A/4\) area law and the holographic information
bound. OI's {[}GR §5{]} recovers the \(A/4\) coefficient from thermal
self-consistency at the cosmological-horizon scale, as the ratio of the
KMS and first-law normalizations that the calibration matches, with the
entropy density emerging as a partial-trace invariant of the
embedded-observer marginal. The derivation does not appeal to specific
string-theoretic content; it invokes only the framework's structural
features (the trace-out, the cosmological-horizon information capacity,
the thermal equilibrium of the hidden sector). The \(A/4\) coefficient
transports to OI from the partial-trace structure, not from
string-theoretic content.

This produces a structural feature shared with string-theoretic horizon
physics: any horizon entropy in OI's framework is bounded by \(A/4\),
and the holographic principle (information content of a region bounded
by area, not volume) holds. The shared feature is at the level of the
partial-trace observational structure, consistent with §14.2's transport
assessment for channel-level unitarity. Recent confirmation comes from
GW250114 {[}GW250114{]}, where Hawking's area theorem was confirmed at
\(99.999\%\) confidence --- a classical-horizon-dynamics result that
both OI and string theory preserve; the \(1/4\) coefficient itself (in
OI as the ratio matched by the horizon calibration, under the horizon
conditions of {[}GR §2, §8.5{]}, in string theory by microscopic state
counting {[}StromingerVafa1996{]}) has no direct empirical test.

The harder question is whether OI produces \emph{full AdS/CFT-style
holography} --- bulk-boundary correspondence with specific dictionary
between bulk fields and boundary operators. Here the answer is more
nuanced. OI's cosmological structure is positive-\(\Lambda\) (de
Sitter-like), not anti-de Sitter; the cosmological horizon is a de
Sitter horizon, not an AdS boundary. The bulk-boundary correspondence in
standard AdS/CFT relies on the asymptotic AdS structure to define
boundary operators uniquely, and this asymptotic structure is absent in
OI's framework.

Two possible directions for emergence remain open:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \emph{de Sitter holography.} Work on de Sitter holography
  {[}StromingerDS2001{]} develops bulk-boundary correspondence for de
  Sitter spacetime with a CFT defined on the asymptotic past or future
  de Sitter boundary. OI's cosmological structure is asymptotically de
  Sitter (under the framework's \(|\nu| \sim 10^{-32}\) Type II RVM), so
  de Sitter holography would be the natural framework. Whether OI's
  substratum produces a specific de Sitter holographic dictionary ---
  bulk graviton fields ↔ boundary CFT operators with specific scaling
  dimensions --- is open. The structural features (asymptotic de Sitter,
  finite horizon entropy, holographic bound) are present; the specific
  dictionary requires further analysis.
\item
  \emph{Approximate AdS in deep IR.} In some limits, de Sitter spacetime
  admits approximate AdS structure (e.g., near the horizon, in certain
  coordinate patches). Whether OI's framework produces an effective AdS
  region with corresponding effective AdS/CFT structure is similarly
  open.
\end{itemize}

The honest assessment: OI naturally produces horizon holography (the
\(A/4\) entropy law and the holographic bound) but does not naturally
produce full AdS/CFT-style correspondence as an emergent feature. A is a
structural consequence of the partial-trace observational structure; the
latter requires specific compactification-dependent content that OI's
substratum does not directly provide.

\hypertarget{graviton-modes-from-long-distance-dynamics}{%
\paragraph{7.7.2 Graviton modes from long-distance
dynamics}\label{graviton-modes-from-long-distance-dynamics}}

OI's {[}GR §3--§5{]} derives linearized Einstein gravity from the
partial-trace structure of the substratum bijection at the
cosmological-horizon scale. The mechanism: the substratum's coherent
excitations include long-wavelength fluctuations of the visible-sector
metric, which are the emergent graviton modes. The framework reproduces
the standard GR structure at the macroscopic level with Type II RVM
corrections, including the linearized-graviton sector with the right
propagator structure (massless spin-2 modes, transverse-traceless
polarization, lightlike propagation).

This emergence is structurally analogous to the string-theoretic
graviton emergence: in both frameworks, gravitons arise as collective
modes of the substratum dynamics. In string theory, the graviton is a
closed-string vibrational mode with worldsheet \(\sigma\)-model
structure. In OI, the graviton is an emergent collective mode of the
substratum's coherent excitations on the cubic lattice, derived through
the partial-trace-and-coarse-grain procedure.

The \emph{structural similarity} is at the level of the IR effective
theory: both frameworks produce massless spin-2 modes coupled to the
stress-energy tensor in the linearized limit, satisfying the equivalence
principle and gauge-invariance under linearized diffeomorphisms. This is
what physicists mean by ``the emergent low-energy theory contains
gravitons.''

The \emph{structural difference} is at the UV completion. In string
theory, the graviton is a fundamental string mode with worldsheet
origin; in OI, the graviton is an emergent collective mode with no
string-mode interpretation. The differences become important at high
energies --- string-scale corrections to graviton propagators in string
theory vs Planck-scale corrections from substratum-level lattice
structure in OI; the existence of an infinite Regge tower of massive
string modes vs OI's finite-but-large substratum modes. These UV
differences are testable in principle but typically inaccessible at
current and near-future energy scales.

The recovery (ii) claim is supported for graviton-mode emergence: OI
produces emergent graviton modes with the structural features that
string-theoretic graviton modes share --- namely the IR effective-theory
content. The framework does not produce the specifically
string-theoretic UV completion (worldsheet structure, infinite massive
tower) but produces the universally-required IR structure that any
successful emergent-graviton framework must reproduce.

\hypertarget{t-duality-and-s-duality-analogs}{%
\paragraph{7.7.3 T-duality and S-duality
analogs}\label{t-duality-and-s-duality-analogs}}

T-duality in string theory is the equivalence of closed-string theories
on a circle of radius \(R\) and radius \(\alpha'/R\), with momentum
modes ↔ winding modes. The duality requires a string-mode structure with
both momentum and winding quantum numbers, neither of which has a direct
analog in OI's substratum.

OI's substratum has lattice-momentum modes (excitations with definite
lattice wavevector), but no winding modes (no extended objects winding
around compact dimensions). The cubic lattice is the physical spatial
structure, not a compactification. T-duality therefore has no clean
analog in OI's framework: the duality-pair structure (momentum ↔
winding) is absent because winding modes are absent.

A weakened analog might exist at the universality-class level. If two
SM-reproducing universality classes have algebra-channel pairs related
by a specific symmetry --- say, an automorphism that exchanges
high-momentum and low-momentum content --- this would be a
T-duality-like feature at the universality-class level rather than the
substratum level. Whether such a structure exists in OI's
universality-class framework is open. The honest assessment: T-duality
at the substratum level has no analog; T-duality-like features at the
universality-class level are speculative.

S-duality in string theory is the equivalence of strong-coupling and
weak-coupling descriptions in certain string theories (most cleanly in
type IIB). The duality maps the string coupling \(g_s \to 1/g_s\),
exchanging fundamental strings with D1-branes (and other extended
objects) under the duality.

OI's framework has emergent gauge couplings derived from the substratum
structure. Whether there is a structural S-duality-like feature ---
e.g., a duality between a strong-coupling regime where the
visible-sector EFT description breaks down and a weak-coupling regime in
a different visible-sector EFT description --- is an open question. The
substratum-level dynamics is fixed (the bijection on the cubic lattice),
so there is no direct analog of varying the string coupling. The
visible-sector EFT couplings can be varied continuously, but whether the
strong-coupling limit maps onto a different weak-coupling EFT
description is not established.

The honest assessment: S-duality analogs in OI are speculative. Some
structural features of S-duality (the existence of dual descriptions of
the same observable physics through different regime variables) might
transport to OI's universality-class framework, but no specific
S-duality-like map has been identified.

\hypertarget{observer-admission-and-the-structure-of-the-landscape}{%
\subsubsection{7.9 Observer admission and the structure of the
landscape}\label{observer-admission-and-the-structure-of-the-landscape}}

Part II §9.6 formalizes a structural distinction between
\emph{observer-admitting} substrata --- those that admit a partition
satisfying C1--C4 --- and \emph{observer-lacking} substrata, where no
such partition exists. The distinction operates at the level of the
substratum's bijection structure, prior to any observer being
instantiated. This section examines how the distinction reframes the
relationship between OI and the string landscape.

\textbf{The landscape, viewed through observer admission.} The string
landscape comprises approximately \(10^{500}\) distinct
compactifications. The standard reading {[}Vafa2005, Susskind2003{]}
treats the landscape as a multiplicity of candidates for ``the
universe,'' with anthropic selection narrowing the candidate set to
those compatible with our observations. Under the observer-admission
reading, the landscape's multiplicity decomposes structurally:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \emph{Observer-lacking compactifications.} Compactifications producing
  four-dimensional vacua without chiral matter, with rapid hidden-sector
  mixing, with insufficient horizon capacity, or with broken
  cosmological structure cannot satisfy C1--C4. They correspond to
  observer-lacking substrata. These compactifications are mathematical
  objects in string theory's space of vacua, but they are not candidate
  physical universes in OI's framework --- observers cannot be embedded
  in them.
\item
  \emph{Observer-admitting compactifications.} Compactifications
  producing four-dimensional vacua with chiral matter, stable
  cosmological structure (de Sitter or near-de Sitter with horizon
  timescales \(\tau_B \sim H^{-1}\)), and sufficient information
  capacity (the cosmological horizon's entropy \(\sim 10^{122}\))
  satisfy C1--C4 and are observer-admitting. The SM-reproducing
  compactifications surveyed in §5 --- heterotic on Calabi-Yau with
  appropriate bundles, Type II / D-brane Pati-Salam constructions,
  F-theory \(SU(5)\) with hypercharge flux --- fall in this class.
\end{itemize}

The observer-admission criterion distinguishes the two classes
structurally. Most compactifications in the landscape are likely
observer-lacking; the SM-reproducing subset is a small fraction. The
numerical multiplicity of the landscape (\(10^{500}\)) is largely a
measure of observer-lacking substrata, not of competing candidates for
our universe.

\textbf{The translation of C1--C4 to string-theoretic structure.} The
structural conditions for observer admission {[}Main §3{]} are
formulated for finite deterministic substrata. Translating them to
string-theoretic compactifications requires identifying analogs in
string-theoretic structure:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \emph{C1 (coupling)} requires substantial coupling between
  visible-sector and hidden-sector dynamics. In string-theoretic terms:
  the four-dimensional EFT must have non-trivial interaction between
  observable matter (chiral fermions, gauge fields, Higgs sector) and
  the gravitational / Kaluza-Klein / hidden gauge sectors.
  Compactifications with completely decoupled hidden sectors fail C1.
\item
  \emph{C2 (memory persistence)} requires the written record to persist
  to readback, the slow bath one realization: the hidden-sector
  correlation time much longer than the observer's measurement
  timescale. In string-theoretic terms: the cosmological structure must
  have a stable phase with horizon timescales \(\tau_B \sim H^{-1}\)
  much longer than laboratory timescales \(\tau_S\). AdS
  compactifications without de Sitter structure, or compactifications
  with rapidly mixing cosmological structure, fail C2.
\item
  \emph{C3 (sufficient capacity)} requires the hidden sector to have
  sufficient information capacity to store the interaction history. In
  string-theoretic terms: the cosmological horizon's information
  capacity (set by the holographic bound at \(\sim 10^{122}\) for our
  observed universe) must be sufficient. Compactifications with horizons
  of insufficient capacity fail C3.
\end{itemize}

These translations are intuitively clear: a compactification reproducing
our universe's cosmological structure satisfies all three. A
compactification producing AdS, anti-de Sitter, or rapidly-mixing
dS-with-very-short-horizon-time fails one or more. The translation is
plausible structurally, though a rigorous version would require detailed
analysis specific to compactification classes.

\textbf{The OI-string relationship reframed.} Under the
observer-admission reading, the relationship between OI and string
theory is structurally distinct from the standard ``two competing
programs'' framing. OI operates entirely within observer-admitting
substrata --- the framework's foundational axiom ``observation occurs''
is taken to be structurally specific to observer-admitting cases (per
§9.6). String theory's landscape encompasses both observer-admitting and
observer-lacking substrata, with the observer-admitting subset being
where SM-reproducing physics resides.

The relationship therefore takes this form:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  OI's universality class lives in the observer-admitting subset.
\item
  Some SM-reproducing string compactifications also live in the
  observer-admitting subset.
\item
  The intersection between OI's universality class and the
  observer-admitting compactification subset is what the
  partial-trace-features analysis of §14 characterizes.
\item
  Compactifications outside the observer-admitting subset are not
  candidates for ``our universe'' in OI's framework --- they are
  observer-lacking substrata, mathematical objects but not physical
  universes.
\end{itemize}

This reframing has substantive consequences for the landscape problem.
The standard landscape problem treats \(10^{500}\) vacua as competitors;
selecting ``the right one'' appears to require either anthropic
reasoning or unprincipled choice. The observer-admission reading
dissolves this: most of the landscape is observer-lacking and not in
competition for ``our universe.'' The candidate space is the
observer-admitting subset, which is much smaller. Within the candidate
space, our universe is in some specific universality class,
distinguished from other observer-admitting universality classes by
specific predictions.

\textbf{Connection to anthropic reasoning.} The standard anthropic
argument explains the landscape's empirical match to our universe
through selection: we observe a universe compatible with observers
because we couldn't observe a universe incompatible with observers. The
observer-admission reading sharpens this. Anthropic selection separates
into two distinct kinds of work:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \emph{Structural exclusion of observer-lacking substrata.} Substrata
  that lack the structural features for observer-admission are not in
  the candidate space at all. There is no anthropic selection within
  them --- they are not competitors. This is structural exclusion, not
  selection.
\item
  \emph{Within-candidate-space selection of universality class.} Among
  observer-admitting substrata, our universe is in some specific
  universality class. Standard anthropic reasoning applies here: we are
  in the universality class that produces our observations, and the
  framework's predictions distinguish OI's class from other
  observer-admitting classes within the candidate space.
\end{itemize}

Observer admission asks which candidate models can support the required
records. Selecting among those candidates by observed couplings, matter
data or dynamics is a further empirical question. The four-feature audit
identifies broad comparison mechanisms, while a particular observational
class still requires the data and map of Definition 9.3. This
distinction preserves the observer-admission proposal without treating a
feature list as a completed classification of the landscape.

\textbf{Implications for the relationship.} The ``two competing
programs'' framing of OI vs.~string theory is not the right
characterization under the observer-admission reading. The structurally
accurate framing is:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{String theory} supplies a broad collection of candidate
  microscopic and effective constructions whose observer-level content
  must be specified for comparison.
\item
  \emph{OI} studies observer-admitting models subject to A1--A6 and the
  additional selected-branch assumptions.
\item
  \emph{A shared observational class} requires particular constructions
  and the map of Definition 9.3; the four-feature audit alone does not
  establish the intersection.
\item
  \emph{Other comparisons} may reveal different observable data or
  shared coarser targets, depending on the chosen carrier.
\end{itemize}

This reframing separates observer admission, microscopic model choice
and observational equivalence. The selected OI branch retains its
conditional predictions, which can distinguish it from other
observer-admitting models; deciding that the models share one
restricted-pair class requires the explicit comparison.

\textbf{Open structural questions.} The observer-admission framing of
the landscape is a structural reading, not a rigorous result. Several
questions remain open:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \emph{Rigorous translation of C1--C4 to string-theoretic structure.}
  The translations above are plausible but not formal. Working out a
  rigorous version for specific compactification classes (heterotic,
  Type II, F-theory) would require detailed analysis specific to each
  class.
\item
  \emph{Numerical estimate of the observer-admitting subset.} The
  standard \(10^{500}\) figure is for the full landscape including
  observer-lacking compactifications. Estimating the observer-admitting
  subset's size --- even to within several orders of magnitude --- would
  substantially clarify the landscape problem's empirical content.
\item
  \emph{Observer-admitting compactifications outside OI's universality
  class.} If observer-admitting compactifications exist that are not in
  OI's universality class, they would be alternative candidates for
  ``the universe'' --- distinct from OI in their predictions, with their
  own internal structure. Identifying such compactifications would be a
  substantial result.
\item
  \emph{Connection to swampland criteria.} The swampland program
  identifies criteria for compactifications consistent with quantum
  gravity (Weak Gravity Conjecture, de Sitter Conjecture, Distance
  Conjecture). The observer-admission criterion is structurally distinct
  but may overlap with swampland criteria --- both restrict the
  candidate compactification space, possibly with different overlap
  regions. Working out the relationship would clarify both frameworks.
\end{itemize}

These questions are not addressed in this paper; they are flagged as
research directions opened by the observer-admission reframing. The
reframing itself --- reading the landscape's multiplicity as containing
predominantly observer-lacking substrata, with the observer-admitting
subset being structurally smaller --- is a substantive contribution that
the framework's structural-realism stance makes available to the
landscape discussion.

\section{Part II: Universality Classes of Embedded Observers (Question

\hypertarget{formalizing-embedded-observation}{%
\subsection{8. Formalizing embedded
observation}\label{formalizing-embedded-observation}}

The notion of ``embedded observer'' in OI's framework is informal: an
observer with finite distinguishable states, embedded in a substratum,
with non-trivial coupling to a hidden sector that has slow dynamics
relative to observation, and high information capacity in the hidden
sector. For the universality-class question to admit precise
formulation, we need a formalization sufficient to define equivalence
relations on substratum-with-observer systems. Three candidate
formalizations are available.

\hypertarget{categorical-formalization}{%
\subsubsection{8.1 Categorical
formalization}\label{categorical-formalization}}

The categorical approach treats embedded observers as functors
\(F: \mathbf{Sub} \to \mathbf{Obs}\) from a category of substratum
descriptions to a category of observable descriptions, with the
universality-class equivalence given by natural equivalence of functors
(substrata producing isomorphic observable descriptions, possibly
through different observation functors). The formalization is
well-defined but requires substantial machinery (explicit construction
of \(\mathbf{Sub}\) and \(\mathbf{Obs}\) with their respective morphism
structures) that has not been worked out in the OI literature. We do not
pursue it further here.

\hypertarget{operational-formalization}{%
\subsubsection{8.2 Operational
formalization}\label{operational-formalization}}

The operational approach treats embedded observers as operational
protocols \((\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{M}, p)\) --- preparations,
measurements, and outcome probabilities --- without committing to a
specific underlying substratum, in the spirit of Mackey's quantum logic
{[}Mackey1963{]} and the generalized probabilistic theories program
{[}Hardy2001, Barrett2007{]}. The universality class is the equivalence
class under operational indistinguishability. This formalization is
closest to empiricist intuition and methodologically clear, but it is
empiricist --- it does not commit to substratum-level realism --- and
may sit uneasily with OI's structural-realism stance, which treats the
equivalence class itself as ontologically real.

\hypertarget{algebraic-formalization}{%
\subsubsection{8.3 Algebraic
formalization}\label{algebraic-formalization}}

The algebraic approach assigns an embedded observer an invariant algebra
of observables and a channel on states. In the Schrödinger picture the
channel is
\(\Phi:\mathcal T_1(\mathcal H_V)\to\mathcal T_1(\mathcal H_V)\), where
\(\mathcal T_1\) denotes trace-class operators. Its adjoint
\(\Phi^*:\mathcal B(\mathcal H_V)\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H_V)\) is
normal, unital and completely positive. The data comprise: - An
observable von Neumann algebra
\(\mathcal A_V\subseteq\mathcal B(\mathcal H_V)\) satisfying
\(\Phi^*(\mathcal A_V)\subseteq\mathcal A_V\); - The restricted
Heisenberg dynamics \(\Phi^*|_{\mathcal A_V}\); - When supplied, a
unitary realization: a hidden space \(\mathcal H_H\), a reference state
\(\rho_H\), and a unitary \(U\) with
\(\Phi(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}_H U(\rho\otimes\rho_H)U^\dagger\)
(Definition 9.2). For pure \(\rho_H\), the associated isometry is the
Stinespring dilation.

The realization contains microscopic data beyond this observable
carrier. The carrier is the pair
\((\mathcal A_V,\Phi^*|_{\mathcal A_V})\). Taking
\(\mathcal A_V=\mathcal B(\mathcal H_V)\) always satisfies invariance; a
proper subalgebra requires a separate invariance check. Equality of the
full ambient channel is stronger than equivalence of these restricted
pairs.

The universality-class proposal compares these pairs by a normal unital
*-isomorphism intertwining the restricted adjoint channels. For a
specified channel family \((\Phi_t)\), the chosen algebra must be
invariant under every \(\Phi_t^*\) and the same isomorphism must
intertwine every \(t\). Neither a single channel nor its separate-time
family alone specifies all joint intervention-word probabilities.

\textbf{Status.} The algebra/channel formalization specifies an
observable carrier using standard operator-algebra and channel
machinery. A chosen OI realization supplies such data through its
observation map. Theorem 23 does not recover a unique realization from
the pair, and Theorem 24 does not classify all transformations
preserving it. Extending the comparison to a string construction
requires its corresponding algebra/channel pair and a map between the
chosen observable structures.

\hypertarget{choosing-a-formalization}{%
\subsubsection{8.4 Choosing a
formalization}\label{choosing-a-formalization}}

The three formalizations are related but not equivalent. The operational
formalization is weakest --- it characterizes observers by their data
without committing to underlying structure. The algebraic formalization
adds structure (algebra of observables, channel dynamics) that
operational data alone does not specify. The categorical formalization
is heaviest --- it requires explicit category-theoretic machinery that
the others do not.

For the purposes of this paper, we adopt the \textbf{algebraic
formalization} as the principal framework. It is well-developed in the
operator-algebra and quantum-information literatures; it captures OI's
framework and string-theoretic emergence directly; and it provides
sufficient structure to define universality classes precisely without
requiring the heavy categorical machinery.

We will return to operational and categorical perspectives where they
illuminate features the algebraic formalization does not capture, but
the principal definitions and arguments use the algebra-channel
structure of §8.3.

\hypertarget{universality-classes-of-embedded-observers}{%
\subsection{9. Universality classes of embedded

\hypertarget{formal-definitions}{%
\subsubsection{9.1 Formal definitions}\label{formal-definitions}}

We adopt the algebraic formalization of §8.4. The following definitions
make precise the universality-class structure used throughout the rest
of the paper.

\textbf{Definition 9.1} (Embedded observer). \emph{An embedded observer
is a triple \((\mathcal H_V,\mathcal A_V,\Phi)\) where:} -
\emph{\(\mathcal H_V\ne\{0\}\) is a separable Hilbert space;} -
\emph{\(\mathcal A_V\subseteq\mathcal B(\mathcal H_V)\) is a unital von
Neumann algebra;} -
\emph{\(\Phi:\mathcal T_1(\mathcal H_V)\to\mathcal T_1(\mathcal H_V)\)
is a Schrödinger CPTP channel whose normal unital CP adjoint satisfies
\(\Phi^*(\mathcal A_V)\subseteq\mathcal A_V\).}

For a specified family of channels, replace the last condition by
invariance under every adjoint in the family. This does not assume that
the family is a semigroup.

\textbf{Definition 9.2} (Substratum-with-observer system). \emph{A
substratum-with-observer system is a quintuple
\((\mathcal{H}_V, \mathcal{A}_V, \Phi, \mathcal{H}_H, U)\) where
\((\mathcal{H}_V, \mathcal{A}_V, \Phi)\) is an embedded observer,
\(\mathcal{H}_H\) is a separable Hilbert space (the hidden-sector state
space), and \(U\) is a unitary on
\(\mathcal{H}_V \otimes \mathcal{H}_H\) such that}
\[\Phi(\rho) = \mathrm{Tr}_H[U(\rho \otimes \rho_H) U^\dagger]\]
\emph{for some hidden-sector reference state \(\rho_H\). The triple
\((\mathcal{H}_H, \rho_H, U)\) is a} unitary realization \emph{of
\(\Phi\). When \(\rho_H = |0\rangle\langle 0|\) is pure, the map
\(V_{U,0}: \psi \mapsto U(\psi \otimes |0\rangle)\) is an isometry
\(\mathcal{H}_V \to \mathcal{H}_V \otimes \mathcal{H}_H\) satisfying
\(\Phi(\rho) = \mathrm{Tr}_H[V_{U,0}\, \rho\, V_{U,0}^\dagger]\), and it
is this isometry --- not \(U\) --- that is a Stinespring dilation of
\(\Phi\).}

\textbf{Remark (why the distinction is load-bearing).} A unitary
realization carries strictly more data than the channel constrains, in
two separate ways, and both matter below. First, when \(\rho_H\) is pure
the channel sees \(U\) only through its action on
\(\mathcal{H}_V \otimes \mathbb{C}|0\rangle\); the action of \(U\) on
the rest of \(\mathcal{H}_V \otimes \mathcal{H}_H\) is entirely free.
Second, when \(\rho_H\) is mixed the realization remains a realization
rather than a Stinespring dilation, and its hidden-sector dimension is
not the Kraus rank --- it can be strictly \emph{smaller}. The completely
depolarizing qubit channel has Kraus rank \(4\), yet
\(\rho_H = \mathbb{1}/2\) with \(U = \mathrm{SWAP}\) realizes it on a
two-dimensional hidden sector. This is not an edge case for OI: §10.4's
construction uses the uniform mixed reference state, so OI's own
\((\mathcal{H}_H, \rho_H, U_\varphi)\) is a unitary realization and not
a minimal Stinespring dilation, and the uniqueness statements below must
not be read as applying to it directly.

\textbf{Definition 9.3} (Observational equivalence). \emph{Two embedded
observers \((\mathcal{H}_V^{(1)}, \mathcal{A}_V^{(1)}, \Phi^{(1)})\) and
\((\mathcal{H}_V^{(2)}, \mathcal{A}_V^{(2)}, \Phi^{(2)})\) are
observationally equivalent, written
\((\mathcal{H}_V^{(1)}, \mathcal{A}_V^{(1)}, \Phi^{(1)}) \sim_{\mathrm{obs}} (\mathcal{H}_V^{(2)}, \mathcal{A}_V^{(2)}, \Phi^{(2)})\),
if there exists a normal unital }-isomorphism
\(\theta: \mathcal{A}_V^{(1)} \to \mathcal{A}_V^{(2)}\) such that*
\[\theta \circ \Phi^{(1)*}|_{\mathcal A_V^{(1)}} = \Phi^{(2)*}|_{\mathcal A_V^{(2)}} \circ \theta.\]

\textbf{Definition 9.4} (Universality class). \emph{A universality class
is an equivalence class under Definition 9.3, represented by the
isomorphism class of \((\mathcal A_V,\Phi^*|_{\mathcal A_V})\). Two
substratum-with-observer systems are in the same class when these
restricted pairs are equivalent, irrespective of their unitary
realizations. For proper \(\mathcal A_V\), this class need not determine
the full ambient channel or its Hilbert-space realization.}

The definitions are deliberately framework-neutral. They invoke standard
machinery from operator-algebra theory (von Neumann algebras, CPTP
channels, *-isomorphisms) without requiring framework-specific
structural commitments. This is essential for the universality-class
framework's purpose: characterizing equivalence at the level of
observation, independent of the specific substratum machinery producing
observable physics.

\hypertarget{relation-to-ois-gauge-group-a-proposition}{%
\subsubsection{9.2 Relation to OI's gauge group: a
proposition}\label{relation-to-ois-gauge-group-a-proposition}}

The established fixed-interface channel invariances give the following
restricted observational equivalence. Population-only preservation and
structural alphabet/graph comparisons do not alone imply an
algebra/channel isomorphism.

\textbf{Proposition 9.5} (Established channel invariances induce
observational equivalence). \emph{Fix a finite nonzero visible Hilbert
space \(\mathcal H_V\), a chosen observable algebra \(\mathcal A_V\)
invariant under every adjoint in the channel family, and the family
induced by a finite permutation realization with uniform hidden
preparation. Let a second realization be obtained by a hidden-factor
permutation applied identically in every visible fibre, or by an
independently decoupled normalized finite ancillary enlargement as in
{[}Substratum Theorem 24(i),(iii){]}. The resulting embedded observers
are observationally equivalent in Definition 9.3, with the identity on
\(\mathcal A_V\) intertwining every restricted adjoint channel.}

\emph{Proof.} Theorem 24(i),(iii) gives equality of the full visible
channels at every time for the stated transformations and preparations.
Their adjoints are therefore equal on \(\mathcal B(\mathcal H_V)\). The
assumed invariant algebra is fixed, so its identity isomorphism
intertwines their restrictions. Population equalities alone are not used
to infer this isomorphism. \(\square\)

The converse claim that every observationally equivalent OI-class
substratum is related by the four proposed transformations is
\textbf{not established}. On the fixed-interface coherent-channel
reading it is refuted for the restricted moves by the six-state Pair C
certificate {[}Substratum §4; ST3/ST4{]}; on the population-only reading
the larger fibre-dependent hidden relabellings preserve rooted counts,
while the all-partitions reading remains open. Stinespring uniqueness
for one channel does not provide semigroup-wide uniqueness of the
generating dynamics.

\textbf{Corollary 9.6} (Universality class contains the established
channel-preserving realizations). \emph{The universality class of a
chosen embedded-observer triple contains every realization obtained by a
finite sequence of the moves in Proposition 9.5, with the visible
algebra and preparation/readout carrier held fixed and every stage
satisfying the relevant preparation and decoupling hypotheses. It may
also contain other realizations. No completeness of these moves or
classification across all observer partitions is asserted.}

Each move preserves the chosen restricted pair by Proposition 9.5, so a
finite sequence does as well. This inclusion does not classify the full
substratum observational gauge structure. Alphabet/graph comparisons
require their own hypotheses, and fibre-dependent population
preservation need not preserve coherent channels.

\hypertarget{many-to-one-structure-of-dilations}{%
\subsubsection{9.3 Many-to-one structure of
dilations}\label{many-to-one-structure-of-dilations}}

Many unitary realizations can produce the same full ambient channel. The
following uniqueness results assume equality of that full channel on
\(\mathcal T_1(\mathcal H_V)\), equivalently equality of its adjoint on
all \(\mathcal B(\mathcal H_V)\). Equivalence on a proper invariant
observable algebra is weaker and does not supply this hypothesis. We
separate the finite-dimensional kernel result from the general
separable-space theorem and distinguish Stinespring isometries from full
unitaries.

\textbf{Proposition 9.7a} (Minimal Kraus uniqueness). \emph{Let
\(\mathcal{H}_V\) be finite-dimensional and let}
\[\Phi(\rho) = \sum_{i=1}^{r} A_i \rho A_i^\dagger = \sum_{j=1}^{s} B_j \rho B_j^\dagger\]
\emph{with \(\{A_i\}_{i \le r}\) and \(\{B_j\}_{j \le s}\) each linearly
independent. Then \(r = s\) and there is a unique unitary
\(W \in \mathrm{U}(r)\) with} \[B_j = \sum_i W_{ji} A_i .\]

\emph{Proof.} Vectorize: the map
\(\rho \mapsto \sum_i A_i \rho A_i^\dagger\) determines, and is
determined by, the positive semidefinite operator
\(M = \sum_i |a_i\rangle\langle a_i|\) where
\(|a_i\rangle = \mathrm{vec}(A_i)\). Linear independence of the family
says the \(|a_i\rangle\) are linearly independent, so
\(r = \mathrm{rank}\,M = s\) and both families are bases of
\(\mathrm{ran}\,M\). Writing \(X\) and \(Y\) for the matrices with those
columns, \(XX^\dagger = M = YY^\dagger\) with \(X, Y\) of full column
rank forces \(Y = X W^{\mathsf T}\) for a unique unitary \(W\).
\(\square\)

Linear independence is exactly minimality, and it is not removable:
without it the family size is not even determined. For any
\(3 \times 2\) isometry \(M\) the family \(C_k = \sum_i M_{ki} A_i\)
realizes the same channel with three members rather than two, and no
unitary relates families of different sizes.

\textbf{Proposition 9.7b} (Finite minimal pure-reference dilation
uniqueness). \emph{Let the visible and both hidden Hilbert spaces be
finite-dimensional, with \(\mathcal H_V\ne\{0\}\). Let
\((\mathcal{H}_H^{(1)}, |0_1\rangle\langle 0_1|, U^{(1)})\) and
\((\mathcal{H}_H^{(2)}, |0_2\rangle\langle 0_2|, U^{(2)})\) be
pure-reference unitary realizations of the same channel \(\Phi\), and
let \(V_a \psi = U^{(a)}(\psi \otimes |0_a\rangle)\) be the associated
Stinespring isometries. If both are minimal ---
\(\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{(A \otimes \mathbb{1})V_a \psi : A \in \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_V),\ \psi \in \mathcal{H}_V\} = \mathcal{H}_V \otimes \mathcal{H}_H^{(a)}\)
--- then \(\dim \mathcal{H}_H^{(1)} = \dim \mathcal{H}_H^{(2)}\) and
there is a unique unitary
\(W: \mathcal{H}_H^{(1)} \to \mathcal{H}_H^{(2)}\) with}
\[V_2 = (\mathbb{1}_V \otimes W)\, V_1, \qquad \text{equivalently} \qquad U^{(2)}(\psi \otimes |0_2\rangle) = (\mathbb{1}_V \otimes W)\, U^{(1)}(\psi \otimes |0_1\rangle) \ \ \forall \psi .\]
\emph{No equality of the full unitaries away from the reference
subspaces follows.}

\emph{Proof.} In finite dimension with nonzero visible space, cyclic
minimality is equivalent to linear independence of the Kraus family
\(A_i=(\mathbb 1_V\otimes\langle i|)V_a\). Proposition 9.7a therefore
gives equal hidden dimensions and the unique unitary with the stated
orientation. This is the scope of the finite-dimensional Lean theorem
\texttt{StinespringUniqueness.proposition\_9\_7b}. \(\square\)

\textbf{Proposition 9.7c} (Separable minimal-isometry uniqueness).
\emph{Let \(\mathcal H_V\ne\{0\}\) and \(\mathcal K_1,\mathcal K_2\) be
separable Hilbert spaces. Suppose
\(V_a:\mathcal H_V\to\mathcal H_V\otimes\mathcal K_a\) are cyclically
minimal Stinespring isometries for the same full ambient channel. Then
there is a unique unitary \(W:\mathcal K_1\to\mathcal K_2\) such that
\(V_2=(\mathbb 1_V\otimes W)V_1\). Consequently the conclusions of
Proposition 9.7b about the prepared isometries hold without
finite-dimensionality.}

\emph{Proof.} On the dense cyclic span define
\[T\!\left(\sum_j(A_j\otimes\mathbb 1)V_1\psi_j\right)=\sum_j(A_j\otimes\mathbb 1)V_2\psi_j.\]
The squared norm on either side is
\(\sum_{i,j}\langle\psi_i,\Phi^*(A_i^\dagger A_j)\psi_j\rangle\).
Equality of the complete adjoint channels makes \(T\) well-defined and
isometric. Minimality on both sides extends it to a surjective isometry,
hence a unitary. Its definition gives
\(T(A\otimes\mathbb 1)=(A\otimes\mathbb 1)T\) for every
\(A\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H_V)\). Fix a unit vector
\(\xi\in\mathcal H_V\) and set
\(W\eta=(\langle\xi|\otimes\mathbb 1)T(\xi\otimes\eta)\). Intertwining
the rank-one operators \(|\psi\rangle\langle\xi|\) gives
\(T(\psi\otimes\eta)=\psi\otimes W\eta\). Unitarity of \(T\) implies
unitarity of \(W\). Any other such \(W\) agrees on the dense cyclic
span, so it is equal to this one. This is the standard minimal
Stinespring uniqueness argument {[}Stinespring1955{]}; the general
separable extension is a written proof here, beyond the
finite-dimensional kernel result. \(\square\)

The full-unitary conclusion is limited to one prepared channel. With
\(\mathcal H_V=\mathcal H_H=\mathbb C^2\), \(U^{(1)}=\mathrm{CNOT}\) and
\(U^{(2)}=\mathrm{CNOT}(\mathbb 1\otimes Z)\), both with hidden
reference \(|0\rangle\), produce the same minimal dephasing channel. The
unique isometry intertwiner is \(W=\mathbb 1\), but the unitaries differ
off the prepared subspace. This freedom does not automatically preserve
the channel family obtained by repeatedly applying \(U\):
\((U^{(1)})^2=\mathbb 1\), whereas \((U^{(2)})^2=Z\otimes\mathbb 1\), so
their time-two coherent channels differ.

\textbf{Remark (the conjugation form).} If the unique \(W\) additionally
satisfies \(W|0_1\rangle=|0_2\rangle\), the isometry relation can be
written as conjugation on the reference subspace. This is an extra
hypothesis, not a freely selectable normalization. For example,
\(U^{(1)}=\mathrm{CNOT}\) and
\(U^{(2)}=(\mathbb 1\otimes X)\mathrm{CNOT}\) with both reference
vectors \(|0\rangle\) give the same minimal channel but have unique
intertwiner \(W=X\), which does not preserve \(|0\rangle\). Even when
the extra hypothesis holds, no full-operator conjugation follows.

\textbf{Remark (limits of semigroup-transfer uniqueness).} Equal channel
families need not imply hidden-unitary conjugation of the generating
dynamics, as the exact Pair C construction in {[}Substratum §4{]} shows
(ST3/ST4). Proposition 24.2 instead gives block-word trace rigidity
under the full word-trace and block-spanning premises. Proposition 9.7b
is a single-channel minimal-isometry uniqueness result, not full
generated-unitary uniqueness for arbitrary dilations.

These propositions identify the many-to-one structure for a fixed
complete channel: minimal pure-reference isometries are related by a
hidden unitary, non-minimal realizations permit enlargement, and a
prepared isometry need not fix its full-unitary extension. General
hidden unitaries need not be finite hidden permutations, and general
channel dilations need not lie in OI's finite-bijection class. These
results therefore do not classify \(\mathcal G_{\rm sub}\) or its
whole-time-family action.

\textbf{Corollary 9.8} (Realization freedom for a fixed prepared
channel). \emph{A fixed full ambient channel on a finite nonzero visible
space need not determine a unique unitary realization. Among minimal
finite pure-reference realizations it fixes the Stinespring isometry up
to the unique hidden unitary of Proposition 9.7b. It does not fix a
full-unitary extension off the prepared subspace, and non-minimal
realizations allow further enlargement. The universality-class datum of
Definition 9.4 is \((\mathcal A_V,\Phi^*|_{\mathcal A_V})\), which can
contain still less information when \(\mathcal A_V\) is proper.}

For general separable spaces the positive isometry-uniqueness statement
follows from Proposition 9.7c. The finite-dimensional kernel result and
the explicit finite countercontrols have separate evidential roles; the
general proof does not extend the kernel theorem's formal scope.

\textbf{Scope (relative to the fixed-reference observational
interface).} Off-reference unitary freedom is invisible to one channel
prepared with the fixed reference state. Independently preparing hidden
inputs, or reusing the hidden system across several times, can reveal
it. Calling this freedom gauge is therefore relative to that single
prepared-channel carrier, not a completeness claim about OI dynamics or
multi-time records.

A structural-realist interpretation can treat the isomorphism class of
the chosen restricted pair as observational content. Whether this
exhausts physically meaningful structure depends on the carrier and the
available preparations. It does not follow that every microscopic
difference is a \(\mathcal G_{\rm sub}\) gauge move.

\hypertarget{relation-to-ois-gauge-group-refined}{%
\subsubsection{9.4 Relation to OI's gauge group,
refined}\label{relation-to-ois-gauge-group-refined}}

With the formal definitions in place, the relationship between
universality classes and OI's gauge group can be stated more precisely
than in §9.2.

OI's proposed \(\mathcal G_{\rm sub}\) is intended to organize
observationally redundant substratum descriptions. Proposition 9.5
proves inclusion only for its two specified coherent-channel
transformations under their preparation and invariance hypotheses.
Theorem 24 does not classify every OI realization of the same restricted
algebra/channel pair. Definition 9.4, in contrast, defines an
equivalence directly on that pair across frameworks; whether non-OI
realizations occupy the same class requires the corresponding algebra
map and intertwining.

The proposed substratum quotient and the algebra/channel quotient can be
compared only after their carriers are fixed. The established
transformations act within a class of Definition 9.4, but equality of
the two full equivalence relations within A1--A6 is not established. A
claim about physical ontology requires more than this restricted
inclusion.

\hypertarget{structure-of-universality-classes}{%
\subsubsection{9.5 Structure of universality
classes}\label{structure-of-universality-classes}}

Universality classes have internal structure beyond bare equivalence.
Three structural features:

\textbf{Sub-classes by structural features.} Equivalent restricted pairs
share the properties actually encoded in the chosen invariant algebra
and its adjoint dynamics. They need not share ambient-channel unitarity
or every multi-time marginal, which require further data. Realizations
in one class may satisfy different microscopic structural conditions;
these conditions can define sub-classes without changing the chosen
observable carrier.

\textbf{Sub-classes by gauge content.} A gauge comparison requires the
observable algebra, channel and the symmetry action being compared. The
Standard Model factors are calculated in the selected OI branch under
its carrier and physical hypotheses. A string construction can reproduce
the same factors through different premises; matching them does not by
itself give an isomorphism of the full embedded-observer triples or a
complete map of substratum gauge groups.

\textbf{Refinement by substratum-symmetry.} A coarse comparison that
omits gauge charges or couplings may group different gauge targets
together. A carrier containing their measured values cannot: equivalence
must preserve them. SM-reproducing model families can therefore contain
multiple classes, and claims at a coarser level must state which data
have been omitted.

This permits a hierarchy of observational carriers: partial-trace
features alone, specified gauge and matter data, and a fuller dynamics
including quantitative predictions. Refining a carrier can split an
equivalence class. Varying a measured Cabibbo angle or mass within a
model family is not a change of representative of one class whose
carrier already includes that quantity.

\hypertarget{observer-admitting-and-observer-lacking-substrata}{%
\subsubsection{9.6 Observer-admitting and observer-lacking
substrata}\label{observer-admitting-and-observer-lacking-substrata}}

The framework's universality classes are populated by
\emph{observer-admitting} substrata --- pairs \((S, \varphi)\) that
admit a partition \(V \subset S\) satisfying the framework's structural
conditions C1--C4 (coupling, record persistence, sufficient capacity,
history readback) {[}Main §3{]}. This restriction defines the scope of
the framework's discourse and is structurally distinct from the absence
of an observer in any specific substratum. We make the distinction
explicit here because it bears on the relationship between OI and other
unification programs in §12.

\textbf{Definition 9.9} (Observer-admitting substratum). \emph{A
substratum \((S, \varphi)\) is observer-admitting if there exists a
partition \(V\) such that the triple \((S, \varphi, V)\) satisfies
C1--C4. A substratum is observer-lacking if no such partition exists.}

Three structural conditions on \(\varphi\) characterize
observer-admission:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{Coupled dynamics:} \(\varphi\) does not decompose as
  \(\varphi_V \times \varphi_H\) for any candidate partition. Substrata
  where visible and hidden sector dynamics factorize cannot satisfy C1.
\item
  \emph{Non-equilibrium phase:} \(\varphi\) admits phases where local
  mixing time is much shorter than overall recurrence time. Under the
  effective-mixing hypothesis and for sufficiently fast-mixing
  equilibria (\(\tau_{\rm mix} \ll \tau_S\)), substrata in perpetual
  equilibrium cannot satisfy C2 in the coarse-grained description
  generated by iterating the one-step effective channel {[}Main §4.6{]}.
\item
  \emph{Sufficient hidden capacity:} potential hidden sectors have
  \(|\mathcal{C}_H|\) large enough to store interaction history without
  overflow. Substrata with insufficient hidden-sector capacity cannot
  satisfy C3.
\end{itemize}

These are structural features of the substratum's bijection \(\varphi\)
itself, prior to any observer being instantiated. An observer-admitting
substratum has the structural capacity for partial-trace observation; an
observer-lacking substratum does not.

\textbf{Proposition 9.10} (Observer-admitting and observer-lacking
substrata are structurally distinct). \emph{Observer-admission is
determined by structural features of \(\varphi\), not by whether an
observer is ``present'' or ``absent'' in any specific substratum. The
classes are mutually exclusive partitions of the space of substrata.}

The proposition is a clarification rather than a deep result: it states
that the observer-admission criterion operates at the level of the
bijection structure, separating substrata into two structurally-distinct
classes. An observer-admitting substratum has the structural features
that allow observation to occur; an observer-lacking substratum does
not. The classes are not ``the same substratum with observation added or
removed'' --- they are different substrata characterized by different
structural features of \(\varphi\).

\textbf{Corollary 9.11} (Universality classes are populated by
observer-admitting substrata only). \emph{The universality classes of
Definition 9.4 are equivalence classes within the observer-admitting
subset. Observer-lacking substrata are outside the framework's
universality-class structure.}

This corollary clarifies the framework's scope. Universality classes
characterize substratum-with-observer systems by their algebra-channel
structure under partial-trace observation; observer-lacking substrata do
not produce algebra-channel pairs through partial trace and are
therefore not in the universality-class structure. They are mathematical
objects (finite sets with bijections) with dynamics, but not with
extractable observational features.

\textbf{The ``physics'' of observer-lacking substrata.} Observer-lacking
substrata have \(\varphi\)-dynamics --- they evolve, they have cycle
structure, they have all the formal features of permutations on finite
sets. But they do not produce \emph{physics} in the framework's sense:
no quantum mechanics, no observable record, no measurement outcomes. The
framework's foundational claim --- that physics is the structural
features of partial-trace observation --- implies that physics is a
feature of observer-admitting substrata, not of all substrata. This is
consistent with the main paper's ``observation occurs'' axiom: the axiom
is taken to be a structural feature of our universe (which is
observer-admitting) rather than a contingent fact about whether someone
happens to be looking.

\textbf{Anthropic implication.} The observer-admission distinction has
structural-anthropic content. Most arbitrary \((S, \varphi)\) pairs do
not admit observers --- random permutations on finite sets typically
lack the coupled-dynamics, non-equilibrium-phase, and high-capacity
features required for C1--C4. Observer-admitting substrata are a
structurally-characterized subset. Our universe is in this subset,
structurally; we are observers in an observer-admitting substratum. This
is structural anthropic selection rather than contingent anthropic
selection: the criterion for ``physical universe'' is
observer-admission, with observer-lacking substrata being abstract
mathematical objects rather than candidate physical universes.

The structural-anthropic reading is consistent with but more precise
than the standard anthropic position. Standard anthropics says we
observe a universe with parameters compatible with observers because we
couldn't observe a universe without those parameters. The
observer-admission reading says: substrata with observers and substrata
without observers are structurally distinct kinds of objects;
observer-admitting substrata are the candidate space for ``physical
universe''; we are in one of them.

\hypertarget{worked-examples}{%
\subsubsection{9.7 Worked examples}\label{worked-examples}}

We illustrate the universality-class definition with three examples.

\textbf{Example 1: A selected OI realization.} Fix a finite \(d=3\)
cubic wave-rule model, H-link with \(m=1\) for its physical carrier, and
a specified visible algebra and preparation/readout map. Its observation
map supplies an algebra/channel pair. Proposition 9.5 places the
established hidden-only and independent finite ancillary transformations
in the same observational class. It does not put every alphabet change,
graph comparison or population-preserving relabelling in that class.
Whether other OI or non-OI realizations produce the same pair is a
separate classification problem. Observer admission is assessed for the
specified triple, as in §9.6.

\textbf{Example 2: String-theoretic class.} A string-theoretic
substratum (e.g., type IIA on a specific Calabi-Yau threefold with
specific fluxes) produces a low-energy effective field theory. The
dilation is the full string-theoretic structure (worldsheet plus target
space, or matrix-model structure plus large-\(N\) limit); the
visible-sector algebra is the four-dimensional effective field theory
operator algebra. The universality class is the class of substrata
producing the same algebra-channel pair through string-theoretic
emergence. Observer-admission is a non-trivial constraint: not all
string compactifications are observer-admitting, as developed in §7.9.

\textbf{Example 3: Cross-class candidate.} A class candidate is the
union: substrata that produce the SM gauge group + three generations +
SM-like Yukawa structure through any mechanism (OI's cubic-lattice
derivation, string-theoretic compactification, possibly LQG-style
structures, causal-set structures, or asymptotic-safety--derived
structures with appropriate matter-emergence). This class is
characterized by the observable target (the SM at low energies) rather
than by the substratum mechanism. All members are observer-admitting
(the SM-reproducing constraint forces observer-admission, since
reproducing the SM requires producing the observed cosmological
structure within which we are observers). Part I (§§3-7) establishes
that, at the level of partial-trace observational features, OI and
string theory are members of this candidate cross-class; §13.1.4 extends
the per-condition analysis to LQG, causal sets, and asymptotic safety,
finding that all three fail OI's A1--A6 (so are outside OI's specific
class) but may share the partial-trace observational features class with
OI and string theory. The question is whether the cross-class is
well-defined and what additional features characterize it.

\hypertarget{open-does-the-oi-string-cross-class-exist}{%
\subsubsection{9.8 Open: does the OI-string cross-class
exist?}\label{open-does-the-oi-string-cross-class-exist}}

The candidate cross-class of Example 3 is well-defined as a set:
substrata producing the SM at low energies. The question is whether it
is a \emph{universality class} in the sense defined --- whether all
members produce equivalent algebra-channel pairs.

Part III §14 establishes partial transport: Born rule and channel-level
unitarity transport directly; the memory (non-Markovian) mechanism
transports, while P-indivisibility is not established cross-framework;
commutant gauge-invariance pattern transports but not the specific gauge
group. Specific Yukawa couplings, CP-violating phases, and other
detailed predictions have not been demonstrated to transport.

If only the structural features at §14.1-14.3 transport, the OI-string
cross-class is universal at the level of these features but not at the
level of detailed predictions. The class might be subdivided further:
the partial-trace-features class is broader; the SM-reproducing class is
narrower; the OI-Cabibbo-and-Koide-predictions class is narrower still.

Establishing definitively whether OI's specific predictions (Cabibbo,
Koide, etc.) transport to the string-theoretic class would require
either (a) finding a string-theoretic compactification reproducing OI's
predictions, (b) showing that any SM-reproducing compactification
automatically produces these predictions, or (c) showing that no
string-theoretic compactification can reproduce these predictions
exactly. Each of (a)-(c) is a substantial research question.

The provisional conclusion: the OI-string cross-class is well-defined at
the level of partial-trace observational features (per §14) and includes
specific gauge-group-and-content sub-classes, but the full structure of
the cross-class --- including whether OI's specific predictions are
universal to the SM-reproducing sub-class or specific to OI's
representative --- is open.

\subsection{10. OI's framework as a worked
example}\label{ois-framework-as-a-worked-example}}

This section uses OI's framework to illustrate how universality classes
interact with substratum-level structure. The aim is to show that
universality classes are not abstract mathematical curiosities but
capture the structural content of OI's reconstruction theorem in a more
general form.

\hypertarget{ois-reconstruction-theorem-in-universality-class-language}{%
\subsubsection{10.1 OI's reconstruction theorem in universality-class
language}\label{ois-reconstruction-theorem-in-universality-class-language}}

Theorem 23 {[}Substratum §3{]} currently states conditional local
lattice/gauge outputs on a selected cubic branch, under the explicit
nearest-neighbor, H-link, representation, amplitude-scale and matter
assumptions. It does not establish that E1--E7/A1--A6 alone select that
branch or that its microscopic realizations are all related by a
complete observational gauge group.

Theorem 23 currently gives conditional local lattice/gauge outputs for
the selected cubic branch and does not show uniqueness modulo all
admissible gauge transformations. H-Bell independently conditions the
existence of a common Bell-composite realization; its uniqueness remains
open.

\hypertarget{what-oi-does-and-does-not-establish-at-the-universality-class-level}{%
\subsubsection{10.2 What OI does and does not establish at the
universality-class
level}\label{what-oi-does-and-does-not-establish-at-the-universality-class-level}}

OI's selected cubic-branch reconstruction supplies conditional
structural outputs, not uniqueness of every realization from
E1--E7/A1--A6. It also does not establish Bell-inclusive uniqueness or a
universal all-partitions gauge quotient; the comparisons with other
universality classes remain separately scoped.

The universality-class language locates results relative to a specified
observable structure. Theorem 23 gives conditional outputs for a
selected realization; it does not identify a unique class from
E1--E7/A1--A6. Distinct classes can agree on a restricted set of tested
Standard Model quantities while differing on fuller channel or
preparation/readout data. Such agreement is not equivalence under
Definition 9.3 without the required map.

\hypertarget{the-structural-realism-implication}{%
\subsubsection{10.3 The structural-realism
implication}\label{the-structural-realism-implication}}

The structural-realist proposal concerns branch-relative outputs and
structures preserved by specified observational maps. Theorem 23 does
not establish a uniquely reconstructed local residue modulo a complete
substratum gauge group. Local-branch selection, all-partitions
equivalence and a common Bell-compatible completion are separate open
questions.

A structural-realist interpretation may treat the chosen algebra/channel
structure as the content shared by equivalent descriptions under
Definition 9.3. The physical warrant of the selected representative and
any additional microscopic commitments require their own arguments. This
interpretation does not itself prove a complete gauge classification or
a unique physical substratum.

The Cabibbo, Koide and related calculations are conditional predictions
of the selected OI branch. If those quantities and their comparison
protocols are encoded in a completed carrier, every equivalent
realization must reproduce them. A different model can share the SM
gauge target yet differ on those quantities, in which case it belongs to
a different observational class on that richer carrier.

\hypertarget{constructing-ois-algebra-channel-pair}{%
\subsubsection{10.4 Constructing OI's algebra-channel
pair}\label{constructing-ois-algebra-channel-pair}}

The universality-class formalism requires a specified algebra, a
well-defined channel and an invariance check. At a finite cutoff OI
supplies an exact trace-out channel from a chosen permutation and hidden
preparation. At the physical EFT scale, the following is a conditional
candidate and a list of matching obligations; it is not yet a
constructed continuum algebra/channel pair.

\textbf{The Hilbert space.} In the selected cubic branch, the finite
visible configuration space gives
\(\mathcal H_{V,\mathrm{cut}}=\ell^2(\mathcal C_V)\). A physical SM
Fock-space interpretation additionally needs a limiting construction and
the stated matter/readout hypotheses, including H-spin' and H-χ'. The
finite trace-out formula alone does not supply that identification. We
denote a candidate EFT space by \(\mathcal H_V^{\rm OI}\) only after
those premises have been specified.

\textbf{The algebra.} A candidate local EFT net must be built from
well-defined bounded localized gauge-invariant observables on that
space. The schematic expression
\[\mathcal A_V^{\rm OI}=\langle\text{bounded localized gauge-invariant observables}\rangle''\]
means the von Neumann closure after their representation and relations
have been supplied. Pointlike fields and their unbounded products cannot
simply be inserted as bounded generators. Relevant smeared operators,
their bounded functions and their domains or affiliations must be
constructed. Candidate operator sectors include:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{Gauge-field invariants.} The Yang-Mills curvature combinations
  \(\mathrm{tr}\, F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{a,\mu\nu}\) for each factor of
  \(SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)\), plus topological terms
  \(\mathrm{tr}\, F \tilde F\).
\item
  \emph{Fermion bilinears.} \(\bar\psi_i \psi_j\),
  \(\bar\psi_i \gamma^\mu \psi_j\),
  \(\bar\psi_i \gamma^\mu \gamma^5 \psi_j\) for the SM fermion content
  (six quarks, six leptons, three generations), with appropriate
  gauge-index contractions.
\item
  \emph{Higgs invariants.} \(\phi^\dagger \phi\), \(|D_\mu \phi|^2\),
  \((\phi^\dagger \phi)^2\), and the Yukawa contractions
  \(\bar\psi_L \phi \psi_R\) structurally fixed by the cubic-group
  representation theory of {[}SM §7{]}.
\item
  \emph{Higher-dimensional operators.} A comparison must specify their
  Wilson coefficients and the exact assumptions behind any proposed
  relations, including the Cabibbo and Koide calculations of {[}SM
  §7{]}. A list of scalar outputs is not itself a presentation of the
  complete observable algebra or a derivation of every coefficient.
\end{itemize}

Gauge invariance requires the localized observables to commute with the
specified gauge action, with the appropriate physical constraints
imposed. The group action, localization, representation and closure are
part of the construction; naming the SM factors alone does not determine
them.

\textbf{The channel.} At a finite cutoff the chosen permutation gives
the exact Schrödinger channel
\[\Phi_{\rm cut}(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}_H\!\left[U_\varphi(\rho\otimes\rho_{H,\rm cut})U_\varphi^\dagger\right].\]
The normalized uniform hidden state is available in this finite setting.
A normalized scalar multiple of the identity on a hidden Hilbert space
requires finite dimension. An EFT channel \(\Phi^{\rm OI}\) requires an
independently specified state and a controlled limit or effective
construction. Its adjoint must preserve the chosen EFT algebra before
Definition 9.3 applies. A mixed hidden reference gives a unitary
realization, not a minimal pure-reference Stinespring dilation;
purifying it changes the hidden space. The following physical features
are matching targets, not consequences of the finite trace-out identity
alone:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{SM RG flow} from the cubic-lattice scale (\(1/\alpha_0 = 23.25\)
  structurally, {[}SM §6.1{]}) down to the electroweak scale
  (\(1/\alpha_1 = 59.00\), \(1/\alpha_2 = 29.57\), \(1/\alpha_3 = 8.47\)
  at \(M_Z\), {[}SM §6.3{]}).
\item
  \emph{Non-perturbative structural features.} Instanton-sum closure
  consistent with \(T\)-invariance and detailed balance.
\item
  \emph{Anomaly structure.} Check the anomaly constraints for the
  selected matter representations and charge assignment ({[}SM §4.6{]}).
\item
  \emph{Higher-dimensional operator coefficients.} Supply the proposed
  Yukawa relations and their stated premises, then determine which are
  actually encoded in the constructed dynamics ({[}SM §7{]}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The pair as universality-class invariant.} Once a channel and an
invariant algebra have been constructed, the isomorphism class of
\((\mathcal A_V^{\rm OI},\Phi^{\rm OI*}|_{\mathcal A_V^{\rm OI}})\) is
the invariant of Definition 9.4. At a fixed finite interface Proposition
9.5 proves preservation for its two named transformations. It proves no
such assertion for every proposed \(g\in\mathcal G_{\rm sub}\), nor does
it automatically transport a finite-cutoff intertwiner to an EFT limit.

\textbf{What the pair captures.} The pair captures exactly the
observables represented in its algebra and their specified adjoint
dynamics. Measured masses, couplings and Wilson coefficients are
invariant under equivalence only insofar as the carrier includes them.
Changing a bijection in a way that changes such data is not
automatically gauge. Hidden microscopic information can remain
unidentifiable, but its empirical irrelevance must be shown for the
chosen carrier.

A relativistic EFT comparison may use nets of local von Neumann algebras
with isotony and microcausality {[}BrunettiFredenhagen2024{]}. Type-III
local factors {[}Yngvason2005{]} require an appropriate infinite-system
or continuum representation; a finite matrix algebra is not type III.
These properties must be established for the proposed limit rather than
inherited by naming an AQFT target. The finite trace-out construction
and standard Stinespring theory {[}Stinespring1955{]} remain valid
within their stated settings.

\hypertarget{status-as-step-1-of-13.1.1s-recipe}{%
\subsubsection{10.5 Status as Step 1 of §13.1.1's
recipe}\label{status-as-step-1-of-13.1.1s-recipe}}

Section 10.4 specifies an exact finite-cutoff channel and a conditional
EFT target. Step 1 of §13.1.1 remains open at the full EFT
algebra/channel level. It requires the physical/limit construction, a
specified algebra and state, and invariance of that algebra under the
adjoint dynamics. The comparison then requires:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \emph{Step 2.} Construct a specific string compactification's pair
  \((\mathcal{H}_V^{\rm string}, \mathcal{A}_V^{\rm string}, \Phi^{\rm string})\)
  at the same EFT scale. Natural candidates are MSSM-like F-theory or
  heterotic compactifications producing the exact observed gauge group
  and chiral content. Particle content and gauge factors specify
  targets; operator relations, representation, closure, states and
  actual channel dynamics must also be constructed, including any
  proposed thresholds, instanton effects or scale dependence.
\item
  \emph{Step 3.} Construct and test a normal unital *-isomorphism of the
  specified observable algebras. Agreement on gauge factors or finitely
  many scalar predictions is a necessary consistency check for a carrier
  containing those data, not a sufficient algebra-isomorphism test.
\item
  \emph{Step 4.} Check
  \(\theta\circ\Phi^{\rm OI*}|_{\mathcal A_V^{\rm OI}}=\Phi^{\rm string*}|_{\mathcal A_V^{\rm string}}\circ\theta\),
  for the same isomorphism \(\theta\) and every channel in the specified
  family. Joint intervention statistics require their own process
  carrier.
\end{itemize}

Completing Step 1 and carrying out Steps 2--4 require a specified
physical EFT on each side. The finite trace-out formula makes one part
of OI's proposal concrete, but does not reduce the remaining task to
comparing already constructed continuum pairs.

\hypertarget{what-14s-findings-establish}{%
\subsubsection{11.1 What §14's findings
establish}\label{what-14s-findings-establish}}

The §14 four-feature audit shows that the Born rule and channel-level
unitarity transport directly between OI and string-theoretic emergence
mechanisms, and that the memory (non-Markovian) mechanism transports
while P-indivisibility is not established cross-framework (§14.3). The
commutant gauge-invariance \emph{pattern} transports but the specific
gauge group does not.

In the terminology of §9, the audit identifies shared features of model
families, not one exact universality class for all OI or string
constructions. Definition 9.3 requires a chosen invariant algebra, the
corresponding adjoint dynamics and an actual intertwining isomorphism. A
feature comparison alone does not supply those data.

Under their respective physical assumptions, selected OI and string
constructions can reproduce the same Standard-Model local gauge factors
through different mechanisms. This places them in a common gauge-target
family. Their matter assignments, couplings and dynamics still need
comparison, and a matching target does not establish Definition 9.3
equivalence or a common global gauge-group action.

\hypertarget{what-additional-work-is-needed}{%
\subsubsection{11.2 What additional work is
needed}\label{what-additional-work-is-needed}}

Section 10.4 gives a finite-cutoff construction and outlines the EFT
target. Completing the OI algebra/channel pair at the chosen physical
scale is still Step 1; the following comparison questions also remain
open:

\textbf{Question 1: Do OI's specific predictions transport?} OI predicts
the Cabibbo angle as \(1/(\pi\sqrt{2})\) from cubic-group representation
theory. Does any SM-reproducing string compactification reproduce this
exact value through its compactification mechanism, or are
string-theoretic predictions for the Cabibbo angle
compactification-specific (and presumably not exactly
\(1/(\pi\sqrt{2})\) in general)? If the values differ, the models cannot
be equivalent on a carrier containing those values. They may still share
a coarser structural target.

\textbf{Question 2: Do the algebras agree?} OI's emergent QFT has a
specific algebra of observables determined by its cubic-lattice
structure (proposed conditionally in §10.4). String-theoretic
compactifications produce algebras determined by the compactification
specifics. Are these algebras *-isomorphic for SM-reproducing
constructions, or merely structurally similar (sharing partial-trace
features but differing on specific algebraic content)?

\textbf{Question 3: Do the channels agree?} Beyond the algebra
structure, the channel \(\Phi\) encodes the dynamics on visible-sector
states. Does OI's \(\Phi^{\rm OI}\) (after completing the construction
required in §10.4) agree with the string-theoretic \(\Phi^{\rm string}\)
on identical SM gauge group plus identical particle content, or does it
differ on detailed dynamical features (e.g., the specific RG flow,
specific anomaly content, specific instanton effects)?

These questions require completing both pairs, constructing an algebra
map and checking the adjoint intertwining. Part III's partial-trace
comparison does not complete that task. Agreement of selected scalar
outputs remains useful evidence, with its carrier stated explicitly.

\item

The provisional characterization is shared interface architecture and a

\hypertarget{implications}{%
\subsection{12. Implications}\label{implications}}

\hypertarget{refined-structural-realism}{%
\subsubsection{12.1 Refined structural
realism}\label{refined-structural-realism}}

The universality-class framework makes the realist commitment relative
to a specified observable carrier. The invariant is the isomorphism
class of \((\mathcal A_V,\Phi^*|_{\mathcal A_V})\). Shared partial-trace
architecture, a common gauge target and membership in a microscopic
A1--A6 family are distinct comparisons; none by itself identifies that
class or a completed \(\mathcal G_{\rm sub}\) quotient.

At a broad level, quantum representations share the probability
dictionary and dilation machinery assumed in their construction; memory
requires the additional conditions discussed in §14.3. At a gauge-target
level, models can share Standard-Model local factors. At a quantitative
level, predictions such as the Cabibbo value are conditional outputs of
a selected branch and invariants of any completed observational carrier
that contains them. They cannot change merely by replacing one
equivalent representative with another.

This is an observation-relative structural realism: refining the carrier
can distinguish models that agree on a coarser target, while an
established isomorphism identifies exactly the content that must remain
invariant. It preserves the selected branch's predictions and makes
their transport assumptions explicit.

\hypertarget{methodology-of-fundamental-physics}{%
\subsubsection{12.2 Methodology of fundamental
physics}\label{methodology-of-fundamental-physics}}

The universality-class framework suggests a methodological reframing of
fundamental physics. Rather than asking ``what is the substratum?'' ---
which assumes a unique answer that committing to a specific framework
presupposes --- we can ask ``what universality class are we in?'' ---
which allows for multiple substratum-level descriptions consistent with
the same universality class.

This is methodologically more agnostic about substratum specifics but
more committed about substratum-class membership. It treats fundamental
physics as a research program of identifying the universality class of
our universe through observational features, with substratum
descriptions being framework-specific tools for representing the class
rather than competing claims about ultimate reality.

The methodological move connects to operationalist and empiricist
positions in foundations of physics, but with structural-realism
preserved at the universality-class level: what's real is the
universality class, not just the operational data, and not the specific
substratum representative.

\hypertarget{connection-to-ois-main-paper}{%
\subsubsection{12.3 Connection to OI's main
paper}\label{connection-to-ois-main-paper}}

OI's main paper starts from observation and proves exact finite-law
representation results under its stated setting. The algebra/channel
framework packages a chosen quantum representation as an invariant
observable algebra and adjoint dynamics. It does not derive that
representation, non-Markovian memory or a complete operational quantum
theory from the words `observation occurs' alone.

The roles remain separate: finite-law quantum representation, the C4
memory-bearing sector, recurrence-scale indivisibility and the coherent
operational lift have distinct hypotheses and evidential status in
{[}Main{]}. A1--A6 and the additional local-branch assumptions supply
the conditional physical model studied in {[}SM{]} and {[}GR{]}.
Definition 9.4 then compares whatever observable pairs have actually
been constructed.

This broader language permits cross-framework comparison without
assuming the same microscopic construction. Agreement on representation
architecture or a list of scalar outputs motivates further work; a claim
of the same observational class requires the normal algebra map and
restricted adjoint intertwining.

The framework predicts that no grand-unified gauge structure exists at

\item

  the predicted values to other values.
\end{enumerate}

This prediction is structurally distinct from string-theoretic
frameworks, which traverse intermediate large gauge groups (heterotic

The conditional no-GUT consequences are catalogued at Level 3 in §12.4:
proton-lifetime estimates, absence of GUT-mechanism monopoles and

These targets are shared with broader no-GUT model families. The
OI-specific construction is its condensate-stabilizer and
induced-coupling mechanism; shared targets do not by themselves
establish one restricted algebra/channel class.

\textbf{Empirical-lean assessment.} The current empirical state
moderately favors the no-GUT model family over standard GUT scenarios
across multiple discriminators. Super-Kamiokande proton-decay bounds
{[}SuperK2020\_proton, SuperK2024\_eta{]} at
\(\tau_p(p \to e^+\pi^0) > 2.4 \times 10^{34}\) years and

{[}LHC\_SUSY\_2025{]}, with the surviving SUSY-GUT scenarios requiring

motivation. NANOGrav 15-year pulsar timing data
{[}NANOGravStrings2023{]} disfavors stable cosmic-string interpretations

No single observation sharply distinguishes OI from all GUT-class

model family containing the selected OI branch, not merely consistency.
The decisive future tests are Hyper-Kamiokande's projected
\(\tau_p \sim 10^{35}\) year sensitivity for \(p \to e^+\pi^0\)
{[}HyperK\_TDR2018{]} and DUNE's \(\sim 6.5 \times 10^{34}\) year

\textbf{Connection to the unified naturalness pattern.} The no-GUT
prediction is an instance of a broader pattern in the framework:
substratum-level structural symmetries dissolve naturalness problems by
forcing emergent parameter values that would otherwise be free. The

problem} --- singlet-taste chiral symmetry provides

\hypertarget{q1-developed-what--isomorphism-would-entail}{%
\subsubsection{13.1.1 Q1 developed: what *-isomorphism would
entail}\label{q1-developed-what--isomorphism-would-entail}}

To establish equivalence at a specified EFT scale requires: (1)
completing OI's observable algebra, channel and physical/limit bridge;
(2) constructing the compactification's corresponding pair on the same
observational carrier; (3) constructing a normal unital *-isomorphism
\(\theta\) of the algebras; and (4) checking
\(\theta\circ\Phi^{(1)*}|_{\mathcal A_1}=\Phi^{(2)*}|_{\mathcal A_2}\circ\theta\)
for the specified channel or family. \textbf{Status:} Section 10.4
supplies an exact finite-cutoff channel and a conditional EFT
specification. The complete EFT version of Step 1 and Steps 2--4 remain
open.

Three possible outcomes are: (a) an algebra isomorphism intertwines the
specified restricted adjoint dynamics, establishing equivalence on that
carrier; (b) the algebras are isomorphic but the required intertwining
fails; or (c) the observable algebras are not isomorphic. In case (a),
the observables and protocols actually encoded and transported by the
comparison share their predictions. Agreement at an SM target or on
finitely many Wilson coefficients does not settle these alternatives,
nor does a comparison on a proper algebra automatically identify the
full ambient channels.

\hypertarget{converged-comparison-protocol}{%
\paragraph{13.1.1.1 Converged comparison
protocol}\label{converged-comparison-protocol}}

A specific protocol for executing Steps 2-4 against a chosen
compactification has been worked out as preparation for offline
computational follow-up. The protocol is:

\textbf{Compactification choice: heterotic line-bundle Calabi-Yau in the
Anderson-Gray-Lukas-Palti class.} Specifically, the Schoen-quotient
model \(X = \tilde{X}/(\mathbb{Z}_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_3)\) with
five-line-bundle SM model {[}Anderson-Gray-Lukas-Palti 2011-2014{]}.
This compactification was selected after considering three candidates
(heterotic line-bundle on Schoen quotient; type IIA Pati-Salam on
intersecting D6-branes; F-theory \(SU(5)\) with hypercharge flux) on
multiple criteria: 4D EFT characterization completeness (heterotic AGLP
has multi-loop Yukawa structure characterized in the literature);
three-generation match (all three candidates produce three generations,
but through structurally distinct mechanisms --- see below); pure SM
gauge group at IR (all three reach SM after appropriate breaking);
bundle-cohomology computational tractability; phenomenological
literature density (heterotic AGLP has the most extensive literature).
The Schoen-\(\mathbb{Z}_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_3\) model dominates on most
criteria and was selected over its alternatives.

\textbf{Comparison scale: below the Stueckelberg \(U(1)\) mass
(\textasciitilde{}\(10^{16}\) GeV).} Heterotic line-bundle
compactifications produce an extra Stueckelberg-massive \(U(1)\) via the
Green-Schwarz mechanism (anomaly cancellation in heterotic with line
bundles); typical Stueckelberg masses in AGLP models are
\(\sim 10^{16}\) GeV, set by the compactification scale. Below this
mass, the heterotic effective theory has only the SM gauge group,
matching OI's structure at the IR. The comparison must therefore be
conducted at scales below the Stueckelberg mass to ensure both sides
have matching gauge structure. This restricts the universality-class
claim to the IR EFT (scales \(M_Z \lesssim \mu \lesssim 10^{16}\) GeV)
but does not prevent the comparison from proceeding.

\textbf{Comparison test: Yukawa textures.} Both frameworks reach three
generations by structurally distinct mechanisms (heterotic uses
bundle-cohomology index of the relevant cohomology groups on the CY, an
entirely topological invariant; OI uses the cubic group's \(T_1\) irrep
with multiplicity 3 from the spatial nearest-neighbor structure). Three
generations is therefore not a discriminator; both frameworks pass this
test by different routes. The natural quantitative test is whether the
\emph{Yukawa textures} arising from the cubic-group reduction in OI
({[}SM §7{]}) match the textures arising from bundle-cohomology
computations on the Schoen-\(\mathbb{Z}_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_3\)
heterotic model. OI's Yukawa textures are characterized in {[}SM
§§7.1-7.3{]} (CKM matrix, charged-lepton masses, PMNS angles); the
heterotic Yukawa textures are characterized in
{[}Anderson-Gray-Lukas-Palti 2013-2014{]}. The comparison reduces to: do
these textures agree at the scale below the Stueckelberg mass?

\textbf{Verdict criteria.} Yukawa-texture agreement is a consistency
check for a carrier containing those observables. A disagreement under
matched conventions and scale rules out equivalence on that carrier;
agreement does not establish a full algebra/channel isomorphism.
Renormalizable agreement with differing higher-dimensional coefficients
can support only a deliberately coarser comparison omitting the latter.
A same-class verdict requires the actual algebra map and adjoint-channel
intertwining.

\textbf{A subtle point about scale-choice.} The comparison-scale
specification matters for the verdict. Below the Stueckelberg mass, both
frameworks have just SM, and the comparison tests Yukawa textures.
Between the Stueckelberg mass and \(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\), the heterotic
theory has an extra \(U(1)\) + GUT-related structure that OI does not
have; the algebras differ at this scale. A comparison at the
\emph{higher} scale would yield a \emph{different-classes} verdict not
because of fundamental disagreement but because of the integrating-out
structure. The honest comparison protocol must specify the scale, and
the IR EFT scale (below Stueckelberg mass) is the natural choice for
testing whether OI and the heterotic compactification can coexist in the
same universality class at all.

\textbf{Status of execution.} The protocol identifies candidate models,
scale conventions and initial quantities to compare. Executing it
requires extracting matching OI and compactification predictions,
completing the specified observable algebras and channels, constructing
a normal algebra isomorphism and checking its restricted adjoint
intertwining. Comparing full dilation unitaries is neither required by
Definition 9.3 nor supplied by a single-channel Stinespring uniqueness
theorem. The physical EFT construction and these comparison steps remain
future work.

\textbf{Connection to the no-GUT structural finding.} The Stueckelberg
\(U(1)\) mass at \(\sim 10^{16}\) GeV is the heterotic
compactification's natural intermediate scale, set by the
compactification structure. This is the string-theoretic analog of the
seesaw scale (per Q8 in §13.3). The comparison scale's choice --- at or
below this Stueckelberg mass --- implicitly settles the comparison's
relationship to Q8: at the IR EFT level, OI and heterotic may share
universality class even though OI lacks a structural intermediate scale
(per Q10's negative resolution in §13.3). Universality-class matching at
the IR is consistent with structurally-distinct intermediate-scale
physics, with the structural difference being absorbed into the channel
rather than being a barrier to algebra-isomorphism. This refines the
§13.1.1 expected-outcome decomposition: outcome (b) is the working
expectation (algebras agree at IR EFT; channels differ at intermediate
scales).

Q3 asks whether the four audit features identify one observational

from the observation axiom alone. The four features are not sufficient:

classes of Definition 9.4. OI's recurrence-scale P-indivisibility

\hypertarget{q4-developed-other-universality-classes-that-may-produce-the-sm}{%
\subsubsection{13.1.4 Q4 developed: other universality classes that may
produce the
SM}\label{q4-developed-other-universality-classes-that-may-produce-the-sm}}

Q4 asks which other model families can reach the SM target and whether
particular members admit a comparison with the OI carrier. The following
per-condition audit concerns their standard microscopic formulations and
A1--A6 compatibility. Such compatibility is a different question from
equivalence of their restricted observable pairs.

\textbf{Loop Quantum Gravity.} LQG's substratum {[}Rovelli2004;
AshtekarLewandowski2004{]} is the space of spin networks --- graphs
labeled by SU(2) representations and intertwiners --- modulo spatial
diffeomorphisms. Dynamics is defined either by Thiemann's Hamiltonian
constraint on the kinematical Hilbert space or by spin-foam amplitudes
summing over histories. Per-condition: A1 (finiteness) \textbf{fails} in
the full theory --- even at fixed graph, the Hilbert space is
infinite-dimensional through unbounded spin labels, and the
sum-over-graphs structure of the full theory makes \(|S| = \infty\)
generically; finite-graph and bounded-spin variants admit a finite
reading but are not standard LQG. A2 (determinism / bijection)
\textbf{fails} --- the dynamics is quantum, with spin-foam amplitudes
constructed as Feynman-like sums rather than deterministic bijections on
configuration space. A3 (bounded coupling degree) holds at fixed graph
valence (typically ≤ 4 in EPRL spin foams) but the bounded-coupling
reading depends on the graph being fixed, which is in tension with
sum-over-graphs. A4 (center independence) \textbf{holds} ---
diffeomorphism invariance is built in. A5 (linearity) \textbf{holds} at
the operator level (the Hamiltonian constraint is a linear operator on
Hilbert space) but is complicated by the constraint structure: solutions
to the constraint are highly nonlinear in the spin-network labels even
when the constraint operator itself is linear. A6 (background
independence) \textbf{holds} --- this is LQG's defining feature. Net: A1
and A2 fail; A4 and A6 hold robustly; A3 and A5 are reframing-dependent.
The structural pattern of failure is similar to string theory's
matrix-model formulation in §6, with the key structural mismatches at A1
and A2 being deeper than the discretization-bridge issue of §6.1 ---
LQG's quantum dynamics and infinite Hilbert spaces are central to the
program rather than artifacts of a particular formulation.

\textbf{Causal sets.} The causal-set program
{[}BombelliLeeMeyerSorkin1987; RideoutSorkin1999; Surya2019{]} takes the
substratum to be a locally finite partially-ordered set, with dynamics
typically modeled by Classical Sequential Growth (CSG) --- a stochastic
generative process that adds events according to a probability measure
on histories. Per-condition: A1 (finiteness) \textbf{holds at any finite
stage} (finite causal sets are genuinely finite) but \textbf{fails} in
the limit of unbounded growth. A2 (determinism / bijection)
\textbf{fails} --- CSG is explicitly stochastic, with each
event-addition governed by a probability rather than a deterministic
update rule. This is the most direct structural divergence from OI's
substratum: where OI takes the substratum dynamics to be deterministic
with stochasticity emerging through partial trace, causal sets take the
dynamics to be intrinsically stochastic at the substratum level. A3
(bounded coupling) holds: each event has a finite number of immediate
predecessors and successors. A4 (center independence) \textbf{holds} ---
the formalism is intrinsically relational. A5 (linearity) does not
translate cleanly --- CSG is described by transition probabilities
rather than a wave equation, so OI's linearity-of-wave-equation framing
has no direct analog. A6 (background independence) \textbf{holds}. Net:
A2 fails outright, A1 fails in the limit, A5 lacks a clean translation.
Causal sets and OI agree on relational structure (A4, A6) but diverge
sharply at the determinism axis (A2). The structural-realist reading:
OI's substratum and the causal-set substratum represent different
commitments at the foundational level, with OI placing the stochasticity
at the partial-trace observational layer while causal sets place it at
the substratum layer.

\textbf{Asymptotic safety.} The asymptotic-safety program
{[}Weinberg1979; Reuter1998; Eichhorn2018{]} takes gravity to be defined
by a non-Gaussian UV fixed point of the renormalization group, with the
metric as the dynamical variable in a continuum quantum field theory
framework. Per-condition: A1 (finiteness) \textbf{fails} --- continuum
QFT. A2 (determinism / bijection) \textbf{fails} --- quantum, path
integral. A3 (bounded coupling) reframing-dependent in the same way as
for matrix models; the natural reading at the spacetime level has
unbounded coupling. A4 (center independence) \textbf{holds} --- general
covariance. A5 (linearity) \textbf{fails} --- Einstein-Hilbert action
plus higher-derivative terms is nonlinear in the metric, with the
nonlinearity essential to producing gravitational dynamics. A6
(background independence) \textbf{holds}, modulo standard subtleties
about expanding around fixed backgrounds. Net: similar pattern to
string-theoretic matrix models --- A2, A5, A1 fail; A4 and A6 hold; A3
reframing-dependent. The structural pattern resembles string theory
closely.

\textbf{Cross-program pattern.} The audited formulations differ from
OI's microscopic A1--A6 family, especially at determinism/bijection A2
and at finiteness A1 in their full settings. The remaining conditions
need their program-specific interpretations as described above. These
differences distinguish model families; they do not exclude a chosen
model from sharing an OI observable pair. The finite-law quantum
representation in {[}Main{]} supplies a concrete reason to keep
microscopic determinism distinct from the representation used for
observations.

\textbf{Restriction-interface comparisons.} The candidate programs can
share quantum-representation or dilation architecture while differing in
microscopic assumptions. Memory and divisibility require the separate
analyses in §14, and symmetry comparisons require specified actions.
These features organize a cross-program audit; exact observational
membership remains a comparison of particular constructed restricted
pairs.

\textbf{Implications for §11 and Limit 1.} The per-program audit
broadens the comparison beyond string theory. It records microscopic
differences and candidate shared mechanisms, rather than enumerating
completed observational classes. For each candidate, the remaining task
is to construct the chosen observable algebra and dynamics and test the
map required by Definition 9.3.

Q7 compares different equivalence relations. RG agreement concerns

restrictions. RG agreement or a holographic dictionary does not
automatically supply that map for every observer choice. No general
ordering of these equivalences is asserted here; the comparison depends
on the scales, algebras and protocols retained.

\hypertarget{open-questions-on-intermediate-scale-physics}{%

The structural investigation of OI's no-GUT prediction (developed in

\textbf{Q8.} \emph{The neutrino mass scale puzzle.} The framework's
{[}SM §8.4{]} predicts Majorana neutrinos via the dimension-5 Weinberg
operator \((LH)(LH)/\Lambda_{\mathrm{seesaw}}\), with
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm{seesaw}}\) treated as a Tier 3 (solution-specific)
parameter. The observed neutrino mass scale \(m_\nu \sim 10^{-3}\) eV
requires \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{seesaw}} \sim 10^{16}\) GeV ---
coincidentally the GUT scale. In standard GUT scenarios, this scale is
structurally explained by gauge-coupling unification. In OI's no-GUT
framework, no GUT scale exists structurally; the natural cutoff is the
lattice scale \(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}/(2\sqrt{\sigma})\), of order
\(M_{\mathrm{Pl}} \sim 10^{19}\) GeV when \(\sigma\) is of order one,
which would give \(m_\nu \sim 10^{-6}\) eV --- three orders of magnitude
too small. The framework currently treats \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{seesaw}}\)
as solution-specific input rather than deriving it. \emph{Open
question:} does the framework have an intermediate scale between \(M_Z\)
and \(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\) that arises structurally from the substratum's
staggered-taste structure or other lattice-level features, and that
could naturally produce \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{seesaw}} \sim 10^{16}\) GeV?
Speculative candidates include scales associated with the
staggered-taste mass spectrum (where different tastes have different
couplings \(|\mu_\Gamma| = 1, |\mu_X| = 1/3\) per {[}SM §4.7{]} Theorem
8b), or scales associated with the boundary-layer thickness in the
visible-vs-hidden sector partition.

\textbf{Q9.} \emph{The hierarchy problem in OI's composite-Higgs
structure.} The framework's {[}SM §7.4{]} predicts
\(\lambda(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}) = 0\) as a structural consequence of the
Higgs being a composite scalar (the singlet taste). This prediction is
empirically supported at \(0.6\sigma\) and identifies the Higgs sector
as composite at the substratum level. Standard composite-Higgs scenarios
in BSM phenomenology dissolve the hierarchy problem via chiral-symmetry
protection (the Higgs as a pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson of an
approximate chiral symmetry). \emph{Partial resolution} (developed in
{[}SM §8.8{]}): the framework structurally implements an analog of this
protection through the singlet-taste chiral symmetry. The \(\mu = +1\)
and \(\mu = -1\) singlet tastes are paired under chirality at the
substratum level (exact discrete symmetry); the Higgs vev spontaneously
breaks this pairing in the emergent EFT. The Higgs is the order
parameter of the broken chirality, with mass protected against
cutoff-scale corrections by Goldstone's theorem.

The framework \emph{derives} cutoff insensitivity --- no
\(O(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2)\) corrections to \(m_H^2\), only
explicit-breaking corrections of order \(g^2 f^2 / (16\pi^2)\) ---
together with the compositeness scale \(f \sim M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\) being
structurally forced. The framework \emph{does not derive} the Higgs vev
value \(v \sim 246\) GeV, which remains Tier 3 (solution-specific). The
boundary: the framework derives why \(m_H\) is not at the cutoff (chiral
protection); it does not derive why \(v\) is at the electroweak scale
rather than at the compositeness scale.

Quantitatively, the framework's partial resolution differs from ``no
resolution'' by a factor of \(\sim 10^{34}\) in the required
fine-tuning. Standard SM without chiral protection requires fine-tuning
\(m_H^2\) to 34 decimal places; the framework's mechanism requires only
that the Tier 3 input \(v\) be at the electroweak scale, with \(m_H\)
following from \(\lambda(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}) = 0\) + RG running.

\emph{Open question:} is there a structural mechanism in OI that derives
\(v \ll f\), or is \(v\) genuinely solution-specific? Two candidate
paths: (i) deriving \(v \ll f\) from substratum structure through a
mechanism currently not in the framework; (ii) accepting \(v\) as
solution-specific, with the structural-vs-solution-specific partition
being part of the framework's content. The framework's current
commitment is (ii): \(v\) sits in the solution-specific category
alongside CP phases, generation-specific Yukawa magnitudes, and other
parameters whose values are not forced by the structural derivation
chain.

\textbf{Q10.} \emph{Possible unified resolution of Q8 and Q9.} Both Q8
(neutrino mass scale) and Q9 (Higgs hierarchy) point to a single
underlying question: does OI's substratum produce a structural
intermediate scale between \(M_Z\) and \(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\) that's not a
GUT scale but plays the roles that GUT scales play in standard scenarios
--- setting the seesaw scale for neutrino masses and protecting the
Higgs mass via chiral mechanisms? \textbf{Tentative resolution:}
negative on the analysis conducted here. The framework's
substratum-level scales are essentially the lattice scale
\(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}/(2\sqrt{\sigma})\), of order \(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\) when
\(\sigma\) is of order one, and emergent scales below \(M_Z\);
candidates for intermediate scales (staggered-taste mass gap,
boundary-layer thickness, the \(\delta_0 = \mathrm{Tr}[Y^2]\)
coincidence at the matching convention \(\mu = M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\),
\(C_2\)-dependent threshold effective scales,
cosmological-horizon-induced scales, emergent dimensional transmutation)
all yield scales at \(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\) modulo \(O(1)\) factors or
scales below \(M_Z\), but none naturally at \(\sim 10^{16}\) GeV. The
seesaw scale and the Higgs mass therefore remain as Tier 3
(solution-specific) parameters in the framework, set by the specific
bijection \(\varphi\) rather than by structural derivation.
\emph{Implication:} OI's predictions on intermediate-scale physics are
limited to what can be derived from \(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\) + EW-scale
physics, with no claim of structural intermediate-scale derivation. This
is consistent with the framework's existing treatment of
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm{seesaw}}\) as Tier 3 (per {[}SM §8.4{]}) and
represents an honest scoping rather than a hidden deficiency. The
negative resolution sharpens the structural-vs-solution-specific
distinction: many parameters that GUT-style frameworks treat as
derivable from unification structure are, in OI, genuinely
solution-specific. This \emph{may} be revisited if a structural
mechanism overlooked in the chat-tractable analysis is identified by
future work, but the chat-tractable conclusion is that no such mechanism
is currently apparent in the framework's existing structure.

\textbf{Q11.} \emph{Quantitative baryogenesis in a spectrum with no
right-handed neutrino.} The framework's substratum-level baryon-number
conservation (per {[}SM §8.7{]}) coexists with sphaleron-mediated
\(B+L\) violation in the emergent EFT. But the OI spectrum contains no
right-handed neutrino --- anomaly cancellation forces exactly five
representations per generation and the singlet taste is the Higgs ({[}SM
§4.9, §8.4{]}) --- so standard type-I thermal leptogenesis, which
requires heavy right-handed Majorana neutrinos, does not directly apply.
\emph{Open question:} the full quantitative calculation --- connecting
whatever ultraviolet physics generates the dimension-5 Weinberg operator
(the \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{seesaw}}\) scale of Q8) to the resulting baryon
asymmetry \(\eta = n_B/n_\gamma \approx 6 \times 10^{-10}\) --- is open,
and the mechanism is not fixed by the framework. Resolution depends
partly on Q8: without a structural derivation of the Weinberg-operator
scale, the baryogenesis prediction inherits the same status (Tier 3 /
solution-specific).

\textbf{Q12.} \emph{Integrated treatment of non-perturbative emergence.}
{[}SM §8.7{]}'s table of substratum-level structural precursors mapping
to emergent-level non-perturbative SM features (T-invariance →
\(\theta = 0\); topology of induced gauge action → sphalerons; bipartite
checkerboard → chiral fermions and anomalies; block-diagonal \(M\) →
substratum \(B\) conservation) provides a unified pattern that organizes
the framework's treatment of non-perturbative SM physics. \emph{Open
question:} is this mapping exhaustive, or are there non-perturbative SM
features (e.g., specific instanton-density distributions, specific
non-perturbative QCD vacuum structure, specific anomaly-inflow
mechanisms) that lack identified substratum-level precursors? A complete
cataloging would either confirm the framework's structural completeness
on this dimension or identify gaps requiring further development.

These five open questions (Q8-Q12) represent the framework's substantive
research frontier as of the no-GUT investigation. Each is a candidate
research direction for future development; together they form a coherent
set focused on whether OI's substratum has the structural richness
needed to resolve standard SM open problems (neutrino masses, hierarchy,
baryogenesis, non-perturbative completeness) without invoking new
physics beyond what the framework already commits to.

\hypertarget{limits-of-the-approach}{%
\subsubsection{13.4 Limits of the
approach}\label{limits-of-the-approach}}

The universality-class framework has limits worth flagging.

\textbf{Limit 1: Programmatic with a partial model audit.} The paper
compares four other research programs with OI's microscopic conditions
and audits candidate shared interface mechanisms. This is not an
enumeration of their observational classes. A1--A6 incompatibility in a
standard formulation does not rule out equality of a chosen observable
pair, and shared quantum or memory architecture does not establish it.
The physical constructions and explicit comparison maps remain the
central unfinished tasks.

\textbf{Limit 2: Depends on operator-algebra technology.} The algebraic
formalization adopted in §8.4 and used throughout §9 depends on
operator-algebra and CPTP-channel theory. These are well-developed but
place the framework in a specific mathematical setting. Frameworks that
do not naturally admit operator-algebra description (e.g., theories with
no clear Hilbert-space structure) may not fit the formalization without
modification.

\textbf{Limit 3: Depends on continuum / discrete bridge.} The
formalization assumes that substratum descriptions, regardless of
discreteness, yield visible-sector reduced laws admitting CPTP-channel
representation with operator-algebra structure --- representability is
universal for finite laws ({[}Main §3.4{]}); operational closure beyond
it is a model hypothesis, not a framework property. For OI's discrete
substratum and string theory's continuum substratum this is plausible
(both yield quantum-representable observable-level descriptions). For
substrata whose visible-sector observable structure is not of this form,
the framework may not apply.

\textbf{Limit 4: partial access and state inference have different
premises.} A chosen proper instantaneous readout is a restriction on the
observational carrier. Whether retained records still fail to identify a
state is a further model-relative question, not an unfalsifiable
universal fact about embedded observers. {[}Substratum Proposition
24.5{]} gives the exact known-model record-fibre criterion and a
reversible swap counterexample to permanent opacity. The framework's
predictive content uses specified dynamics, C4 readback, carrier/matter
assumptions and physical completion hypotheses. Those commitments can be
assessed independently; the interpretation of incomplete observation
does not derive them or supply the coherent measurement, Bell or gravity
bridges.

\hypertarget{cross-framework-audit-of-observational-structures}{%
\subsection{14. Cross-framework audit of observational
structures}\label{cross-framework-audit-of-observational-structures}}

This section develops the constructive content of recovery (v) ---
shared observational invariants --- by examining whether structural
features of OI's trace-out also follow from string theory's emergence
mechanisms. The framing is that observation is a structurally privileged
operation: OI's substratum and string theory's substrata are different,
but the \emph{act of observation} on each substratum produces
observational features that may be substratum-independent.

We proceed feature by feature. For each, we characterize the structural
feature in OI's framework, identify the analogous mechanism in
string-theoretic emergence, and assess whether the feature transports
across frameworks. Four feature classes are examined: Born-form quantum
probability structure, channel-level unitary dilation,
memory/non-Markovian marginal dynamics with divisibility tracked
separately, and commutant gauge-invariance.

The discussion is technical but exploratory. The aim is not to prove
cross-framework equivalence at the observational level --- that would
require substantial work beyond chat-track scope --- but to establish
whether the proposed shared invariants are structurally well-defined in
both frameworks. If they are, recovery (v) admits constructive
sharpening; if some don't transport, the framework's universality claims
must be refined.

\hypertarget{the-born-rule}{%
\subsubsection{14.1 The Born rule}\label{the-born-rule}}

\textbf{OI's framing.} At the finite observable-law layer, OI supplies
an exact fixed-basis Born-form representation rather than a
parameter-free derivation of the quadratic probability functional
{[}Main §3{]}. A finite reversible realization lifts to a permutation
unitary, and with \(Q_{\mathrm{fb}}\) defined by the dictionary
\(p=|U|^2\), tracing over the hidden register reproduces the visible
transition law exactly. The important scope distinction is that the
construction does \textbf{not select} the exponent: permutation-unitary
entries are \(0\) or \(1\), so \(|U|^p\) gives the same transition table
for every \(p>0\). For a general quantum representation the visible
density matrix \(\rho_V = \text{Tr}_H |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\) gives
\(p(a)=\text{Tr}(\Pi_a\rho_V)\) in the usual way; identifying that full
coherent observable/instrument structure simultaneously across all
interventions is part of the operational-lifting frontier. OI's proved
contribution at this layer is therefore the exact Born-form
representation of classical deterministic substrata, not an independent
selection theorem for why nature chooses the quadratic dictionary.

\textbf{String-theoretic analog.} String theory uses standard quantum
mechanics throughout --- in matrix-model formulations (BFSS, IKKT), in
worldsheet conformal field theory, and in the boundary CFT of AdS/CFT
correspondence. The Born rule is built in as a feature of QM. When an
observer in a string-theoretic context measures an observable, the
probability is given by the standard quantum-mechanical trace formula on
the boundary CFT or the worldsheet.

\textbf{Transport assessment.} The \textbf{Born-form representation
structure} transports straightforwardly: once a framework is represented
quantum mechanically and reduced by partial trace, subsystem
probabilities use the standard quadratic trace rule. The distinction is
that OI constructs an exact fixed-basis quantum representation starting
from a classical reversible substratum, whereas string theory assumes
the quantum formalism from the outset. This comparison should not be
read as an OI derivation of the exponent \(2\) itself; Main §3.1/§3.4
makes explicit that the permutation construction is exponent-blind and
that the dictionary's physical origin is a separate frontier item.

The shared-invariants reading is therefore representation-theoretic
rather than a new Born-rule uniqueness theorem. The quadratic Born rule
itself is empirically tested at the \(10^{-3}\)--\(10^{-4}\) level via
multi-path interferometry {[}Sinha2010, Söllner2012{]}, with ongoing
precision improvements through ultracold-atom platforms
{[}Kanthak2025{]} (further discussion in §15.5).

\hypertarget{channel-level-unitarity}{%
\subsubsection{14.2 Channel-level
unitarity}\label{channel-level-unitarity}}

\textbf{OI's framing.} At the finite cutoff, a permutation-unitary lift
of the bijection and the specified hidden preparation give a unitary
realization of the visible CPTP channel. Stinespring's theorem
separately supplies a prepared isometry for a full channel, with unitary
realizations in an appropriate ancillary setting. The visible channel
can be non-unitary even though its joint realization is unitary. This is
a dilation/realization statement, not recovery of a unique full hidden
unitary from the visible channel; the finite and separable uniqueness
results in §9 concern minimal prepared isometries.

\textbf{String-theoretic analog.} AdS/CFT bulk reconstruction has a
closely analogous structure. Boundary CFT operators are encoded in bulk
operators with redundancy, and the encoding is a quantum error
correcting code {[}HarlowBulkReconstruction2018,
AlmheiriDongHarlow2014{]}. Bulk reconstruction from boundary subregions
(``entanglement wedge reconstruction'') shows that bulk operators in the
entanglement wedge of a boundary subregion can be reconstructed from
operators on that subregion. The reconstruction is unitary at the level
of the full bulk-boundary system, with effective non-unitarity arising
when restricting to a subregion.

The structural parallel is direct: in AdS/CFT, the ``trace-out'' is
restriction to a boundary subregion, and the ``visible-sector channel''
is the effective dynamics on that subregion after tracing over the
complement. The bulk-boundary system as a whole is unitary; the
subregion-level channel is generally non-unitary; the channel admits a
unitary dilation given by the full bulk reconstruction.

\textbf{Transport assessment.} Channel-level unitarity transports across
frameworks. Both OI and AdS/CFT have the same structural feature: a CPTP
channel at the observable level lifting to unitary dynamics on a
dilation. The dilation in OI is the Stinespring lift to
\(\mathcal{H}_V \otimes \mathcal{H}_H\); in AdS/CFT, the dilation is the
full bulk-boundary system before restricting to a subregion.

The mechanisms are different: OI has an explicit permutation-unitary
realization at a finite cutoff with its chosen hidden state, while a
holographic construction uses its own encoding and subsystem
restrictions. Their common comparison is a channel/realization
architecture. Minimal Stinespring uniqueness for a full channel neither
identifies those physical constructions nor proves equality of their
restricted observable pairs.

\hypertarget{memory-non-markovianity-and-recurrence-scale-indivisibility}{%
\subsubsection{14.3 Memory, non-Markovianity, and recurrence-scale
indivisibility}\label{memory-non-markovianity-and-recurrence-scale-indivisibility}}

\textbf{OI's framing.} OI's C4 readback condition is first a statement
of \emph{conditional memory}: in general
\(P(a_3\mid a_2,a_1) \neq P(a_3\mid a_2)\) because hidden-sector
correlations retain information about visible history {[}Main §2.3,
§3.4{]}. That property is not identical to P-indivisibility. In Main's
divisibility sense, P-indivisibility means that for some pair of rooted
visible maps there is no stochastic propagator that divides the later
map through the earlier one. What OI proves for one fixed finite
reversible representative is more specific: a non-permutation rooted
map, which C4 supplies and every non-deterministic visible step also
supplies, together with finite recurrence returning the visible map to
the identity, gives an indivisibility witness somewhere in the
recurrence cycle, since a non-permutation stochastic map has no
stochastic inverse. Bell is a separate constraint: a response-complete
deterministic completion that is both measurement-independent and
ontically parameter-independent obeys \(|S_{\rm CHSH}|\le2\), so
Bell-violating OI composites retain measurement independence and
relinquish ontic parameter independence while preserving operational
no-signaling {[}Main §3.3{]}. Thus C4 memory is the temporal mechanism,
recurrence supplies an indivisibility witness that also holds without
accessible memory, and neither memory nor indivisibility is a Bell
selector.

\textbf{String-theoretic analog.} Holographic effective field theories
explicitly classify modes by their Markovianity. The sound scalar in
compressible relativistic fluids dual to AdS planar black holes is a
non-Markovian field with Markovianity index \(3-d\) for a
\(d\)-dimensional fluid. The holographic Schwinger-Keldysh formalism
{[}HolographicSK2024, HolographicNonHydro2024{]} derives effective field
theories that include non-Gaussian noise and non-Markovian dynamics as
features of the boundary effective description, arising from integrating
out bulk degrees of freedom. This supplies a close analog of the
\emph{memory/non-Markovian} part of OI's mechanism.

The structural parallel is therefore at the memory level: in both
frameworks, restricting attention to a subset of degrees of freedom can
produce history-dependent effective dynamics when the integrated-out
degrees of freedom retain correlations across time. Partial trace or
coarse-graining by itself does not force this outcome; a memoryless
hidden sector can produce a Markovian reduced law.

\textbf{Transport assessment.} The memory/non-Markovian mechanism
transports across frameworks. The stronger P-indivisibility property
does \textbf{not} follow merely from the cited holographic
non-Markovianity statements, and is not claimed here for string theory
without a separate divisibility analysis. OI's recurrence-scale witness
uses additional structure that the cross-framework analogy does not
automatically supply: one fixed finite reversible representative plus a
non-permutation rooted map, which C4 readback supplies.

This distinction is important for the Bell comparison as well.
Holographic non-Markovian effective theories provide an analog of
hidden-memory dynamics, not by themselves a proof of the standard
quantum Bell correlation set. In OI, a response-complete deterministic
completion that combines the pointwise graph cone with a
measurement-independent pre-setting ensemble is Bell-local and satisfies
\(|S_{\rm CHSH}|\le2\); quantum Bell violation therefore requires the
separately stated ontic-parameter-dependent branch, with operational
no-signaling retained. Hidden memory and stochastic indivisibility alone
do not supply that branch.

The memory mechanism has independent theorem-level corroboration.
{[}BrandnerMeyer2025{]} establishes a rigorous weak-memory regime for
autonomous linear evolution equations with hidden-degree-of-freedom
coupling, providing a controlled framework for the non-Markovian visible
dynamics OI invokes. {[}TomizukaTakeda2026{]} constructs a structurally
similar hidden-model decoherence picture independently, motivated by
classical--quantum dynamics in gravity. {[}Barandes2026{]}'s
stochastic-quantum theorem concerns indivisible stochastic processes and
unitary quantum systems; Main §3.4 retains that result for the
compressed form and interpretation under the translation hypothesis,
while keeping indivisibility distinct from conditional-memory
non-Markovianity and keeping fixed-basis representability internal. The
framework's central technical move therefore sits within an established
theorem-level neighborhood without identifying these different notions
by definition (§15.1 develops this in detail).

\hypertarget{commutant-gauge-invariance}{%
\subsubsection{14.4 Commutant
gauge-invariance}\label{commutant-gauge-invariance}}

\textbf{OI's framing.} The selected single-copy H-link/H-cust branch
uses the exact six-link cubic representation to constrain an equivariant
condensate. For generic distinct block values, its stabilizer in
\(\mathrm U(6)\) is
\(G_0=\mathrm U(3)\times\mathrm U(2)\times\mathrm U(1)\); the commuting
algebra of the cubic action itself is block-scalar. The physical local
gauge interpretation and further central reduction use the covariance,
quotient and charged-observer-action premises in {[}SM §4.6{]}.
Gauge-invariant observables lie in the commutant of the specified
physical gauge action, but tracing out an arbitrary hidden factor does
not by itself project every surviving operator into that commutant.

\textbf{String-theoretic analog.} Gauge invariance in string-theoretic
emergence has multiple sources, but the closest analog to OI's commutant
structure is the \emph{open-string sector} of D-brane stacks. A stack of
\(N\) D-branes has \(U(N)\) gauge symmetry generated by the open-string
excitations on the brane worldvolume; the commutant of the worldvolume
action gives the gauge group. Restricting to closed strings (graviton
sector) gives the singlets under the gauge group, analogous to OI's
trace-out preserving only commutant operators.

For AdS/CFT specifically, the boundary CFT has a global symmetry that
becomes a bulk gauge symmetry under the duality. Boundary operators
charged under the global symmetry correspond to bulk fields charged
under the gauge symmetry; gauge-invariant bulk physics corresponds to
singlet boundary operators. The commutant structure is preserved across
the duality.

\textbf{Transport assessment.} The pattern is a comparison of symmetry
actions and their invariant observable algebras. Selected OI and string
constructions can reach the same local SM factors through different
assumptions. The OI route uses the conditional condensate stabilizer and
physical reduction; compactification constructions use their own gauge
data. Matching the factors does not identify the full observable pairs
or their global gauge actions.

The shared structure is the use of symmetry-invariant observables once a
physical symmetry action has been specified. The mechanism selecting
that action and its group is model-dependent. OI's exact cubic link
decomposition is one input to its conditional physical construction,
while a string compactification supplies different microscopic input.

This partial transport supports comparison of invariant-observable
structures under stated symmetry hypotheses. It does not prove that
every partial trace preserves only gauge singlets, or that cubic
symmetry alone selects the physical SM action. Exact class membership
still requires the restricted algebra/channel map.

indivisible, and which gauge structures the substratum admits. OI and
string theory can intersect on several coordinates while reaching them
through different mechanisms.

\hypertarget{implications-for-the-oi-string-relationship}{%
\subsubsection{14.6 Implications for the OI-string
relationship}\label{implications-for-the-oi-string-relationship}}

The cross-framework analysis of §14.1--14.5 establishes that recovery
(v) --- shared observational structure --- has substantive content, but
it also fixes the scope of that claim. The commonality is strongest at
the representation/dilation interface and becomes conditional once
memory, divisibility, or substratum symmetry is involved.

The microscopic formulations audited in §6 are structurally distinct. At
the observational interface they share quantum representation and
dilation architecture, and they can exhibit analogous hidden-memory
mechanisms. OI's recurrence-scale P-indivisibility uses C4 together with
a fixed finite reversible realization; no corresponding string-theoretic
theorem is inferred from holographic non-Markovianity. These findings
motivate comparison without deciding Definition 9.3 equivalence.

This intersection is non-trivial but should not be overstated. A
framework that already uses quantum states and partial trace inherits
the standard quantum probability rule for its subsystem and the usual
dilation architecture. It does \textbf{not} automatically inherit
non-Markovianity, P-indivisibility, Bell causal locality, or OI's
specific commutant output. Those require additional dynamical,
statistical, or symmetry structure.

The constructive content of recovery (v) is therefore: \textbf{the
restriction/partial-trace interface is a locus of shared architecture,
not by itself a derivation of every observational feature audited here}.
This connects to OI's ``observation occurs'' principle while preserving
Main's distinction between universal representation, C4 memory,
recurrence-scale indivisibility, and the still-conditional
operational-local lift.

What does not transport --- the specific gauge group, the specific
particle content, the specific predictions --- is what distinguishes
substratum descriptions empirically. OI's predictions for the Cabibbo
angle and Koide ratio are features of OI's specific substratum-symmetry
derivation; string theory's compactifications would need to reproduce
these as features of specific compactification choices. The two
frameworks agree on the \emph{kind} of physics observation produces;
they differ on the \emph{specific content}.

This is a more refined picture than the §7.1 framing of ``OI and string
theory as independent programs.'' They are independent at the substratum
level, share structural features at the partial-trace level, and may or
may not converge at the specific-content level depending on which
compactifications string theory can realize as SM-reproducing. The
recovery (v) analysis identifies precisely where the intersection lives:
at the level of partial-trace structural features, not above and not
below.

\hypertarget{loop-quantum-gravity-four-feature-audit}{%
\subsubsection{14.7 Loop quantum gravity: four-feature
audit}\label{loop-quantum-gravity-four-feature-audit}}

The §14.1--14.6 audit was conducted for OI vs string theory. The same
four features admit similar audit for other quantum-gravity /
unification programs identified in §13.1.4. This section and the next
two perform the audit for loop quantum gravity, causal sets, and
asymptotic safety. The aim is the cross-program characterization of
where the partial-trace observational features hold.

\textbf{Born rule.} Loop quantum gravity (LQG) {[}RovelliSmolin1990,
Thiemann2007{]} uses standard quantum mechanics throughout: spin-network
states form a Hilbert space with inner products, observables are
operators on this Hilbert space, and probabilities are computed via the
standard Born rule on spin-network projection operators. The Born rule
transports without modification.

\textbf{Channel-level unitarity.} LQG dynamics is implemented through
spin-foam path integrals {[}EPRL2008, FreidelKrasnov2008{]} that produce
transition amplitudes between spin-network states. The spin-foam
amplitude is unitary at the level of the full path integral; effective
dynamics on visible-sector observables (e.g., on smeared geometric
observables averaging over Planck-scale spin-network structure) is CPTP,
with the unitarity recovered at the spin-foam level via
Stinespring-style dilation. Channel-level unitarity transports, with the
dilation living in the spin-foam path integral rather than the OI
substratum bijection.

\textbf{Memory / divisibility.} Spin-foam dynamics on visible-sector
observables, after integrating over hidden internal-edge data, plausibly
produces non-Markovian effective dynamics. The mechanism is structurally
similar to OI's trace-out: the visible-sector marginal can carry memory
of substratum-level dynamics through correlations established in earlier
spin-foam evolution. This supports a plausible memory/non-Markovian
analog, but it does not by itself establish P-indivisibility in Main's
stochastic-propagator sense. Both the existence of the effective memory
law and any stronger divisibility failure require technical work; this
is the most underdeveloped of the four feature classes for LQG.

\textbf{Commutant gauge-invariance.} LQG uses its connection-variable
gauge symmetry and diffeomorphism constraints to define physical
invariant observables. This can be compared with OI's selected physical
gauge action, whose local SM factors require the H-link/H-cust
condensate-stabilizer construction and further reduction. Matching a
symmetry-restriction pattern does not identify the specific gauge action
or observable pair. The additional matter-sector construction required
to reach the SM target remains a separate part of the LQG comparison.

\textbf{LQG transport summary.} The quantum-probability formalism,
channel-level unitary-dilation pattern, and commutant gauge-invariance
pattern are present in the comparison. The memory/divisibility feature
is only plausible at the memory level; P-indivisibility remains open.
LQG therefore shares several interface-level structures with OI without
yet matching OI's recurrence-scale indivisibility result.

\hypertarget{causal-sets-four-feature-audit}{%
\subsubsection{14.8 Causal sets: four-feature
audit}\label{causal-sets-four-feature-audit}}

Causal set theory {[}BombelliEtAl1987, Sorkin2003{]} takes the
substratum to be a discrete, locally finite partially ordered set with
sequential growth dynamics. The structural setup differs from OI in that
the substratum dynamics is fundamentally stochastic (sequential growth
via classical or quantum sequential growth) rather than deterministic.

\textbf{Born rule.} Classical sequential growth (CSG)
{[}RideoutSorkin1999{]} is fundamentally classical-stochastic, not
quantum. The Born rule does not directly arise. Quantum sequential
growth (QSG) {[}DowkerSurya2020{]} adapts the framework to produce
quantum probabilities through quantum measure theory. Whether QSG
produces the standard Born rule or a generalization (with potentially
observable higher-order interference terms) is an open technical
question. Transport: open / uncertain. The current status is that the
framework is structurally compatible with the Born rule but does not
exhibit it as an exact internal representation identity, as OI's
finite-horizon equivalence does (\(S\iff D\iff Q_{\mathrm{fb}}\),
{[}Main §3.4{]}). The comparison is about exhibiting the Born form, not
about deriving it: neither framework selects the quadratic exponent,
which in OI is part of the definition of \(Q_{\mathrm{fb}}\) rather than
a derived consequence ({[}Main §3.1{]}).

\textbf{Channel-level unitarity.} CSG sequential growth lacks a clean
unitary lift at the substratum level --- the dynamics is fundamentally
stochastic. QSG's quantum-measure formulation can be reformulated
through a CPTP channel structure with appropriate dilation, but the
Stinespring dilation is structurally different from OI's (the dilation
acts on a different Hilbert space, with different microcausality
properties). Transport: structurally yes, but the dilation mechanism
differs.

\textbf{Memory / divisibility.} Causal-set coarse graining can plausibly
produce history-dependent effective dynamics when fine-grained causal
structure retains correlations. That is structurally similar to OI's
hidden-memory mechanism. The comparison supports a plausible
memory/non-Markovian analog; it does not establish P-indivisibility
without a separate divisibility analysis.

\textbf{Commutant gauge-invariance.} Causal sets are intrinsically
diffeomorphism-invariant: the causal-order structure determines the
manifold up to volume measure, and any continuous diffeomorphism
preserves the order. Observable physics is determined by order-invariant
quantities. The commutant gauge-invariance pattern transports, but the
gauge group is the trivial diffeomorphism group rather than a Yang-Mills
structure. Causal sets must reach SM gauge structure through additional
matter content, similar to LQG.

\textbf{Causal sets transport summary.} The commutant/order-invariance
pattern transports most directly. Quantum-probability and
unitary-dilation structure depend on the QSG formulation, while memory
is plausible and P-indivisibility remains unaudited. Causal sets are
therefore related at several structural levels but do not presently
match the full OI feature vector.

\hypertarget{asymptotic-safety-four-feature-audit}{%
\subsubsection{14.9 Asymptotic safety: four-feature
audit}\label{asymptotic-safety-four-feature-audit}}

Asymptotic safety {[}Weinberg1979, Reuter1998, ReuterSaueressig2019{]}
proposes that quantum gravity is non-perturbatively renormalizable
through a non-trivial UV fixed point of the renormalization group. The
framework operates within standard QFT machinery with non-perturbative
RG analysis.

\textbf{Born rule.} Standard QFT machinery is used throughout; the Born
rule is built in via the standard quantum-mechanical formalism.
Transport: yes, without modification.

\textbf{Channel-level unitarity.} Standard QFT unitarity is inherited at
the level of the asymptotic-safety effective action. The visible-sector
effective dynamics --- derived by integrating out high-momentum modes in
the RG flow --- is CPTP, with the dilation living at higher RG scales.
Channel-level unitarity transports.

\textbf{Memory / divisibility.} Integrating over high-momentum modes in
an RG flow can generate non-local-in-time or memory-bearing effective
interactions. This gives a strong mechanism-level analog of OI's
visible-hidden memory picture, with the split operating in momentum
space rather than the spatial substratum. The existence of such
non-Markovian effective structure does not by itself certify
P-indivisibility in Main's divisibility sense; that stronger property
would require a separate propagator analysis.

\textbf{Commutant gauge-invariance.} Asymptotic safety preserves
gauge-invariance of the effective action under the RG flow via Ward
identities. Observable physics is determined by gauge-invariant
effective operators. The commutant gauge-invariance pattern transports;
the specific gauge group is whatever Yang-Mills structure is included in
the matter content (typically the SM gauge group, in matter-included
formulations).

\textbf{Asymptotic safety transport summary.} Standard quantum
probability and unitarity are inherited from the QFT framework, the RG
split supplies a strong memory-mechanism analog, and gauge-invariance is
preserved. The comparison does not establish P-indivisibility from
non-Markovianity alone. Mechanistically this remains one of the closest
cross-program parallels to OI, but not an all-four-feature theorem.

and admit unitary dilation; several programs also exhibit or plausibly
generate memory-bearing effective dynamics when degrees of freedom are
integrated out. The stronger P-indivisibility statement does not follow
from non-Markovianity alone and is proved in OI here only at recurrence
scale from C4 plus a fixed finite reversible representative. Thus
§15.3's universality claim is an architectural transport claim, not a
theorem that partial trace forces every feature in the table. For OI
specifically the split is sharper than ``integrating out generates
memory'': partial trace alone does not force memory --- the memory-free
reversible witnesses are exactly the visible-decoupled maps, whose
visible dynamics carries no hidden dependence. But in the uniform
full-product finite reversible setting, the \(T\)-level form of (C1) is
a non-permutation one-step visible matrix, and then memory follows in
general rather than only by census: {[}Main §3.4{]}'s
non-permutation-witness lemma gives P-indivisibility at recurrence
scale, while every first-order Markov process is P-divisible.
Equivalently, the visible factor of a finite deterministic bijection has
zero asymptotic entropy rate (its entire history is fixed by one of
finitely many initial substratum states), whereas a stationary
first-order Markov chain with non-permutation \(T\) has
\(H(X_{t+1}\mid X_t)>0\). That second route needs less than uniformity
and less than \(|V|=2\): it needs a \(\varphi\)-invariant prior ---
which makes the visible process stationary --- giving positive weight to
some visible state whose \(T\)-row is non-degenerate.
Uniform-on-the-full-product is one such prior, so the ensemble censused
above is a special case, and the theorem covers the non-uniform
invariant priors that {[}Main §3.4{]}'s arbitrary-kernel
(response-table) extension admits (check 10,
\texttt{equivalence\_recovery\_probes.py}, with a prior supported on a
visible-decoupled block as the control that correctly does not fire).
The sharp form is worth stating on its own: the visible factor of a
finite reversible substratum has zero entropy rate under every invariant
prior, so whatever randomness an embedded observer records is bounded by
\(\log|\mathcal{C}_V \times \mathcal{C}_H|\) and is exhausted at
recurrence scale --- the same scale at which the indivisibility witness
appears, and for the same reason. The exhaustive checks are therefore
regressions of that theorem, not its proof: at \(|V|=2\) and
\(|H|=2,3,4\), respectively, all \(16/16\), \(648/648\), and
\(39{,}168/39{,}168\) \(T\)-level-C1 bijections carry visible memory;
the \(|H|=4\) complement consists of exactly \(1{,}152=2(4!)^2\)
visible-decoupled bijections (checks 7--9,
\texttt{equivalence\_recovery\_probes.py}). The \(648\) denominator
coincides with {[}Main §3.1{]}'s entanglement-breaking census but the
diagnostic is different: that census classifies channels as
measure-and-prepare, while this one classifies visible processes as
memory-bearing, and the two must not be merged.

\emph{Symmetry-restriction comparisons require specified actions.}
Several audited models use invariant observables, but their physical
gauge constructions differ. The OI route is the conditional
condensate-stabilizer/central-reduction branch; other programs use their
own compactification or matter-sector assumptions. Shared local factors
are a target match, not evidence of one exact observable class without
the associated algebra map.

\emph{Asymptotic safety has the closest mechanism-level similarity to
OI}. Its visible-hidden split via momentum-space RG flow is structurally
closest to OI's spatial-substratum trace-out. Both produce non-Markovian
visible dynamics through the same architectural pattern (unitary on the
dilation, CPTP on visible). This suggests asymptotic safety and OI may
be more closely related than either is to string theory or LQG, and may
share more universality-class structure.

The cross-program audit refines §13.1.4's per-condition A1--A6 analysis
by identifying which observational structures recur even where the
structural conditions differ. The evidence supports a shared
\emph{interface architecture} --- coarse graining/restriction,
quantum-probability form where quantum formalism is present, dilation
structure, and possible hidden-memory effects --- but not a blanket
derivation of P-indivisibility or of the Born exponent from A1, A3, A4,
A6 plus the SM target. Those stronger properties must be established by
the program-specific dynamics. Section 14 is therefore a transport
audit, not an independent derivation theorem.

\hypertarget{theoretical-and-empirical-corroboration}{%
\subsection{15. Theoretical and empirical
corroboration}\label{theoretical-and-empirical-corroboration}}

The framework's claims are of mixed types. Some are directly empirical
(the Born rule, channel-level unitarity, non-Markovian marginal
observation) and admit precision experimental tests. Others are
structural --- the partial-trace mechanism, the algebra-channel
formalism, the universality-class hypothesis --- and are corroborated
through theorem-level results in independent literatures and through
analog constructions in adjacent fields. This section collects the most
consequential corroborations, organized by claim type. The ordering
reflects strength of support: §15.1 documents the strongest external
corroboration (theorem-level results for the framework's central
technical mechanism); §15.5 acknowledges what remains under-supported.

\hypertarget{the-partial-trace-mechanism}{%
\subsubsection{15.1 The partial-trace
mechanism}\label{the-partial-trace-mechanism}}

The framework's central technical move --- that visible-sector dynamics
under trace-out of a hidden sector is generically non-Markovian and
admits a unitary quantum description --- has theorem-level corroboration
in three independent recent results.

\textbf{Stochastic-quantum correspondence at theorem level.} Barandes
{[}Barandes2026{]}, building on {[}Barandes2023, Barandes2025{]},
establishes a precise correspondence theorem: every indivisible
stochastic process on a configuration space corresponds to a unitarily
evolving quantum system, and conversely, every quantum system can be
represented as such a process. The theorem provides a first-principles
explanation for why quantum theory uses complex Hilbert spaces,
linear-unitary evolution, and the Born rule. This is the machinery
{[}Main §3.4{]} retains --- for the compressed form and its
interpretation under the translation hypothesis, the fixed-basis
representation's existence internal --- rather than the gate from the
deterministic substratum to the emergent description, established at
theorem-level rigor in the foundations literature independent of the OI
framework's specific content. {[}Main{]}'s finite-horizon
process-dilation theorem, with its response-table extension, is
consistent with the same correspondence: for each finite horizon \(K\),
every stochastic process on a finite configuration space, P-divisible or
not, is reproduced on that horizon by the visible marginal of a finite
deterministic bijection, depending on \(K\), from a hidden prior
independent of the visible initial state ({[}Main §3.4{]}); no single
finite bijection reproduces a nonperiodic process exactly at all times.

\textbf{Weak-memory regime for hidden-degree-of-freedom dynamics.} Meyer
and Brandner {[}BrandnerMeyer2025{]}, following {[}Brandner2025a{]},
prove that for autonomous linear evolution equations describing systems
coupled to inaccessible degrees of freedom, integrating out those
degrees of freedom yields well-defined non-Markovian visible dynamics in
a weak-memory regime, with explicit error bounds and a convergent
perturbation scheme. The setting is general open quantum systems with no
stake in OI being right, and the conclusion is that visible-sector
dynamics with hidden-sector memory is a mathematically clean
construction with a rigorous regime of validity. The framework's C2
persistence condition --- in its slow-bath realization --- and the
resulting non-Markovian visible dynamics fall within this regime, so the
framework's central technical move sits inside an established theorem.

\textbf{Hidden-model decoherence producing classical--quantum dynamics.}
Tomizuka and Takeda {[}TomizukaTakeda2026{]} construct an explicit
hidden model in which a mediator is coupled to additional unobserved
environmental degrees of freedom, and derive the resulting reduced
dynamics by tracing them out. The reduced dynamics is generically
non-Markovian, reflecting environmental memory effects. Their motivation
is the question of whether classical--quantum dynamics in gravity can
arise as effective descriptions of fully quantum systems; the
construction is structurally identical to OI's mechanism
(visible--hidden partition, unitary on the dilation, trace-out producing
non-Markovian visible dynamics) developed independently for a different
physics question. The structural identity is independent corroboration
that the OI mechanism is the standard way the open-systems literature
now thinks about hidden-degree-of-freedom dynamics.

\textbf{Experimental implementation in quantum metrology.} The
hidden-Markov-model framework underlying the partial-trace mechanism has
direct experimental implementation in quantum metrology
{[}QECNonMarkovian2025{]}: the model is Markovian on the joint system +
environment and non-Markovian on the visible-sector reduced state, with
correlations stored in the environment and read back producing the
framework's signature non-Markovian behavior. The framework's mechanism
is therefore not only mathematically rigorous but realizable in current
experimental quantum-information platforms.

\hypertarget{the-algebra-channel-formalism}{%
\subsubsection{15.2 The algebra-channel
formalism}\label{the-algebra-channel-formalism}}

The §8.3 / §9.1 algebra-channel formalization adopted in this paper
draws on standard machinery from algebraic quantum field theory (AQFT).
The formalism's reliability and expressive power are established in the
AQFT literature.

\textbf{AQFT canonical references.} The algebraic-QFT literature cited
in {[}BrunettiFredenhagen2024{]} provides the local-algebra, state and
covariance framework used as an EFT target in §10.4. Type-III local
factors {[}Yngvason2005{]} concern appropriate relativistic
infinite-system representations. OI's exact finite-cutoff construction
has finite matrix algebras; obtaining a local AQFT net, its type and its
channel invariance requires the physical limit described there. The
curved-spacetime algebraic literature {[}LabuschagneMajewski2025{]}
supplies relevant mathematical tools, rather than completing that
construction.

\textbf{Stinespring dilation as universal device.} Stinespring's theorem
supplies a prepared isometry for the full channel, with unitary
realizations available in an appropriate ancillary setting. Section 9
distinguishes that isometry from a full unitary and separates
finite-dimensional kernel uniqueness (Propositions 9.7a--9.7b) from the
separable written proof (Proposition 9.7c). OI's uniform mixed hidden
reference is a unitary realization rather than a minimal pure-reference
dilation. These full-channel results do not identify arbitrary
restricted-algebra observers' ambient channels or their multi-time
generating dynamics. The channel-learning literature
{[}RandomStinespring2025{]} provides comparison tools under its own
operational assumptions, not the missing cross-framework intertwiner.

\hypertarget{structural-realism-in-operational-and-dual-theory-contexts}{%
\subsubsection{15.3 Structural realism in operational and dual-theory
contexts}\label{structural-realism-in-operational-and-dual-theory-contexts}}

The hierarchical structural-realism position developed in §2 and §12 is
supported by independent work in philosophy of physics that arrives at
compatible positions through different routes.

\textbf{Operational theories as structural realism.} Adlam
{[}Adlam2022{]} argues that operational axiomatizations of quantum
mechanics (Hardy {[}Hardy2001{]}, Barrett {[}Barrett2007{]}, and the
broader Mackey {[}Mackey1963{]} tradition) can be interpreted as a novel
form of structural realism: probing the modal structure of possible
operational theories, with quantum mechanics as a specific point in this
space. The view aligns with Structure's two-dimensional hierarchy
(observation × gauge): the observational axis corresponds to Adlam's
modal-structure-of-operational-theories framing, and the gauge axis
corresponds to Structure's parallel articulation of the
gauge-equivalence levels at which structural commitment lives. The
framework's structural-realism commitment is therefore situated within
an active research program in philosophy of physics, not a novel
position in isolation. Operational reconstruction theorems characterize
quantum theory under their own axiom sets; {[}Main §3.2{]} distinguishes
those hypotheses from the exact finite-law representation and the
additional coherent operational lift. They do not establish Theorem 23's
missing inverse selector or a complete substratum gauge class.

\textbf{Common-core realism in QM-string dualities.} Dawid and Franzmann
{[}DawidFranzmann2025{]} develop a structural-realism account
specifically for dualities in quantum mechanics and string theory,
accommodating ontological pluralism while preserving structural realism
through what they call ``common core realism'' --- the proposal that
invariant structure across dual models is the proper target of realist
commitment. This is precisely the conceptual move §12.1 makes in the
universality-class framing: the realist commitment is to structure
invariant across substratum descriptions, with specific substratum
content being framework-relative. Their work cites parallel programs by
Matsubara--Johansson, Rickles, Huggett, and De Haro--Butterfield that
converge on similar positions. The universality-class proposal of §9 can
be read as one technical articulation of common-core realism specialized
to embedded-observer systems.

\hypertarget{universality-classes-in-adjacent-literatures}{%
\subsubsection{15.4 Universality classes in adjacent
literatures}\label{universality-classes-in-adjacent-literatures}}

The universality-class framework of §9 is not unique to
embedded-observer systems; analogous structures appear in dissipative
quantum systems and many-body operator dynamics, where they are working
analytical tools rather than conjectural propositions.

\textbf{Emergent universality classes in dissipative quantum systems.}
Zhou et al.~{[}ZhouEtAl2025{]} develop an effective field theory
description for the universal dynamics of dissipative quantum systems
with dipole moment conservation, identifying a strongly interacting
non-equilibrium fixed point that governs equal-time phase fluctuations.
The work is independent of OI but uses the same conceptual apparatus ---
universality classes characterized by specific structural features
(dipole conservation in their case; partial-trace observational features
in OI's), with framework-specific content (subdiffusive vs diffusive
transport in their case; specific gauge structure in OI's). The
existence of working analyses of this kind in active research programs
is corroboration that the universality-class framework is a productive
analytical tool, not merely a programmatic gesture.

\textbf{Random-matrix universality from fast-slow decomposition.} Lunt
et al.~{[}Lunt2025{]} prove the emergence of a universal random-matrix
description of fast-mode dynamics in many-body quantum systems, via the
memory-function formalism with a careful split into fast and slow modes.
The fast-mode universality is direct analog to OI's partial-trace
structure: hidden-sector (fast) dynamics produce universal observational
features at the visible-sector (slow) level, independent of the specific
microscopic content. The result is theorem-level for the random-matrix
universality class; the same architecture (fast-slow decomposition
producing universality at the slow-mode level) is the architecture OI's
framework uses to produce SM-reproducing universality at the
visible-sector level.

\textbf{Holographic Schwinger-Keldysh as analog of OI's hidden memory.}
Recent work on holographic Schwinger-Keldysh contours
{[}AmmonEtAl2025{]}, extending earlier work {[}HolographicSK2024{]},
establishes that the AdS/CFT correspondence prescribes a specific
Schwinger-Keldysh contour structure for real-time correlation functions,
with non-trivial out-of-time-order four-point functions exploring the
full four-fold geometry. §14.3 identifies this as a holographic analog
of OI's hidden-memory (non-Markovian) mechanism; it does not establish
P-indivisibility, which §14.3 leaves unestablished cross-framework. The
recent literature provides concrete prescriptions making the
memory-level analog precise, supporting the §14.3 transport claim at
that level.

\hypertarget{empirical-anchors}{%
\subsubsection{15.5 Empirical anchors}\label{empirical-anchors}}

Direct experimental confirmation applies to the partial-trace-level
features identified in §14 (Born rule, channel-level unitarity,
non-Markovian marginal) and to the framework's specific predictions
developed in {[}SM{]}, {[}GR{]}, and {[}Juno{]}. The following are the
most recent and precise confirmations.

\textbf{Born rule precision.} The cleanest test of higher-order
interference is the multi-path interferometry experiment of
{[}Sinha2010{]}, which bounded the magnitude of three-path interference
to less than \(10^{-2}\) of the expected two-path interference, ruling
out third- and higher-order interference in optical regimes. The bound
was tightened to \(10^{-3}\)--\(10^{-4}\) by {[}Söllner2012{]} with
detector-nonlinearity calibration. Recent proposals using ultracold-atom
interferometry {[}Kanthak2025{]} aim for further precision via
Bose--Einstein condensate platforms that avoid systematic errors from
material slits. The accumulated bound is consistent with the Born rule
as a partial-trace-level invariant.

\textbf{Non-Markovian dynamics in physical systems.} Direct experimental
observation of non-Markovian visible dynamics from hidden slow degrees
of freedom: {[}Mehl2012{]} in colloidal systems and
{[}Groeblacher2015{]} in macroscopic micromechanical oscillators. These
confirm that the trace-out mechanism is realized in physical systems and
that the resulting non-Markovian behavior is operationally accessible.
Recent experimental implementations in metrology
{[}QECNonMarkovian2025{]} demonstrate the same mechanism in
quantum-information platforms.

\textbf{Cosmological tests of the framework's GR-emergence cluster.}
{[}DESI\_DR2\_2025{]} reports rejection of the cosmological constant at
2.8σ--4.2σ depending on the supernovae dataset, with potential phantom
crossing at \(z \approx 0.5\) and a 2σ+ detection of nonzero neutrino
masses in extended-parameter cosmology. The framework's {[}GR §7{]}
carries dark energy in Type II running-vacuum form --- the functional
form the local expansion shared by every smooth model, the content the
Type II channel and the magnitude; the {[}Bertini2025{]} direct fit to
DESI DR2 + DES-Y5 + Planck 2018 reports
\(\nu = -(2.5 \pm 1.3) \times 10^{-4}\), consistent with zero at 2σ; the
framework's expected magnitude \(|\nu| \sim 10^{-32}\) (conditional on
the Type II reading) is itself indistinguishable from zero at this
precision, so the two are consistent. DESI Year 5 results, expected
within the publication horizon, will be discriminating.

\textbf{Neutrinoless double-beta decay.} {[}LEGEND2024{]} reports
LEGEND-200 sensitivity \(T_{1/2} > 2.8 \times 10^{26}\) yr at 90\% CL on
\(0\nu\beta\beta\) in \(^{76}\)Ge (combined with GERDA + Majorana
Demonstrator). The framework's {[}SM §8.4{]} predicts Majorana
neutrinos, so \(0\nu\beta\beta\) should be observed; LEGEND-1000 is
designed to reach the inverse-ordering range. A positive observation
would confirm the framework's neutrino-sector prediction; a null result
at LEGEND-1000 sensitivity would constrain the framework. The swampland
literature {[}IbañezMartinLozanoValenzuela2017{]} independently
constrains neutrino-sector parameters via the Weak Gravity Conjecture,
providing a cross-validation pathway: the framework's normal-ordering
Majorana prediction sits within the WGC-allowed region.

\textbf{Black-hole entropy.} GW250114 (LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA, September 2025)
confirmed Hawking's area theorem at \(99.999\%\) confidence ---
classical horizon dynamics the framework preserves; the \(1/4\)
coefficient of {[}GR §5{]}, conditional on H-slope with the horizon and
frame conditions, has no direct empirical test. The observation is a
non-discriminating consistency for the cosmological-horizon application
of the partial-trace mechanism.

\hypertarget{what-remains-under-supported}{%
\subsubsection{15.6 What remains
under-supported}\label{what-remains-under-supported}}

Three classes of claims remain without independent support and would
benefit from focused future work.

\textbf{The cross-framework class-membership proposal.} Proposition 9.5
establishes a restricted positive inclusion for two channel-preserving
transformations, and Definition 9.4 supplies the precise observational
equivalence. Whether particular non-OI microscopic models occupy the
same class, or how that class compares with the full proposed
\(\mathcal G_{\rm sub}\) quotient, remains open. The analogous
literature in §15.4 motivates the program but supplies neither the
physical OI EFT pair nor the required comparison map. Completing §10.4
and the §13.1.1 construction is the concrete route to that test.

\textbf{Per-condition A1--A6 audit for LQG, causal sets, and asymptotic
safety} (§13.1.4). Structure's analysis is the only cross-program
per-condition comparison of its kind. Independent corroboration would
require another author independently performing the analysis and
arriving at compatible conclusions. The framework provides specific
testable claims --- e.g., A2 (determinism) is the most discriminating
condition across candidate programs --- that can be checked against each
program's foundational documents.

\textbf{Observer-admission distinction} (§9.6). The structural
distinction between observer-admitting and observer-lacking substrata is
novel; the closest concept in the literature is anthropic selection
(Vilenkin, Tegmark), which is statistical/probabilistic rather than
structural. The distinction is not directly testable as stated; its
empirical content lies in the predictions it underwrites within the
SM-reproducing observer-admitting class, which are tested through the
{[}SM §7{]} prediction set.
