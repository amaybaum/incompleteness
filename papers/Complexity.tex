\hypertarget{abstract}{%
\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}}

The Observational Incompleteness (OI) framework develops a conditional
lattice/gauge construction {[}1, 2{]}. Within A4's assumed
periodic-lattice class, E5--E7 select \(d=3\) subject to the continuum
conditions of {[}2, §3.1--§3.2{]}; identifying the geometric link space
with the physical carrier requires H-link, with H-spin' and H-χ'
entering the generation and chirality readings. This paper explores
consequences for orbital structure, the periodic table, bonding
geometry, scale hierarchies and chemistry, conditional on the stated
spin, statistics, atomic and matter assumptions. Three generations
permit a physical CKM phase, whose value is a solution-specific input; a
mass hierarchy also requires the stated Yukawa and taste-breaking
assumptions. Chiral SU(2), conditional on H-χ' {[}2, §4.8{]}, supplies a
parity-violating interaction whose molecular energy difference and
preferred sign require the stated molecular and observed weak-sector
inputs. An illustrative four-parameter estimate uses independent
log-uniform weights over the stated ranges.

The nominal sequence space for peptides of length 50 is \(20^{50}\).
Autocatalytic-set models require co-present species, a reaction network
and a food set. The available chemistry must also supply a template
copier for the replication scenario. In an existing template replicator,
a sequence can supply a persistent record for copying. Given net growth
and heritable differences in reproductive success, Darwinian evolution
can occur. The companion paper {[}38{]} examines conditional routes from
evolution to artificial computation and gives the assumptions of the BQP
model.

The finite-horizon readback result applies when the chosen observable
has a next-step dependence on history at fixed present state. Enzyme
turnover and engineered quantum devices motivate tests with that
diagnostic. For example, protein scaffold motion can be slow compared
with electronic transitions. With the retention model specified,
\(\tau_B/\tau_S\) estimates a window for possible readback. The
amplitude, observability and utility of any return depend on the
coupling and the experiment.

\hypertarget{introduction}{%
\subsection{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}}

The fine-tuning problem in physics rests on a counterfactual: if the
laws of physics or the values of fundamental constants were different,
the universe would not permit complex structures, and we would not exist
to observe it. The anthropic principle elevates this observation into an
explanatory principle --- our existence selects the laws from an
ensemble of possibilities.

The OI framework {[}1, 2{]} challenges the premise. The companion papers
prove that an embedded observer in a finite deterministic system with
conditions C1--C4 (coupling, record persistence, sufficient capacity,
history readback) has a visible-sector description with positive
conditional memory at an accessible order, and that this description,
like every finite observable law, admits an exact fixed-basis unitary
representation {[}1{]}, and that, under the hypotheses stated there, the
dynamics whose form the framework's additional structural requirements
fix up to one coefficient determines the Standard Model's complete
structure {[}2{]}. If the laws are derived rather than contingent, the
largest source of anthropic variation --- the structure of the laws
themselves --- is eliminated.

This paper traces the consequences. §2 establishes the structural chain
from (S, φ) to the full preconditions for organic chemistry --- orbital
structure, the periodic table, carbon's bonding geometry, the
nuclear-atomic scale hierarchy given the coupling values and masses,
water's solvent properties, and the conditions for a thermal window for
dynamic chemistry. §3 estimates the viable fraction of the parameter
space using existing literature. §4 traces the chirality chain from the
electroweak sector to biological homochirality, its first link
conditional on the framework's open hypothesis H-χ' {[}2, §4.8{]}. §5
extends the chain through autocatalytic networks, template replication,
the origin of life (heredity in a template replicator as molecular
C1--C4 memory), and Darwinian evolution --- closing with a
forward-pointer to the companion paper {[}38{]} that continues the chain
through general intelligence, AI, and the BQP characterization for
embedded observers. §6 addresses the existence question. §7 applies
C1--C4 to biological systems, predicting non-Markovian enzyme kinetics
with implications for drug design. §8 identifies where OI corrections
reach engineering relevance --- quantum computing, quantum sensing, and
precision metrology --- and proposes three engineering frontiers:
engineered partitions (the bath as programmable memory), non-Markovian
quantum computation (P-indivisible gate sequences), and quantum
materials with designed C1--C4 architectures.

\hypertarget{the-structural-chain}{%
\subsection{2. The Structural Chain}\label{the-structural-chain}}

\hypertarget{from-s-ux3c6-to-orbitals}{%
\subsubsection{2.1 From (S, φ) to
orbitals}\label{from-s-ux3c6-to-orbitals}}

Within A4's assumed periodic-lattice class, the physical filters E5--E7
select \(d = 3\) subject to the continuum conditions {[}2,
§3.1--§3.2{]}. Integer rank is an input of A4; renormalizability of the
specified Yang--Mills coupling is a downstream consequence. In three
spatial dimensions, the rotation group is SO(3), whose irreducible
representations are labeled by angular momentum quantum number
\(l = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\) with \((2l+1)\) states each. These are the
atomic orbitals: s (\(l = 0\), spherical, 1 orientation), p (\(l = 1\),
dumbbell, 3 orientations), d (\(l = 2\), 5 orientations), f (\(l = 3\),
7 orientations).

On this selected three-dimensional continuum branch, rotational symmetry
supplies the SO(3) orbital algebra. In \(d=2\), angular momentum has a
single component and there is no three-dimensional bonding geometry. For
an unregularized point-Coulomb potential \(V(r)\sim-r^{2-d}\) with
\(d>4\), the singular potential scales faster than the kinetic term near
the origin and the Hamiltonian is unbounded below. In \(d=4\) the
inverse-square term has the same scaling as the kinetic term. Hardy's
inequality bounds the quadratic form below at subcritical coupling,
while supercritical coupling permits fall to the center. The subcritical
scale-free model also lacks the ordinary hydrogenic bound-state series
{[}3, 4, 45{]}.

\hypertarget{from-orbitals-to-the-periodic-table}{%
\subsubsection{2.2 From orbitals to the periodic
table}\label{from-orbitals-to-the-periodic-table}}

The orbital calculation assumes a branch describing physical spin-1/2
fermions and the continuum conditions needed for the spin-statistics
theorem {[}2, §§3.1, 4.2{]}. Pauli exclusion then allows two electrons
per spatial orbital. The shell capacities follow:

\[s: 2, \quad p: 6, \quad d: 10, \quad f: 14\]

The periodic table's period lengths --- 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32 --- are
determined by the filling order of these shells. This orbital counting
is conditional on the selected \(d = 3\) branch and the stated spin and
statistics assumptions. The dimension selection inherits A4, E5--E7 and
the continuum conditions {[}2, §3.1--§3.2{]}.

\hypertarget{carbons-bonding-geometry}{%
\subsubsection{2.3 Carbon's bonding
geometry}\label{carbons-bonding-geometry}}

Element 6 (carbon) has electron configuration 1s² 2s² 2p² --- four
valence electrons. In d = 3, four valence electrons admit three
hybridization modes:

\emph{sp³ hybridization:} Four equivalent orbitals pointing to the
vertices of a regular tetrahedron (bond angle 109.5°). This is the
geometry of methane (CH₄), diamond, and the backbone of all organic
macromolecules --- proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, carbohydrates.

\emph{sp² hybridization:} Three planar orbitals at 120° plus one π bond.
This is the geometry of graphite, benzene, and aromatic chemistry.

The \emph{possibility} of these hybridizations --- and hence the
possibility of three-dimensional macromolecular architecture --- is a
structural consequence of the angular momentum algebra in d = 3.

\hypertarget{beyond-carbon-the-extended-structural-chain}{%
\subsubsection{2.4 Beyond carbon: the extended structural
chain}\label{beyond-carbon-the-extended-structural-chain}}

Four additional structural results extend the chain beyond orbital
chemistry.

\textbf{Matter-antimatter asymmetry.} The three-generation branch
inherits H-link and H-spin' {[}2, §4.7{]} and permits a physical CKM
phase; its actual value is a solution-specific input {[}2, §5.4{]}. A
nonzero CP-violating phase supplies one of the Sakharov conditions for
baryogenesis {[}5{]}. The weak-chirality reading of partition-induced
parity violation additionally assumes H-χ' {[}2, §4.8, Theorem 13{]}.
Whether these ingredients produce the observed asymmetry depends on the
remaining baryogenesis dynamics and parameters.

\textbf{Mass hierarchy.} On the stated physical-carrier and matter
branch, the Higgs mechanism {[}2, §4.7{]} gives fermion masses through
Yukawa couplings. The taste-breaking construction permits
generation-dependent couplings, but the existence of the observed light
fermions also requires the stated mass and coupling inputs; it is not
fixed by the Higgs field alone.

\textbf{Nuclear binding.} In standard QCD, SU(3) color confinement
produces bound hadrons, and residual strong forces can bind hadrons into
nuclei for suitable parameter values (§3). A derivation of these
mechanisms for the framework's induced gauge measure is open {[}2,
§6.5{]}; SU(3) symmetry alone does not establish them.

\textbf{Chiral chemistry.} Given the physical weak interaction, carried
in the framework under H-χ' {[}2, §4.8{]}, molecular energy calculations
can distinguish mirror-image molecules. The sign depends on the molecule
and its conformation (§4).

\hypertarget{the-scale-hierarchy}{%
\subsubsection{2.5 The scale hierarchy}\label{the-scale-hierarchy}}

The framework derives two independent gauge groups --- SU(3) and U(1)
--- with independent coupling strengths {[}2, §6{]}. Given their values
and the masses, the two couplings set two widely separated energy
scales:

\[E_{\text{nuclear}} \sim \Lambda_{\text{QCD}} \sim \text{MeV}, \qquad E_{\text{atomic}} \sim \alpha^2 m_e c^2 \sim \text{eV}\]

The ratio \(E_{\text{nuclear}} / E_{\text{atomic}} \sim 10^6\) is
parametric: the two independent gauge groups supply two independent
couplings, and the separation of the resulting scales rests on the
couplings' and masses' values. The universal induced coupling
\(1/\alpha_0 = 23.25\) at the matching scale \(\mu = M_{\text{Pl}}\) is
itself structural --- determined by \(N_f = 6\) and the Dynkin index
(ambient coefficient; for hypercharge under H-observer-bundle and
H-Y-vertex, {[}SM §6.5{]}) --- and the framework reproduces all three SM
gauge couplings at \(M_Z\) through non-perturbative gauge self-energy
corrections and RG running, with three threshold parameters fitted to
the three observed couplings {[}2, §6.3{]}. This separation has a
consequence: nuclei are stable against chemical reactions. Chemistry
rearranges electrons (eV) without touching nuclei (MeV). Atoms are
permanent building blocks that can be assembled, disassembled, and
reassembled without being destroyed.

\hypertarget{water}{%
\subsubsection{2.6 Water}\label{water}}

Oxygen has ground-state configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁴. Valence bonding and
two lone pairs help explain the bent shape of water (H₂O). The bond
angle of about 104.5° is a measured molecular property.

Water's bent geometry and bond polarity give it a permanent dipole. Its
molecular interactions support a hydrogen-bonding network. Hydrogen
bonding has a characteristic energy of order 0.2 eV, between typical
covalent bond energies and the thermal energy of about 0.025 eV at 300
K. The network contributes to the liquid's high specific heat, its
density maximum near 4°C and its ability to solvate ions and polar
molecules.

The orbital algebra constrains possible bonding geometries. Water's
geometry, dipole and hydrogen bonding require molecular inputs. Its
liquid phase depends on temperature and pressure.

\hypertarget{the-thermal-window}{%
\subsubsection{2.7 The thermal window}\label{the-thermal-window}}

A thermal window for the chemistry considered here requires stable bonds
at the selected temperature, reactions at useful rates and a liquid
solvent. For an Arrhenius reaction with prefactor \(A\) and barrier
\(E_a\), the characteristic rate is \(A e^{-E_a/kT}\). To proceed on a
target timescale \(t\), the rate must satisfy
\(A e^{-E_a/kT}t\gtrsim 1\), which can hold for \(E_a>kT\). The coupling
values and masses set the atomic scale (§2.5). The bonds, barriers,
prefactors, solvent and operating temperature determine whether the
conditions overlap.

\hypertarget{the-question}{%
\subsubsection{3.1 The question}\label{the-question}}

The structural chain establishes that organic chemistry is
\emph{possible}. For it to be \emph{actual}, the 18 free parameters of
the SM must fall in a viable region. How large is this region?

Traditional fine-tuning studies {[}6, 7, 8{]} vary both the structure
and the parameters. Here the structure is held fixed on the selected
lattice/gauge branch, reducing the question to parameters alone: the
gauge group, dimension, generation count, and discrete symmetries are
not varied (§3.4).

\hypertarget{the-most-constrained-parameters}{%
\subsubsection{3.2 The most constrained
parameters}\label{the-most-constrained-parameters}}

Not all 18 parameters are equally constrained. The literature identifies
four as critical for complex chemistry:

\textbf{The fine-structure constant α.} Governs atomic binding,
molecular bond strengths, and the balance between electromagnetic and
thermal energies. Stable atoms require \(\alpha \lesssim 1\) (above
this, relativistic effects destabilize inner shells). Complex chemistry
requires bond energies \(\gg kT\) at habitable temperatures, giving
\(\alpha \gtrsim 10^{-3}\) {[}8{]}. The viable range spans roughly two
orders of magnitude: \(10^{-3} \lesssim \alpha \lesssim 10^{-1}\), with
our universe at \(\alpha \approx 1/137 \approx 7.3 \times 10^{-3}\) ---
within the viable range but not near its center.

\textbf{The electron-to-proton mass ratio \(m_e / m_p\).} Determines the
separation of nuclear and atomic scales. Stable atoms with a rich
periodic table require \(m_e / m_p \ll 1\) (so electrons orbit far from
the nucleus). Complex chemistry breaks down for
\(m_e / m_p \gtrsim 10^{-1}\) (electron clouds too compact for
directional bonding). The viable range is roughly
\(10^{-5} \lesssim m_e / m_p \lesssim 10^{-1}\), spanning four orders of
magnitude. Our value: \(m_e / m_p \approx 5.4 \times 10^{-4}\).

\textbf{The quark mass ratio \(m_d - m_u\).} Controls the proton-neutron
mass difference and hence nuclear stability. If \(m_d - m_u\) changes
sign (proton heavier than neutron), hydrogen is unstable ---
catastrophic for chemistry. If \(m_d - m_u\) is too large, no bound
nuclei beyond hydrogen exist. The viable range is approximately
\(1 \lesssim (m_d - m_u) / \text{MeV} \lesssim 5\) {[}9{]}, about a
factor of 5. Our value: \((m_d - m_u) \approx 2.5\) MeV.

\textbf{The strong coupling \(\alpha_s\).} At the QCD scale
(\textasciitilde1 GeV), \(\alpha_s\) determines nuclear binding. If
\(\alpha_s\) is \textasciitilde10\% smaller, the deuteron is unbound and
big bang nucleosynthesis fails to produce helium --- but stellar
nucleosynthesis could still produce carbon {[}8{]}. If \(\alpha_s\) is
\textasciitilde30\% larger, di-protons are bound, stars burn through
hydrogen catastrophically fast. The viable range is roughly
\(0.8 \alpha_s^{\text{obs}} \lesssim \alpha_s \lesssim 1.3 \alpha_s^{\text{obs}}\),
about a factor of 1.6 {[}10{]}.

violation}\label{molecular-parity-violation}}

The weak neutral current contributes a parity-violating term to the
molecular Hamiltonian. In chiral molecules this term can give different
energies to the two enantiomers {[}11, 12{]}:

\[\Delta E_{\text{PV}} \sim G_F \alpha Z^5 m_e c^2 \left(\frac{a_0}{R}\right)^3\]

Here \(G_F\) denotes the Fermi constant, \(Z\) a nuclear charge and
\(R\) a bond length in the scaling estimate. A representative order
estimate for amino acids is \(|\Delta E_{\text{PV}}|\sim 10^{-19}\) eV
{[}11{]}. The sign depends on molecular structure and conformation.
Alanine calculations illustrate why the observed handedness of the weak
force alone does not imply universal L stabilization {[}44{]}.

\hypertarget{the-amplification-argument}{%

argument}\label{the-amplification-argument}}

The energy difference \(10^{-17} \, kT\) is far too small to directly
select one handedness. The enantiomeric excess at thermal equilibrium
would be \(ee \sim 10^{-17}\). The question is whether amplification
mechanisms can promote this bias to complete homochirality.

Three mechanisms are experimentally demonstrated. Autocatalytic
amplification (the Soai reaction {[}13{]}) amplifies \(ee \sim 10^{-5}\)
to \(> 99\%\). Crystallization (Viedma ripening {[}14{]}) drives racemic
crystal mixtures to homochirality from small initial biases. Chiral
polymerization with cross-inhibition {[}15{]} amplifies small monomer
\(ee\) to polymer homochirality.

The illustrative thermal excess of order \(10^{-17}\) lies well below
the biases amplified in the cited Soai experiments. In a pool of \(N\)
monomers the statistical excess can be of order \(N^{-1/2}\). For
\(N\sim 10^6\) this is about \(10^{-3}\). Whether a molecule-specific
parity-violating bias can survive sampling fluctuations and influence
amplification across pools is an open origin-of-life question.

The finite-horizon result concerns the information that returns to an

\textbf{Von Neumann's construction.} Von Neumann exhibited
self-reproduction in a specific automaton architecture {[}20{]}. It uses
a constructor, a mechanism to copy the instructions and a controller
that sequences their use.

A chemical counterpart would need the following specific functions.

\emph{(a) Construction.} A template-directed reaction could assemble a
product from monomers. Its selectivity and catalytic rate are properties
of the proposed chemistry.

\emph{(b) Instruction copying.} Complementary pairing can guide
synthesis of a complementary strand. Completing a repeatable copying
cycle needs the additional chemical functions discussed in §5.5.

\emph{(c) Control.} Environmental cycling --- temperature fluctuations,
wet-dry cycles, concentration gradients --- drives alternation between
construction and copying phases. It is a further environmental
condition: the thermal window (§2.7) allows chemistry at the prevailing
temperature but does not by itself supply fluctuations on timescales
relevant to chemistry.

A candidate molecular system needs compatible construction, copying and
control. Environmental cycles could supply some control functions where
the chemistry responds on the required timescales. The autocatalytic-set
model alone leaves the existence of such a system open.

\textbf{RNA as template and catalyst.} Sequence storage, complementary

\textbf{Replicators under population dynamics.} C1--C4 do not by
themselves imply copying or growth: the finite bijections that realize

grow. Given a template replicator in a feedstock-rich environment, with
replication exceeding decay and copying fidelity high enough to keep
variants heritable, standard population dynamics gives exponential
growth while feedstock lasts, and the replicator can come to dominate
its environment. Exponential growth is not peculiar to replicators:
mass-action autocatalysis also grows exponentially while its feedstock
lasts. The persistence of a replicator population is conditional on
sustained feedstock and on replication exceeding decay. Whether a
replicator arises in a given aqueous organic chemistry depends on
chemical premises --- catalytic closure that includes template copying,
co-presence of the relevant species, environmental cycling --- that the
autocatalytic argument (§5.3) addresses only within Kauffman's model and

before the first replicator, whose duration depends on solution-specific
parameters (concentration, temperature fluctuation rates, mineral
surface availability).

\hypertarget{mathematical-existence}{%
\subsubsection{6.2 Mathematical
existence}\label{mathematical-existence}}

(S, φ) is a finite set and a bijection. This is a mathematical object
--- a structure in the space of logical possibilities. It requires no
physical substrate, no creator, and no cause. It exists in the same
sense that the integers exist or that the permutation group \(S_n\)
exists: as a logically consistent structure that cannot fail to be
well-defined.

If mathematical structures are taken as necessarily existent --- the
Platonic position --- then (S, φ) \emph{necessarily} exists. Not
``happens to exist'' or ``was created'' but cannot fail to exist,
because there is no logical contradiction in its definition. On this
reading, ``why is there something rather than nothing?'' dissolves: the
question assumes non-existence is the default and existence requires
explanation. For mathematical structures, existence is the default.
Non-existence would require a logical inconsistency, and (S, φ) has
none.

\hypertarget{observable-mathematics}{%
\subsubsection{6.3 Observable
mathematics}\label{observable-mathematics}}

The framework makes this sharper than generic Platonism. Tegmark's
Mathematical Universe Hypothesis {[}7{]} asserts that all consistent
mathematical structures are equally real. The OI framework says
something more specific: among all mathematical structures, only those
containing embedded observers are \emph{observable from within}. The
characterization theorem {[}1, §3.4{]} identifies which structures
contain embedded observers: exactly those of the form (S, φ) with
conditions C1--C4. The reconstruction theorem {[}33, §3{]} fixes the
local lattice/gauge residue of the framework's physics under its stated
inputs; it does not select a unique Bell-inclusive completion.

At the level Theorem 23 actually controls, the structural inputs, within
the nearest-neighbor class, select one local lattice/gauge residue. This
does not imply one unique Bell-inclusive substratum: preparation-indexed
Bell-composite structure remains underdetermined even when H-Bell
compatibility holds. The framework's mathematical-existence discussion
therefore concerns the existence of admissible deterministic structures,
not uniqueness of the full ontology.

\hypertarget{the-reframing}{%
\subsubsection{7.1 The reframing}\label{the-reframing}}

Biological experiments can test whether particular degrees of freedom
retain information that affects later observations. For a declared
visible process, the finite-horizon implication uses a matched-present
history contrast (C4). The existence and source of that contrast must be
assessed in each candidate biological system.

\hypertarget{c1c4-in-proteins}{%
\subsubsection{7.2 C1--C4 in proteins}\label{c1c4-in-proteins}}

Consider an enzyme's active site as the visible sector and the protein
scaffold's conformational degrees of freedom as the hidden sector.

\textbf{C1 (coupling).} The active site is covalently bonded to the
scaffold --- coupling is continuous and bidirectional. Electronic
transitions at the active site induce conformational strain in the
scaffold; conformational changes in the scaffold modulate the active
site's electronic structure. This is the standard picture of allostery.

\textbf{C2 (memory persistence).} Electronic transitions at the active
site occur on femtosecond timescales (\(\tau_S \sim 10^{-15}\) s).
Conformational changes of the protein scaffold occur on microsecond to
millisecond timescales (\(\tau_B \sim 10^{-6}\) to \(10^{-3}\) s). The
ratio: \(\tau_S / \tau_B \sim 10^{-12}\) to \(10^{-9}\). This is the
\emph{inverse} of the Markovian regime --- exactly the slow-bath
realization of persistence. The scaffold retains correlations for
\(\sim 10^9\) electronic transitions before relaxing.

\textbf{C3 (capacity).} A typical enzyme has \(\sim 10^3\) to \(10^4\)
atoms, each with multiple conformational degrees of freedom. The
conformational state space is exponentially large:
\(\sim q^{N_{\text{conf}}}\) with \(N_{\text{conf}} \sim 10^3\). This
exceeds the active site's electronic state space (\(\sim 10\) to \(100\)
relevant states) by tens of orders of magnitude.

\textbf{C4 (history readback).} A conformational record would satisfy C4
if two histories of the same current visible state gave different
next-step laws through that record. Allostery motivates this comparison.
The matched-present contrast is an experimental condition.

The protein model motivates coupling, persistence and capacity. Those
features can coexist with zero accessible memory. A positive C4 contrast
would yield accessible conditional memory on the tested window, and
P-indivisibility would require a separate witness.

The finite reversible-substratum construction in {[}1, §2.3{]} has its
own closure premises. The enzyme and scaffold exchange energy and matter
with their surroundings. The observable-law implication of a measured
readback gap transfers to that application. The mechanism and accessible
P-indivisibility remain separate tests from recurrence of a closed
finite model.

\hypertarget{the-prediction}{%
\subsubsection{7.3 The prediction}\label{the-prediction}}

If a conformational record persists and influences later turnovers at
fixed present visible state, the protein scaffold is a candidate source
of accessible memory.

\textbf{History readback.} Compare later catalytic transition laws for
histories with matched measured present states and different prior
treatments or events. Nonexponential waiting times and unconditioned
rate correlations can motivate that experiment. They also occur in
Markov models of a sufficiently detailed current state.

\emph{Non-Markovian signatures.} The mutual information
\(I(X_{<t}; X_{>t} | X_t)\) between past and future catalytic events,
conditioned on the present state, is positive wherever the scaffold's
record is read back: by the readback lemma {[}1, §2.3{]}, a readback gap
\(\delta\) at a history of probability \(p_0\) gives
\(I(X_{<k}; X_{k+1} \mid X_k) \geq p_0\delta^2/\ln 2\), and the
past--future quantity is at least as large. Its lower bound is set by
\(\delta\) and \(p_0\), which are open for the protein system.

\hypertarget{existing-evidence}{%
\subsubsection{7.4 Existing evidence}\label{existing-evidence}}

\textbf{Single-molecule enzyme kinetics.} The cited studies report
fluctuating catalytic rates and turnover correlations {[}21, 22{]}, and
the T4 lysozyme study resolves conformations at several exchange times
{[}34{]}. These results motivate tests of scaffold-mediated readback for
a declared present state.

\textbf{Dispersed kinetics.} Stretched-exponential waits and lag
correlations can arise by projection or mixing of Markov states with
different rates {[}35{]}. Establishing C4 for a specified readout
requires the conditional contrast of §7.3.

\textbf{Photosynthetic energy transfer.} Engel et al.~{[}23{]} reported
oscillations in two-dimensional spectra of the FMO complex. Duan et
al.~{[}36{]} reported short electronic coherence in their spectroscopy.
Cao and Plenio {[}37{]} study vibronic effects in a model. These results
motivate separating electronic and vibrational contributions to the
signals. The matched-present history contrast and its role in transport
remain open.

\textbf{Cryptochrome radical-pair magnetoreception.} Behavioral
responses to radiofrequency fields and long-lived radical pairs in
cryptochrome preparations motivate a radical-pair mechanism {[}39,
40{]}. The measurements of Xu et al.~{[}43{]} show magnetic sensitivity
in vitro for European robin cryptochrome 4. The responsible receptor in
vivo, the relevant coherence lifetime and a matched-present readback
witness remain separate questions.

\textbf{Conformationally gated tunneling.} The isotope effects reviewed
in {[}24{]} motivate models of tunneling coupled to conformational
motion. A test of C4 would compare histories at the same measured
present.

\hypertarget{implications-for-drug-design}{%
\subsubsection{7.5 Implications for drug
design}\label{implications-for-drug-design}}

\textbf{Allosteric drugs.} The framework predicts that allosteric
effects carry temporal correlations through the scaffold --- the drug's
binding event writes conformational information that is returned to the
active site over the conformational timescale. Optimal design should
account for these temporal correlations, not just equilibrium binding
affinities.

\textbf{Drug resistance.} Resistance mutations often occur far from the
active site. The framework predicts they cluster in regions that
maximally alter the scaffold's slowest conformational modes --- altering
the \emph{memory structure} of the hidden sector. Testable via normal
mode analysis.

\textbf{Enzyme engineering.} The framework suggests optimizing the
\emph{coupling architecture} (C1--C4 between active site and scaffold),
not just the active site's electronic structure. Better-tuned
\(\tau_S / \tau_B\) ratios should yield both higher catalytic rates and
more robust performance.

\hypertarget{epistemic-status}{%
\subsubsection{7.6 Epistemic status}\label{epistemic-status}}

The application of C1--C4 to protein systems rests on the
model-independent consequence of §7.2: write-and-readback coupling to a
persistent high-capacity sector yields history-dependent dynamics. The
enzyme-over-scaffold trace-out is open and dissipative, so the
characterization theorem itself does not apply to it (§7.2, scope note).
The specific signatures (non-exponential kinetics, fluctuating rates,
conformational gating of tunneling) are consistent with existing
experimental data but have not been quantitatively tested against the
framework's specific predictions (positive \(I(X_{<t}; X_{>t} | X_t)\)
at accessible orders, history-dependent catalytic rates). The medical
implications (allosteric temporal correlations, resistance as
memory-structure alteration) are consequences of the prediction but have
not been independently tested.

The summary: the framework expects enzyme kinetics to be non-Markovian
wherever the scaffold writes and reads back catalytic history. The
prediction is consistent with existing evidence and makes specific
testable claims. Whether the non-Markovian corrections are large enough
to matter for practical drug design is an empirical question. The ratio
\(\tau_S / \tau_B \sim 10^{-12}\) to \(10^{-9}\) fixes how long the
scaffold retains a record, of order \(10^{9}\) to \(10^{12}\)
active-site transitions (§7.2), not the size of the correction, which is
set by how strongly the record modulates the active site (the gap
\(\delta\) of §7.3) and is open for the protein system.

\hypertarget{the-gps-analogy-where-oi-corrections-reach-engineering-relevance}{%
\subsection{8. The GPS Analogy: Where OI Corrections Reach Engineering
Relevance}\label{the-gps-analogy-where-oi-corrections-reach-engineering-relevance}}

\hypertarget{the-precedent}{%
\subsubsection{8.1 The precedent}\label{the-precedent}}

Nobody building a satellite navigation system in the 1950s would have
predicted they would need general relativity. GPS requires relativistic
corrections because satellite clocks experience different gravitational
potentials --- 45 μs/day from GR, 7 μs/day from SR. Without corrections,
position drifts \textasciitilde10 km/day. The practical application
emerged when engineering precision reached the scale where the
correction mattered.

The framework's specific correction is non-Markovian dynamics:
deviations from standard Markovian models wherever a bath retains a
record and reads it back. Their size is set by the coupling and the
readback, not by \(\tau_S / \tau_B\); the ratio sets how long the record
is retained, of order \(\tau_B / \tau_S\) system events under uniform
erosion {[}1, §2.3{]}. At the cosmological scale,
\(\tau_S / \tau_B \sim 10^{-32}\). But \(\tau_S / \tau_B\) is
scale-dependent. The question: where does engineering precision reach
the scale where OI corrections matter?

\hypertarget{quantum-computing}{%
\subsubsection{8.2 Quantum computing}\label{quantum-computing}}

\textbf{Noise assumptions in error correction.} Independent errors are a
common benchmark. A persistent bath can contribute temporal correlations
when its records affect later error probabilities at fixed measured
present. The circuit, noise model and decoder determine whether
accounting for correlations improves correction.

\textbf{The physical system.} Superconducting qubits (transmons) are
coupled to two-level systems (TLS) in the substrate --- structural
defects in the amorphous oxide layer. TLS dynamics is slow
(\(\tau_B \sim \mu\text{s}\)) relative to gate operations
(\(\tau_S \sim \text{ns}\)). The ratio:
\(\tau_S / \tau_B \sim 10^{-3}\). This is 29 orders of magnitude larger
than the cosmological case.

\textbf{The proposed test.} Compare error predictions for matched
present records after different histories. A TLS bath can contribute
temporal correlations, as can leakage, drift and other carriers. A
history-aware decoder could improve logical error rates if it uses
reliable information left out of the comparison decoder.

\textbf{The scale.} For the illustrative bath timescales the ratio
estimates a retention length, of order \(\tau_B / \tau_S \sim 10^3\)
gates, not the per-gate size of the correction, which depends on the
qubit--TLS coupling and is not derived here. Circuits of \(10^3\) gates
or more span the bath's correlation time; how much the correlated
component adds to the error budget over \(10^4\) to \(10^6\) gates
depends on that size and on whether the correlated errors add
coherently.

\textbf{A decoder-level test on existing data.} Compare a
history-conditioned error model with a drift- and measurement-matched
baseline. Evidence of accessible history dependence concerns C4. A
divisibility witness requires a specified family of preparations and
reconstructed maps, such as a revival of distinguishability in a rooted
population law {[}1, §2.3{]}. A decoder benefit would be a separate
comparison at fixed code, noise and operating point.

\textbf{Status of the syndrome-level test (carried out).} This test has
been run against Google's Willow surface-code detection-event streams
across distances 3, 5, and 7 in both bases, and its corrected claims
ladder is recorded here in full, including the rungs that were withdrawn
or revised. An initial pass reported per-experiment positives, but its
null lacked a measurement-error control and detected only
known-Markovian structure; those verdicts are \emph{withdrawn}. A second
pass reported nineteen of 336 experiments positive, but under a
stringent empirical null built from each experiment's own detector error
model, none survive multiple-comparison control --- the nominal
per-experiment significances were overdispersed at the real geometries
and are not calibrated, so \emph{no individual-experiment detection is
defensible}. The analysis reports an aggregate trend beyond the matched
detector-error model: lag-\(\geq 2\) predictability rising with cycle
depth (mean Spearman \(\rho = +0.74\) across 28 series --- imperfectly
independent given the shared device --- with per-series permutation
\(p \approx 5\times10^{-5}\)) and, more weakly across the three code
distances, with distance; auxiliary long-memory (non-saturating
windowed-rate variance) and shot-scale non-stationarity corroborate it.
The result is relative to that detector-error model and comparison. No
information-backflow signature is present anywhere; however, this
syndrome test leaves coherent revival at the proposed bath timescale
unresolved. Free evolution gives a separate test (§8.3). The attribution
of the surviving memory between benign carriers (heating and leakage
accumulation, \(1/f\) rate modulation --- divisible, grade-ii) and any
genuine P-indivisible component is undecidable from syndrome streams and
remains open.

The reported device-level result is an aggregate syndrome trend in the
stated comparison. Individual detections, a mechanism attribution and a
measured decoder gain remain open. The backflow proxy gave a null result
in this venue. The cross-platform kernel hypothesis and free-evolution
test of §8.3 are proposed follow-ups.

\hypertarget{quantum-sensing}{%
\subsubsection{8.3 Quantum sensing}\label{quantum-sensing}}

\textbf{Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers.} NV centers in diamond detect
magnetic fields with sensitivity approaching the standard quantum limit,
bounded by the coherence time \(T_2\). Standard models assume Markovian
decoherence --- coherence decays exponentially and is gone.

\textbf{The OI proposal.} Slow environments in diamond, including
paramagnetic impurities, can provide modes that persist beyond the
electronic spin dynamics. A bath record returning to the measured
observable would create accessible memory. Coherent revival needs a
separate witness at the sensing readout.

\textbf{The application.} Sensing protocols that exploit the revival ---
choosing a readout time with demonstrated signal recovery --- could
improve sensitivity relative to a specified control protocol if the
returned signal and resulting variance yield a larger sensitivity at
equal resources. The size of the improvement is not fixed by
\(\tau_S / \tau_B\): it depends on the revival amplitude, which requires
an NV-specific model of the bath, readout and control (§8.9).

\hypertarget{precision-metrology-and-atomic-clocks}{%
\subsubsection{8.4 Precision metrology and atomic
clocks}\label{precision-metrology-and-atomic-clocks}}

\textbf{Current situation.} Optical lattice clocks achieve fractional
frequency uncertainty \(\sim 10^{-18}\) --- sensitive enough that GR
corrections from a 1 cm height difference are measurable. At this
precision, the atoms' interaction with the lattice light field may
satisfy C1--C4: the lattice photons provide coupling (C1), the optical
cavity has slow modes (C2), and the photon field has high capacity (C3).

\textbf{The proposed clock test.} A cavity record that changes the
transition law at fixed present data could produce measurable
correlations. The lifetime separation informs the experiment design. The
coupling, conditional contrast and comparison with other noise
mechanisms determine what it detects.

\hypertarget{engineered-partitions-the-bath-as-a-resource}{%
\subsubsection{8.6 Engineered partitions: the bath as a
resource}\label{engineered-partitions-the-bath-as-a-resource}}

Standard quantum engineering treats the environment as the enemy ---
decoherence destroys quantum information, and the goal is to isolate
qubits from their surroundings. The framework suggests a fundamentally
different design philosophy: \emph{engineer} the C1--C4 memory
architecture and realize the target quantum process as its finite-test
shadow ({[}Main §3.4{]}) --- the conditions do not by themselves select
the operational mechanism.

\textbf{The design principle.} Instead of isolating a qubit from all
environmental coupling, deliberately couple it to a \emph{slow,
high-capacity hidden sector} with controllable properties. The hidden
sector stores correlations written during one gate operation and returns
them during a later operation. The bath is not noise --- it is
programmable quantum memory.

\textbf{Concrete platform: qubit + spin chain.} A qubit coupled to a
linear chain of \(L\) ancillary spins. The chain is the engineered
hidden sector. Its correlation time scales as \(\tau_B \sim L^z\) (where
\(z\) is the dynamical exponent --- for a Heisenberg chain, \(z = 1\)).
Its capacity scales exponentially: \(\sim 2^L\) states. By tuning \(L\)
(the lengths below are illustrative estimates from \(\tau_B \sim L^z\),
not derived critical lengths):

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Short chain (\(L \lesssim 5\), \(\tau_B \sim \tau_S\)): how far the
  dynamics departs from the Markov benchmark, and whether any record
  survives to readback, depends on the coupling, the chain's conditional
  mixing and its readback; the ratio \(\tau_B/\tau_S\) alone establishes
  neither memorylessness nor record survival.
\item
  Long chain (\(L \gtrsim 20\), \(\tau_B \gg \tau_S\)): memory-bearing
  regime where the chain retains its record and reads it back (C4).
  Correlations written at gate \(k\) can then return at later gates,
  within a retention time of order \(\tau_B / \tau_S\) gates under
  uniform erosion. Coherent recovery depends on the identified readout
  dynamics. P-indivisibility of the qubit's population law on such
  windows is a further property, certified only by a divisibility
  witness.
\item
  Whether a record survives to readback depends on the chain's
  conditional mixing, not on a threshold in \(\tau_B / \tau_S\); where a
  record survives and the chain reads it back (C4), the visible law
  carries accessible finite-horizon memory. On a fixed finite reversible
  representative, where recurrence returns the visible map to the
  identity and a readback gap forces a non-permutation rooted map, that
  memory also shows up as stochastic indivisibility of the visible
  population law across the recurrence cycle {[}1, §2.3{]}. Neither is a
  claim about generic open-system non-Markovianity of a quantum channel.
\end{itemize}

This is testable in existing platforms: superconducting qubits coupled
to engineered spin chains {[}25{]}, NV centers in diamond with
controllable nuclear spin baths, or trapped ions with engineered phonon
modes. The prediction: where the chain retains and reads back its record
(C4), the qubit's dynamics acquires accessible conditional memory,
visible in history-dependent multi-time statistics; revival of a
coherence-sensitive signal is a separate test using specified
preparations and readout.

\hypertarget{non-markovian-quantum-computation}{%
\subsubsection{8.7 Non-Markovian quantum
computation}\label{non-markovian-quantum-computation}}

\textbf{The independent-noise benchmark.} The ideal gates specify a
circuit. Fault-tolerance models allow different forms of noise, with
guarantees depending on the stated assumptions.

\textbf{Candidate correlated dynamics.} If a common bath retains and
returns information about earlier gates, the effect of gate \(k\) can
depend on the gate history at fixed present observable state. A long
bath timescale provides an opportunity for that comparison.

\textbf{Distinguishability revival.} An increase in distinguishability
for a specified pair of preparations certifies failure of positive
divisibility for the relevant map family. A population revival can occur
without returning the coherent signal used by a computation. Error
correction or sensing benefit requires a logical or signal-level
comparison for the specified controls, noise and readout.

\textbf{The open question.} Whether a specified bath and control model
improves a computation over an equal-resource baseline depends on its
operational dynamics and resource accounting. The readback result and a
divisibility witness identify different properties of the model.

\hypertarget{quantum-materials-by-design}{%
\subsubsection{8.8 Quantum materials by
design}\label{quantum-materials-by-design}}

\textbf{Heavy-fermion materials as candidate memory-bearing systems.}
Heavy-fermion compounds (CeCoIn\(_5\), YbRh\(_2\)Si\(_2\), UPt\(_3\))
contain localized f-electrons forming a slow bath (\(\tau_B \sim\) ps to
ns) coupled to itinerant conduction electrons (\(\tau_S \sim\) fs). The
ratio: \(\tau_S / \tau_B \sim 10^{-3}\) to \(10^{-6}\). These materials
exhibit anomalous transport --- non-Fermi liquid resistivity
(\(\rho \sim T^\alpha\) with non-integer \(\alpha\)), logarithmic
specific heat, and memory effects in magnetoresistance. The standard
explanation invokes Kondo physics and quantum criticality. The OI
framework provides a structural reframing: if the conduction electrons
satisfy C1--C4 with the f-electron bath, their dynamics is non-Markovian
on accessible windows --- the readback gap forces positive conditional
memory {[}1, §2.3{]} --- and the anomalous transport is then a
consequence of non-Markovian electronic dynamics rather than a deviation
from normal behavior requiring a special mechanism. P-indivisibility of
the electronic population law is a further property, not implied by
C1--C4 on those windows.

\textbf{The design principle.} In natural materials, \(\tau_S / \tau_B\)
is fixed by the chemistry. In engineered metamaterials, it can be tuned.
Candidate platforms: (i) \emph{Molecular junctions} --- single molecules
bridging electrodes, with floppy side chains as slow bath
(\(\tau_S / \tau_B \sim 10^{-9}\)); prediction: history-dependent
conductance. (ii) \emph{Cold-atom lattices} --- two species with
independently tunable tunneling rates; prediction: non-thermal momentum
distributions at large \(\tau_B / \tau_S\). (iii) \emph{Photonic
crystals with quantum dots} --- engineered bandgap suppresses fast decay
while maintaining coupling; prediction: non-exponential fluorescence
with partial revivals.

\hypertarget{quantitative-performance-estimates}{%
\subsubsection{8.9 Quantitative performance
estimates}\label{quantitative-performance-estimates}}

Device applications require a measured or modeled C4 contrast for
accessible memory. Distinguishability revival tests divisibility on a
specified window. Coherence recovery and metrological benefit need
separate tests of the relevant signal or logical channel. The ratio
\(\tau_B/\tau_S\) informs the choice of windows under the retention
assumptions.

\textbf{Quantum error correction: a structural correlation, not a
quantified gain.} Standard surface codes assume independent (Markovian)
errors. Correlated errors from a slow bath extend over
\(\sim \tau_B / \tau_S\) gates. A Markovian decoder sees these
correlations as a higher effective error rate --- it
\emph{underperforms}. A correlation-aware decoder exploits the
predictability: correlated errors are easier to correct than random
errors. For superconducting qubits with TLS baths
(\(\tau_S / \tau_B \sim 10^{-3}\), correlations over \(\sim 10^3\)
gates), a correlation-aware decoder could reduce the effective error
rate at the same physical error rate. The size of that reduction, and
hence any change in the physical-to-logical qubit overhead, is not
quantified here: it requires a code, a decoder and a noise spectrum,
none of which the framework supplies.

\textbf{Coherence extension.} An exponential decay \(e^{-t/T_2}\) can
serve as a coherence benchmark where applicable. For a fixed pair of
preparations evolved under the same family of channels, an increase in
trace distance certifies failure of positive divisibility on the tested
interval (§8.7). Coherence recovery requires a revival in the relevant
coherence-sensitive signal. Its timing and amplitude depend on the bath
and coupling. A coherence-time gain requires comparison with a specified
benchmark under matched controls and resources.

\textbf{Quantum sensing.} A standard NV magnetometry estimate is
\(\delta B \sim 1 / (\gamma \sqrt{T_2 \cdot T_{\text{meas}}})\). A
proposed sensitivity gain requires recovery of the field-sensitive
signal and a comparison under specified bath dynamics, readout, control
and measurement resources.

\textbf{Quantum materials: qualitative regime change.} Where an
engineered bath with \(\tau_S / \tau_B \sim 10^{-1}\) couples strongly
to the conduction electrons and reads its record back, non-Markovian
corrections need not be perturbative; their strength is set by the
coupling, while the ratio only confines the retention window to
\(\sim 10\) electronic events. Where the corrections are
\(\mathcal{O}(1)\), standard band theory gives qualitatively wrong
predictions, and the material's electronic properties are determined by
memory-bearing (non-Markovian) dynamics, not by Markovian
approximations. This is not a percentage improvement --- it is a new
regime of matter. The heavy-fermion precedent (non-Fermi liquid
transport, unconventional superconductivity, hidden order) suggests that
qualitatively new phenomena emerge when the non-Markovian corrections
become \(\mathcal{O}(1)\).
