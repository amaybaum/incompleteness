## 2. The finite clause fails at L4 (countercontrol recovered)

`⟨R_z(π/2), cyc3⟩` on one token is the octahedral rotation group; with `cnot` it generates a finite group of signed
permutations of the sixteen table coordinates, of order 384 (F1). A finite group maps the 4-dimensional manifold of pure product states
to a finite union of 4-dimensional manifolds, which cannot cover the 6-dimensional manifold of pure states; F1 exhibits an
exact unreachable state, `φ₀`: no element of the group carries its table to a rank-one (product) table. By the second
half of the stage-4 exclusion theorem (claim D [A]: an unreachable pure state seeds an exotic invariant cone with
H1–H3), the finite clause leaves an exotic cone — EXOTIC-E, as stage 4 recorded for every finite extension (its Clifford
group with the transpose, order 23040, used the same `φ₀` [A]). The flow alone fails the same way (F3: `cnot` and the
flow generate monomial unitaries whose permutations are `{id, (10 11)}`, and no permuted magnitude pattern of `φ₀` is a
product pattern), as does `cyc3` alone (a subgroup of F1's group).

## 3. A strictly weaker sufficient clause: two exact rotations

A_miss asks for a flow. Two exact rational rotations suffice: `R_z(θ₀)` with `cos θ₀ = 3/5` and `cyc3`. `θ₀/π` is
irrational (Niven [L]; F2 checks no power up to 2000 is the identity), so `⟨R_z(θ₀)⟩` is dense in the `z`-circle,
its `cyc3`-conjugate in the `x`-circle, and the closure of the generated group is SO(3). H3 makes `K` closed (a dual
cone is an intersection of closed half-spaces) and `actT` is continuous in `R`, so invariance passes to the closure and
L4 applies: `K = Q3` [W + X + L]. The dividing line between this clause and the failing finite one is the closure of the
token group (SO(3) versus the octahedral group), which is exactly claim D's reachability condition. This sharpens
HO-2d (`{R_z(θ), R_x(θ)}` with `cos θ = 3/5`) by replacing `R_x(θ)` with the native `J = cyc3`.
