## N2 — does the *warrant* of Axiom 1 (its indubitability) require a perspective?

The indubitability argument (Methodology §2.1, §5.4; Main §1.2) is performative: "to doubt it is to
entertain the doubted against the affirmed, which is to register a distinction". The *content* secured is
structural (some tokened differentiation occurs); the *status* "cannot be doubted" is relative to an
entertaining/doubting perspective. Whether that perspective must be phenomenally conscious is not settled
and is not needed: the inferential step uses only that doubting is itself a tokened differentiation.
**Verdict N2: content structural; warrant first-person-epistemic (perspective-relative), not phenomenal by
any step of the argument.** (ELABORATING → feeds the split below)

## N3 — does the corpus state the primitive consistently on whether it includes a subject?

- Methodology §2.1 L59: the indubitable fact "is **not** 'I exist.' 'I' is a substantial noun; it imports a
  self, a persisting subject … It is not 'an observer exists,' for the same reason".
- Methodology §2.3 L77: "It does not deliver a subject: … the evidence does not say it is differentiation
  *for someone*, registered *by* an entity that has it."
- Methodology §6.3 L269: "it rejected 'I exist' and 'an observer exists' for importing a persisting subject".
- Main §1.2 L38: "the cogito made precise, which is irreducibly perspectival: **a doubting *I***, not a
  free-floating fact. Read this way, the primitive already contains … a locus that registers it (the
  observer)".

**Verdict N3: NEW — a direct contradiction between companion papers.** Main grounds the perspectival
reading in a subject ("a doubting I", "the observer") that Methodology's distillation of the same primitive
explicitly excludes. Methodology is the more careful anchor (it isolates the locus as indexical, not as a
subject). The contradiction is wording-level: Methodology derives the same count (two axioms) and the same
lemma set without the subject, so removing the subject from Main changes no count, lemma or result.

## N5 — pressure test of the strongest reading (FOUNDATIONAL-QUESTION)

Candidate: the perspectival reading that makes V ⊊ S analytic is justified by appeal to the first-person
cogito ("the floor the cogito secures is already a registering"); if "registering from a within-place
against a remainder" were irreducibly phenomenal, the formal V would be an abstraction of a viewpoint and
the step to arbitrary physical subsystems would be an uncounted bridge premise.

Skeptical checks:
(a) Is the locus notion used in the derivation phenomenal? No — Methodology §4.2 grounds it in the
    indexicality of tokens; "against a remainder" is representational/correlational (N1).
(b) Is the reading choice hidden? No — both papers state it as an explicit, adopted reading with the
    disclosed alternative (thin reading ⇒ V ⊊ S becomes a third axiom) and state that the choice "changes no
    result downstream of the structure (S, φ, V)" (Main L40, Methodology L132).
(c) Is there an extra bridge from the doubter's locus to "physical subsystem"? "Physical" is not an
    independent predicate in the framework: S is the whole, V the locus, physics is what is derived from
    (S, φ, V). The doubter's own registering is an instance of the structure by the lemma chain; no
    additional realization premise is consumed by any result.
(d) Does the solipsistic/evil-demon scenario defeat the proper-part claim? The remainder need not be an
    external world, only a part of S that is not the locus; Main's diagonal argument (no complete
    self-inclusive representation) supplies it without phenomenal content.
**Verdict N5: the FOUNDATIONAL reading does not survive.** Worst case is the already-disclosed count
change (two axioms ↔ thin reading plus a third), not an unledgered premise.
