# K2.0 — the d = 3 native CNOT extensions (read-only, from L42 `fdebc6e3`)

Scripts: `k20_classify.py`, `k20_part2.py` … `k20_part7.py` (sympy exact unless marked). Nothing committed.

**Conditional on** NB-1's S1/S2 structure at d = 3 (written proof; exact at d = 3 in the NB-1 probe). With
`p = q = 1` (N = diag(1, 1, −1, −1) on u, x, y, z) the family is `(M₀, A, K)`: `M₀ = diag(1, ε₁, ε₂, 1)` (S1, Rt, F),
`A`, `K` real 2×2 on `T = ⟨x, y⟩`.

## Findings

1. **Relations** (exact, symbolic). Rt and Rc force `A` diagonal, `K` antidiagonal, for each of the four `M₀`.
2. **Value formula** (exact, symbolic, all four `M₀`):
   `(1+s_z f_z)(1+g_x t'_x) + (s_z+f_z)(g₋·t'₋) + (f·As)(g_x+t'_x) + (f·Ks)(g₋×t'₋)`, `t' = M₀t`.
3. **Positivity** (written, with exact algebra). With `W(s) = [As, Ks]`, P holds iff `σ_max(W(s)) ≤ 1` for unit `s`
   (necessity: at `s_z = f_z = 0`, `g = (c, …)`, `t' = (c, …)` the minimum over `c` has discriminant `r² − 1`); P⁻
   applies it to `G⁻¹`, which lies in the same family with the reciprocal entries. Hence `|aᵢ| = |kᵢ| = 1` and
   `a₁k₁ + a₂k₂ = 0` (column orthogonality); sufficiency: `(f·As, f·Ks)` then ranges over exactly the complex CNOT's
   disc, so every value range equals the CNOT's. **32 admissible gates**, each invertible with its inverse in the set,
   8 with `G² = I`. Numerical cross-check: each gate alone has minimum ≈ 0 on products.
4. **Classes.** Relabellings that fix the corners and commute with N are `diag(1, ±1, ±1, 1)` on each factor.
   - 8 classes under all 16 relabellings (O-type), 16 under the 4 orientation-preserving ones (SO-type).
   - **The family is discrete.** There is no continuous family.
5. **Closure positivity is the selector.** Require every element of `⟨G, N⊗I, I⊗N⟩` (order 8 or 16) to map `min` into
   `max`. **8 of 16 SO-classes fail**, each with an exact integer witness at value **−2** on pure axis states (e.g.
   `s = u+x, t = u+y, f = u∓x, g = u−y`). **8 survive.**
6. **The survivors are exactly Wigner symmetries** (exact Choi rank-1 test):

   | SO-classes | the gate | invariant composite cone |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | 1, 13 | unitary: CNOT (`M₀ = I`), and a second CNOT-type unitary with `M₀ = R_z(π)` | `Q` |
   | 6, 10 | antiunitary: the global transpose `T` composed with a unitary | `Q` |
   | 0, 12 | the `PT_B`-conjugate of a unitary — the reflected branch | `PT_B(Q)` |
   | 7, 11 | the `PT_B`-conjugate of an antiunitary | `PT_B(Q)` |

   The failures include `PT_B` applied after a unitary on one side only (4, 5, 8, 9) and four gates of no Wigner type
   (2, 3, 14, 15).
7. **Dense local rotations** (numerical). Words mixing each survivor with generic local SO(3) rotations stay at a
   minimum of ≈ 0. Consistent with: `PT_B` conjugation and `T` both normalize local SO(3).

## Answers

1. **Finite or continuous?** Finite: 32 gates.
2. **O(3)-equivalent to CNOT?** Not in general. 6 of 8 O-classes contain no unitary gate. After closure positivity,
   every survivor is a unitary CNOT-type gate up to a local reflection and the global transpose `T`, which is a symmetry
   of `Q`.
3. **SO(3) branches?** Among survivors there are 8 SO-classes, spanned by two independent Z₂'s and one benign split:
   - the **cone orientation**: `Q` vs `PT_B(Q)`;
   - the **gate time-orientation**: unitary vs antiunitary;
   - the `M₀` split: two unitary CNOT-type gates, with the same cone.
4. **Intermediate cones.** For each survivor, `Q` or `PT_B(Q)` is invariant; the orbit hull `C_H` of `min` is strictly
   inside it (the K2.1 dimension/extremality argument). The other invariant cones between `min` and `max` are **not
   classified here**.
5. **An orientation-sensitive principle.** The cone Z₂ is `Q` vs `PT_B(Q)`, linearly isomorphic by a reflection of B.
   Exchange does not distinguish them: `PT_B(Q) = PT_A(Q)` is Σ-invariant. A candidate native selector is
   three-copy uniformity: pairwise relative orientations of copies obtained by local reflections form a Z₂ cocycle, and
   a uniform "mirror" rule on all three pairs violates it. This holds only for tripartite cones that are local
   relabellings of the quantum one; it is untested, and not a theorem. The antiunitary Z₂ does not change the cone. It
   is excluded by any requirement that native gates lie in the identity component of a connected group, which is K2.2's
   resource and not a finite principle.

# K2.2 — dense local closure (read-only, exact where marked)

Script: `k22a_closure.py` (integer arithmetic; controls in `k22a_controls.py`).

**K2.2a (exact).** For each of the 8 surviving SO-classes, take the Lie algebra generated by local `so(3)⊕so(3)`
(Bloch coordinates, `u` fixed) together with its conjugates by every power of `G` (orders 2 or 4). It is 15-dimensional:
- classes 1, 13 (unitary) and 6, 10 (antiunitary): equal, as a span, to the image of `su(4)`;
- classes 0, 12 and 7, 11 (the `PT_B`-conjugates): equal to `PT_B · su(4) · PT_B`.

The connected group of that algebra is compact, so the closure of `⟨SO(3)×SO(3), G⟩` is:
- `PU(4)` for the unitary classes;
- `PU(4) ⊔ T·PU(4)` for the antiunitary classes, since `G = T·U` with `G ∉ PU(4)` by its Choi rank;
- the `PT_B`-conjugates of these for the reflected classes.

The two unitary CNOT-type classes have the same closure.

**K2.2b (written).** Let `K` be that closure and `C` a closed convex cone with `min ⊆ C ⊆ max` and `K(C) = C`.
- **Lower bound.** `C ⊇ conv(K·min) = Q`: `PU(4)` is transitive on the pure states of `CP³`, and each is reached from a
  product state.
- **Upper bound.** `C ⊆ ⋂_{g∈K} g(max) = Q`. Here `max` is the dual of the product effects; `K` carries them onto all
  pure effects; `Q` is self-dual.

So `C = Q` on the Q branch and `C = PT_B(Q)` on the reflected branch. The antiunitary coset changes neither, since `T`
preserves `Q`. The two cones are isomorphic by the local reflection `I⊗R`, an `O(3)` but not `SO(3)` relabelling.

# K2.3 — orientation coherence on three copies (read-only; `k23_cocycle.py`, exact)

**Scope, as authorized.** Only tripartite composites `(L_A⊗L_B⊗L_C) Q_ABC` with `L_X ∈ O(3)`. The branch of a pair is
its two-party marginal cone, `(L_X⊗L_Y) Q_XY`, which is `Q` or `PT(Q)` (`L_Z` fixes `u`, so tracing out `Z` commutes
with the relabelling).

**Exact:**
1. For all 64 pairs of sign relabellings `diag(1, ±1, ±1, ±1)`, the pair's cone is `Q` exactly when
   `det L_X = det L_Y`, and `PT(Q)` otherwise. The test is a Choi rank-1 Wigner test on `L_X⊗L_Y` and on
   `PT_B·(L_X⊗L_Y)`, and exactly one of the two holds each time.
2. `Q ≠ PT(Q)`: the Bell state's partial transpose has eigenvalue `−1/2`.
3. `PT_A(Q) = PT_B(Q)`: `PT_A = T·PT_B` in Bloch coordinates, and `T` is an antiunitary symmetry of `Q`. So the branch
   of a pair does not depend on which party is reflected.
4. Over all 512 triples, the pairwise branches `(ε_AB, ε_BC, ε_CA)` always have even parity. All four even assignments
   occur; no odd one does.

**Written.** `O(3) = SO(3) ⊔ R·SO(3)`, and local `SO(3)` acts by unitaries, preserving both `Q` and `PT(Q)`. So the
branch is `ε_XY = s_X ⊕ s_Y` with `s_X = [det L_X = −1]` for every `O(3)` relabelling. Hence, within this class, pairwise
branches are globally realizable iff `ε_AB ⊕ ε_BC ⊕ ε_CA = 0`. Every realizable assignment is the coboundary of local
orientation bits, and relabelling each copy by `L_X⁻¹` returns standard quantum theory: **within the class, orientation
is a local gauge.**

**Not claimed.** That every tripartite GPT completion with Bloch-ball marginals obeys the parity rule, or that no exotic
completion has all three pairs mirrored.

## K2.2 controls — the closure table (exact; `k22a_controls2.py`, log `k22a_controls2.log`)

| gate | order of G | generated Lie algebra (dimension) | identification |

**Why 105 means exactly `so(15)`** (exact). Each failing `G` fixes `u⊗u` and is orthogonal on its 15-dimensional
complement. The local generators are antisymmetric and vanish on `u⊗u`. So the algebra lies in `so(15)`, and
dimension 105 makes it equal. The connected closure is `SO(15)`.

**No admissible cone** (written, with an exact input). The `SO(15)`-invariant closed convex cones with the normalization
axis are the Lorentz-type cones `L_c = {‖w‖ ≤ c·x₀}`.
- Pure product states have `‖w‖² = 3`, computed exactly, so `min ⊆ L_c` needs `c ≥ √3`.
- Pure product effects have complement norm `√3`, so `L_c ⊆ max` needs `c ≤ 1/√3`.
- Both cannot hold: no `SO(15)`-invariant cone lies between `min` and `max`.

**Reading.** Lie richness does not select quantum theory: without positivity the native algebra becomes all of
`so(15)`. Positivity is what cuts it to `su(4)`, up to the orientation relabelling. The failing gates are excluded
not for lacking dynamics but for having too much.
