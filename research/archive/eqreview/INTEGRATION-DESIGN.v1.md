## 1. Summary

1. **What the target consumes.** `oiPlus_iff_qm` (CarrierGeneralOIPlus:207) is a statement about a
   `FiniteOperationalTheory A`, whose operations are already linear maps on complex matrices. How a native theory was
   identified with matrices is an input to it, not part of it. The forward direction therefore needs only
   **operational equivalence**: a per-token presentation of the native theory as matrices (§4).
2. **K2.** In that reading K2 is EQ-C's Theorem A: `K ∈ {Q3, R_B Q3}`. The twin `R_B Q3` *is* QM under the per-token
   adapter Pauli∘(id⊗reflY). Continuous exchange (CX) is not needed.
3. **Three or more tokens.** A per-token presentation exists only if the twist bits are consistent (they form a cut).
   EQ-C's Theorem C derives this from idle extension of pair dynamics (IE₂), and a spanning-tree version gives every n
   (§5.2). So the operational reading needs IE₂, not CX. Under a presentation, IE₂ is the deterministic reversible
   instance of OI⁺'s own conjunct `ObservationalIndependence`, stated on the native composite before any presentation
   exists.
4. **Representation-specific reading.** Requiring one adapter per *type* is strictly stronger: the twin has no
   type-uniform presentation (exact). This reading needs the native exchange to be physical: EX + IE₂ at three or more
   copies, or CX at two. QM satisfies both, so the converse is the same under either reading.
5. **Correction to the assessment.** The J/K flows through `gC5` are reversible operations of an admissible composite,
   the orbit cone, at every t. This is verified at d = 5. What excludes them is local transitivity inside the same group
   (W5/W7). OI's route carries that clause as IE₁ at dimension d.

## 2. Corrections and confirmations

### 2.1 The flows through gC5

The assessment says that these flows "do not establish ... physically reversible operations".

**What was checked (d = 5).** For every t, G_t maps every product state of `eball 5` into `maxCone`, and
G_{−t} = G_t⁻¹.
- EQ-B gives a written proof. EQ-B states the result for every odd d ≥ 3; it is independently verified here at d = 5.
- My independent exact certificate is `review_jkflow_positivity.py`, 8/8:
  - S1: the pairing value, as an identity in (c, s);
  - S2: αβ − a′² − b′² = (2 − 2c)(PM − |ω|²);
  - S3: 4(PM − |ω|²) is a cone slack plus a ball slack plus a Bessel bracket;
  - S4: 4AB − C² is a sum of nonnegative terms.
- The group law, the inverse, normalization and G_π = `gC5` are checked in `review_eqB.py` (8/8).

**Consequence.** Let `K_orb` be the closed convex cone generated by {G_t(x⊗y) : t ∈ ℝ, x, y ∈ eball 5}.
- **Closed.** The generating set is compact (a continuous image of S⁴×S⁴×S¹) and lies in the hyperplane ω₀₀ = 1.
- **Admissible.** It contains the products and lies in `maxCone`, which is a closed convex cone.
- **Invariant.** G_s(K_orb) = K_orb for every s, by the group law.

So {G_t} is a continuous one-parameter group of reversible transformations of an admissible, locally tomographic
composite of two 5-balls. It passes through `gC5` at t = π. The distinction between a matrix path and a path of
reversible operations is therefore not the one that matters.

**The distinction that excludes them: joint invariance with local transitive dynamics.** No cone between the products
and `maxCone` is invariant under both `gC5` and the quarter turn of one copy's homogeneous plane (3,5). The witness is
W5, with exact value ≈ −1.4138, reproduced in `review_eqB.py`; W7 is the d = 7 analogue (EQ-B).

In OI's route this clause is IE₁ at dimension d: the composite lift of K∞-Trans's group. For OI, the assessment's
practical conclusion therefore stands, and the flows are excluded. The reason is this clause, not a lack of positivity
along the path. Published no-go theorems are consistent with the flows for the same reason: as EQ-B records them, they
place the local transitive groups inside the connected reversible group. The literature hypotheses are unverified
(egress).

## 4. Presentations — the two readings made exact

**Definition (per-token presentation).** A native theory has:
- system tokens s, each with a state body Ω_s;
- composites of tokens, each with a cone K in ⊗(ℝ⊕V_s);
- available operations.

A per-token presentation chooses, for every token, an affine isomorphism α_s : Ω_s → D(ℂ^{n_s}). For every composite,
⊗α_s must carry its cone onto the PSD cone and its available operations into the presented theory. The theory is
**operationally equivalent to QM** when some per-token presentation makes the presented theory satisfy
`ExactAllFiniteEndomorphicQuantumOps` (equivalently, `OIPlus`). A **type-uniform** presentation additionally requires
α_s to depend only on the type of s.

**Facts at two qubit tokens.** Coordinates are Pauli coordinates, and R = `reflY` = diag(1,−1,1) acts on the Bloch
vector.

| fact | statement | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | The twin `R_B Q3` has the per-token presentation (Pauli, Pauli∘R) onto `Q3` | exact (`review_integration` U2: (I⊗R)² = id); EQ-C P1 |
| P2 | The twin has no type-uniform presentation | exact (U1) + the written reduction below |
| P3 | Per-token presentations of an n-token composite are unique up to local SO(3)ⁿ and one global transpose R^{⊗n} | written (below) |
| P4 | Under a per-token presentation with ε_i ≠ ε_j, the native exchange of copies i, j is presented as T_ij∘SWAP, which is not CP | exact: EQ-C P4.2 (Choi rank 16) and P6.2 (eigenvalue −1/2 under idle extension) |

**Proof of P2.**
1. Every α ∈ O(3) is U·φ with U ∈ SO(3) and φ ∈ {I, R}.
2. A uniform adapter (α, α) sends the twin's Bell point to (U⊗U)·(φ⊗φR)·Φ⁺. Its spectrum is that of (φ⊗φR)Φ⁺.
3. For φ = I this is the partial transpose on B, and for φ = R it is the partial transpose on A. Both have eigenvalue
   −1/2 (U1, exact).

**Proof of P3.**
1. Let two choices differ by δ ∈ {I, R}ⁿ, up to SO(3)ⁿ. Then ⊗δ_i must preserve the PSD cone.
2. Suppose δ_i ≠ δ_j for some pair. Take a Bell state on (i, j) tensored with a product state elsewhere. ⊗δ acts on
   (i, j) as a partial transpose, up to the pair's global transpose, so the image has eigenvalue −1/2.
3. Hence δ is constant: either δ = I, or δ = R^{⊗n}, the global transpose. The global transpose is complex conjugation,
   an automorphism of the whole quantum theory, since it maps Kraus families to Kraus families.

## 5. K2 and Kₙ in operational form

### 5.1 K2 (two copies): Theorem A as the K2 statement

**Hypotheses on K ⊆ W 3** (EQ-C T3):
- local tomography (LT), encoded by `W 3` (COMP-1);
- K is a convex cone, `prodState x y ∈ K` for x, y ∈ `eball 3`, and K ⊆ `maxCone (eball 3)`. This is `CandidateCone`
  (K2Guard:95) plus convexity;
- IE₁: `actC R` and `actT R` preserve K for every R ∈ SO(3);
- either (a) a linear G that maps K ∩ {ω₀₀ = 1} onto itself and maps some product to a non-product, or (b) a
  continuous one-parameter group of such maps not inside L.

**Conclusion.** K ∈ {Q3, R_B Q3}; that is, **K is Q3 up to a per-token adapter**. No closedness hypothesis is needed.

**Global-cone closure, step by step.** This answers the assessment's precaution.

| step | content | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Compactness is automatic.** Every map in the group H generated by G and L preserves cl K, by continuity, and cl K is admissible. Its slice at ω₀₀ = 1 is compact, because max's slice is bounded. It has nonempty interior, because it contains the product hull, which spans by LT. The affine maps preserving such a body form a compact group, so H̄ is a compact Lie group (closed-subgroup theorem) | written (standard) |
| b | **First-order condition.** For X ∈ Lie(H̄), e^{tX} preserves cl K ⊆ max. At a pure product p, every product effect vanishing at p has zero derivative along Xp. So Lie(H̄) ⊆ V1 | written; V1 is defined exactly so |
| c | V1 = l ⊕ M1 ⊕ M2 ⊕ N, of dimension 33. A compact H̄ ⊇ L has no N-component, and [M1, M2] ⊄ V1. Hence Lie(H̄₀) ∈ {l, l⊕M1, l⊕M2} | exact (EQ-C P2; my independent rank 207 and 33 null vectors) + written (P2.4e, P2.5) |
| d | **Lie(H̄₀) = l is impossible.** It would make H̄₀ = L, which is normal in H̄, so G would normalize L. That contradicts "G maps a product to a non-product", because the admissible normalizer of L is local O(3)² ⋊ {1, SWAP} | exact (P3) + written |
| e | **H′ = ⟨L, G L G⁻¹⟩ is the full PU(4)-image.** H′ is arcwise connected, so it is a Lie subgroup (Yamabe). Its algebra lies in Lie(H̄₀) and contains both l and Ad_G l ≠ l. M1 is an irreducible l-module (End_l(M1) = ℝ, exact), and Hom_l(M1, l) = 0. So the algebra is l ⊕ M1, and H′ is the full PU(4)-image. The same argument applies to M2 | exact (End) + literature (Yamabe 1950; standard) |
| f | **Lower bound.** H′ ⊆ H preserves K itself, not only cl K. PU(4) is transitive on pure states, so K contains every pure state, and K ⊇ Q3 by convexity | written (standard) |
| g | **Upper bound.** Suppose an element of K is not PSD, with negative eigenvector v. A unitary carries v to \|00⟩, a product, which gives a negative product-effect value and contradicts K ⊆ max. So K ⊆ Q3 | written (standard) |

The gate case (a) and the continuous case (b) differ only in the generator used in step e (G L G⁻¹ or the
one-parameter group). Steps a–b also settle the compactness question: compactness never has to be assumed.

**What CX adds** (EQ-C Theorem B): exactly Q3 in the native chart, which is type-uniformity at two copies. The Lean
target does not need it.

### 5.2 Kₙ over copies: the n-copy extension of Theorem C (proposed lemma)

**Lemma (Kₙ-COPIES).** Status: written sketch, not yet reviewed as a proof.

Hypotheses:
- K_n ⊆ (ℝ⁴)^{⊗n} is a convex cone. It contains the n-fold products and is nonnegative on n-fold product effects.
- K_n is invariant under local SO(3)ⁿ (IE₁ at n copies).
- Γ is a spanning tree on the copies. For each edge {i, j} of Γ, a continuous one-parameter group of
  normalization-preserving pair maps, not inside the pair's local group, acts on K_n as g ⊗ id (IE₂ on Γ).

Conclusions:
- K_n = PSD_n after per-copy reflections ε ∈ {I, R}ⁿ;
- ε is unique up to the global flip (P3);
- the presented reversible group contains PU(2ⁿ).

**Sketch.**
1. **Pair cones.** For each edge, take the cone generated by the conditional pair states (id ⊗ e_rest)(K_n). It is
   admissible, convex and locally invariant, and it is invariant under the edge group, because g ⊗ id commutes with
   id ⊗ e_rest. Theorem A(b) then puts the group generated by the edge group and L_ij at PU(4) or R_B PU(4) R_B.
2. **Orientations.** Root Γ and choose ε along the tree. Given ε_i, exactly one choice of ε_j standardizes the edge
   {i, j}, because R⊗R = T normalizes PU(4) and R_A PU(4) R_A = R_B PU(4) R_B. Since a tree has no cycles, the choices
   are consistent.
3. **Generation.** The standard su(4)'s on the edges of a connected graph generate su(2ⁿ).
   - This is standard. It is checked exactly here for paths at n = 3, 4, 5, where the Pauli-string closure has 63, 255
     and 1023 elements (`review_integration` P).
   - Countercontrols: two disjoint edges at n = 4 close at 30. One edge plus local terms at n = 3 closes at 18, which
     matches EQ-C P6.1b.

   By Yamabe, the generated group is all of PU(2ⁿ).
4. **The cone.** Steps f–g of §5.1, at dimension 2ⁿ, give K_n = PSD_n.

**Corollaries.**
- **Parity rule.** The native twist bits satisfy ε_ij = ε_i ⊕ ε_j for every pair, not only for tree edges. This is
  EQ-C T6(ii), extended to every pair.
- **Type-uniform variant.** It holds iff every tree edge is untwisted in the native chart. Any one of the following
  supplies it:
  - **EX + IE₂**, where EX says that the native exchange of copies i, j is available. If ε_i ≠ ε_j, the exchange is
    presented as T_ij∘SWAP, which does not extend idly (EQ-C T6(iii); exact P6.2). EX alone at two copies does not
    exclude the twin, because `R_B Q3` is SWAP-invariant (EQ-C P1.8);
  - **uniform composition** (the EQ-C T6 corollary);
  - **CX** on each edge (EQ-C T4).

**IE₂ is not ad hoc.** Under any presentation, native IE₂ for a reversible pair map g is the following instance of
`HasParallelReferenceExtension`:
- O = Unit;
- F = the presented g;
- R = the spectator copy's carrier;
- e is a suitable reindexing.

So it is the deterministic reversible instance of OI⁺'s `ObservationalIndependence`, required before the presentation
exists. This is a correspondence between statements, not a derivation: the forward direction must still assume IE₂
natively.

## 9. Precautions and open walls

- **Compactness.** It is automatic for any group of reversible maps of an admissible cone (§5.1 a). A formal round
  carries it as a lemma, not as a hypothesis.
- **The global cone.** See §5.1 e–g. Closedness of K is a consequence, not a premise.
- **No-go scope.** The published theorems exclude d ≠ 3 only together with local transitivity in the same connected
  group. The J/K flow (d = 5, every t) is the exact witness that continuity, reversibility and admissibility do not
  suffice on their own.
- **Open walls.**
  - IE₁: the composite lift of `driveWords3`; no joint tower exists (CompositeInterface:54-55);
  - BP at capacity two;
  - a compactness source for FiniteRank;
  - kernel vocabulary for Kₙ's kinematic half;
  - deterministic generalized port-based teleportation (EQ-D T9);
  - balanced d ≡ 1 mod 4 with d ≥ 13.
- **Literature.** Unverified throughout, because egress is blocked. The owner's references are the ones to check:
  - Masanes et al. 2014 (J. Math. Phys. 55, 122203);
  - de la Torre et al. 2012 (PRL 109, 090403);
  - Krumm–Müller 2019 (npj QI 5, 7).

  The questions are: (i) their exact hypotheses on local groups inside the connected reversible group; (ii) how de la
  Torre et al. treat the partial-transpose composite.
