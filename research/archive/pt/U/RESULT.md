### 1.2 The surgery theorem and the three cones (verdict: NEW — the decisive node)

**Theorem S** [W, over the [X] ingredients cited in Theorem E]. Let `Q ⊆ W 3` be a closed convex cone with
`Q = dualW Q`, and `Z = {z₁, …, z_m}` with:
- (a) `⟨z_i, z_j⟩ = 0` for `i ≠ j`;
- (b) every `q ∈ Q` violates at most one of the constraints `⟨q, z_i⟩ ≥ 0`;
- (c) (projection lemma, PL) for each `i` and each `q ∈ Q` with `⟨q, z_i⟩ < 0`: `Π_i q := q − (⟨q,z_i⟩/|z_i|²) z_i ∈ Q`;
- (d) `−Σσ_i z_i ∉ Q` for `σ ≥ 0`, `σ ≠ 0`.

Then `K_Z := (Q ∩ Z*) + cone Z` is a closed convex cone with `K_Z = dualW K_Z`. If a group of `ipW`-orthogonal maps
preserves `Q` and permutes `Z`, it preserves `K_Z`. If `SEP ⊆ Q` and `Z ⊆ maxCone`, then `SEP ⊆ K_Z`.

*Proof.* Self-positivity: `⟨q, q′⟩ ≥ 0` on `Q`, `⟨q, z_i⟩ ≥ 0` on `Q ∩ Z*`, `⟨z_i, z_j⟩ ≥ 0` by (a). Closedness: by (d),
`(Q ∩ Z*) ∩ −cone Z = {0}`, and a sum of two closed convex cones meeting only at 0 in this way is closed (normalize a
divergent sequence and pass to a limit). Dual: `(Q ∩ Z*)* = cl(Q* + cone Z) = Q + cone Z` (bipolar theorem; closed by
(d)), so `K_Z* = (Q + cone Z) ∩ Z*`. Let `y = q + Σσ_i z_i` in it (`q ∈ Q`, `σ ≥ 0`). If `q ∈ Z*`, `y ∈ K_Z`. Otherwise,
by (b), exactly one `i` has `c := −⟨q, z_i⟩ > 0`; by (a), `0 ≤ ⟨y, z_i⟩ = −c + σ_i|z_i|²`, so `σ_i ≥ c/|z_i|²`, and
`y = Π_i q + (σ_i − c/|z_i|²) z_i + Σ_{j≠i} σ_j z_j`, where `Π_i q ∈ Q` by (c), `⟨Π_i q, z_i⟩ = 0`, and
`⟨Π_i q, z_j⟩ = ⟨q, z_j⟩ ≥ 0` for `j ≠ i` by (a) and (b). So `y ∈ K_Z`. Invariance and `SEP ⊆ K_Z` are immediate. ∎

### 1.3 U2 — linear images of Q3 (verdict: NO-EXOTIC-IN-CLASS; the local Wigner step proved)

**(b) Local Wigner theorem** [W + X `u2_wigner`]. Let `(a, b) ↦ ψ_ab` send pairs of rays of `ℂ²` to rays of `ℂ⁴` with
`|⟨ψ_ab|ψ_a′b′⟩|² = |⟨a|a′⟩|² |⟨b|b′⟩|²` (unit representatives). Then `ψ_ab ∥ U(V₁a ⊗ V₂b)` with `U` unitary and each
`V_i` unitary or antiunitary. (The form `U(V₁b ⊗ V₂a)` is the case `U·SWAP`.)

*Proof.*
1. *Qubit Wigner.* A ray map `χ: P(ℂ²) → P(H)`, `dim H = 2`, preserving transition probabilities is induced by a
   unitary or an antiunitary. Through the Bloch dictionary `|⟨b|b′⟩|² = (1 + n·n′)/2` [X W1] it preserves dot
   products on `S²`; the images `f_i` of `e_i` are orthonormal and `χ(n)·f_i = n·e_i`, so `χ` is the restriction of
   some `O ∈ O(3)`. Rotations are `Ad` of unitaries (Euler angles; `Ad(cI − isZ)`, `Ad(cI − isY)` are the
   rotations about z and y by `2t` [X W3]), and `reflY` is complex conjugation [X W2], so `det O = −1` gives an
   antiunitary.
2. *Slices.* For fixed `a` and orthonormal `b, b⊥`: `|⟨ψ_ab|ψ_ab′⟩|² + |⟨ψ_ab⊥|ψ_ab′⟩|² = 1`, so
   `ψ_ab′ ∈ H_a := span(ψ_ab, ψ_ab⊥)`; likewise every `ψ_a′b ∈ G_b := span(ψ_ab, ψ_a⊥b)`, and `H_a ⊥ H_a⊥` because
   `⟨a|a⊥⟩ = 0`.
3. *Normal form.* The four `ψ_xy` (`x, y ∈ {0,1}`) are orthonormal. Apply the unitary `U₀^H` with `U₀|xy⟩ = ψ_xy`.
   Now `H_x = |x⟩ ⊗ ℂ²`, `G_y = ℂ² ⊗ |y⟩`, and by step 1 the slice maps are `b ↦ |x⟩ ⊗ D_x K^{ε_x} b` and

4. *Cross ratio.* Write `ψ_ab = Σ c_xy |xy⟩`. Since `ψ_ab ∈ G_b = span(ψ_0b, ψ_1b)`, `c_xy = λ_x (D_x K^{ε_x} b)_y`.
   For generic `a, b`, `κ := c₀₀c₁₁/(c₀₁c₁₀)` therefore depends on `b` only; by the symmetric argument it depends on
   `a` only. So `κ` is constant, and then `ε₀ = ε₁` [X W4].
5. *Products.* After the unitary `diag(1,1,1,κ̄)`, `c₀₀c₁₁ = c₀₁c₁₀`: every `ψ_ab` with generic `(a, b)` is a product

6. *Segre step.* For generic `a`: `ψ_a0 ∈ ℂ²⊗|0⟩`, `ψ_a1 ∈ ℂ²⊗|1⟩`, and `ψ_ab = λψ_a0 + μψ_a1` with `λμ ≠ 0` is a
   product. By [X W5] (`det` of the coefficient matrix of `λu⊗v + μu′⊗v′` is `λμ det[u u′] det[v v′]`) the first
   factors are parallel. So `ψ_ab ∥ f_a ⊗ (·)` for all `b`; symmetrically `ψ_ab ∥ (·) ⊗ g_b`. Hence `ψ_ab ∥ f_a ⊗ g_b`.
7. *Factors.* With `b = b′`: `|⟨f_a|f_a′⟩|² = |⟨a|a′⟩|²`, so `f = V₁` by step 1, and likewise `g = V₂`. Undoing
