### Stage 4: the leading candidate looks mathematically strong

There is a particularly elegant argument for why full single-token rotations, combined with CNOT, might be sufficient.

Let and be two qubits, with CNOT controlled by . In the -eigenbasis of ,

\\mathrm{CNOT}=I_A\\otimes P_+ + Z_A\\otimes P_-.

Combining CNOT with arbitrary rotations on generates independent conditional rotations:

U_+\\otimes P_+ + U_-\\otimes P_-,
\\qquad U_+,U_-\\in SU(2).

Every pure two-qubit state can be written as

|\\psi\\rangle=|a_+\\rangle|+\\rangle+|a_-\\rangle|-\\rangle.

Starting from a suitable product state, the independent rotations can produce both components. Consequently, every pure two-qubit state is reachable from some product state.

If a self-dual cone (under the standard trace pairing) contains every product projector and is invariant under these transformations, it must contain every pure-state projector. It therefore contains the full quantum positive cone; self-duality forces the reverse inclusion.

So the cone must equal the quantum cone.

This is a strong sufficiency argument, and potentially a short formal theorem.

### The one issue I would insist Stage 4 resolves

Where does the right to perform these rotations on an entangled composite come from?

There are two fundamentally different statements:

* A single isolated token admits all rotations.

* Those rotations can also act on that token while it belongs to an arbitrary entangled composite, preserving admissibility.

The second statement is an extension-of-operations principle. It does not automatically follow from the first.

This connects directly to the circularity concern established in the earlier equivalence work: one cannot silently assume idle extension when attempting to derive its consequences.

If Y can derive that extension from genuinely observer-native premises, the result becomes especially significant. If the extension must instead be adopted independently, the mathematical theorem remains valid, but its physical interpretation is conditional.
