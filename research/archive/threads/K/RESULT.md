## §1. `ball3` with `ball3Drive` (KF:449)

`ball3Drive` has flow `rot3 t = Rz(t)` (KF:351, 411), `t₀ = π`, and `J = cyc3 : (x,y,z) ↦ (z,x,y)` (KF:416, 425).

- `cyc3 ∈ SO(3)` and `cyc3 e_z = e_x` (1.01, 1.02). So `cyc3 Rz(t) cyc3⁻¹ = Rx(t)` identically in `t` (1.03, 1.08).
- **Generation.** `G_D ⊇ {Rz(α) Rx(β) Rz(γ)} = SO(3)` by the zxz Euler decomposition, and every generator lies in
  SO(3). Hence `G_D = SO(3)` exactly, with no closure taken.
  - Exact zxz Euler words reproduce six Cayley-transform rotations with rational entries; one of them is a pure `Rz`
    (1.10.0–1.10.5).
  - The generated Lie algebra `Lie⟨L_z, cyc3 L_z cyc3⁻¹⟩` has dimension 3, computed over ℚ (1.11).
- **Transitivity.** Explicit word: `Rz(φ+π/2) Rx(θ) e_z = (sinθ cosφ, sinθ sinφ, cosθ)`, identically in θ and φ (1.07).
  - The boundary states of `ball3` are exactly the unit sphere. The ⇒ direction is the argument inside
    `supportingEffectComplete_ball3` (KF:1056–1062), which concerns only the body. For ⇐, take `y = −x`.
  - The stabilizer of `e_z` is `Rz(ℝ) ⊆ G_D` (1.09).
- **Mutation controls.** Each mutated `J` below fails D9 and leaves `G_D` with Lie dimension 1:
  - `J = Rz(π/2)` gives orbit of `e_z` = `{e_z}` (1.12);
  - `J = diag(1,−1,−1)` gives orbit `{±e_z}` (1.13).
- **Every drive on `ball3`** (2.N1–2.N2, written; exact instances 2.01–2.02):
  1. An affine bijection preserving `ball3` in both directions is orthogonal.
  2. By D2, D3 and D6, `flow` is a nontrivial continuous homomorphism ℝ → SO(3), so its image is a full circle about
     some axis `a`.
  3. D9 forces `J a ∉ {±a}`. Otherwise `J R_a(θ) J⁻¹ = R_a(±θ)` would be a flow member.
  4. Two full circles about distinct axes generate SO(3). Cap argument: `G·a` is a union of closed caps of fixed
     radius `2∠(a, Ja)` about its own points, so it is open and closed in S², hence all of S². Citation-level
     alternative: Lowenthal 1971.
- **D3 is load-bearing** for this general statement (2.N3, written).
  - Take an additive `φ` with `φ(ℝ) = ℚπ` (Hamel basis, `φ(1) = π`), and set `flow t = Rz(φ t)`, `t₀ = 1`,
    `J = cyc3`.
  - This passes every field except `flow_continuous`, and generates a countable group, which cannot be transitive on S².
  - D3 is not load-bearing for the dimension ≤ 2 exclusions (Thread F, B5/B6).

## §2. Abstract drivable bodies: what follows from the fields alone

**Follows (written, 3.N4).** Assume `Ω` compact and convex, of finite affine dimension.
- `G_D|Ω` is non-abelian (by D9).
- The flow moves some extreme point, and that point's flow orbit is a non-constant continuous curve of extreme points.
  So `ext Ω` is uncountable, which recovers the polytope exclusion.
- The closure of `G_D|Ω` is compact, and orthogonal for an averaged inner product centred at the centroid.

**Dimension classification (written).** Let `Ω` be compact, convex and drivable, of affine dimension `n`. Steps:
1. Restrict to `aff Ω`. The flow and `J` map `Ω` onto itself, by D4 with `flow(−t) = flow(t)⁻¹`, and by D7/D8.
2. Average an inner product over the compact closure of `Aut(Ω)`, centred at the centroid.

By dimension:
- **n ≤ 2.**
  - n ≤ 1: the flow is trivial on `Ω`, so D6 fails.
  - n = 2: the flow is a full circle, so `Ω` is a disk, and `O(2)` conjugates `R(t)` to `R(±t)`, so D9 fails (3.12).
    This is Thread F B6, re-derived.
- **n = 3.**
  - The flow is a full circle about an axis `a`. D9 gives `J a ≠ ±a` (the §1 argument verbatim), and so
    `G_D ⊇ SO(3)`.
  - An SO(3)-invariant compact convex set is a ball about the centroid. So `Ω` is an **ellipsoid**, and `G_D` is
    transitive on its boundary.
  - No hypothesis beyond the drive fields, compactness, convexity and dimension is used.
- **n ≥ 4: FALSE.** Exact checks:
  - **Bidisk `D×D ⊂ ℝ⁴`** (3.01–3.06).
    - Drive `R(t)⊕I`, `N = R(π)⊕I`, `J` = block swap. `G_D ⊇ SO(2)×SO(2)` is transitive on the extreme points
      `S¹×S¹`.
    - `(e₁,e₁)` and `(e₁,0)` are both boundary states. The first is extreme; the second is a midpoint, so it is not
      extreme. Hence even `Aut(Ω)` is not transitive on the boundary.
  - **Cone over B³ in ℝ⁴**, `{0 ≤ h ≤ 1, |x| ≤ 1−h}` (3.07–3.10).
    - Drive `Rz⊕1` with `J = cyc3⊕1`.
    - The apex is the only isolated extreme point, so `Aut(Ω)` fixes it. Even extreme-point transitivity fails, for
      `Aut(Ω)` as well as `G_D`.
  - **B⁴ with drive `Rz⊕1`, `J = cyc3⊕1`** (3.11).
    - The body *is* a ball, but `G_D ≅ SO(3)` preserves `x₄` and so is not transitive on S³.
    - So "Ω is a ball" and "the drive group is boundary-transitive" separate in dimension ≥ 4.

**Sharp statement.** For compact convex bodies:
- drivability implies boundary transitivity of `G_D` exactly in affine dimension ≤ 3 (vacuously in ≤ 2);
- it fails in every dimension ≥ 4. The three countermodels embed in any higher dimension by adding fixed coordinates.

## §3. Sourcing: which landed object supplies the drive

**Field-neutral: none.** The candidates, one by one:
- `ball3Drive` is a semantic control on a stipulated body. The KF header (lines 6–9) says the module "sources
  nothing".
- Every landed field-neutral body constructor is a finite stage (`FiniteStage.states` KF:84), that is, a polytope.
  Polytopes are not drivable (Thread F B5; written).
- `DrivesElementary` (SS:77) and `QuantumArchitecture` (SS:86) are hypotheses over ℂ matrix carriers.
  - The only landed class satisfying `DrivesElementary` is `fullClass` (SS:147), which is QM by definition.
  - The substratum class fails it: `substratumClass_not_drivesElementary` SC:370 and `substratum_residual` SC:383 are
    kernel results.

**Group-level form of the residual on the qubit Bloch ball** (imported ℂ; exact 4.01–4.03):
- Diagonal phases act as `Rz`, and the swap acts as `diag(1,−1,−1)`. Every monomial unitary of `Fin 2` therefore sends
  `e_z` to `±e_z`.
- So the substratum group acts through `O(2)_z`, and the orbit of a pole is two points: not transitive.
- A continuous flow inside this group is a `Rz`-flow. A monomial `J` then fails D9, which matches Thread G result 5.
- The read-write family (ReadWriteControl:87) is permutation-valued. A continuous map ℝ → a finite group is constant,
  so it carries no nontrivial flow (4.N4).

**What the landed matrix objects do assemble (imported ℂ; exact 4.04–4.09).** Take the CT2 gate flow
`gateFlow σ t = unit (permMat σ) t` (LA:47; group law LA:75; `gateFlow_one` LA:84; region level `swapQ`
SwapLayer:372 with `swapQ_add_time` SwapLayer:494). On the two configurations exchanged by a swap gate it acts on
their Bloch ball as `Rx(πt)` (4.05). Then:
- `N = gateFlow 1` is the substratum's own swap gate.
- `J` = the quarter phase, which the substratum supplies (`phaseOperator_supplied` SC:333) and which acts as `Rz(−π/2)`,
  conjugates `Rx` to `Ry`. So D9 holds (4.08), and the generated group is SO(3), transitive on S² (4.09).
- The transition flow is the gate flow up to a global monomial phase: `e^{−itX} = e^{−it} gateFlow(2t/π)` (4.07).

In this assembly every generator lies in the substratum class except **the gate flow at non-integer times**. At
`t = 1/2` it has two nonzero entries in one column (LA:138; 4.06), so it is not monomial.

**Kernel counterpart at matrix level (DERIVABLE, a one-line composition, not landed).**
`exactReachability_of_hcontrol` (OR:625) applied to `transition_hermitian` (LRS:211), `ctrl_unitary` (LRS:293) and
`hControl_star i₀ j₁ h` (LRS:352) gives `ExactReachability (transition i₀ j₁) (ctrl i₀)`. That is: every unitary is a
finite word in the transition flow, permutations with the quarter phase, and global phases. `reachable` (RS:136) is
`Subgroup.closure`, the abstract generated subgroup, so no topological closure is involved.

**What remains open, and why it is not field-neutral.**
- No landed theorem identifies the non-integer times of the CT2 interpolation with substratum evolution.
  - SI's header lists "the continuous-time extension" as an open distinction.
  - SOD's header states that the composite drive is a path and not a group.
- The interpolation is not unique: every odd branch `e^{iπ(2k+1)t}` reaches the gate at `t = 1` (4.12).
- It uses ℂ. The single-site binary swap gate is an odd permutation (`det = −1`), and `det A(1) = det(A(1/2))² > 0`
  for any real one-parameter group, with no continuity used. So it lies on no real-linear flow of the permutation
  representation (4.10–4.11).
  - Scope of this remark: region swaps on 2 and 3 binary sites are even, so it is not a general obstruction.
- **Named open target:** `elementaryDrivability_of_substratum`. The elementary (two-level) body of the completion
  carries an `ElementaryDrivability` whose flow is induced by substratum dynamics. Given affine dimension 3, §2 then
  yields boundary transitivity and the ellipsoid. A Bloch translation lemma (Thread F, B12) is also needed to move
  matrix-level objects into KF vocabulary; none exists.

## §4. Does drivability force a ball? (literature, citation level)

- **Dimension 3: yes**, up to an affine map (§2, written). The only consumer-side link is landed: Lemma B
  (`eq_closedBall_of_frontier_subset_sphere` KF:770) turns "frontier on one sphere" into "closed ball". Boundary
  transitivity by an isometry group fixing the centre supplies exactly that premise.
- **Dimension ≥ 4: no** (bidisk, cone, B³×[−1,1], B⁴ with a non-transitive drive).
- **Citations:**
  - Affine automorphisms preserve the John/Löwner ellipsoid. F. John 1948; P. M. Gruber, *Convex and Discrete
    Geometry* (2007).
  - Hence a convex body whose affine automorphism group is transitive on its boundary is an ellipsoid.
  - Compact groups transitive on spheres: Montgomery–Samelson 1943; Borel 1949–50.
  - Two circle subgroups generate SO(3): Lowenthal 1971.
- **Qutrit control (5.01, 5.N1).** Even in the imported kinematics, the `d = 3` quantum body is not boundary-transitive
  under any affine automorphism: minimal faces of boundary states have dimension 0 or 3. So boundary transitivity can
  only be posed for the two-level body.

## Surprising, or worth cross-propagating

1. **Dimension is the whole story.** For compact bodies, drivability *alone* gives the ellipsoid and boundary
   transitivity in affine dimension 3, and gives neither in dimension ≥ 4. So the transitivity half of K∞-R costs
   nothing once the elementary body has dimension 3, and nothing in the drive fields fixes the dimension.
   - Route caution (assumption-watch): NB-1/K1 delivers `d ∈ {1,3}` only *for balls*
     (`NativeGateBall`, ROADMAP K1). Using it to supply dimension 3, and then dimension 3 to get the ball, is circular.
2. **The landed objects already assemble a transitive drive in the ℂ regime.** It uses the CT2 gate flow with the
   substratum's phase as `J`, and the substratum's own swap gate as `N`. The residual of SC:383 / Thread G thereby
   narrows to one named object: the non-integer-time members of `unit g t`. That object is ℂ-valued and branch-dependent.
3. **D3 matters for transitivity even on `ball3`**, through the Hamel countermodel, although the exclusions of dimension
   ≤ 2 do not use it.
4. Boundary transitivity fails for the `d = 3` quantum body, so it is a two-level-body property, not a completion-wide
   one.
