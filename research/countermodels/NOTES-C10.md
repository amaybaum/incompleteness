### C10.2 — the mechanism, for every simple-spectrum case [W, EBF [A]]
Let `G ∋ cnot` be compact (unitary/antiunitary conjugations) with identity component a torus `H₀` of simple spectrum.
`G` normalizes `H₀`, so it permutes the eigenlines. If some `G`-orbit `O` of eigenlines contains no product vector, put
`m = max_{b′ ∈ O} max_{ψ product} |⟨b′|ψ⟩|² < 1` (the `H₀`-part fixes each eigenline, so `m` is the largest overlap of
`b ∈ O` with `G·SEP`) and `z_{b′} = I − c P_{b′}`, `c = min(2, 1/m) > 1`. The set `G·SEP ∪ {z_{b′} : b′ ∈ O}` is
self-positive (`1 − c|⟨b′|gψ⟩|² ≥ 1 − cm ≥ 0`; `⟨z_{b′}, z_{b″}⟩ = 4 − 2c ≥ 0` for distinct, orthogonal eigenlines), so
EBF gives a `G`-invariant self-dual cone with H1–H3 containing `z_b ∉ Q3`: **exotic (EXOTIC-E)**, CONDITIONAL on EBF.
It is **explicit (EXOTIC-X)** when `O = {b}` (single-defect cone, as in C10.1) or when every `b′ ∈ O` is maximally
entangled (`c = 2`: an orthonormal Bell-type set, Theorem S / C4.2 item 2).
So B3.C holds for every simple-spectrum torus with an eigenline orbit free of products. This contains HO-2's "no
product eigenline" case and every case with one to three product eigenlines in which some entangled eigenline is not
`G`-equivalent to a product eigenline (for instance whenever `G` fixes an entangled eigenline, as in C10.1). It does not
reach the cases where `G` carries every entangled eigenline onto a product one.

## OPEN (residual region of B3.C after this node)

- Degenerate 2-tori. Not attempted.
