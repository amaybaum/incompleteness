## 1. The density certificate

Let G ⊆ O(3) contain a rotation U of infinite order with axis n, and some V ∈ G with V n not parallel to n. Then
V U V⁻¹ ∈ G ∩ SO(3) is a rotation of infinite order about V n. The closure of G ∩ SO(3) is an infinite closed
subgroup of SO(3); in a subgroup of type SO(2)_m or O(2)_m every element of infinite order is a rotation about m
(the other component of O(2)_m consists of half-turns); two infinite-order rotations about non-parallel axes
exclude both types; so the closure is SO(3) [W + L]. Infinite order is certified exactly: a rotation by θ of finite
order has e^{iθ} a root of unity, so 2 cos θ = tr − 1 is an algebraic integer; for tr − 1 = a + b√2 with b ≠ 0 the
minimal polynomial is x² − 2a x + (a² − 2b²), and a coefficient outside ℤ certifies infinite order [W]. Both
certificates are checked exactly, and the countercontrol R01² (a quarter turn) fails the infinite-order test as it
must [X CC2].

## 2. Why the Hadamard pair is not dense

H01 and H12 are reflections (eigenvalues 1, 1, −1). A group generated by two involutions is dihedral: its
orientation-preserving part is the cyclic group generated by U′ = H01H12, and both reflections fix the line where
their mirror planes meet, which is the axis of U′. So the closure is the set of orthogonal maps fixing n′: the circle
of rotations about n′ together with the reflections in planes through n′ — infinite, one-dimensional, proper [W],
with the fixing checked exactly [X D2; CC1 confirms that the pair's own generators do not move n′].
**Correction of reading (O3-T7, audit X5).** O3-T7's statement — the closure of what two overlapping balanced mixers
generate contains a one-parameter subgroup — holds for both pairs. Density is a further property: it holds for the
kernel's rotation datum, and for any pair once the exchanges are available, but not for the Hadamard pair alone,
whose infinite-order product (X5) generates only a circle and its reflections.

## 3. The quarter phase and SU(3)

su(3) = so(3) ⊕ p with p = {iS : S real symmetric traceless}, and so(3) acts on p by the five-dimensional
irreducible representation (traceless symmetric 3 × 3 matrices under conjugation by SO(3)). A subalgebra h with
so(3) ⊆ h ⊆ su(3) is so(3) ⊕ (h ∩ p), and h ∩ p is an so(3)-submodule of p, hence 0 or p [W]. If the closure of a
group contains SO(3) and S0, its Lie algebra (Cartan [L]) contains so(3) and Ad(S0) so(3), which leaves so(3) [X D4],
so its intersection with su(3) is su(3), and the closure contains SU(3). With the determinants of the generators in
{±1, i}, the closure is {U : det(U)⁴ = 1}. Every unitary of ℂ³ is a phase times an element of SU(3), so the group
generated by the balanced rotation datum, the exchanges and the quarter phase gives dense control up to phase on three
states. The groups are countable, so no closure is attained: density without exactness, as for the kernel's
irrational fixed gate (`denseUnitaryControl_of_fixedGate` [K DiscreteCompletion.lean:1522],
`fixedGateTheory_not_qm` [K :1948]).
